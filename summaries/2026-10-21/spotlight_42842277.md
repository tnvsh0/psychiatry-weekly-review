### Temporal, Geographic, and Polysubstance Patterns in Orphine Analogue Detections
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhu DT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 7

INTRODUCTION Benzimidazol-2-ones, also known as “orphine analogs” or simply “orphines,” are synthetic opioids traced to pharmaceutical discovery programs in the 1960s, beginning with compounds such as bezitramide and R-6890 (spirochlorphine). 1 – 3 These drugs are μ-opioid receptor agonists, with some analogs estimated to be approximately 10 times more potent than fentanyl, and can produce classic opioid toxicity, including respiratory depression. 2 – 5 Orphine analogs first appeared in the US illicit drug supply in the third quarter of 2020 (2020 Q3) with brorphine. 2 As nitazene-class benzimidazole opioids have proliferated, 6 scheduling actions targeting these substances may have increased market pressure toward new synthetic opioids, including N -propionitrile chlorphine, colloquially known as “cyclorphine.” 2 , 7 Given limited data on this emerging drug class, we examined temporal, geographic, and polysubstance patterns in orphine analog detections. METHODS This cross-sectional study used data on orphine-positive biospecimens from the Center for Forensic Science Research and Education’s NPS Discovery program, a drug early warning system. 8 Data from 2020 Q3 through 2026 Q2 were analyzed. NPS Discovery tests biospecimens (e.g., blood, urine, serum, plasma) submitted by forensic laboratories, medical examiners, coroners, and clinical and public health agency partners. Drug identifications were made using liquid chromatography quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (LC-QTOF-MS), with supplemental mass spectrometry platforms used as needed. This analysis focused on five orphine analogs: brorphine, chlorphine, spirochlorphine, N -propionitrile chlorphine, and SR-17018 (5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine). We described orphine-positive biospecimen counts by quarter and analog, US Census region, state, and co-detected psychoactive drug categories ( eMethods in Supplement 1 ). Co-positivity was defined as the presence of one or more additional psychoactive drug in an orphine-positive biospecimen. We also identified biospecimens in which naloxone was detected. This study was deemed exempt by the Virginia Commonwealth University Institutional Review Board under 45 CFR §46. The STROBE reporting guideline was followed. RESULTS From 2020 Q3 through 2026 Q2, we identified 142 orphine-positive biospecimens, representing 1.0% of total biospecimens (n=142/14,261), 0.6% of fentanyl-positive biospecimens (n=51/9,004), and 3.0% of nitazene-positive biospecimens tested during this period (n=13/430). The most commonly detected analog was N -propionitrile chlorphine (n=102; 71.8%), followed by brorphine (n=38; 26.8%), spirochlorphine (n=6; 4.2%), 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine (n=3; 2.1%), and chlorphine (n=1; 0.7%) ( Figure 1A ). Quarterly counts showed two distinct waves: an early wave of 36 orphine-positive biospecimens in 2020 Q3, all involving brorphine, followed by a later wave from 2025 Q4 through 2026 Q2 dominated by N -propionitrile chlorphine, present in 100 of 102 biospecimens (98.0%) during this period. Orphine-positive biospecimens were concentrated in the South (n=75; 52.8%) and Midwest (n=53; 37.3%), with fewer in the Northeast (n=8; 5.6%) and West (n=6; 4.2%) ( Figure 1B ). Across 22 US states, detections were most common in Tennessee (n=50; 35.2%), Illinois (n=37; 26.1%), Texas (n=14; 9.9%), and Minnesota (n=10; 7.0%). Tennessee and Texas detections all involved N -propionitrile chlorphine, whereas Minnesota detections all involved brorphine. Illinois had a mixed pattern, with 24 brorphine-positive and 13 N -propionitrile chlorphine-positive biospecimens. Polysubstance involvement was common ( Figure 2 ). Orphine-positive biospecimens were most often co-detected with benzodiazepine-related compounds (n=53; 37.3%). Frequently co-detected synthetic opioids included fentanyl-related compounds (n=51; 35.9%) and nitazene-related compounds (n=13; 9.2%). Co-detection of stimulants was also common, including methamphetamine/other amphetamines (n=51; 35.9%) and cocaine-related compounds (n=29; 20.4%). Other co-detections included medetomidine-related compounds (n=16; 11.3%) and xylazine (n=11; 7.7%). Naloxone was detected in 33 orphine-positive biospecimens (23.2%). DISCUSSION This study documents the proliferation of orphine analogs across 22 US states, with a sharp rise since late 2025 driven by N -propionitrile chlorphine, frequently co-detected with fentanyl-related compounds. Naloxone appeared in nearly one-fourth of orphine-positive biospecimens, consistent with attempted reversal of opioid-induced respiratory depression. 4 These findings provide timely evidence of orphine analogs as an emerging drug threat in the US illicit drug supply. One possible explanation for this shift is China’s generic scheduling of nitazene analogs in July 2025, which may have created an opening for the introduction of alternative synthetic opioid classes, including orphine analogs. 2 , 6 , 7 Our study showed that N -propionitrile chlorphine-positive biospecimens rose rapidly immediately following this regulatory intervention, while only a minority of orphine-positive biospecimens also contained nitazene-related compounds, consistent with this market substitution hypothesis. This study has limitations. NPS Discovery data are based on both random and for-cause testing of submitted biospecimens. 8 Although biospecimens can be received from across all 50 states, submissions are not systematically received from all jurisdictions; thus, findings may not be nationally representative. 8 These findings have important implications for clinicians, public health agencies, and policymakers. Orphine analogs are not routinely included in forensic or clinical toxicology testing, fentanyl and nitazene test strips do not reliably detect orphine analogs, and no orphine-specific test strips are available. 2 , 7 Clinical guidance for managing orphine-involved overdoses, development of orphine-specific test strips, broader access to confirmatory testing, and naloxone distribution remain key priorities. As several internationally reported analogs were not observed in this dataset, including fluorphine and iodorphine, 7 proactive surveillance is critical to monitor their potential entry into the US illicit drug supply.
