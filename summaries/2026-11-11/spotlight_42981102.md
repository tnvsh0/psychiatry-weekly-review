### Mobile Units for Integrated Addiction and HIV Prevention Services for People Who Inject Drugs: The HPTN 094 Randomized Clinical Trial
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Shoptaw S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Key Points Question Does providing 26 weeks of integrated HIV prevention and addiction services for people who inject drugs using peer navigation delivered in a mobile unit produce superior outcomes compared to peer navigation to existing standard-of-care community-based services alone? Findings In this randomized clinical trial, HIV prevention and addiction outcomes were similar between study arms, though the intervention showed a greater reduction in all-cause mortality compared with standard of care. Meaning The findings suggest that integrated mobile service delivery models may reduce all-cause mortality for people who inject drugs.

This randomized clinical trial evaluates the use of preexposure prophylaxis for HIV and medications for opioid use disorder among individuals who inject drugs.

Importance Integrated models to deliver HIV preexposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and medication for opioid use disorder (MOUD) for people who inject drugs are needed. Objective To determine whether integrated PrEP and MOUD services delivered on a mobile unit with manualized peer navigation improved PrEP and MOUD use compared with peer navigation to existing community-based services. Design, Setting, and Participants This 2-group, open-label, randomized clinical trial was conducted between May 2021 and September 2024 in Houston, Texas; Los Angeles, California; New York, New York; Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; and Washington, DC, among people aged 18 years and older currently injecting drugs, without HIV, with OUD, and not taking MOUD but willing to consider MOUD. Interventions The intervention arm (n = 203) consisted of 26 weeks of manualized peer navigation plus integrated PrEP, MOUD, and limited clinical and supportive services delivered on a mobile unit. The active control arm (n = 206) consisted of 26 weeks of manualized peer navigation to available community-based PrEP, MOUD, harm reduction, and supportive services. Main Outcomes and Measures The co–primary outcomes were PrEP use and MOUD use at 26 weeks. Secondary outcomes included PrEP and MOUD use at 52 weeks, HIV incidence, and all-cause mortality. Results Of 842 people who inject drugs who were screened, 409 were randomized: 134 (32.8%) female; 146 (11.2%) aged 30 years and younger vs older than 30 years. At the 26-week visit, the intervention and active control arms were similar for confirmed PrEP use: 11/203 (5.4%) vs 6/206 (2.9%) and MOUD use: 30/203 (14.8%) vs 22/206 (10.7%), respectively. At the 52-week visit, intervention and active control arms were similar for confirmed PrEP and MOUD: 4/203 (2.0%) vs 4/206 (1.9%) and MOUD use: 27/203 (13.3%) vs 29/206 (14.1%). One HIV acquisition occurred in the intervention and 2 in the active control arms; HIV incidence rate of 0.9 per 100 person-years (95% CI, 0.2-2.6). Four deaths were confirmed in the intervention arm and 13 in the active control arm (hazard ratio, 0.29; 95% CI, 0.1-0.9; P = .03). Conclusions and Relevance In this randomized clinical trial, mobile delivery of integrated HIV prevention, addiction, and navigation services did not improve primary outcomes beyond peer navigation alone. The significantly lower all-cause death rate at 52 weeks in the intervention arm bears replication for delivering integrated PrEP and MOUD services to people who inject drugs. Trial Registration ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04804072

Introduction Opioid use disorder (OUD) is a major driver of HIV acquisition in the US and a leading contributor to mortality among people who use opioids, whether alone or in combination with stimulants. Beyond HIV acquisition and overdose, people who inject drugs experience elevated risks of comorbid conditions, including hepatitis C, bacterial infections related to nonsterile injection practices, injection-related wounds, and other sexually transmitted infections. People who inject drugs face substantial barriers to accessing key services and support necessary for consistent engagement in HIV prevention and addiction treatment. These barriers include poverty, unstable housing, food insecurity, and limited access to health care, all of which impede regular use of HIV preexposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and medication for OUD (MOUD). Siloed medical and addiction treatment systems further restrict access by fragmenting care for primary health services, HIV PrEP, and MOUD. Mobile units offer a way to address multiple barriers faced by people who inject drugs in accessing integrated services, including HIV prevention, MOUD, primary care, and social services. Mobile unit-based care differs from care based in conventional brick-and-mortar settings for people who inject drugs along key features of location, integration, immediacy, and service proximity. Yet, rigorous evaluation of mobile unit-based interventions for people who inject drugs remains limited. This study tests whether advantages of integrated HIV and MOUD services using a mobile unit combined with peer navigation would produce statistically significant improvements in primary outcomes of PrEP and MOUD compared to peer navigation to existing standard of care, community-based services alone for people who inject drugs in 5 US cities. Methods Trial Design and Oversight HPTN 094 was a 2-arm, individually randomized, controlled, open-label study that evaluated whether an integrated intervention delivered in a mobile unit improved use of HIV PrEP and MOUD at 26 and 52 weeks ( Figure 1 ). The trial protocol and statistical analysis plan are in Supplement 1 . A single institutional review board, Advarra, provided oversight of the research. Biannual data monitoring oversight was provided by an independent study monitoring committee at the HPTN. All participants provided written informed consent. The study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials ( CONSORT ) reporting guideline. Participants Eligibility criteria included current injection drug use, being aged 18 years and older, the ability to provide informed consent, not currently using but willing to use MOUD, self-reported sharing injection equipment, or having condomless sex in the last 3 months with people with HIV or persons of unknown HIV serostatus, and being able and willing to provide adequate contact information of a person or persons who could help locate participants for follow-up visits. Participants were excluded from the study if they self-reported MOUD in the past 30 days as part of a treatment program or if they were enrolled in another interventional study. Although people with HIV were recruited and randomized, enrollment numbers were insufficient to support rigorous analysis in that group. Study Sites HPTN 094 was conducted in Houston, Texas; Los Angeles, California; New York, New York; Philadelphia, Pennsylvania; and Washington, DC. The cities represented diverse large urban contexts with substantial HIV and overdose risks among people who inject drugs. Study sites were selected based on prior experience conducting research on HIV prevention and addiction for people who inject drugs, on geographic diversity, and on HIV prevalence in people who inject drugs. Mobile Units Los Angeles, Houston, and New York chose identical 33-ft medical units that contained an examination table, space for processing laboratory samples, and a toilet; Philadelphia and Washington, DC, chose 26-ft, similarly equipped medical units. Data collection during screening, enrollment, week 26, and week 52 occurred in the mobile unit for all participants.
