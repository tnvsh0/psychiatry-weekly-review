### Awareness-dependent Attentional Templates: Neural Mechanism Differentiation Driven by Visual Working Memory Load
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhang Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 22

Visual working memory (VWM) guides attention via internal attentional templates, but whether this relies on a "single" or "multiple" templates and how awareness and VWM load regulate its neural mechanisms remain unclear. This study used a VWM-guided visual search paradigm with backward masking (awareness manipulation) and EEG. Behavioral results showed that aware VWM supported multi-template attention guidance, while subjectively unaware VWM only allowed single-template guidance. EEG revealed that alpha oscillations over posterior electrodes indexed attention allocation. Neural mechanisms differed by VWM load: Single-item maintenance relied on posterior alpha-alpha phase coupling, while multi-item maintenance depended on theta-alpha cross-frequency coupling between anterior and bilateral posterior regions. These findings clarify how awareness regulates VWM attentional template mode and reveal load-specific neural coupling, providing evidence for understanding working memory-attention-awareness interactions.

### The Effect of Perceptually Challenging Conditions on Speech and Sign Language Perception
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Figuerola CT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 22

Language is often processed in perceptually challenging conditions. Although the neural correlates of speech processing in such conditions have been well studied, this is not the case for sign language. Thus, it is unknown whether the neural mechanisms involved in this process are comparable across language modalities. One reason for the lack of studies in sign language might be attributed to the difficulties in adapting perceptual manipulations from the context of speech to sign language. In the present study, we aim to address the gap in the literature by investigating whether the neural correlates for processing language presented in perceptually challenging conditions are similar across the visual and auditory modalities, using manipulations of background noise and spectral degradation that are comparable between languages and a within-subject design. We recruited 22 hearing signers who did a perceptual attention task while in an MRI scanner, in which the signal is either clear, presented in background noise, or spectrally degraded. Overall, our results suggest that language processing in perceptually challenging conditions relies primarily on modality-specific mechanisms rather than a shared, modality-independent neural system. Whereas speech processing appeared relatively robust to perceptual degradation, sign language recruited additional visual indicating modality-dependent compensatory strategies. These findings refine current neurobiological models of language by demonstrating that perceptual challenge interacts fundamentally with language modality.

### Semantic generation masks value directed remembering in memory but not metamemory: Evidence from truth-telling and lie fabrication
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kaya S, Besken M | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 26

Value-directed remembering (VDR) is typically robust, with people recalling high- over low-value information. The current study examined whether this selectivity survives when value must compete with a demanding conceptual manipulation requiring retrieval and manipulation of information from semantic memory: generating truthful answers versus fabricating lies in response to general-knowledge questions. Across five experiments, participants generated truthful answers or fabricated lies in response to general-knowledge questions tagged as low (1 point) or high value (10 points), provided item-by-item judgments of learning (JOLs), and received a free recall task; Experiments 1-4 used mixed (within-subject) lists, and Experiment 5 used pure (between-subjects) lists. Lie fabrication reliably reduced JOLs yet boosted free recall when mixed with truth-telling; in a pure-list design, lying and truth-telling produced equivalent recall while JOLs still favored truth. Crucially, value increased JOLs but never improved recall in any experiment. A single-paper meta-analysis of JOLs confirmed significant effects of both encoding condition and value, showing that participants can use value in their memory predictions even while fabricating lies. In contrast, the meta-analysis of recall showed a reliable benefit of lie fabrication over truth overall but no effect of high over low value. Thus, the conceptual demands of the encoding task eliminated value-based memory advantages. Taken together, these findings suggest that value cues reliably influence metacognitive judgments but do not necessarily translate into improved memory when encoding involves a concurrent resource-intensive conceptual task These results align with an effortful encoding view, where value guides selective memory only when sufficient cognitive resources remain available, and demanding conceptual tasks can deplete resources, causing value cues to be overlooked.

### Time pressure amplifies gender differences in mental rotation through shifts in evidence accumulation and response caution
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu Y, Lourenco SF | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 23

Meta-analytic data provide evidence for a gender difference favoring men on mental rotation tasks, especially under time pressure. Why might time pressure exaggerate this gender difference? Here we used drift diffusion modeling (DDM) to examine potential mechanisms underlying gender differences in mental rotation. Adult participants (N = 160; 80 men, 80 women) performed a mental rotation task with two levels of difficulty under conditions that emphasized either accuracy or speed. DDM parameters-including drift rates, decision thresholds, and non-decision times-were analyzed. We found robust gender differences in decision thresholds when speed was emphasized, with women showing smaller thresholds (less evidence accumulation) than men. Drift rates, an index of processing efficiency, showed weaker and less consistent gender differences; women's drift rates decreased under time pressure but men's remained unchanged. No reliable gender differences emerged in non-decision times. Implications of time pressure on gender differences in mental rotation performance are discussed in relation to affect and strategy use.

### The relationship between product-based creativity and ADHD traits in children and the associations with later well-being
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fang H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 25

Creativity is increasingly recognized as a positive aspect among adults with ADHD and those with high ADHD scores, but evidence in youth is limited, particularly regarding product-based creativity. It also remains unclear whether higher creativity benefits the lower well-being typically associated with ADHD traits. This study investigated associations between ADHD traits and product-based creativity in children, as well as longitudinal associations with well-being. Data were sourced from the Twins Early Development Study, a population-based sample of twins. ADHD traits (age 8), product-based creativity (age 9, writing task), and well-being (age 16) of 1,170 children were assessed. Correlation and regression analyses were used to explore the associations of ADHD trait dimensions (inattentiveness and hyperactivity/impulsivity) with writing-based creativity. Moderation analyses were conducted to examine whether this writing-based creativity moderated the association between childhood ADHD traits and later well-being. Additionally, we conducted exploratory analyses on children meeting screening criteria for suspected ADHD. We observed a small negative correlation between inattentiveness and writing-based creativity, which remained significant after controlling for hyperactivity/impulsivity, sex, IQ, reading, and writing. The writing-based creativity did not moderate the negative association between ADHD traits and well-being, nor did we observe differences in well-being between high-creativity and low-creativity groups among children meeting screening criteria for suspected ADHD. Future research utilizing diverse creativity measures beyond writing tasks is warranted to explore the developmental relationship between ADHD traits, creativity, and well-being. Additionally, our results highlight that taking both a creativity-domain-specific and a lifespan perspective is needed when studying and understanding ADHD-related positive aspects.
