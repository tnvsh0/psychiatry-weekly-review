### Longitudinal associations between maternal emotional states during pregnancy and infant temperament across the first 24 months postpartum
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wong RS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 24

This study examined the associations between maternal emotional states during pregnancy and infant temperament across the first 24 months postpartum in 1,600 Chinese mother-infant dyads in Hong Kong (maternal  Mage  = 33.42,  SD  = 4.10; 53.3% male infants). During pregnancy (T0), mothers completed questionnaires on demographics, depressive symptoms, and positive affect. At 6 months postpartum (T1), they repeated the depressive symptoms questionnaire and completed questionnaires on mother-infant bonding and infant temperament; the same questionnaires were administered again at 12 months (T2) and 24 months (T3) postpartum. After adjusting for demographic covariates, T0 maternal depressive symptoms were associated with higher T3 offspring negative affectivity ( β  = 0.17,  p  < .001), whereas T0 maternal positive affect was associated with higher T3 offspring surgency ( β  = 0.21,  p  < .001) and effortful control ( β  = 0.24,  p  < .001). Maternal postnatal depressive symptoms and perceived bonding difficulties explained much of these associations, highlighting the importance of maternal well-being across the prenatal and postnatal periods in shaping mother-infant bonding and early socioemotional development.

### Dynamic analysis of mother-preschooler interactions: Understanding its role in the associations between maternal emotion dysregulation and child psychopathology
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vanwoerden S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 23

Theory and research highlight the role of maternal emotion socialization in the development of emotion dysregulation and associated psychopathology in offspring. This study described and quantified emotion socialization dynamics (i.e., mutual negativity and positivity) between mothers and their preschool-aged children, testing associations between dyadic dynamics and maternal emotion dysregulation and examining the predictive power of dyadic dynamics for symptoms of child psychopathology. Participants were 76 mother-child dyads (mothers: 22-46 years old,  M  = 33.17 (4.83); children 36-47 months old,  M  = 42.40 (3.78), 55.6% female, 47% racial/ethnic minoritized status) who completed frustration-eliciting and naturalistic play tasks. Child emotions and mothers' emotion socialization behaviors were coded. Mutual negativity during play was positively associated with mother-rated child aggression ( β  = 0.25,  p  = .017), whereas mutual positivity during play was negatively associated with teacher-rated attention ( β  = 0.35,  p  = .001) and internalizing problems ( β  = -0.25,  p  = .011). Findings suggest that mother-child dynamics during play may have unique associations with child mental health symptoms and highlight the utility of examining emotion socialization as a dyadic process.

### Foster care intervention protects cognitive control skills in young adults exposed to institutional rearing
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Valadez EA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 23

Prior work from the Bucharest Early Intervention Project (BEIP) suggests that institutional care impairs cognitive control, whereas foster care may help preserve these skills. However, it remains unclear whether these effects persist into adulthood. We examined EEG data from a flanker task administered at BEIP's 22-year follow-up. Participants ( N  = 127; Age:  M  = 23.5 years,  SD  = 1.0, range = 21.4-25.6; 61% Romanian ethnicity) were initially enrolled during childhood while in institutional care in Bucharest, Romania and included individuals randomized to remain in institutional care (CAUG;  n  = 42; 50% female) or transition to foster care (FCG;  n  = 46; 54% female), along with never-institutionalized controls (NIG;  n  = 39; 67% female). At the 22-year follow-up, young adults in the FCG showed event-related potential (ERP) markers of performance monitoring comparable to the NIG but different from the CAUG ( η2 p  = 0.06). In contrast, the CAUG showed convergent evidence of disrupted cognitive control, including poorer behavioral performance and altered neural responses across ERP and time-frequency measures relative to the NIG. These neural disruptions were associated with lower IQ and elevated mental health symptoms. Overall, results highlight enduring effects of early caregiving on cognitive control in adulthood.

### Effects of parental stress-buffering on multiple measures of positive valence systems functioning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhou Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 23

Adolescence involves social reorientation toward peers, increasing exposure to interpersonal stressors and vulnerability to psychopathology. The current study investigated the potential buffering effects of high-quality parent-child relationships (PCRs) on the associations between peer stress exposure and multiple measures of positive valence systems (PVS) functioning. The study included 165 adolescents, ages 9-16 (81 girls,  M   age  = 11.93, 78.18% White). Interviews were conducted to gather data on PCR quality and peer stress exposure. Adolescents self-reported their reward sensitivity, and behavioral reward sensitivity was derived from a reward learning paradigm. The reward positivity (RewP) was recorded during three tasks using monetary, social, and food reward stimuli. Contrary to our predictions, adolescents who had higher-quality PCR showed a blunted monetary RewP ( β  = 0.17,  p  = .020) and reduced behavioral reward sensitivity ( β  = 0.15,  p  = .040), when also reporting high levels of peer stress exposure. No significant interaction was found with other measures as the outcomes. The results suggest that high-quality PCR may not uniformly buffer against the effects of peer stress exposure and highlight differential effects for different PVS measures. Future research across multiple units of analysis is needed to elucidate the complex pathway to PVS-related psychopathology during this critical developmental stage.

### Efficacy and effectiveness of parent training program as a transdiagnostic intervention: A meta-analysis of its impact on internalizing and externalizing problems in children
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Lee SH et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 22

Parent training programs (PTPs) aim to improve parenting skills and reduce children's behavioral and emotional problems. Given that recent research has extended PTPs' application from externalizing difficulties to internalizing symptoms - problem domains that frequently overlap in children - evaluating their effects together can clarify PTPs' broader impact. This meta-analysis synthesized findings from 34 randomized controlled trials involving children aged 3-12 years to examine the efficacy of PTPs and identify factors influencing treatment outcomes. Across studies, PTPs significantly reduced both internalizing ( g  = 0.28) and externalizing ( g  = 0.37) problems, yielding effect sizes comparable to those reported in prior parent training research. Importantly, PTPs also demonstrated significant cross-domain effects, indicating that programs targeting externalizing problems may also reduce internalizing problems, and vice versa. Moderator analyses indicated that only children's clinical diagnoses significantly predicted externalizing outcomes. These findings suggest that PTPs may be implemented flexibly across varying delivery conditions. Despite small overall effects, heterogeneity, and publication bias, the findings support the effectiveness and adaptability of PTPs. Future research should employ mechanism-informed, scalable designs to enhance accessibility and optimize treatment efficiency.

### The pathway to effortful control in adolescence: The role of parental personality, family environment, and genetic factors
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Child Development (Child Dev) *(IF: 5.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chen Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 24

Effortful control (EC) is the ability to intentionally inhibit a dominant response in service of goals. The intergenerational transmission of EC might represent a dynamic interaction between genetic and environmental factors, while few studies have explored this relationship in adolescence. We used a longitudinal adoption design to investigate the contributions of theoretically relevant and heritable family factors to EC from childhood to adolescence. Participants (N = 495; 42% female; 56% White) were from the Early Growth and Development Study, which began in 2002. Children's heritable predisposition was indexed by Birth Mothers' (BM) self-reported EC at 5 months. Primary and Secondary Adoptive Parents (AP1, AP2) reported their perceptions of home order/routines, parenting stress, and the APs' own EC at 4.5 years. Child EC was assessed via AP1 and AP2 reports at age 4.5 and by adolescent self-report at age 11. We found that AP1 and AP2 EC positively predicted home order/routines, which in turn predicted higher child EC at 4.5 and 11 years. AP2's EC negatively predicted parenting stress at age 4.5. Although these findings do not rule out genetic influences, they underscore the role of the home environment and distinct caregiver pathways in the intergenerational transmission of EC.

Effortful control (EC) is the ability to stop, think, and regulate behavior to meet goals. While many studies examine this temperament in early childhood, fewer follow children into adolescence. Using data from 495 adoptive families, this study examined how genetic and family environmental factors contribute to children's EC at age 11. Birth mothers' EC represents the genetic influence, while adoptive parents' EC and home environments represent environmental influence. We found that APs with stronger self-­regulation tended to provide more organized homes and experience less parenting stress when children were at age 4.5, which in turn predicted their children's better EC at age 11. These findings highlight the important role of home environments in children's self-regulation.

### Music listening and young children's mood and behavior: The parents as home DJs study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Child Development (Child Dev) *(IF: 5.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Cho E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

Using an experience sampling method (ESM), this study examined how parents incorporated music listening into daily routines and its perceived effects on children's mood and behavior. Parents (N = 562; 98% mothers; 79% White; 96% affiliated with an early childhood music program) of children aged 0-5 years completed a sign-up survey and a 7-day ESM protocol in 2021. Music listening was ubiquitous in family life, often used for entertainment and emotion regulation. In over 75% of episodes, parents reported noticeable-mostly positive-changes in their children. Mixed-effects models showed stronger effects when children liked the music and it was less familiar as well as when the children were already demonstrating active behavior and the music was more energetic and pleasant.

Music listening is a natural part of daily life with young children—but how can caregivers choose music that supports their child's mood and behavior regulation? This study followed 562 parents of children aged 0–5 over 1 week via daily online survey. Music listening was common across households, being often used for both entertainment and mood regulation purposes. In over 75% of music-listening episodes, parents reported noticeable, mostly positive changes in their child's mood. Recorded music was especially effective for mood regulation when children liked the music, when it was relatively unfamiliar, and when it was energetic and pleasant. Thoughtfully chosen music may serve as a simple and accessible tool for supporting children's emotional well-being in everyday family life.

### Is liking math enough? The effects of math interest and confidence on the math anxiety-performance relation
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Maghami-Sharif Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 24

It has been well established that higher math anxiety is associated with lower math performance. Thus, it is important to understand what individual-level factors might play a role in the math anxiety-performance relation to inform intervention efforts. Grounded in the expectancy-value theory and control-value theory with converging support from the broaden-and-build theory, we conceptualized math interest and confidence as stable positive attitudes and hypothesized that they would buffer the negative relation between math anxiety and both concurrent and later performance. Regression analyses were conducted in two samples of elementary school children ( n ₁ = 310;  n ₂ = 3,007) spanning kindergarten through third grade. We examined the interaction effects of math anxiety and math interest, as well as math anxiety and math confidence, on children's math performance. Additionally, we explored age-related differences in these interactions. Results suggest that positive math attitudes did not reduce the negative relation between math anxiety and performance. Rather, there was some weak evidence that there is a stronger negative relation between math anxiety and performance among children with higher levels of math interest. Similarly, there was a stronger negative relation between math anxiety and performance among children with higher levels of math confidence, a finding that was stable across multiple analyses. There were no age-related differences in these interactions. These findings provide a nuanced understanding of the complex interplay between math attitudes, anxiety, and performance in elementary school, underscoring the need for targeted interventions to specifically address math anxiety. ### MTHFR Gene Variants and Major Depressive Disorder in Mexican-Mestizos
**כתב עת:** 📄 Behav Sci (Basel) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Casillas-Ávila MP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 14

Background: Major depressive disorder (MDD) arises from genetic and environmental factors. We evaluated functional variation in methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase ( MTHFR ), circulating MTHFR protein, and homocysteine (HCY) in Mexican-Mestizo participants. Methods: We analyzed 1507 individuals across four cohorts: a primary case–control sample (358 DSM-IV-TR MDD cases, 170 psychiatrically screened controls), a community cohort ( n = 204), and a general population cohort ( n = 775) for allele-frequency context. We genotyped rs1801133 (C677T) and rs13306560 using TaqMan assays. Serum MTHFR and HCY were measured by ELISA in available subsamples (n reported where applicable). Primary analyses tested association of genotypes with MDD under codominant, allelic, and recessive models; false discovery rate (FDR) correction accounted for multiple genetic models. Exploratory multivariable logistic regression adjusted for sex, age, and adverse childhood experiences. Results: The rs13306560 A allele was rare (~2.6%) and showed no association with MDD. Under a recessive model, the rs1801133 TT genotype was more frequent among cases (33.3%) than controls (23.5%) (χ 2 p = 0.036); this association did not survive FDR correction (q = 0.117) and is reported as hypothesis-generating. In an exploratory adjusted logistic regression ( N = 446) using the post hoc recessive coding, TT homozygotes had higher odds of MDD versus CC/CT (OR = 1.98, 95% CI 1.23–3.17, p = 0.005). Serum MTHFR concentrations were higher in cases than controls (median 1834.8 vs. 1241.9 pg/mL; Mann–Whitney U = 343, p = 0.017). Serum MTHFR displayed a borderline positive correlation with HAM-D scores (Spearman r = 0.24, p = 0.050). Conclusions: In this Mexican-Mestizo sample, MTHFR C677T (rs1801133) homozygosity and elevated circulating MTHFR emerged as preliminary, hypothesis-generating candidates for MDD risk. The genetic association did not meet multiple-testing correction (q = 0.117) and requires independent replication; the serum MTHFR case–control difference survived correction (q = 0.034) but warrants confirmation given the small biomarker subsample. A mechanistic study is needed to clarify causality and clinical relevance.

1. Introduction Major depressive disorder (MDD) is among the most prevalent and disabling psychiatric conditions worldwide, characterized by persistent sadness, anhedonia, neurovegetative dysregulation, and substantial functional impairment ( American Psychiatric Association, 2013 ). Twin and family studies estimate its heritability at between 41 and 49%, with the remaining variance attributable to environmental adversity and gene–environment interactions ( Kendler et al., 2018 ). The impact of environmental stressors varies; among the most consistent moderators are adverse childhood experiences (ACEs)—early-life parental loss, abuse, neglect, and exposure to addiction or domestic violence—which increase lifetime MDD risk, lower the age of onset, and modify clinical trajectory ( Dagnino et al., 2025 ; LeMoult et al., 2020 ). Developmental stress is therefore considered a critical factor through which inherited biological vulnerability interacts with early-life adversity to shape depressive trajectories across the lifespan. One of the most biologically plausible pathways linking genetic susceptibility to depression involves folate-dependent one-carbon metabolism and the homocysteine–methylation cycle. Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR; EC 1.5.1.20) catalyzes the irreversible reduction of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, the principal methyl donor required for the re-methylation of homocysteine (HCY) to methionine and the subsequent synthesis of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM). The SAM pathway is essential for monoaminergic neurotransmitter synthesis, phospholipid metabolism, epigenetic regulation, and DNA methylation—all processes implicated in the neurobiology of mood disorders ( Kurkinen et al., 2021 ). Dysregulation of this pathway may therefore contribute to depressive phenotypes through impaired methylation capacity, oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, and altered neurotransmission ( Wan et al., 2018 ). The most studied functional variant in this pathway is the MTHFR C677T variant (rs1801133), which causes an alanine-to-valine substitution and produces a heat-labile enzyme with markedly reduced activity ( Frosst et al., 1995 ). T-allele carriers exhibit impaired folate metabolism and elevated plasma HCY levels, particularly when folate intake is low ( Crider et al., 2011 ; Guéant-Rodriguez et al., 2006 ). Because hyperhomocysteinemia has been consistently linked to depressive symptoms, cognitive impairment, neurotoxicity, and vascular damage, it is regarded as both a biomarker and a potential link in affective disorders. Consistent with this, a large meta-analysis of 81 studies reported that the C677T variant is significantly associated with an increased risk of schizophrenia and major depression, and with bipolar disorder under the recessive model; these associations were stronger in Asian populations ( Zhang et al., 2022 ) ( Figure 1 ). The Mexican-Mestizo population (most present-day Mexicans) is notably underrepresented in psychiatric genetic studies. Candidate-gene association studies in psychiatric disorders have shown poor replication overall compared with genome-wide approaches, and this record warrants cautious interpretation of any single-cohort finding; MTHFR C677T nonetheless remains a defensible candidate because it has a well-characterized functional effect on enzyme activity within a specified biological pathway (folate-dependent one-carbon metabolism) repeatedly, if inconsistently, linked to depression risk, and because it has rarely been studied in Amerindian-admixed populations such as Mexican-Mestizos, where the T allele reaches unusually high frequency. To our knowledge, no previous study has examined the combined contribution of the genetic factors rs13306560 and rs1801133, serum MTHFR enzyme levels, homocysteine concentrations, and ACEs to MDD. In the present study, we evaluated the contribution of two MTHFR variants, rs1801133 and rs13306560, along with circulating MTHFR enzyme and HCY levels, to MDD risk. Specifically, we tested the following hypotheses: (1) that the functional variants rs13306560 and rs1801133 are associated with MDD independent of childhood adversity and sociodemographic covariates; and (2) that altered serum levels of MTHFR and homocysteine are associated with depressive state. 2.
