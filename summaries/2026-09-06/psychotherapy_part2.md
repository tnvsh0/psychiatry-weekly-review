### Menstrual Cycle Informed Psychotherapy: Preliminary findings in Women with Spider Phobia
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zorzini G et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 5

BACKGROUND: Anxiety disorders are the most frequent mental disorders worldwide. Although exposure therapy is the gold-standard treatment for anxiety disorders, almost half of patients fail to achieve sufficient improvements. While much has been learned about for whom psychotherapy works and why, far less is understood about when it works. Emerging evidence indicates that estradiol and progesterone may affect fear extinction, yet studies examining the effects of menstrual cycle phase on exposure therapy outcomes remain lacking. This study is the first to investigate to what extent biologically informed menstrual cycle phase affects exposure therapy outcomes in women with spider phobia.

METHOD: N=45 women with spider phobia in 1) the early to mid-follicular phase, 2) the mid-luteal phase, or 3) using hormonal contraceptives (HC), participated in a group-based exposure therapy. Symptoms were assessed at baseline, one-week post-therapy, and at 12-14 weeks follow-up, using the Spider Phobia Questionnaire (SPQ), the Fear of Spiders Questionnaire (FSQ), and a behavioral approach test (BAT).

RESULTS: Both phobic fear and avoidance behavior improved significantly over time. Although improvement did not differ between menstrual cycle groups, higher progesterone levels on the day of exposure therapy were associated with less improvement in behavioral avoidance.

CONCLUSION: The present findings provide preliminary evidence that ovarian hormones on the day of exposure therapy may affect exposure-based outcomes, particularly reductions in behavioral avoidance. More research in larger samples is needed to further investigate menstrual cycle phase effects in exposure-based treatment. If replicated, the findings would attest to the potential of bio-rhythm informed psychotherapy.

### Emotional imagery across psychopathology: A translational perspective for youth mental health
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Clinical Psychology Review (Clin Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Lau-Zhu A, Di Simplicio M | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 10

Adolescence or youth, spanning ages 12-24, represents peak vulnerability for mental health problems but also offers cognitive plasticity and thus optimal opportunity for interventions. Meanwhile, the science of mental imagery is fuelling innovations in mental health, but mostly in adults. In this review, we aim to spotlight mental imagery in youth mental health, highlighting its potential for driving theoretical and therapeutic innovations. We synthesise the current evidence for imagery's role in understanding and addressing psychopathology, focussing on emerging youth data. Central to this review, we propose a novel framework in which mental imagery acts as a "developmentally sensitive amplifier" during adolescence, intensifying key cognitive-emotional processes that are salient at this developmental stage - particularly threat, reward, and self-processing - and thereby increasing their influence on the development of psychopathology. Therapeutic implications and translational considerations are discussed within a developmental and delivery context in youth. By integrating cognitive, clinical, and developmental sciences, imagery-based approaches could yield powerful interventions to tackle the global challenge of youth mental health.

### Changes in emotion regulation are associated with treatment outcomes in web-based eating disorder interventions: analyses of two randomized controlled trials
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hartmann S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 28

OBJECTIVE: Web-based interventions effectively reduce eating disorder symptoms; however, the associated mechanisms remain largely unknown. This study examined whether changes in emotion regulation processes were linked to treatment response in two web-based cognitive-behavioral self-help interventions for bulimia nervosa (BN) and binge eating disorder (BED), and tested pre-treatment emotion regulation as a moderator.

METHOD: Data from two randomized controlled trials (N = 308, age: M = 32.76, SD = 10.12; female: 96.43%) were analyzed. Latent-change models and network intervention analyses were used to estimate indirect associations between treatment and changes in behavioral symptoms (primary outcome), global eating disorder psychopathology, and clinical impairment (secondary outcomes) through changes in emotion regulation difficulties and strategy use. Moderator effects were tested using linear mixed models.

RESULTS: Significant indirect pathways from treatment allocation to pre- to post-treatment outcome changes through concurrent decreases in emotion regulation difficulties emerged for BN and BED (partially standardized indirect paths: |ab| ≥ 0.10). Treatment was linked with changes in global eating disorder psychopathology and clinical impairment through concurrent, combined decreases in rumination, suppression, and avoidance in BN (partially standardized overall indirect paths: |ab| ≥ 0.17) and combined increases in acceptance, reappraisal, and problem-solving in BED (partially standardized overall indirect paths: |ab| ≥ 0.19). No significant differences in the indirect paths between BN and BED were found. Baseline-to-mid-treatment changes in emotion regulation did not predict subsequent changes in outcomes. No consistent moderator effects were detected.

CONCLUSION: Reductions in emotion regulation deficits were associated with concurrent symptom declines in web-based eating disorder treatments, pointing to emotion regulation as a candidate change mechanism.…

### When good news falls short: Prospective role of positive belief updating in predicting post-trauma distress
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hay DE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 18

Adaptive functioning relies on the ability to update beliefs in response to new information. However, belief updating is often optimistically biased, with individuals more readily revising their beliefs in response to desirable (good news) than to undesirable (bad news) information. Deficiencies in positive updating have been linked to psychological distress. However, the temporal association between positive belief updating and psychological distress remains unclear. Given that trauma exposure strongly challenges prior beliefs and places heightened demands on belief updating, the present study aims to examine whether baseline belief updating abilities prospectively predict the tendency to develop depressive and posttraumatic stress symptoms 3 months following trauma exposure. In a community sample of Israeli adults (N = 117), belief updating was assessed at T0 (six months prior to the October 7th attacks) using a validated belief updating task. Depressive and posttraumatic stress symptoms were assessed at T0 and at T1 (three months following the October 7th attacks), along with cumulative trauma exposure. Reduced positive updating was associated with higher posttraumatic stress at T1, even when controlling for trauma exposure and baseline (T0) symptoms. Findings suggest that erosion in optimistic bias can prospectively predict psychological distress following trauma exposure. Belief updating may represent a cognitive process relevant to vulnerability and resilience following trauma and stress.

### Measuring Trait-Level Overcontrol: Does the Measurement Fit the Theory?
**כתב עת:** 📄 Assessment | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Balling C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 4

Radically Open Dialectical Behavior Therapy (RO-DBT) was developed to target overcontrolled coping style and is gaining traction as a gold-standard treatment in the transdiagnostic intervention repertoire. The RO-DBT manual offers two instruments that explicitly measure overcontrol: the Adult Styles of Coping Word-Pair Checklist (ASC-WP) and the Overcontrol Trait Rating Scale (OCTRS). There is virtually no psychometric information about these measures, and they have not been examined through a construct validity framework. A sample of 482 undergraduates, oversampled for overcontrolled coping style, completed these measures via self-report. Confirmatory Factor Analyses (CFAs) did not display one-factor solutions of these instruments, suggesting that a strictly unidimensional representation of these measures was not supported. When considerations of interpretability were paired with quantitative support for exploratory factor analyses, obvious factor structures did not form and were not easily reconciled with theory. However, the OCTRS factor structure was stronger than that of the ASC-WP. Nomological network analyses of the measures revealed that in general, the OCTRS evidenced promising convergent validity and weak discriminant validity, while the ASC-WP demonstrated globally weak construct validity. Taken together, these results suggest that refining the measurement of overcontrol is crucial, and use of the ASC-WP measure warrants particular caution.
