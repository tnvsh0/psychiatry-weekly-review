### New Horizons in the Development of Treatments for Substance Use Disorders
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Mariani JJ, Levin FR | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 1

Substance use disorders (SUDs) are a major public health problem in the United States and cause substantial morbidity and mortality. There are meaningful gaps in the available SUD treatment options, and the development of new therapies is urgently needed. While medications with U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval are available for alcohol, nicotine, and opioid use disorders, there are no approved pharmacotherapies for cannabis, cocaine, or methamphetamine use disorders. Behavioral treatments for SUDs have significant limitations in effectiveness and accessibility, and there is a need for the development of both new behavioral treatment options and new models of treatment delivery. The next generation of treatments for SUDs will likely come from a diverse set of interventions, including new drug classes, new technologies, and new methods of delivery.

BACKGROUND: Caregivers of individuals with schizophrenia face emotional and psychological challenges that affect their subjective well-being (SWB). Despite their vital role, caregiver-focused interventions are limited. Structured programs that promote SWB by strengthening communication and psychosocial skills are needed to support caregivers effectively. The aim of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of the "Communication Skills-Focused Psychoeducation Program(CSFP)",which was developed to improve the SWB of caregivers of individuals diagnosed with schizophrenia.

METHODS: It was conducted in an experimental design with randomization, two intervention groups, and one control group, with pre-test, post-test, and follow-up measurements. First, caregivers completed assessments on Subjective Well-Being scale (SWBS), communication skills (CSS), and caregiving burden (ZBI). Afterward, 72 caregivers were randomly assigned to three groups: CSFP, General Psychoeducation (GEP), and control. The CSFP-group received an 8-week, the GEP-group received a 5-week psychoeducation program, and the control group continued routine treatment. The effects of the intervention were evaluated immediately after the intervention and at the 1st and 3rd months of follow-up.

### Association of Lithium Use With Suicide and Self-Harm Among Chinese Adolescents and Adults With Bipolar Disorder: A Nationwide Multicenter Retrospective Cohort Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Clin Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מחקר רב-מרכזי | **מחברים:** Xu N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 10

Objective:  Bipolar disorder (BD) greatly increases the risk of suicide. While lithium is recognized for reducing suicide and suicidal behaviors, evidence regarding its association with suicidal ideation remains limited. This study evaluated lithium's association with reduced suicidal ideation and behaviors among adolescents and adults with BD in China.  Method:  This multicenter, retrospective cohort study included participants aged 12-45 years from 15 psychiatric centers across mainland China who met  DSM-IV  criteria for BD, recruited between April 19, 2024, and November 30, 2024. Patients receiving lithium for ≥80% of the first 6 months in the preceding year formed the lithium group; those without lithium exposure served as controls. The primary outcome was suicidal ideation assessed by the Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale; secondary outcomes included suicidal behaviors, nonsuicidal self-injury (NSSI), and aggression.  Results:  Of 603 screened patients, 585 (290 lithium; 295 controls) met inclusion criteria. At 1-year follow-up, lithium use was significantly associated with a lower likelihood of suicidal ideation (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] = 0.57, 95% CI = 0.38-0.86,  P  = .008) and suicidal behaviors (aOR= 0.60, 95% CI = 0.39-0.90,  P  = .02). No difference was observed in NSSI, while lithium users reported lower hostility scores ( P  = .01). Subgroup analyses indicated more pronounced protective associations among females, adults, higher-income individuals, and those without comorbid anxiety, rapid cycling, or predominantly depressive episodes, regardless of psychotic symptoms.  Conclusions:  Lithium was associated with a 40% lower risk of suicidal ideation and reduced suicidal behaviors in BD. These findings reinforce lithium as a first-line treatment for suicide prevention and highlight its comprehensive antisuicidal role-from lower rates of ideation to fewer behaviors-supporting confident, evidence-based clinical use.
