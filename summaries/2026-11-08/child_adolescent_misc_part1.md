### Knowledge and Misinformation Regarding Autism Risk Factors in a Survey of US Adults
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Health Forum *(IF: 120.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Perlis RH et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

IMPORTANCE: With the rise of misinformation about autism risk, including recent statements from US government officials, the extent to which the public understands environmental autism risk factors is not known.

OBJECTIVE: To characterize beliefs about autism risk factors among US adults and identify characteristics influencing these beliefs.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This cross-sectional survey study used data from an internet-based survey of adults aged 18 years or older residing in all 50 US states and the District of Columbia and was administered between December 18, 2025, and January 27, 2026. Multiple regression models assessed sociodemographic and other individual characteristics, including political affiliation and trust in institutions, for association with endorsement of misinformed beliefs.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: The primary outcome was endorsement of individual risk factors, scored on a 5-point Likert scale from definitely false to definitely true.

RESULTS: Of the 30 388 adults who completed the survey, 100 did not provide an answer to the childhood vaccination item, leaving 30 288 in the analysis. Among the 30 288 participants (mean [SD] age, 46.6 [15.9] years), 13 702 (45.2%) identified as men, 16 393 (54.1%) as women, and 193 (0.6%) as nonbinary. The most commonly endorsed cause was genetic factors (20 380 of 30 254; unweighted, 67.4%; weighted, 66.7%), an evidence-based risk factor. However, 8370 of 30 288 (unweighted, 27.6%; weighted, 26.6%) endorsed childhood vaccinations and 7140 of 30 211 (unweighted, 23.6%; weighted, 23.1%) endorsed acetaminophen in pregnancy as autism risk factors. In regression models, endorsement of vaccine-autism risk beliefs was greatest among adults aged 35 to 44 years (vs 18 to 24 years, adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 1.64; 95% CI, 1.44-1.86) and lowest among those aged 65 years and older (aOR, 0.64; 95% CI, 0.55-0.74). Endorsement was also associated with men (aOR, 1.26; 95% CI, 1.19-1.34), African American or Black race (compared with White race, aOR, 1.25; 95% CI, 1.15-1.35), Hispanic ethnicity (aOR, 1.13; 95% CI, 1.03-1.24), and strength of Republican political affiliation (strong Republican vs strong Democrat, aOR, 3.83; 95% CI, 3.47-4.24). More frequent social media use was associated with lower odds of endorsing misinformation (multiple times daily vs less than weekly, aOR, 0.72; 95% CI, 0.66-0.78).

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Results of this survey study suggest that non-evidence-based beliefs about autism risk were prevalent among US adults and were strongly associated with sociodemographic differences and political affiliation. Limiting public health impact of such non-evidence-based information will likely require targeted interventions that recognize the heterogeneity of such beliefs.

### A Systematic Review of Qualitative Research Upon the Perceptions of Parents and Caregivers of Children with Autism About Health, School, and Community Support Networks
**כתב עת:** 📄 Evid Based Pract Child Adolesc Ment Health *(IF: 3.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** סקירה שיטתית | **מחברים:** De-Nardin EMS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background : Family members and caregivers of children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) report challenges and barriers in accessing support networks, which are associated with detrimental impacts on clinical, social, and quality-of-life outcomes. Objective : To explore how parents and caregivers of children with ASD, experience the support network provided by health and education services and the community. Method : This is a systematic review of the qualitative research about health, school and community support networks for parents and caregivers of children with ASD using a thematic synthesis of the studies. We searched the terms in the PubMed, EMBASE, ERIC and APA PsychNet databases for qualitative studies (interviews, focus groups and questionnaires with open questions) and produced a thematic synthesis by codes and themes based on the selected qualitative studies. Results : The synthesis resulted in 24 selected articles. Three themes emerged: health support networks (search for diagnosis, emotional support, post-diagnosis, financial and family impacts, access to specialized services); school support networks (inclusive school, training, emotional support); and community support networks (stigma and social exclusion, judgments, and emotional support). Parents and caregivers of children with ASD perceive difficulties in their trajectories with the various support networks. Conclusions : The limited emotional support received by parents was a common perception in health, school and community networks. Socioeconomic, demographic, ethnic and cultural characteristics seem to influence differently the perceptions on support networks.

### Region-Adapted Multi-Nutrient Supplementation in Chinese Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder: A 12-Week Pilot Randomised Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Nutr Sci Vitaminol (Tokyo) | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** He Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Nutritional supplementation has produced inconsistent effects on autistic symptoms, and the evidence base is largely derived from Western populations. This study evaluated the preliminary efficacy and tolerability of a region-adapted multinutrient intervention in Chinese children with autism spectrum disorder. In this 12-wk pilot, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 50 children were assigned to receive either the intervention or a placebo. The primary endpoint was the change in the Autism Behavior Checklist total score. Secondary outcomes included developmental functioning assessed using the Psychoeducational Profile, Third Edition; gastrointestinal symptoms assessed using the Gastrointestinal Severity Index; and sleep assessed with the Children's Sleep Habits Questionnaire. Between-group differences were analysed using baseline-adjusted models. All participants completed follow-up, and no serious adverse events were reported. The change in the Autism Behavior Checklist total score did not differ between groups (p=0.44). Receptive language on the Psychoeducational Profile, Third Edition improved more in the intervention group (difference in change=2.38; 95% confidence interval, 0.19-4.57; p=0.03). Other subscales showed mixed findings, including problem behaviour favouring control. Regarding tolerability, while abdominal pain improved in the intervention group (p=0.01), reports of night waking (p=0.01) and looser stools (p=0.05) increased. The prespecified primary endpoint was not met. Exploratory findings suggested a signal in receptive language, alongside tolerability trade-offs. These results require confirmation in adequately powered trials with refined endpoint selection and structured safety monitoring.

### Outcomes of sensory integration therapy in children with autism spectrum disorder: a non-randomized pre-post intervention study
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Pediatr | **סוג מחקר:** מחקר קליני | **מחברים:** Rahman RMA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Background Sensory processing abnormalities have been linked to autism symptoms. Sensory integration therapy (SIT) affects autism symptom severity, sensory profile and related socio-demographic/clinical variables. Methods Thirty patients, aged 3 to 10 years, meeting autism spectrum disorder (ASD) case definition were recruited from SIT clinic, Child and Adolescent Psychiatry Department, Okasha Institute of Psychiatry, Ain Shams University, Cairo, between May 2023 and January 2025, assessed by socio-demographic/clinical questionnaire, Arabic versions of Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia for School-Age Children Present and Lifetime Version, Stanford–Binet Intelligence Scale Fifth Edition, Childhood Autism Rating Scale (CARS), and Sensory Profile Caregiver Questionnaire. Participants received a 12-week SIT program (one 30-minute session weekly), then post-intervention reassessment using CARS and Sensory Profile. Results The most common age was 3–5 years (46.66%), with males comprising 80%. Most participants (73.33%) IQ scores were 60–64. Mean CARS scores decreased from 33.9 ± 3.42 to 31.95 ± 3.4, mild autism increasing to 76.67%, moderate decreasing to 10% and unchanged severe cases. Mostly affected domains were vestibular processing, emotional/social responses, behavioural outcomes of sensory processing and fine motor/perceptual. Post-intervention, the most improved were vestibular processing, emotional/social response, emotionally reactive, inattention/distractibility ( p -value < 0.001), auditory processing, modulation of body position and movement affecting activity level ( p -value 0.001). The least were sensory processing related to endurance/tone and low endurance/tone. Conclusion SIT effectively reduced autism severity and improved key sensory and behavioural domains of sensory processing, particularly in mild to moderate cases. Trial registration ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06882642 , registered March 2025 (retrospectively registered).

Introduction Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across multiple contexts, together with restricted and repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities [ 1 ]. Sensory processing differences, including hyper-reactivity, hypo-reactivity, and sensory-seeking behaviors, are recognized as a core diagnostic feature of ASD in the DSM-5-TR and are highly prevalent across the spectrum [ 1 ]. These sensory differences are associated with impairments in daily functioning, emotional regulation, attention, and social participation [ 2 ]. Sensory integration therapy (SIT), originally developed by A. Jean Ayres, aims to improve the brain’s ability to process and integrate sensory input through structured, play-based activities. Despite its widespread clinical use, the evidence base for SIT remains mixed. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses have reported variable outcomes, with some studies demonstrating functional improvements and others showing limited or inconsistent effects. In a systematic review, Schoen et al. identified six eligible studies of Ayres Sensory Integration (ASI) in children with ASD, including two high-quality randomized controlled trials. The review concluded that ASI may improve functional performance, participation, and individualized goal achievement in children with ASD [ 3 ]. In a systematic review by Case-Smith et al. a total of 19 studies were reviewed: 5 examined the effects of sensory integration therapy and 14 sensory-based intervention. The studies defined sensory integration therapies as clinic-based interventions that use sensory-rich, child-directed activities to improve a child’s adaptive responses to sensory experiences. Two randomized controlled trials found positive effects for sensory integration therapy on child performance using Goal Attainment Scaling [ 4 ]. In one of the largest randomized controlled trials to date, named Sensory Integration Therapy for sensory processing difficulties in children with Autism spectrum disorder (SenITA trial), there were no significant benefits of SIT over standard care on primary behavioral outcomes, although subgroup and secondary findings suggested potential benefits in specific domains [ 5 ]. Most sensory integration therapy (SIT) research has been conducted in North America, Europe, and other high-income countries, with relatively limited evidence from low- and middle-income countries and under-represented populations. Furthermore, much of the literature originates from controlled research settings rather than routine clinical outpatient practice [ 3 ]. Although previous studies have evaluated sensory outcomes, functional performance, or behavioral measures separately, relatively few investigations have concurrently examined changes in autism severity and detailed sensory processing profiles following SIT [ 4 ]. Understanding which domains are most responsive to SIT, and whether clinical variables influence outcomes, remains of clinical relevance. The present study aimed to evaluate the effects of a 12-week SIT program on autism severity and sensory processing patterns in children with ASD. A secondary aim was to explore potential predictors of treatment response. We hypothesized that participation in the 12-week sensory integration therapy program would be associated with a reduction in autism severity and improvements in sensory processing domains among children with autism spectrum disorder. Methods Study design and setting This study employed a single-arm, non-randomized pre–post interventional design. Participants were recruited from a sensory integration therapy outpatient clinic within Ain shams university-affiliated child and adolescent psychiatry department, Cairo, Egypt, between May 2023 and January 2025. Sampling method Convenient sample. Participants Inclusion criteria included children aged 3–10 years, if they met DSM-5 diagnostic criteria for ASD and demonstrated sensory processing difficulties based on caregiver report. Exclusion criteria included comorbid neurological disorders (e.g., epilepsy, cerebral palsy), significant sensory or motor impairments, intellectual functioning below an IQ of 60, or typical performance on the Sensory Profile. Patients did not receive medications or other neuro modulatory intervention, or had past history of last receiving medications or neuro modulatory interventions, maximum 3 months before the start of study. Ethical considerations The study was approved by the institutional research ethics committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Ain Shams University ( Approval No. FMASU MD71/2023 ). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. An informed written consent was obtained from all participant guardian in the study before conducting the interview with ensured confidentiality. ### Associations between neurodevelopmental traits and perinatal depression: a prospective study in Japanese women
**כתב עת:** 📄 Arch Womens Ment Health | **סוג מחקר:** מחקר רב-מרכזי | **מחברים:** Hasegawa K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Purpose This study examined how attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD) traits relate to depressive symptoms from late pregnancy through early postpartum in Japanese women, alongside perinatal factors. Methods In a multicenter prospective study, 1,104 women completed questionnaires at late pregnancy (T1), 2 weeks postpartum (T2), and 1 month postpartum (T3). Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), ADHD traits with the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS), and ASD traits with the Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ). Chi-square tests evaluated timepoint-specific associations, multivariable logistic regression identified independent predictors of EPDS positivity at T2, and a linear mixed-effects model assessed longitudinal changes. Results EPDS scores and EPDS positivity were highest at T1 and declined postpartum (16.7%, 8.6%, 4.0%). Scores were correlated across timepoints. In univariate analyses, antenatal EPDS positivity showed associations with ASD traits (AQ+: OR = 6.08, 95% CI 2.17–16.99) and ADHD traits (ASRS+: OR = 4.86, 95% CI 3.06–7.72). In multivariable analysis, independent predictors of EPDS positivity at T2 included ADHD traits (OR = 1.37, 95% CI 1.13–1.66), primiparity, psychiatric treatment history, and antenatal EPDS scores. ASD traits were not associated with T2 EPDS positivity after adjustment for antenatal EPDS scores. In the mixed-effects model, both ADHD and ASD traits were associated with higher EPDS scores during pregnancy, with attenuated effects postpartum. Conclusion Neurodevelopmental traits are associated with perinatal mental health, and trait- and timing-informed support may enhance maternal psychological stability and caregiving outcomes. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00737-026-01781-4 .

Article Highlights ADHD traits were associated with depressive symptoms from pregnancy through postpartum. ASD traits were associated with depressive symptoms mainly during pregnancy. Risk factors for postpartum depression include first-time mothers, psychiatric history, elevated antenatal EPDS scores. Many neurodevelopmental traits remain unrecognized in clinical settings. Personalized support based on trait profile and timing can improve maternal mental health. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00737-026-01781-4 .

Introduction Perinatal depression is a major mental health concern that affects approximately 10 − 20% of women worldwide (Bauman et al. 2020 ; Tokumitsu et al. 2020 ; Wang et al. 2021 ). It is associated with a range of adverse outcomes, including impaired bonding, increased maltreatment risk, and later child difficulties (Dubber et al. 2015 ; Ayers et al. 2019 ; Sasayama et al. 2025 ). Previous studies have identified obstetric and sociodemographic predictors, including psychiatric history, primigravidity, cesarean delivery, and male infant birth (Liu et al. 2022 ; Yang et al. 2022 ). While psychiatric history remains a well-established psychological predictor, emerging evidence suggests that neurodevelopmental traits, specifically those associated with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD), may represent significant yet underrecognized vulnerability factors. Core features of ADHD (inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity) and ASD (communication and social difficulties, restricted interests, sensory atypicalities, and limited adaptability) may substantially hinder adaptation to pregnancy and parenting, thereby increasing the risk of depression. An umbrella review by Kang et al. ( 2024 ) reported an odds ratio (OR) of 3.0 (95% confidence interval [CI] 1.53–5.92) for depression among individuals with ADHD traits. Similarly, a meta-analysis by Hudson et al. ( 2019 ) found that 48.6% of individuals with ASD experienced depression. In the perinatal context, women with ADHD face multiple risks during pregnancy and early parenting (Kittel-Schneider et al. 2021 ). Dorani et al. ( 2021 ) reported that 57.6% of women with ADHD experienced postpartum depression exceeding the rate in the general population. Likewise, qualitative studies have shown that women with ASD often struggle with communicative and sensory aspects of pregnancy and parenting (Gardner et al. 2016 ; Rogers et al. 2017 ). Hirokawa et al. ( 2019 ) conducted a large-scale study of Japanese women and used the Autism-Spectrum Quotient-10 (AQ-10) Japanese version to assess autistic traits, focusing on characteristics often described as part of the broader autism phenotype (BAP). They reported that women with such traits were at increased risk of postpartum depression and insecure maternal attachment at one month. Taken together, these findings suggest that women with neurodevelopmental traits may have heightened vulnerability to perinatal depression, underscoring the importance of early screening and timely intervention. However, few studies have prospectively examined how such traits relate to depressive symptoms across the perinatal period. In this manuscript, the term “perinatal depression” refers to depressive symptoms occurring during pregnancy and the early postpartum period. In the context of our study design, “perinatal” corresponds to the assessment window from late pregnancy to one month postpartum. Although DSM-5 defines postpartum depression as a major depressive episode with peripartum onset, in this study we distinguish between “antenatal depression” and “postpartum depression” based on the timing of assessment. Given that the study was not preregistered, the analyses were exploratory in nature. Accordingly, this study examined how ADHD and ASD traits relate to depressive symptoms from late pregnancy through the early postpartum period, aiming to provide practical insights for more inclusive screening strategies and tailored mental health interventions in maternal care. Eligible participants were pregnant Japanese-speaking women aged 20 years or older who were able to complete the questionnaires. Gender identity was not assessed in this study. We therefore use the term “women” throughout this manuscript, consistent with the recruitment setting and the perinatal literature, while acknowledging that not all pregnant individuals identify as women. At the 36–37 weeks antenatal checkup (T1), participants received the first survey set and provided written informed consent. Those who agreed completed questionnaires on neurodevelopmental traits, depressive symptoms, and sociodemographic characteristics. The second and third surveys were administered at the 2-week (T2) and 1-month postpartum checkups (T3), assessing depressive symptoms, childbirth experiences, and childcare. Neurodevelopmental traits were not reassessed postpartum as these traits are considered relatively stable over the short study period. All data were anonymized and stored securely. Responses obtained at 14 ± 7 days postpartum were classified as T2, and those at 30 ± 7 days as T3. Questionnaires with missing data at a given timepoint were excluded from the corresponding analyses for that timepoint, while available data from other timepoints were retained.
