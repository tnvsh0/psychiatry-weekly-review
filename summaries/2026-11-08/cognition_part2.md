### Confounds in Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging-Part III: Auxiliary Data
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Inglis B, D'Esposito M | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

In the first part of this series, we reviewed the pathways that bring about confounding nuisance signals in fMRI. Then, in the second part, we considered how human factors can modify these pathways. In this, the third and final part, we review auxiliary data that may allow researchers to identify and perhaps correct for nuisance signals and their modifiers. We consider three broad categories of data: (1) measures that can be collected synchronously with the fMRI, (2) other MRI scans that either capture trait information or attempt to define the state that exists during an fMRI scan in the same session, and (3) non-MRI measures that can be collected before or after the scan and that also capture trait or relevant state factors influencing the fMRI. We conclude with some recommendations on ways to improve fMRI experiments going forward.

### Distributed Neural Dynamics Underlie the Shift from Movement Preparation to Execution
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yin Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Understanding how neural population activity supports the transition from movement preparation to execution remains a central challenge in systems neuroscience. Invasive recordings in nonhuman primates indicate that motor preparation and execution occupy orthogonal subspaces within the primary motor cortex (M1), yet how these neural dynamics evolve across broader networks and support skilled, memory-guided action remains poorly understood. We used source-resolved magnetoencephalography during a memory-guided finger-sequence task to track population dynamics across a cortico-hippocampal network. Manifold trajectories and decoding revealed that preparation and execution of movement sequences occupy distinct, yet not fully orthogonal, neural subspaces-a separation that generalized across all regions but was most pronounced in motor regions. Crucially, we identified a hierarchical spatio-temporal transition across the brain: The state shift originated in the hippocampus via theta-band modulations and rippled through the pre-SMA, SMA, and lateral premotor areas before culminating in M1 via broadband activity. This progression follows the functional hierarchy of descending motor pathways, with M1 shifting last, 160 msec before the first button press. Although sequence identity could be decoded from all areas during execution, only the hippocampus and M1 maintained predictive representations of upcoming sequences during preparation. These findings demonstrate that the initiation of complex motor sequences relies on a well-timed integration of hippocampal memory systems with primary motor output, rather than being a process localized to the motor cortices alone.

### When looking away means looking inward: contextual interpretation of gaze aversions determines their attentional value
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zehngut A, Yuval-Greenberg S | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Eye movements are typically understood as supporting outward orienting, directing attention toward locations of interest in the external environment. However, not all eye movements reflect outward orienting. Gaze aversions, the common tendency to look away during effortful cognitive processing, serve as social signals of inward-oriented attention and ongoing processing. Because observers continuously monitor others' eye movements and use gaze-related cues to guide their own behavior, distinguishing between outward-oriented gaze-shifts and inward-oriented gaze aversions is a basic challenge in human interaction. Here, we examine the hypothesis that this distinction depends on the context in which gaze shifts appear. While participants reorient their attention following an outward-oriented gaze shift, they withhold doing so when the context suggests that the gaze shift reflects inward-oriented processing. To test this hypothesis, we relied on the Gaze Cueing Effect (GCE), an attentional orienting to observed gaze shifts that is known to be modulated by top-down context and interpretation. In two pre-registered experiments (total N = 90), participants performed a gaze-cueing letter-discrimination task while viewing identical gaze shifts within different contexts. One group was led to interpret the gaze shifts as occurring during effortful thinking, whereas the other group received no thinking-related context. Across both experiments, attentional orienting was significantly reduced when gaze shifts were perceived as reflecting internal cognitive processing rather than attention shifts. These findings demonstrate that gaze aversions function as communicative signals that dynamically regulate interpersonal attention, and that gaze following depends fundamentally on the inferred social meaning of the observed eye movement.

### The folk concept of moral agency
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Demaree-Cotton J, Semler J | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

Whether a particular entity is a 'moral agent' determines the sense in which its behaviour is morally evaluable. Theories of moral agency aim to capture and explain commonsense distinctions between the possession or absence of moral agency across different kinds of being. But little is known about the ordinary concept of moral agency, its application across different entities, and its relation to the more specific notion of moral responsibility. In this paper, we investigate the folk concept of moral agency through philosophical analysis and two empirical studies. We draw conceptual distinctions between three components of moral agency: intentional action (acting rather than behaving), moral action (acting morally rightly or wrongly), and responsible action (being blameworthy for one's actions). We then investigate whether people differentiate between the three components of moral agency, and the extent to which their judgments depend on the perceived psychological and behavioural capacities of the entity in question. We find that people attribute varying degrees of moral agency to different entities while differentiating between the proposed components. These moral agency attributions are partly, but not fully, explained by people's attributions of underlying capacities. While intentional action ascriptions were linked to the attribution of mental states like desires, beliefs, and sensations, moral action and moral responsibility were linked to ascriptions of capacities for a 'deep self'. Philosophical theories of moral agency should account for these patterns in folk attributions.

### From linguistic anchors to acoustic skeletons: the dual-pathway model of voice identity representation
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fang L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Achieving perceptual constancy for voice identity across the speech-song continuum represents a fundamental cognitive challenge. Does the auditory system rely on precise linguistic cues to "anchor" identity, or does it utilize multi-dimensional acoustic variability to build a generalized prototype? In this study, we trained listeners to recognize voices via three modalities, i.e., reading (linguistic focus), non-lexical singing on /la/ (structural focus), and singing (dual focus). We then tested their recognition of the voices under immediate, cross-modal, and one-week delayed conditions. Experiment 1 revealed that training with lyrics (reading and singing) yielded high initial accuracy, demonstrating a rapid encoding advantage of linguistic cues. However, Experiments 2 and 3 uncovered significant asymmetries. While identities learned through singing transferred seamlessly to non-lexical singing, those learned through reading incurred substantial cross-modal processing costs and marked retrieval latencies despite remaining above chance level. After a one-week delay, testing the reading group on non-lexical vocable singing revealed a behavioral dissociation. While identification accuracy remained well above chance (M = 0.62), response times spiked dramatically to 4722 ms. This latency reflects an active, exhaustive memory search triggered by persistent coarse acoustic invariants when fine-grained lexical and phonetic anchors are missing. These findings refine a Dual-Pathway Model of voice processing in which a linguistic-phonetic pathway provides language-bound precision, while an acoustic-melodic pathway exploits acoustic variability to extract a domain-general acoustic skeleton that is robust against cross-modal transfer and temporal decay.

### Accuracy of parent and teacher reports of early math skills
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** King YA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

BACKGROUND: It is important for parents and teachers to understand preschooler's early mathematics skills to best provide appropriate mathematics support. Comparing parents' and teachers' accuracy in reporting children's mathematics skills may help to ensure developmentally appropriate early math support in home and classroom environments.

AIM: The present study examined the accuracy of parents' and teachers' reports of preschoolers' early mathematics skills (i.e., counting, numeral identification, and quantitative and spatial word knowledge).

SAMPLE: Participants included 117 children 3 to 5 years old (M = 4.27, SD = .63, 49.6 % female) and their parent and Head Start teacher.

METHOD: Parents and teachers completed surveys reporting on each child's understanding of mathematics concepts via items from different mathematics measures. Children were directly assessed on their mathematics skills via the same or similar items.

RESULTS: Generally, parents' and teachers' reports were positively associated with direct assessments of children's mathematics skills. Parents' and teachers' reports of children's mathematical skills ranged from 47 % to 75 % accurate, and parents were more accurate than teachers in reporting children's quantitative word knowledge. The direction of inaccuracies (over-or underestimation) varied by mathematics skill type, such that caregivers tended to overestimate children's numeracy skills and underestimate their mathematical language knowledge.

CONCLUSION: Future research is needed to examine how the accuracy of parents and teachers reports influences early mathematics development, and how potentially improving caregiver accuracy might support children's mathematics learning.
