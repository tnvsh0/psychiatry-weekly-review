### Claustrum Transcriptome Alterations in Schizophrenia
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Arion D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Key Points Question Is the transcriptome of the dorsal claustrum (dCLA) altered in schizophrenia? Findings In this case-control study, transcriptome profiling of the dCLA revealed numerous alterations in schizophrenia, including upregulation of immune response and transcription regulation genes and downregulation of mitochondrial activity and oxidative phosphorylation genes, and the number of differentially expressed genes detected was larger than in the anterior cingulate or prefrontal cortices. The pattern of alterations was highly concordant across these 3 regions but less concordant with altered gene expression in striatal regions. Meaning These findings suggest a molecular disease process in schizophrenia that is conserved across interconnected brain regions but more pronounced in the dCLA.

Importance Schizophrenia (SZ) is associated with transcriptome alterations in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), key regions in a distributed network that mediates cognitive functions impaired in the illness. The subcortical dorsal claustrum (dCLA) is structurally densely interconnected with the ACC and dlPFC and functionally coactivated with these regions during certain cognitive tasks. Objective To identify SZ-associated transcriptome alterations in the dCLA. Design, Settings, and Participants This case-control study used RNA sequencing data for dCLA homogenates from postmortem human brain of individuals with SZ and unaffected comparison (UC) individuals. Brain donations were obtained from the Allegheny County Office of the Medical Examiner, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Data analysis was performed from January 12, 2024, through May 22, 2026. Exposure Schizophrenia. Main Outcomes and Measures Gene expression differences in the dCLA between individuals with SZ and UC individuals were the primary outcome. Secondary outcomes included gene set enrichment in the dCLA and comparisons of gene expression differences across the dCLA, ACC, dlPFC, putamen, and caudate between individuals with SZ and UC individuals, including rank-rank hypergeometric overlap (RRHO) analyses. Results In the final dCLA samples from 96 individuals, including 47 individuals with SZ (10 [21.3%] female; mean [SD] age, 49.0 [13.3] years) and 49 UC individuals (10 [20.4%] female; mean [SD] age, 49.9 [14.2] years), there were 1717 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between individuals with SZ and UC individuals, with 1149 (66.9%) of the DEGs upregulated in SZ. Many upregulated DEGs were associated with immune response or transcription regulation, and many downregulated DEGs were associated with mitochondrial activity or oxidative phosphorylation. In comparisons between individuals with SZ and UC individuals with data from the dCLA, ACC, and dlPFC, the number of DEGs detected was 4 to 12 times greater in the dCLA (1242 DEGs) than in the ACC (289 DEGs) or dlPFC (102 DEGs). However, threshold-free RRHO analyses revealed a conserved pattern of SZ-associated gene expression alterations across all 3 regions. In comparison, concordance of gene expression alterations was lower with the putamen and caudate, subcortical regions with weaker connections to the dCLA. Conclusions and Relevance The results of this case-control study suggest that the dCLA is a prominent site of molecular alterations in SZ. These alterations might reflect the dCLA’s extensive connectivity with affected cortical regions, distinctive intrinsic circuitry, and/or enrichment of vulnerable cell types.

This case-control study investigates schizophrenia-associated transcriptome alterations in the dorsal claustrum.

### Targeted Use of Kudzu for Binge Drinking Among Adults With Alcohol Use Disorder: A Phase 2b Randomized Trial
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Santos GM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

OBJECTIVE: The aim of this study was to determine the efficacy and acceptability of targeted use of kudzu, an herbal supplement, in adults with alcohol use disorder (AUD) who binge drink.

METHODS: In a double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 120 adults with AUD who binge drink were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to targeted (as-needed) use of oral kudzu extract (2 grams taken on days when binge drinking was anticipated) or matching placebo, plus weekly counseling for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was number of binge-drinking days in the past week. Prespecified secondary outcomes included number of self-reported binge-drinking weeks, number of drinks, and measurements of changes in alcohol use using biomarkers, including ethyl glucuronide (EtG) and phosphatidylethanol (PEth).

RESULTS: Retention was high: 88% completed the trial, and 86% of weekly follow-up visits were completed. In the primary intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis, assignment to kudzu versus placebo reduced number of binge-drinking days (incidence rate ratio [IRR]=0.73, 95% CI=0.54, 0.99); as-treated (taking medication ≥2.7 days per week) analysis estimated a stronger effect (IRR=0.58, 95% CI=0.38, 0.89). Secondary outcomes showed lower number of binge-drinking weeks in ITT analysis (relative risk [RR]=0.79, 95% CI=0.62, 0.99). In as-treated analyses, reductions in number of binge-drinking weeks (RR=0.68, 95% CI=0.47, 0.98) and PEth positivity (RR=0.82, 95% CI=0.67, 0.99) were also significant. The number of binge-drinking days showed significant sustained reductions at the 3-month posttreatment visit (RR=0.68, 95% CI=0.47, 0.97). The overall frequency of adverse events was similar between groups. No serious adverse events related to kudzu occurred during follow-up.

CONCLUSIONS: Targeted use of kudzu reduced self-reported binge-drinking outcomes among adults with AUD during treatment compared to placebo. Greater reductions in binge drinking and PEth were observed among those who took medication more frequently. Reductions in self-reported binge drinking persisted at the posttreatment visit. Kudzu may be a helpful pharmacologic treatment for binge drinking among people with AUD.

### Deficient Monetary Reward Processing in Schizophrenia: A Meta-Analysis and Computational Modeling of a Monetary Incentive Delay Task
**כתב עת:** 📄 Schizophr Bull | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Jiao J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Abstract Background and Hypothesis Evidence regarding monetary incentive-processing deficits in schizophrenia (SZ) remains inconsistent. Clarifying how Monetary Incentive Delay (MID) task performance relates to model-derived learning indices may help characterize motivational and incentive-processing abnormalities in SZ. We hypothesized that patients with SZ would show altered behavioral performance and model-derived expected-value and prediction-error (PE) indices associated with clinical symptom severity. Study Design We integrated a meta-analysis of MID studies (30 studies; SZ/healthy controls (HCs), n = 785/755) with an independent subject-level MID task (SZ/HCs, n = 60/74). A Rescorla–Wagner model with a fixed learning rate was used to derive group-comparable mean absolute PE values and expected-value (Q) indices. Study Results The meta-analysis showed small-to-moderate behavioral differences in SZ including prolonged reaction times (RT) and reduced reward-trial correct rate (CR). In an independent subject-level computational study, patients with SZ showed prolonged RT, lower reward- and loss-trial CR, and higher neutral-trial CR than HCs, consistent with reduced cue-specific modulation. Model-derived analyses showed higher absolute reward PE and absolute loss PE (ABSLPE), lower Reward-Q, and more negative Loss-Q in SZ under the current Rescorla–Wagner framework. Loss-related indices, particularly ABSLPE and Loss-Q, were associated with Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale-Negative scores. Trial-level logistic regression analyses further indicated weaker cue-related modulation of hit probability in SZ. Conclusions These findings suggest that SZ is associated with altered MID task performance and model-derived PE- and Q-related indices. These indices may help characterize incentive-processing abnormalities in SZ, but their clinical and neurobiological significance requires validation in future longitudinal and multimodal studies.

### Primary care exploration of suicidal ideation by general practitioners before suicidal acts: a national observational study of the French Sentinelles Network
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Prim Care | **סוג מחקר:** מחקר תצפיתי | **מחברים:** Bertherat I et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 6

Background The exploration of suicidal ideation (SI) during primary care consultations with general practitioners (GPs) is crucial for suicide prevention. We performed a retrospective assessment of the prevalence of SI exploration by GPs during the six months preceding suicidal acts with the aim of identifying associated factors, such as the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Methods We performed bivariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses on data from weekly questionnaires completed by 459 GPs from the French Sentinelles Network (2013–2023) regarding 1,156 suicidal acts: 928 suicide attempts (SA) and 205 suicides (S). Results GPs had explored SI during the six months before a suicidal act for 54.9% (95%CI 52.0–57.8) of patients. The prevalence of SI exploration was higher after 2020 (post-COVID period) than before 2020 (61.8% [95%CI: 57.6; 65.8] versus 48.2% [95%CI: 44.0; 52.3], p < 0.001). Factors positively associated with SI exploration were: post-COVID period (OR = 2.25, 95%CI [1.5–3.4], p < 0.001), spontaneous expression of SI (OR = 20.52, 95%CI [9.1–55.7], p < 0.001), consultation for psychological reasons (OR = 3.37, 95%CI [1.9–6.0], p < 0.001), history of SA (OR = 2.10, 95%CI [1.4–3.2], p < 0.001), depression (OR = 1.92, 95%CI [1.3–2.9], p = 0.001), SD versus SA (OR = 1.86, 95%CI [1.1–3.3], p = 0.024), substance misuse (OR = 1.82, 95%CI [1.1–3.0], p = 0.020), consultation within the last month (OR = 1.63, 95%CI [1.1–2.4], p = 0.010). Limitations The findings may be subject to a social desirability bias. Conclusion The exploration of SI needs to be improved, particularly for patients consulting for somatic conditions. GPs may have become more aware of SI since the COVID-19 pandemic. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-026-03465-5 .

Highlights • The COVID pandemic may have increased GPs’ awareness of suicidal ideation • Suicidal ideation is insufficiently explored by GPs when patients have somatic comorbid conditions • Most suicide risk factors influence the exploration of suicidal ideation by GPs Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12875-026-03465-5 .

Introduction Suicide is a major global health issue. In France, 250,000 suicide attempts (SA) and 9,000 deaths from suicide (SD) are reported annually (13.4/100,000 inhabitants), with suicide constituting the second leading cause of death in the15-24 years age group [ 1 ]. During a suicidal crisis, suicidal ideation (SI) emerges and becomes increasingly invasive, eventually leading to a suicidal act (SD ou SA). 82.7% of patients displaying suicidal behavior report suicidal ideation or planning [ 2 ]. SI is the third most important predictor of suicide [ 3 ]. In primary care, the goal is to provide care and allow patients to express their suffering, thereby helping them to overcome the crisis and prevent a suicidal act [ 4 ]. General practitioners (GPs) play a key role in suicide prevention, as they often manage common mental disorders, as instance mood and anxiety disorders [ 5 – 7 ], particularly in certain areas of France in which access to psychiatrists is very limited. About 45% of patients who died by SD suicide [ 8 , 9 ] or made a SA [ 10 ] consulted a primary care physician during the month preceding the suicidal act. However, a significant proportion of patients do not openly express their suicidal thoughts or behaviors [ 11 ]. For example, in a Dutch sentinel network study [ 12 ], the practitioner considered suicidal behavior a matter of concern at the last consultation in only about 30% of cases. There were, therefore, missed opportunities to prevent suicide in those patients whose suicidal behavior was not detected. General practitioners frequently report difficulties exploring SI with their patients, which they attribute to a lack of training or time constraints [ 13 ], further contributing to these missed opportunities. Improving the recognition of SI is, therefore, essential to improve suicide prevention. This aspect has become all the more important since the recent COVID-19 pandemic, which contributed to a deterioration of mental health in the general population and an increase in the risk of suicidal ideation [ 14 , 15 ]. The exploration of suicidal ideation is particularly challenging for GPs because suicide risk can increase rapidly in primary care patients [ 2 ], and individuals experiencing SI may be less engaged in care [ 16 ]. Patients may also choose not to discuss their suicidal ideation with their GP [ 17 , 18 ]. More frequent consultations, allowing the GP to build a trust relationship, could help. Previous studies in diverse primary care settings populations showed that about 8% of primary care patients had experienced SI during the month preceding the consultation [ 19 , 20 ]. Some of these patients consulted for somatic conditions, making it harder for the GP to identify the suicide risk [ 21 ]. Studies on depressed patients have shown that SI is explored in 40% to 66% cases [ 22 , 23 ]. However, the frequency of SI exploration by GPs in patients who attempt or die by suicide has never been evaluated. Patients could also self-disclose suicidal ideations to the practitioner, which can be limited for several reasons. Patients could limit the expression of SI: for example because they don’t have it at the time of the consultation, or because they feared the outcome of disclosure, including stigma, overreaction, and loss of autonomy [ 11 ]. In depressed patients, the severity of depression [ 23 ] and the patient’s age and sex [ 22 ] have already been shown to be associated with the likelihood of SI being explored by the GP. The influence of other risk factors for suicide on the likelihood of GPs exploring SI remains unknown. These factors include previous SA [ 24 ], alcohol misuse [ 25 ], long-term unemployment [ 26 ], and the diagnosis of a chronic disease [ 27 ]. The influence of other factors associated with an increase in SI, such as the COVID-19 pandemic [ 14 , 28 ], on the likelihood of GPs exploring SI has also been little studied. Furthermore, the influence of other characteristics of the suicidal act — such as the violent or non-violent nature of the act, as defined in previous studies [ 28 , 29 ] — is also unknown. There is some evidence to suggest that SD and SA define two different populations SD [ 30 ]. We therefore forced this variable into the multivariate model, as it has been shown that a variable non-significant in bivariate logistic regression can be significant in a multivariate model [ 31 ]. The characteristics of the GPs have also been shown to be associated with the likelihood of detecting SI in a population with clinical anxiety or depression. Key GP characteristics include the recent participation of the GP in training programs on depression [ 23 ], the duration of the consultation [ 23 ], GP age, and having undergone psychotherapy [ 32 ]. The factors influencing the exploration of suicidal ideation by GPs may partly reflect their knowledge of the risk factors associated with suicide risk, shedding light on areas in which training is required to improve the detection and prevention of suicidal behavior. This approach could help to identify profiles for which SI is underexplored, including patients for which GPs do not even consider evaluating SI despite the existence of a real risk. In this context, the objectives of our study were: 1) to evaluate the prevalence of the exploration of suicidal ideation by GPs in the six months preceding a suicidal act in primary care patients and to assess the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on this prevalence and 2) to identify factors patient and GP characteristics associated with the exploration of SI.
