### Mindfulness-Based Intervention Improves Cognitive Function in Patients With Schizophrenia in Remitted: A Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Int J Methods Psychiatr Res | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Lyu P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

ABSTRACT Background Patients with schizophrenia exhibit deficits in facial emotion recognition (FER), attention, P300, and quality of life (QoL), which severely hinder functional recovery. This study aims to explore the effects of mindfulness‐based intervention (MBI) on these outcomes and the potential mediating mechanisms. Methods In this 8‐week randomized, single‐blind, controlled study, a total of 70 remitted patients with schizophrenia were randomly assigned to the MBI group or the control group. The Five‐Factor Mindfulness Scale (FFMQ), FER, P300 indices, Continuous Performance Test‐Identical Pairs (CPT‐IP), and Schizophrenia Quality of Life Scale (SQLS) were measured at baseline and week 8 to assess mindfulness levels, cognitive function, neurophysiological parameters, and QoL. Linear mixed models evaluated intervention effects. Mediation analyses used pre‐post change scores with bootstrap resampling. Results For the primary outcome, MBI significantly improved FFMQ ( β = 16.750, 95% CI [7.769, 25.731], p < 0.001). For the exploratory outcomes, significant improvements were observed for P300 latency ( β = −63.602, 95% CI [−88.651, −38.554], p < 0.001) and CPT‐IP ( β = 8.219, 95% CI [4.004, 12.433], p < 0.001). FER and P300 amplitude showed nominal improvements only, and SQLS showed a non‐significant trend. A significant indirect effect through FER change was observed for change in SQLS (indirect effect = −6.343, 95% CI [−11.467, −2.288]), but CPT‐IP change did not mediate P300 amplitude improvement. Conclusion MBI significantly improved the primary outcome of mindfulness (FFMQ) and showed promising exploratory improvements in processing speed (P300 latency) and sustained attention (CPT‐IP). FER change may represent a potential indirect pathway associated with change in SQLS. These findings support MBI as a promising cognitive rehabilitation intervention. Trial Registration https://www.chictr.org.cn , identifier ChiCTR 2500106735, registered on: 29/07/2025

1 Introduction Schizophrenia is a severe psychiatric disorder, with its global age‐standardized point prevalence estimated at 0.29% (GBD 2019 Mental Disorders Collaborators 2022 ), while in China, this figure stood at 0.31% (Huang et al. 2019 ). The impact of schizophrenia on patients extends beyond positive symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, delusions). Cognitive deficits including impairments in attention and facial emotion recognition (FER), and social functional impairments particularly reduced quality of life (QoL), constitute more persistent burdens that impede patients' reintegration into society even after clinical stability is achieved (M. Dong et al. 2019 ; Owen et al. 2016 ). Deficits in FER and attention may lead to poorer social functioning and QoL (Kohler et al. 2010 ; Pavlova et al. 2023 ; Rocca et al. 2018 ). For instance, attentional distraction frequently undermines patients' ability to complete daily tasks, while difficulties in FER precipitate failures in social interaction. These challenges ultimately contribute to a decline in QoL (M. Dong et al. 2019 ). Currently, while antipsychotic medications are available for the treatment of schizophrenia, their side effects frequently result in medication discontinuation and distress. Additionally, these medications do not consistently improve cognitive and social functions (Wykes et al. 2017 ). Furthermore, a number of patients with schizophrenia exhibit either non‐response to antipsychotic medications or persistent residual symptoms (such as blunted affect, social withdrawal, cognitive impairment) throughout the course of their illness (Javed and Charles 2018 ). Consequently, social rehabilitation for patients with schizophrenia confronts considerable challenges. Fortunately, international guidelines recommend combining psychosocial interventions with antipsychotic medications for the treatment of schizophrenia (Gaebel et al. 2021 ; Galderisi et al. 2021 ). As highly effective supplementary approaches, common psychosocial interventions include cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) and mindfulness‐based intervention (MBI). Originating from meditation, mindfulness centers on “non‐judgmental awareness of present‐moment experiences.” Recent studies have demonstrated that MBI improves emotion regulation (Vancampfort et al. 2021 ), positive and negative symptoms (Böge et al. 2021 ; K. H. Lee 2019 ; Shen et al. 2023 ), neurocognitive functions (Shen et al. 2023 ), and QoL (Xue et al. 2024 ) in patients with schizophrenia. FER is a core component of social cognition in schizophrenia and a key factor influencing social functioning (Lyu et al. 2024 ). Patients with schizophrenia rely on FER to communicate with the general public, and this ability is crucial for effective communication and social interaction because it allows individuals to understand others' emotional states and respond appropriately (Faghel‐Soubeyrand et al. 2020 ; Gao et al. 2021 ; Martínez‐Cao et al. 2024 ). Impaired FER in these patients hinders their ability to identify others' diverse emotional states (Kohler et al. 2010 ), and they exhibit particularly notable deficits in recognizing negative emotions (anger, fear, sadness) (Barkhof et al. 2015 ). This impairment causes misunderstandings, social isolation, and deterioration of interpersonal relationships, which exacerbates the social withdrawal common in these patients, severely disrupts their social functioning, and may even triggers inappropriate social reactions (e.g., aggressive behavior), thereby making their social interactions more challenging (Bulgari et al. 2020 ; X. Dong et al. 2024 ; S. C. Lee et al. 2020 ). The research results show that attention‐based mindfulness is beneficial to FER in healthy individuals (Giannou et al. 2023 ). However, research on the effect of MBI on FER in patients with schizophrenia remains limited, and the relationship among mindfulness, FER, and QoL also lacks clarity. Therefore, further in‐depth research is warranted to address these gaps. Attention is one of the cores of neurocognition. A large multi‐site study using the CPT‐IP neuropsychological test to measure sustained attention or vigilance found that attention deficits exist in schizophrenia, are independent of symptom severity, and are related to functional capacity of daily living (Rocca et al. 2018 ). P300, as a component of event‐related potential (ERP), is closely related to cognitive activity. Perrottelli et al. found reduced auditory and visual P300 amplitudes in first‐episode schizophrenia (FES) patients and high‐risk psychiatric populations (Perrottelli et al. 2021 ), suggesting that P300 abnormalities may already be present before the full onset of psychosis. P300 deficits are considered an endophenotype of schizophrenia and are used to measure neurobiological susceptibility to this disorder (Chun et al. 2013 ). Recently, a study found that patients with opioid use disorder exhibited significant changes in P300 following an 8‐week MBI (Garland et al. 2025 ). However, the effect of mindfulness on P300 in schizophrenia patients remains unclear, as does the relationship among mindfulness, attention, and QoL. This study aimed to investigate the effects of a MBI on mindfulness levels, FER, attention, QoL, and P300 in remitted patients with schizophrenia through an 8‐week randomized clinical trial, and to clarify the underlying mechanisms among these variables. ### [Confidential enquiry into maternal suicide: lessons for clinical practice]
**כתב עת:** 📄 Tijdschr Psychiatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lommerse KM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

BACKGROUND: In the Netherlands, suicide is the leading cause of maternal death up to one year postpartum. Understanding underlying causes and care processes is essential to reduce maternal mortality. Structured case analysis can help identify factors contributing to deaths in the perinatal period.

AIM: To identify gaps in care for peripartum women experiencing suicidality and to formulate recommendations.

METHOD: Retrospective qualitative analysis of five cases reviewed in 2021 and 2024 by the Dutch Audit Committee Maternal Mortality and Morbidity. Information was available from primary care and/or mental health services. Additional committee documentation was used to formulate lessons learned, and a vignette was derived from discussed case histories.

RESULTS: The analysis yielded lessons in five themes: 1. identification of non-medical risk factors and suicidality, 2. regional network agreements for perinatal mental healthcare, 3. safety-planning in peripartum suicidality, 4. reporting and 5. peer-support.

CONCLUSION: The analysis highlighted important lessons for the care of peripartum women with suicidality, particularly in the areas of recognition, multidisciplinary collaboration, and access to specialized perinatal psychiatric care. Limitations include incomplete documentation and underreporting.
