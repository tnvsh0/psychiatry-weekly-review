### Narrative identity, mental illness, and recovery: A systematic review across diagnoses
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Clinical Psychology Review (Clin Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Thomsen DK et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Individuals across a range of mental illnesses struggle with identity problems. These problems are important to investigate because they may represent vulnerability to mental illness, emerge as consequences of psychopathology, and pose barriers to recovery. The narrative identity approach is especially suited to illuminate identity problems because it combines a first-person perspective with systematic quantitative studies and provides a strong theoretical foundation for recovery-oriented care. We present a pre-registered systematic review across diagnoses to examine 1) how mental illness is associated with narrative identity and 2) whether narrative identity is related to recovery. After screening 2,327 abstracts, we identified 75 relevant papers. The studies sampled individuals with a range of diagnoses and employed a multitude of narrative identity and recovery measures. Our synthesis suggests that across the examined mental illnesses, narrative identity is less coherent and with adversely affected meaning-making and self-event connections as well as more negatively emotionally toned with lower agency and lower communion themes. The evidence was most robust for schizophrenia, which was more often studied. Regarding relations between narrative identity and recovery, we could not detect consistent patterns, likely due to the complex pairing of multiple narrative identity and recovery measures combined with small sample sizes. Future research could examine a broader array of mental illnesses with respect to transdiagnostic and disorder-specific narrative identity problems and prioritize well-powered, prospective and carefully controlled studies to gain a deeper understanding of the sources of narrative identity problems and the predictive power of narrative identity in relation to recovery.

### Pilot trial of a brief tailored online treatment for rumination and worry in premenstrual dysphoric disorder
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Upton E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

BACKGROUND: Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) affects up to 8% of menstruating individuals and involves severe cyclical mood disturbance. Accessible psychological interventions are needed. Repetitive Negative Thinking (RNT; rumination, worry) is implicated in PMDD severity and represents a novel treatment target.

METHODS: This open, single-group pilot trial examined the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary outcomes of a brief, clinician-supported online CBT intervention targeting RNT in PMDD. N = 37 Australian women with elevated premenstrual distress (91.9% [n = 34] of whom had provisional PMDD) and elevated RNT received the 4-module program over 4-8 weeks. Assessments were conducted at baseline, post-treatment, and 5-week follow-up (one menstrual cycle post-treatment). Primary outcomes were PMDD symptom severity and impact (PSST). Secondary outcomes included RNT, rumination, worry, depression, suicidality, anxiety, distress, wellbeing, and functioning. Satisfaction and qualitative feedback were collected post-treatment.

RESULTS: Satisfaction was good (93% mostly-very satisfied), and participants reported improved understanding of PMDD and gaining practical skills to manage RNT and premenstrual symptoms. Two thirds (64.9%) completed the program. Linear mixed models showed significant, large improvements from baseline to post-treatment and follow-up in PMDD symptom severity and impact (ps < 0.001; g = 1.18-1.81), as well as RNT, rumination, worry, depression, anxiety, wellbeing, and functioning (ps < 0.05; g = 0.52-1.82).

CONCLUSIONS: This pilot supports the preliminary feasibility, acceptability, and positive outcomes of a brief RNT-focused online CBT intervention for PMDD. Future research should examine how to improve completion rates, and evaluate the program in a randomised controlled trial with prospective symptom charting to confirm PMDD diagnoses.

### [The design process and protocol for a cognitive-behavioral intervention to reduce electronic cigarette use among university students]
**כתב עת:** 📄 Rev Esp Salud Publica | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Enríquez Chacón JR et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

OBJECTIVE: Electronic cigarette use has become an emerging Public Health concern among university students due to its increasing prevalence and the risks associated with nicotine exposure. Cognitive-behavioral interventions have shown promise in addressing this behavior; however, evidence on structured interventions adapted to the Mexican context remains limited. The aim of this study was to design an intervention to reduce electronic cigarette use among university students and establish the protocol for its implementation.

METHODS: The intervention was developed through the stages of discovery, design, and refinement. Its components were based on the Theory of Planned Behavior, while its activities were informed by the taxonomy of behaviour change methods. Subsequently, a pilot experimental longitudinal study with pretest, posttest, and three-month follow-up assessments will be conducted. A total of sixty university students aged eighteen/twenty-four years from a Mexican public university will be recruited through convenience sampling and randomly assigned to the study groups. The intervention will consist of six fifty-minute face-to-face sessions incorporating educational components, cognitive restructuring, and social and personal skills training. Statistical analyses will be exploratory and will include between-group comparisons, repeated-measures analyses, and estimation of effect sizes.

CONCLUSIONS: The protocol will provide preliminary evidence on the feasibility and initial evaluation of an intervention aimed at reducing electronic cigarette use, generating information to inform the design of future studies and prevention strategies for the Mexican university student population.

OBJECTIVE: El consumo de cigarrillos electrónicos constituye un problema emergente de Salud Pública entre los jóvenes universitarios debido al incremento de su uso y los riesgos asociados a la exposición a la nicotina. Las intervenciones cognitivo-conductuales han mostrado utilidad para abordar esta conducta; sin embargo, existe limitada evidencia de intervenciones estructuradas adaptadas al contexto mexicano. El objetivo del presente estudio fue diseñar una intervención para disminuir el consumo del cigarrillo electrónico en jóvenes universitarios y establecer el protocolo para su implementación.

METHODS: La intervención fue diseñada mediante las etapas de descubrimiento, diseño y refinación; los componentes incluidos se fundamentaron en la Teoría de la Conducta Planeada y las actividades a partir de la taxonomía de métodos de cambio de comportamiento. Posteriormente, se realizará un estudio piloto experimental y longitudinal con mediciones pretest, postest y seguimiento a tres meses. Se incluirá a sesenta estudiantes universitarios de entre dieciocho y veinticuatro años de una universidad pública mexicana, seleccionados mediante muestreo por conveniencia y asignados aleatoriamente a los grupos de estudio. La intervención constará de seis sesiones presenciales de cincuenta minutos con componentes educativos, reestructuración cognitiva y entrenamiento en habilidades sociales y personales. Los análisis estadísticos serán exploratorios e incluirán comparaciones entre grupos, mediciones repetidas y estimación de tamaños del efecto.

CONCLUSIONS: El protocolo permitirá obtener evidencia preliminar sobre la factibilidad y evaluación inicial de una intervención para disminuir el consumo de cigarrillos electrónicos, aportando información para el diseño de estudios posteriores y estrategias de prevención en población universitaria mexicana.

### Efficacy of cognitive behavioral therapy for anxiety and depression symptoms in Parkinson's disease patients: a systematic review and meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Neurol Sci | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Wu Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

BACKGROUND: Anxiety and depression are highly prevalent non-motor symptoms in Parkinson's disease (PD), severely compromising health-related quality of life. Conventional pharmacological management is frequently constrained by adverse effects, polypharmacy, and pharmacokinetic interactions. Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) represents a promising structured non-pharmacological strategy, but an updated, comprehensive synthesis of clinical trial evidence is warranted.

OBJECTIVE: To systematically evaluate the efficacy and clinical safety profile of CBT for alleviating anxiety and depressive symptoms in patients with PD.

METHODS: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, and Web of Science were searched from database inception to October 2025. RCTs evaluating CBT (≥ 4 sessions over ≥ 4 weeks) versus control conditions (e.g., treatment as usual, waitlist, active controls) in adults with PD reporting anxiety and/or depression outcomes were included. Two reviewers independently screened literature, extracted data, and assessed methodological quality using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled via random-effects models. Subgroup, sensitivity, and publication bias analyses were performed.

RESULTS: Twenty-one RCTs comprising 892 patients with PD (CBT: n = 465; control: n = 427) were included. Meta-analysis demonstrated that CBT was associated with statistically significant reductions in anxiety symptoms (16 studies; SMD = -0.66, 95% CI -0.83 to -0.50; P < 0.001; I 2  = 79.1%) and depressive symptoms (14 studies; SMD = -0.61, 95% CI -0.78 to -0.44; P < 0.001; I 2  = 86.6%). Subgroup analyses indicated enhanced therapeutic effects in interventions with durations exceeding 10 weeks and when evaluated using population-adapted instruments (e.g., GAI, GDS). Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses confirmed the robustness of pooled estimates, and Egger's tests revealed no significant publication bias.

CONCLUSION: CBT produces significant, clinically meaningful reductions in anxiety and depressive symptoms in patients with Parkinson's disease. Despite substantial between-study heterogeneity and variations in intervention delivery, structured CBT should be recommended as an integral component of multidisciplinary PD care. Larger multicenter RCTs with standardized disease-specific core outcome sets and longer follow-up are warranted.

### Longitudinal trajectories of treatment response in patients with longstanding eating disorders
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eat Disord | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Dunbar EP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov-Dec

Debate within the eating disorders (ED) field has led to the proposal of novel, and sometimes controversial, approaches to addressing the needs of those with a long illness course. However, it remains unclear whether treatment response is indeed predictably determined by illness duration. This study utilized latent growth mixture modeling (LGMM) to examine symptom trajectories in response to Multi-Diagnostic Eating Disorder Dialectical Behavior Therapy (MED-DBT) and identified which classes patients with a longstanding ED (L-ED) were most likely to fall into. Participants were 278 adults (188 anorexia nervosa (AN), 90 bulimia nervosa (BN)) attending a partial hospitalization program delivering MED-DBT. Data were provided at admission, 1 month into treatment, discharge, and 6-month and 1-year follow-ups. Symptoms were assessed using the Eating Disorder Examination-Questionnaire (EDE-Q). Classes were compared on demographic and clinical characteristics including L-ED status. A 5-class model best fit the AN data and a 3-class model best fit the BN data. Class membership differed by treatment history, clinical impairment, and anxiety, and in BN also by depression and BMI. L-ED status was distributed across classes. Findings demonstrate that adults with L-ED show diverse trajectories of change and can experience meaningful improvement in response to MED-DBT. Results challenge assumptions that individuals with a L-ED are treatment-resistant and underscore the importance of continuing to deliver treatments that promote change for this population.

### A proposed decision-making tool for addressing OCD and co-occurring PTSD within a MED-DBT framework
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eat Disord | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Dunbar EP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov-Dec

Co-occurring obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) are common and clinically consequential among adults with eating disorders (EDs), yet clear guidance for treatment prioritization and sequencing remains limited. This brief report introduces a structured decision-making tool designed to support Stage-2 prioritization of OCD versus PTSD treatment within a multi-diagnostic ED-dialectical behavior therapy (MED-DBT) program. The tool integrates readiness criteria, diagnostic confirmation, and decision points to guide clinicians in treatment planning. We describe the feasibility of integrating and delivering this structured decision-making tool within a MED-DBT program, along with preliminary clinical trends from a pilot sample. More than half of program admissions during the study period flagged for probable OCD/PTSD, though only a small subgroup met Stage-2 readiness criteria, consistent with patterns in higher levels of care. Among patients receiving protocol-based exposure and response prevention or trauma treatment, early symptom trajectories were favorable across ED, OCD, PTSD, and impairment domains. These preliminary findings support the feasibility of structured prioritization within MED-DBT and highlight pathways for future evaluation.
