### A multi-institutional investigation of psilocybin's effects on mouse behavior
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lu OD et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 2

Studies reporting the effects of psychedelic drugs on mouse behavior are rapidly emerging. However, the reproducibility and reliability of these effects remain uncertain. Here we implemented a multi-institutional collaborative approach to assess which effects of psilocybin on mouse behavior are robust and replicable. Five independent laboratories performed the same experiments to test the acute and persistent effects of psilocybin (2 mg kg -1 , intraperitoneally) on various behaviors. Psilocybin acutely increased anxiety- and avoidance-related behaviors and decreased fear expression in a robust and replicable manner. Surprisingly, however, psilocybin did not have replicable effects 24 h after administration in terms of reducing anxiety- and depression-like behaviors or facilitating fear extinction learning. Additionally, we did not observe psilocybin-induced alterations in social preference or social reward learning. Our characterization of psilocybin's behavioral effects, using ~200 male and female mice per experiment across five laboratories, demonstrates several robust acute effects and suggests that some of psilocybin's reported persistent effects in mice may be modest and/or inconsistent.

### Redox-dependent remodeling of mitochondrial nucleoids contributes to bioenergetic decline during neuronal aging
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Cheng XT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 3

Summary Why mitochondria progressively fail in aging neurons remains a fundamental question in neurobiology. Although bioenergetic decline and oxidative stress are hallmarks of aging, whether redox signaling remodels mitochondrial nucleoids (mt-nucleoids) to impair neuronal bioenergetics remains unclear. Using aged mouse neurons and human iPSC-derived neurons, we demonstrate that redox-driven mt-nucleoid remodeling underlies reduced mtDNA transcript abundance and bioenergetic decline. Combining MINFLUX nanoscopy with single-molecule transcription mapping, we identify distinct mt-nucleoid architectures governed by TFAM-mediated mtDNA compaction. Aging-associated oxidative stress drives mt-nucleoid remodeling from compact, transcriptionally active structures toward decompacted forms with reduced mtDNA transcript abundance and impaired mitochondrial ATP production. This remodeling involves species-specific TFAM redox mechanisms: cysteine oxidation promotes human TFAM polymerization, whereas methionine oxidation weakens mouse TFAM-mtDNA interactions. Notably, expressing an oxidation-resistant TFAM variant restores mt-nucleoid organization, mtDNA transcript abundance, and ATP production in aged neurons, identifying mt-nucleoid architecture as a potential target for preserving neuronal bioenergetics during aging.

Introduction A central challenge in aging research is understanding why mitochondria—the primary source of cellular energy—progressively fail in long-lived tissues. This question is especially important for neurons, which are uniquely vulnerable to bioenergetic deficits because of their high metabolic demands, elaborate morphologies, and postmitotic longevity. 1 , 2 Recent advances underscore this problem: (i) mitochondrial transcripts encoding electron transport chain (ETC) subunits decline broadly with age, 3 , 4 (ii) mitochondrial dysfunction accelerates aging, 5 , 6 whereas preserving mitochondrial integrity delays functional decline, 7 and (iii) brain-wide respiration atlases identify aging as a major determinant of mitochondrial performance. 8 Together, these findings highlight a fundamental gap in understanding how age-associated changes in mitochondrial genome organization and regulation—beyond mitochondrial genome (mtDNA) mutations 9 , 10 —drive bioenergetic decline. Although mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress are defining hallmarks of aging, 11 – 13 how redox imbalance disrupts mitochondrial genome organization over the lifespan remain largely unknown. The mtDNA, which encodes 13 essential ETC subunits, 14 is packaged with its transcriptional machinery into ~100-nm mt-nucleoids. 15 – 17 Mitochondrial Transcription Factor A (TFAM), the principal nucleoid-associated protein, binds, bends, loops, and compacts mtDNA through dimerization, thereby organizing the genome for transcription. 18 – 20 Disruption of TFAM-dependent mtDNA packaging and transcription has been implicated in aging and neurodegeneration. 21 Individual mt-nucleoids can exist in transcriptionally active or quiescent states, 22 but how these structural states are regulated and affect mitochondrial gene expression and bioenergetics remains poorly understood. Because mitochondria are a major source of reactive oxygen species (ROS), 23 , 24 mt-nucleoids are chronically exposed to oxidative stress, which increases in aging neurons. 25 Although reversible protein oxidation contributes to physiological redox signaling, persistent oxidative stress disrupts redox homeostasis and promotes protein dysfunction and tissue aging. 26 Whether age-associated oxidative stress remodels mt-nucleoid architecture to impair mitochondrial transcription and bioenergetic function, and whether these changes can be reversed, remains unknown. Addressing this question has been limited by the lack of mammalian neuronal aging models that integrate nanoscale mt-nucleoid analysis with measurements of mitochondrial ATP production, redox homeostasis, and mtDNA transcript abundance at single-organelle and single-molecule resolution. Here, using dorsal root ganglion neurons (DRGNs) from young adult and aged mice, we combine MINFLUX nanoscopy with live ATP and redox imaging, in situ transcription mapping, biomolecular phase-separation assays, and machine-learning modeling to define a mitochondrial aging signature. Aged neurons exhibit disorganized mt-nucleoids, reduced mitochondrial respiration and ATP production, elevated oxidative stress, and reduced mtDNA transcript abundance. Similar remodeling occurs in aged mouse hippocampal neurons. MINFLUX imaging (~3-nm localization precision) reveals an aging-associated mt-nucleoid architecture characterized by decompacted mtDNA and reduced TFAM localization density. Mechanistically, oxidative stress drives this transition through species-specific TFAM redox mechanisms: disulfide-mediated polymerization of human TFAM and methionine oxidation–dependent weakening of mouse TFAM–mtDNA interactions. Despite these distinct mechanisms, both converge on mt-nucleoid decompaction. These redox-dependent changes are reproduced in human iPSC-derived cortical neurons exposed to oxidative stress, whereas expression of a redox-resistant, mtDNA-packaging-competent TFAM variant restores mt-nucleoid compaction, mtDNA transcript abundance, and ATP production in aged neurons. Together, our findings establish redox-dependent mt-nucleoid remodeling as a conserved mechanism linking oxidative stress to mitochondrial dysfunction during neuronal aging and identify TFAM redox regulation as a potential therapeutic target for age-associated neurodegeneration. Results Establishing an aging mouse neuron model To investigate how aging remodels mt-nucleoids, we established a primary neuronal aging model using DRGNs isolated from young adult and aged mice. Because postmitotic neurons do not divide, DRGNs from aged mice provide a tractable system for investigating age-dependent remodeling of mt-nucleoids and mitochondrial bioenergetics. Unlike intact aged brain tissue, cultured DRGNs enable nanoscale visualization of individual mt-nucleoids together with measurements of mitochondrial ATP production, redox dynamics, and mtDNA transcripts. Although many neuronal populations from aged animals exhibit poor survival in culture, 27 , 28 DRGNs are unusually resilient and retain key physiological properties. 29 – 35 We therefore compared DRGNs from young adult (1 month, P1M) and aged (24 months, P24M) mice. Neurons from both age groups extended elaborate axonal arbors and maintained dense mitochondrial networks throughout the soma and distal processes ( Figure S1A ). Thus, this preparation provides a tractable model for examining mt-nucleoid architecture, mitochondrial bioenergetics, redox homeostasis, and mtDNA transcript abundance during neuronal aging. ### Network disruption in post-stroke depressive symptoms: a Bayesian multicohort study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Klingbeil J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

Post-stroke depressive symptoms are common, but studies on the contributions of lesion locations to depression symptoms remain inconsistent. Bayesian statistics offer the possibility to clarify these inconsistencies by providing evidence in favor and against observed associations. We reanalyzed four previously published observational cohorts with 1,864 patients with stroke characterized for depressive symptoms from France, Korea, Germany and the USA. We performed voxel-wise Bayes factor mapping using general linear models. Lesion maps were used to test for associations of post-stroke depressive symptoms with lesion location. Network level associations were tested based on structural disconnection and functional lesion network maps estimated by reference to normative connectome data. Due to a sample size with broad lesion coverage, Bayesian inference was informative in large parts of the brain. Lesion associations were found in clusters in the right hemisphere including cortical (frontal, insular, parietal) and subcortical regions (striatum, amygdala, hippocampus) and adjacent white matter. Evidence of no contribution to post-stroke depressive symptoms was observed in large parts of the left hemisphere. Structural disconnection was linked to depressive symptoms in the bilateral frontal white matter prominently involving the corpus callosum and anterior thalamic radiation. Lesion network mapping revealed evidence of an association of post-stroke depressive symptoms with network connectivity in the left posterior cingulate cortex and precuneus, and less consistently, the bilateral anterior insula, along with widespread absence of associations in other regions. Depressive symptoms were most strongly associated with right frontal lesions and right-dominant structural disconnection in frontal cortico-subcortical circuits.

### Gaze reinstatement during working memory for natural scenes
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Dong Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

The information we hold in mind with working memory (WM) may propagate beyond the cortex and out to the peripheral motor system. For instance, directionally biased hand and eye movements can express simple visuo-spatial features during WM maintenance. If such sensorimotor inflections are a form of WM representation, they may also express more detailed and task-adaptive information about WM content. Here, we ask whether gaze patterns carry item-specific detail about complex stimuli held in mind, and whether gaze during WM maintenance adapts to upcoming behavioral demands. To do this, we tracked male and female human eye movements during WM for naturalistic images, and we manipulated which image properties were most task-relevant (visual or semantic). In two experiments, we found that the eyes moved in item-specific spatiotemporal sequences during WM maintenance. Delay period gaze patterns retraced the scanpaths from stimulus encoding, echoing the 'gaze reinstatement' that has been observed during long-term memory retrieval. Therefore, gaze patterns during WM may reinstate visual encoding processes for maintenance. We also used time-resolved representational similarity analyses, guided by convolutional neural network modeling, to test the specificity of WM gaze patterns across a trial. We found that gaze representations were more distinct when the task prioritized visual detail, and they also ramped up in anticipation of the WM probe. Therefore, oculomotor WM signals are malleable to when and how the content will be used. WM may recruit the most primary structures in sensorimotor processing, where eye movements reflect flexible and future-oriented WM content information. Significance Statement  The eyes are an outwardly accessible extension of the brain. They filter sensory input and enact motor output, but may also offer a rich window into underlying cognition. For instance, pupil dilation can reflect changing cognitive load, and biased eye movements can reflect basic spatial information about content held in mind with working memory (WM). WM is a critical neuro-cognitive function, but its physiological bases remain under debate. Here, we show that during WM the eyes can move in intricate patterns that read out the complex, real-world stimuli held in mind. This work shows how cognitive states influence oculomotor functions, and also advances theories of WM maintenance to include the most basic structures in sensorimotor processing.

### Learning-dependent hippocampal-striatal reorganization during sequential decision-making in pigeons
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yang L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 3

Adaptive sequential decision-making requires animals to learn how actions shape future states and rewards while adjusting behavioral strategies as experience accumulates. Although the hippocampus is implicated in state-structure representation and the striatum in value learning, how learning strategies evolve from exploratory sampling to efficient exploitation and how hippocampal-striatal interactions reorganize alongside this transition remain unclear. We trained six pigeons of undetermined sex to perform a probabilistic two-step decision task while simultaneously recording behavioral choices and hippocampal-striatal local field potentials. Strategy evolution was tracked using a dynamic reinforcement-learning framework in which model-based, model-free, and weighted model-based/model-free models were fitted across sliding windows. Hippocampal-striatal functional coordination was assessed using phase-locking analysis, and directed predictive influence was quantified using kernel Granger causality. The weighted model best explained choice dynamics, indicating a shift from model-based-like control during early acquisition toward model-free-like control during later stable performance. This behavioral shift was accompanied by two complementary neural changes: hippocampal-striatal phase coordination increased across learning, whereas hippocampus-to-striatum directed predictive influence decreased and covaried positively with model-based-like strategy weight. Direction-ratio analysis showed that hippocampus-to-striatum remained the predominant direction throughout learning, indicating reduced influence rather than directional reversal. After acquisition, hippocampus-to-striatum directed influence was not uniformly reduced but remained relatively preserved during rare, low-value, unrewarded, or less expected events. Together, these findings reveal a learning-dependent reorganization of hippocampal-striatal dynamics, in which increasing functional coordination is accompanied by reduced but condition-dependent hippocampal-to-striatal influence as behavior becomes more stable and efficient. Significance Statement  Adaptive learning requires coordinated changes in behavioral strategy and neural interactions as animals progress from exploration to stable performance. Here, we show that pigeons gradually shifted from flexible, model-based-like control toward more stable, model-free-like behavior during sequential learning. In parallel, hippocampal-striatal interactions underwent a complementary reorganization: phase coordination progressively increased, whereas hippocampus-to-striatum directed predictive influence decreased with declining model-based-like control. Importantly, this directed influence remained relatively preserved during rare, low-value, or unrewarded events. These findings link behavioral strategy transition to the dynamic reorganization of hippocampal-striatal interactions, revealing how neural systems may simultaneously support efficient learned behavior while retaining sensitivity to behaviorally informative events.
