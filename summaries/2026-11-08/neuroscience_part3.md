### Single-neuron encoding of rapidly learned visual information reshapes human perception
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Armendariz M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Abstract Any study of learning must capture neural or behavioural changes over time. Stimuli are therefore presented more than once, before and after learning takes place—a sequence that cannot be randomised across time. This design creates vulnerability to temporal confounds that cause illusory neural effects. We investigate this issue in a classic ‘one-shot’ learning paradigm, in which ambiguous two-tone images become recognisable after viewing a photo cue. Across common fMRI designs, we found that spurious correlations driven by the temporal proximity of stimuli created strong artefacts in multivariate data. Among certain experimental designs, we found strong neural markers of perceptual learning depending on how stimuli were distributed across these artefacts. This paradigm-dependent effect was present not only in the primary visual cortex (V1), but also in a non-visual control region (S1), indicative of an artefactual origin rather than a genuine perceptual learning effect. We present a principled approach to correct for this temporal order confound. Once applied, the artefactual learning signature was eliminated from both V1 and S1, and while the corrected signal retained information about image identity in V1, this was absent in S1. Our work demonstrates how current multivariate neuroimaging approaches to understanding learning could be influenced by methodological artefacts that misdirect interpretations towards distributed neural processing, and offers a practical framework to distinguish true learning from measurement error.

1 Introduction Sensory representations are constantly updated as we learn. For example, long-term exposure to a particular species of animal can enhance their subtle features, while hearing a familiar voice can make you instantly recognise someone you had assumed was a stranger. In cognitive neuroscience, these perceptual dynamics are often studied with cueing tasks ( Carrasco, 2018 ; Schacter & Buckner, 1998 ), in which cues that manipulate knowledge or attention can alter neural responses to subsequent stimuli. With the emergence of multivariate fMRI techniques, this is typically measured via representational similarity analysis (RSA), where variations in neural representations to different stimuli are assessed by correlating their multi-voxel responses ( Kriegeskorte et al., 2008 ). While these measures are informative and robust to various univariate signal fluctuations ( Kriegeskorte et al., 2008 ; Roth & Merriam, 2023 ), structured noise in the temporal domain can affect their results ( Alink et al., 2015 ; Cai et al., 2019 ; Mumford et al., 2014 ). This could prove particularly problematic for learning paradigms, as they often require stimuli to be presented in a specific order (e.g., pre-cueing target, cue, post-cueing target), making full randomisation or counterbalancing impossible in the temporal domain. A popular cueing task uses two-tone images to study perceptual reorganisation, or ‘one-shot’ learning ( Armendariz et al., 2025 ; Chang et al., 2016 ; Davies et al., 2018 ; Dolan et al., 1997 ; Flounders et al., 2019 ; González-García et al., 2018 ; González-García & He, 2021 ; Hsieh et al., 2010 ; Lazarova et al., 2025 ; Ludmer et al., 2011 ; Teufel et al., 2015 , 2018 ; van Loon et al., 2016 ; Yoon et al., 2007 ). In this phenomenon, an unrecognisable two-tone image, created by smoothing and contrast-binarising a greyscale photo (see Fig. 1A ; Mooney, 1957 ), becomes recognisable only after exposure to the original photo. Perceptual reorganisation can therefore be robustly triggered by the sequential presentation of a two-tone image, its corresponding photo cue, and the same two-tone again ( Ramachandran, 1994 ). Because the newly acquired knowledge can provoke distinct perceptual experiences in response to two presentations of the same stimulus, two-tone paradigms offer a well-controlled approach for measuring top-down modulation of sensory inputs across the brain. RSA can reveal whether two-tone images are represented more similarly to their photo versions after cueing, in line with the phenomenological experience of perceptual reorganisation ( Fig. 1A ). Hsieh and colleagues were the first to identify this neural marker of perceptual reorganisation ( Hsieh et al., 2010 ). They observed increased correlations between photo and two-tone stimuli following cueing in both the Lateral Occipital Cortex (LOC)—a higher-order area known to play a critical role in object recognition ( Grill-Spector et al., 2000 ; Malach et al., 1995 )—and in the early visual cortex, suggesting widespread knowledge-based modulation of neural representations ( Hsieh et al., 2010 ). Van Loon and colleagues later extended these findings, observing perceptual reorganisation effects from the primary visual cortex (V1) to the posterior fusiform (pFs), including intermediate processing stages. These were diminished in lower-order areas when NMDA-mediated feedback processing was pharmacologically inhibited ( Van Loon et al., 2016 ). More recently, González-García and colleagues replicated findings of distributed knowledge-driven processing, and further identified classically ‘non-visual’ areas, such as the frontoparietal (FPN) and default-mode (DMN) networks, as encoders of perceptual reorganisation ( González-García et al., 2018 ; González-García & He, 2021 ). Strikingly, these studies find that early visual areas represent cued two-tones more similarly to their photo versions than to the same two-tones viewed before cueing ( González-García et al., 2018 ; Hsieh et al., 2010 ; van Loon et al., 2016 ). While top-down processing has previously been found to modulate representations throughout the visual hierarchy in other perceptual tasks ( Kok et al., 2012 , 2016 ; Lamme & Roelfsema, 2000 ; Poltoratski & Tong, 2020 ; Self et al., 2013 ; Yon et al., 2023 ), the findings from perceptual reorganisation studies extend this considerably by reporting effects of prior knowledge that are widespread across the cortex, and that can dominate incoming visual input. The above findings offer compelling insights into long-standing neuroscientific debates about the distribution of conscious perceptual processing, leveraging a range of paradigm designs to reach corroborating conclusions about the strength and distribution of top-down neural signals. However, the designs used share a feature that is common to all perceptual reorganisation paradigms: uncued two-tones (‘Pre’ condition) must always precede their corresponding photo versions (‘Cue’ condition), which, in turn, must always precede the corresponding cued two-tones (‘Post’ condition, Fig. 1A ). As this presentation order is necessary for triggering and measuring perceptual reorganisation, it results in a temporal consistency across subjects that cannot be avoided by trial randomisation or counterbalancing. This raises questions about the potential influence of temporal artefacts on RSA measures of perceptual reorganisation, which may be overlooked when using conventional methods of reporting these data ( Fig. 1C ). In fMRI, structured noise can arise from both physiological and methodological sources, and can act across multiple temporal levels. For example, the subject’s motion, breathing, heart-rate and attentional drift, alongside thermal fluctuations, scanner instability, equipment radio frequency signals, and preprocessing and analysis artefacts can alter neural signals between scanning sessions, within a single session, between fMRI runs, and within single runs ( Aguirre et al., 1997 ; Alink et al., 2015 ; Cai et al., 2019 ; Mumford et al., 2014 ). The combined impact of structured noise on multi-voxel patterns may therefore be complex, and variable across subjects, set-ups, and paradigm designs. ### P2RX7 drives inflammatory microglia and pathogenic extracellular vesicle secretion in tauopathy
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bodart-Santos V et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

P2X purinoreceptor 7 (P2RX7), an ATP-gated ion channel, contributes to Alzheimer's disease (AD) progression, but its molecular mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we show that genetic disruption of P2rx7 mitigates brain atrophy, tau accumulation, and cognitive impairment in PS19 mice. Microglia-specific, but not astrocyte-specific, deletion of P2rx7 suppresses tau propagation. Single-cell RNA sequencing identifies a previously unrecognized first-responding microglial state (FRM) with prominent P2rx7 expression. P2RX7 promotes the transition from FRM to an inflammatory microglial state enriched in extracellular vesicle (EV) biogenesis. P2RX7 increases EV loading of tau and of mitochondrial proteins and DNA, which activate cytosolic DNA sensing and interferon signaling in recipient microglia. Tau-containing microglial EVs disrupt neuronal endolysosomal integrity and facilitate endosomal tau escape and phosphorylation. Thus, P2RX7 regulates the FRM branch point, driving inflammatory microglial transition and EV-mediated spread of tau and toxic mitochondrial molecules and highlighting the P2RX7-EV axis as a therapeutic target in AD.

### Filamin-A links transcriptional disruption to dopaminergic decline in progressive supranuclear palsy
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tsujikawa K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Progressive supranuclear palsy is a late-onset neurodegenerative disorder characterized by tau aggregation and prominent dopaminergic dysfunction, yet the upstream molecular processes linking transcriptional abnormalities to dopaminergic failure remain poorly understood. To explore these mechanisms, we performed an integrated multi-omics analysis using a mouse model with overexpression of human filamin-A, a cytoskeletal protein genetically implicated in progressive supranuclear palsy. Analyses were conducted in young adult mice (5-7 months) and aged mice (14-16 months) of both sexes, with transcriptomic, epigenomic, biochemical, metabolomic and behavioural profiling performed across multiple brain regions. In parallel, post-mortem human brain tissue from 11 individuals with progressive supranuclear palsy and five neurologically normal controls was analysed for biochemical validation using samples from the frontal cortex and striatum. Transcriptomic profiling identified immediate early gene-centred transcriptional dysregulation as a prominent and early molecular alteration. This change, accompanied by chromatin accessibility alterations, was associated with the activation of activator protein 1-dependent gene programmes and region-specific changes in dopaminergic protein markers across the cortex, striatum and midbrain. Multi-layered analyses revealed cortical monoamine depletion together with early striatal dopaminergic sensitization, indicating a close association between immediate early gene dysregulation and dopaminergic circuit imbalance. With ageing, the coupling was lost, coinciding with dopaminergic exhaustion and deficits in locomotor, cognitive and social behaviours. In human analyses, post-mortem brain samples from individuals with progressive supranuclear palsy showed reduced dopamine D2 receptor expression compared with neurologically normal controls, together with region-dependent alterations in c-Fos expression and enrichment of activator protein 1-related motifs in regions with reduced chromatin accessibility. Together, these findings suggest that filamin-A-dependent cytoskeletal stress acts as an upstream molecular context in which immediate early gene-centred transcriptional dysregulation emerges and becomes linked to dopaminergic vulnerability in progressive supranuclear palsy.

### Opioid-glutamate interactions modulate intrinsically rewarded communication in European starlings
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhao C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 3

Affiliative, non-sexual vocal communication is essential for social learning and bonding, yet relatively little is known about its neural bases. Past research on songbirds demonstrates that song produced in affiliative, non-sexual flocks correlates positively with a bird's intrinsic reward state, and the link between song and reward is regulated by mu opioid receptors (MORs) in the medial preoptic area (MPOA). RNAseq studies also reveal a strong positive association between affiliative song and glutamatergic gene expression in MPOA, with NMDA receptor 1 (GRIN1) and glutamate receptor scaffolding protein (Shank2) correlating strongly with affiliative singing. In male European starlings we tested the hypothesis that MOR-glutamate interactions in the MPOA regulate affiliative communication. Peripheral administration of the MOR agonist fentanyl increased affiliative singing and calling behavior while reducing stress-related behavior, beak wiping. The effects on vocal but not stress-related behavior were prevented by administration of the NMDA receptor antagonist MK-801. c-Fos mapping revealed that fentanyl increased neural activity in caudal MPOA (cMPOA) and interconnected regions implicated in social behavior, song production, and reward, and that these effects were reversed by MK-801. To identify a local site of MOR-glutamate interaction, we used AAV shRNA to downregulate Shank2 in MPOA. This manipulation prevented fentanyl from stimulating affiliative singing behavior, also without affecting stress reduction, suggesting that MOR-glutamate interactions in the MPOA facilitate social communication but not associated stress reduction. Together these findings provide the first experimental evidence that MOR-glutamate interactions within the MPOA, particularly the cMPOA, contribute to affiliative, rewarding vocal communication. Significance Statement  Affiliative, non-sexual vocal communication is fundamental to social cohesion and well-being, yet its neural mechanisms are poorly understood. This study identifies a critical role for interactions between mu opioid receptors and glutamatergic signaling in promoting non-sexual, rewarding vocal communication. In a songbird experimental system, we demonstrate that NMDA receptor activity and the postsynaptic glutamate receptor scaffolding protein Shank2 are required for opioid facilitation of affiliative song. Our findings further reveal the medial preoptic area, particularly its caudal subdivision, to be an important site of this opioid-glutamate interaction. Together, these results extend opioid-glutamate interaction frameworks beyond classic reward circuits and suggest the existence of a conserved neural mechanism supporting affiliative social behavior across vertebrates.
