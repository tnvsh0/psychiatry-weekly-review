### Why is lower family socio-economic status associated with conduct problems in children and adolescents? A systematic review of longitudinal studies
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Clinical Psychology Review (Clin Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Piotrowska PJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Socio-economic status (SES) has been extensively linked to child and adolescent conduct problems (CP). Understanding the origins of this link is important for informing policy to reduce social inequalities. Potential competing explanations have been investigated and a comprehensive review of the strength of this evidence is required to inform progress towards an integrated model of the underlying mechanisms. We systematically searched ten databases for studies published in English between 1960 and 2026. The eligibility criteria included a quantitative design, a community sample of children or adolescents, measures of SES and CP, longitudinal analyses of the association between SES and CP, and results informing on the potential mechanisms. We identified 45 papers that met the eligibility criteria, comprising 48 individual studies included in the review. Family stress pathways were the most frequently studied mechanisms; however, the results were mixed. There was some support for pathways involving warm and supportive parenting and somewhat weaker support for supervision and discipline pathways. Investment pathways involving cultural/educational resources, neighbourhood characteristics, and models incorporating family stress and investment pathways were found to have received considerable support. There was also evidence that factors such as child's diet and associations with antisocial peers may contribute. These findings support an integrated model of the mechanisms linking SES and CP. This model can provide targets for interventions aiming to reduce CP inequalities and sets the agenda for research to elucidate the mechanisms further.

### The effectiveness of virtual reality-based rehabilitation in patients with neck pain: a systematic review and meta-analysis with trial sequential analysis of randomized controlled trials
**כתב עת:** 📄 Front Public Health | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Jike A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background Neck pain is a widespread condition impacting daily functioning and quality of life. Virtual Reality (VR) technology has gained attention as a non-pharmacological intervention in rehabilitative medicine, offering potential benefits for pain management. This study systematically reviewed and conducted a meta-analysis on the effectiveness of VR rehabilitation for neck pain. Methods Following PRISMA guidelines, a comprehensive literature search was performed across databases (e.g., PubMed) up to October 1, 2024, targeting randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing VR-based rehabilitation with alternative interventions for neck pain. Data extraction focused on outcomes like the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) and Neck Disability Index (NDI). Quality and bias were assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool, and the GRADE approach evaluated evidence certainty. Trial Sequential Analysis (TSA) was also employed to ensure robustness in conclusions. This protocol was prospectively registered with PROSPERO (CRD42022334319). Results The meta-analysis, which included 11 randomized controlled trials with 476 participants, demonstrated that virtual reality-based rehabilitation significantly reduced VAS scores [471 patients, mean difference (MD) −0.70, p = 0.01] and NDI scores (411 patients, MD −3.06, p < 0.0001) within the first week. Furthermore, significant reductions in VAS (315 patients, MD −1.00, p < 0.05) and NDI scores (315 patients, MD −3.81, p = 0.005) were observed from the first week to 6 months in the VR-based rehabilitation group. Trial sequential analysis further confirmed the statistical strength and reliability of these findings. Improvements in cervical mobility were observed, as evidenced by increased neck flexion and extension. Conclusion VR-based rehabilitation shows promise as an effective strategy for managing neck pain, improving both pain levels and functional capacity. Systematic review registration https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD42022334319 , identifier PROSPERO (CRD42022334319).

Introduction Neck pain represents a prevalent musculoskeletal condition, with epidemiological characteristics indicating substantial incidence and prevalence rates, imposing significant burdens on both individuals and society at large ( 1 ). More than 70 percent of the global population experiences neck pain at least once in their lifetime, with 50–85% likely to experience recurrence within 1–5 years of the initial episode ( 2 , 3 ). Neck pain is a prevalent condition across all age demographics and genders, exhibiting a particularly high prevalence among individuals aged 45 to 54 years and among females ( 3–5 ). Non-specific neck pain, characterized by the absence of detectable systemic disease as the underlying cause, constitutes the predominant form among neck pain cases ( 6 , 7 ). This condition not only hinders individuals’ daily functioning and vocational abilities but can also lead to chronic functional impairment and a reduced quality of life. Neck pain has become a significant public health issue, calling for effective interventional strategies to alleviate its substantial impact on both individual well-being and societal prosperity. Virtual Reality (VR) technology presents a groundbreaking approach to addressing this issue. VR generates immersive digital environments using head-mounted displays and other devices ( 8 ). With advanced head-mounted displays and related peripherals, users are immersed in a multisensory experience that integrates visual, auditory, and haptic feedback ( 9–11 ). In recent years, VR applications have experienced significant growth in rehabilitative medicine, showing remarkable effectiveness in pain management protocols. Scientific studies have highlighted VR’s ability to alleviate pain through mechanisms such as attention diversion, emotional modulation, and enhanced motor engagement ( 12–14 ). VR technology has emerged as a pioneering non-pharmacological intervention, increasingly integrated into neck pain rehabilitation protocols, showing particularly promising potential for pain alleviation and enhancing cervical mobility ( 15 , 16 ). Although several systematic reviews have examined the benefits of VR for neck pain, they may have inadvertently overlooked the nuanced short-term and long-term efficacy of VR in modulating neck pain ( 17 , 18 ). Furthermore, these studies may face the risk of false-positive outcomes due to multiple testing and the limitations of available data. With the rapid advancement of technological capabilities and rising service demands, there has been a corresponding increase in publications in this field. Consequently, there is an urgent need to update systematic reviews and meta-analyses, which will provide substantial value to both clinical practitioners and research investigators. The objective of this study was to conduct a thorough assessment of the effectiveness of virtual reality rehabilitation for patients suffering from neck pain. Additionally, the study implemented trial sequential analysis (TSA) to verify the statistical reliability and methodological robustness of the meta-analytic findings, thereby enhancing the validity and scientific rigor of the derived conclusions. Methods Protocol and registration This systematic review adhered rigorously to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to ensure thorough methodological rigor and comprehensive reporting ( 19 ). The study protocol was registered with the international open-access Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO, number: CRD42022334319). Literature search An extensive literature search was performed using databases including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and the Cochrane Library, covering publications from their inception until October 1, 2024. The aim was to identify all relevant randomized controlled trials (RCTs) examining the use of virtual reality-based rehabilitation for neck pain. Search terms combined both indexed terms and medical subject headings (MeSH), such as “virtual reality,” “augmented reality,” “mixed reality,” “neck pain,” and “RCT.” Details of the search strategy applied to each database can be found in Additional file 1 . Study selection and inclusion criteria Inclusion criteria were: (1) population: patients experiencing neck pain; (2) intervention: implementation of virtual reality-based rehabilitation; (3) comparison: other interventions (e.g., non-VR approaches, standard care, or no intervention); (4) outcomes: evaluation using the visual analogue scale (VAS) and neck disability index (NDI); (5) study design: exclusively RCTs. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (1) studies lacking sufficient data; (3) studies without accessible full-text articles. Data extraction Data extraction was meticulously performed by two independent reviewers utilizing a piloted, standardized extraction form. Discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through consensus. Extracted data from each study included: (1) study characteristics such as authorship details, sample size, participant age, and specifics of the intervention versus comparator; (2) outcomes including VAS scores, NDI scores, and range of motion. This evaluation framework considers seven essential domains to determine methodological quality and bias risk: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other biases. Each bias domain was classified as high, unclear, or low risk. Risk of bias graphs were generated using RevMan software (version 5.3). ### Effects of mindfulness-based childbirth preparation education on psychosocial outcomes in pregnancy: a randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Pregnancy Childbirth | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Ocak S, Yılmaz T | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 10

Background Mindfulness-based interventions have been increasingly integrated into antenatal education programs to improve maternal psychosocial well-being. However, evidence regarding their effects on perceived stress, depressive symptoms, childbirth self-efficacy, and postpartum post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) remains limited and inconsistent. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of Mindfulness-Based Childbirth Preparation Education (MBCPE) on psychosocial outcomes in primiparous pregnant women. Methods This study was designed as a prospective, randomized controlled trial. The study was completed with 74 pregnant women, including 36 participants in the intervention group and 38 participants in the control group. The intervention group received Mindfulness-Based Childbirth Preparation Education over eight weeks, while the control group received Information-Based Childbirth Preparation Education over four weeks. Outcome measures included the Perceived Stress Scale, Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale, childbirth self-efficacy, and postpartum PTSD symptoms. Statistical analyses were conducted to compare group differences, with significance set at p < 0.05. Results A statistically significant difference was found between the intervention and control groups in terms of Perceived Stress Scale and Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale mean scores ( p < 0.05), indicating lower stress and depressive symptoms in the intervention group. However, no significant differences were observed between groups in childbirth self-efficacy or postpartum PTSD symptoms assessed at the fourth postpartum week ( p > 0.05). Conclusions Mindfulness-Based Childbirth Preparation Education appears to be effective in reducing perceived stress and depressive symptoms during pregnancy and the postpartum period. However, it does not appear to provide an additional benefit for childbirth self-efficacy compared with the control group. No significant between-group difference was observed in postpartum PTSD symptoms assessed at the fourth postpartum week. These findings provide preliminary support for the inclusion of mindfulness-based components in antenatal education to enhance maternal psychosocial well-being. Trial registration The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov on November 1, 2023 ( NCT06111716 ).

As childbirth is an inherently unpredictable and uncontrollable process, it is often accompanied by stress, fear, and anxiety [ 1 ]. In this context, high-quality prenatal care services play a crucial role in improving maternal and neonatal health outcomes. Childbirth preparation education is a key component of antenatal care, aiming to enhance women’s physical and psychosocial readiness for childbirth and the postpartum period and to facilitate a positive birth experience [ 2 ]. Childbirth preparation education can be delivered using various approaches, among which mindfulness-based interventions have gained increasing attention. Mindfulness is defined as the intentional and non-judgmental awareness of present-moment experience [ 3 ]. In a broader conceptualization, it refers to an open, accepting, and compassionate awareness of ongoing experiences [ 4 ]. The MBCP program, developed by Nancy Bardacke based on the MBSR model, provides structured education on labor, breastfeeding, and postpartum adaptation [ 7 , 13 , 14 ]. Although program duration varies across studies, adapted mindfulness-based childbirth education programs have commonly been delivered over eight weekly sessions [ 10 , 11 ]. ### Comparative effectiveness of cognitive behavioral therapy versus group-based optimism training on psychological outcomes in hemodialysis patients: a randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychol | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Rabiei L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 8

Background Patients undergoing hemodialysis (HD) face significant psychological challenges that affect their well-being. Nevertheless, no comparative investigation has yet examined the efficacy of cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) versus group-based optimism training on psychological outcomes in this population. Hence, this study aimed to compare the effectiveness of CBT versus group-based optimism training on psychological outcomes in HD patients. Methods This three-arm, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial was conducted from September 2024 to May 2025 with a sample of 135 HD patients. Participants were selected through simple random sampling and were randomly allocated to two experimental groups and one active control group. Prior to the intervention, all three groups completed the relevant questionnaires. The CBT and group-based optimism training programs were then implemented for the first and second experimental groups, respectively, each consisting of eight 90-minute sessions delivered once per week for eight consecutive weeks. The control group received standard educational interventions. Following the completion of the training sessions, the questionnaires were completed again immediately and again two and four-months later. The data were analyzed using SPSS software version 21. Ethical considerations All procedures performed on human participants complied with the ethical guidelines of the relevant institutional and national research committees, as well as the Helsinki Declaration and its subsequent revisions, or equivalent ethical frameworks. Results Repeated-measures ANOVA revealed significant time × group interactions for anxiety (η² p = 0.068), depression (η² p = 0.085), optimism (η² p = 0.101), and hope (η² p = 0.073), all p < 0.001. Both CBT and optimism training significantly improved all outcomes versus control ( p < 0.001). However, optimism training showed superior long-term effects over CBT for depression (d = 0.45, p = 0.032) and optimism (d = 0.36, p = 0.041), while both interventions were equally effective for anxiety and hope ( p > 0.05). Conclusion The findings indicated that both CBT and group-based optimism training were effective in reducing anxiety and enhancing hope. Nevertheless, a distinction emerged at long-term follow-up, as optimism training yielded more sustained reductions in depression and greater improvements in optimism. This suggests that, although CBT is effective for acute symptom reduction, optimism training may offer additional advantages for sustained psychological well-being. Clinical trial number IRCT20240609062065N4, 2025-12-01, ‘retrospectively registered’. Introduction Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a leading cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide, characterized by a progressive and irreversible decline in renal function. When kidney function drops to approximately 10% of normal capacity, patients reach the end-stage renal disease (ESRD) phase, a life-threatening condition that requires renal replacement therapy [ 1 , 2 ]. According to the updated Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, CKD currently affects approximately 850 million individuals worldwide. Of these, nearly 4.6 million patients receive kidney replacement therapy (KRT), including HD, peritoneal dialysis, or kidney transplantation [ 3 ]. As the primary treatment for ESRD, HD imposes a significant psychological burden on patients. The procedure is inherently stressful and has been consistently associated with a high prevalence of psychosocial disorders [ 4 , 5 ]. Among these, depression and anxiety are the most commonly reported psychiatric comorbidities. Notably, depression is approximately three times more prevalent among HD patients than in the general population, a vulnerability that stems from the interplay of biological, psychological, and socioeconomic factors [ 6 , 7 ]. Beyond the direct stressors of treatment, patients with ESRD face profound disruptions in self-concept, familial roles, and social status. These challenges, compounded by financial dependency, often impede familial adaptation and social engagement, thereby perpetuating a maladaptive psychological cycle [ 8 ]. Given these multifaceted psychological challenges, the development of effective interventions to alleviate depression and anxiety in HD patients is of considerable clinical importance. Two therapeutic approaches have demonstrated particular promise: CBT and optimism training. Based on Beck’s cognitive theory, CBT could targets maladaptive thought patterns through cognitive restructuring and behavioral techniques [ 9 ]. Optimism training, rooted in positive psychology, employs structured exercises designed to cultivate positive future expectancies and has been shown to effectively reduce negative affective states [ 10 , 11 ]. A review of the literature indicates that several studies have been conducted in line with the present investigation. For example, Bahmani et al. demonstrated that cognitive‑existential group therapy significantly enhanced hopefulness and reduced depression in women undergoing HD [ 12 ]. Also, Shokrpour et al. examined the effectiveness of positive thinking training on stress, anxiety, depression, and quality of life in HD patients. At baseline, no significant differences were found between the groups. Following the intervention, stress and anxiety levels decreased considerably, and quality of life improved. However, depression scores did not change significantly [ 2 ]. The inclusion criteria were included: (1) a documented diagnosis of HD, and (2) willingness to participate in the research. Also, the exclusion criteria were included: (1) having a confirmed diagnosis of a psychological disorder by a psychiatrist, and (2) having disability that would prevent participation in the classes. ### Autism, anorexia nervosa, and the prevention of death: evidence supports adapted treatment, not assisted death
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eat Disord | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Adams M, Downs J | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov-Dec

The practice of assisted death (AD) for people with eating disorders (EDs) is expanding, and a concerning revelation is the over-representation of autistic individuals among those with EDs who are offered (and complete) AD. Informed by our lived experience with EDs and being diagnosed with autism in adulthood, we examine the intersection of autism, EDs, and AD. We believe that the lack of autism assessment, autism-affirming treatment, and the over-specialization of ED care, exacerbated by futility narratives, are systemic failures that have contributed to autistic people with EDs being offered, and sometimes complete, AD. We challenge the notion of futility in ED care that justifies AD for autistic people with EDs and advocate for neurodiversity-affirming, trauma-informed adaptations to treatment that address the challenges autistic individuals may face in recovery, such as Multidiagnostic Eating Disorder Dialectical Behavior Therapy. We propose changes to safeguard autistic individuals and ensure they are offered meaningful pathways to treatment rather than being abandoned to premature death.
