### Sequential Sampling from Memory Underlies Perceptual Decisions Unyoked from Actions
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sharma P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Perceptual decision-making refers to the class of decisions in which sensory evidence is used to categorize percepts and guide actions. Conventionally, categorical decisions are thought to precede motor actions. However, recent studies in nonhuman primates challenge this assumption-when perceptual decisions were uncoupled from the actions they bear upon, animals postponed the decisions until relevant response options were revealed. To determine whether this postponement stems from cognitive limitations unique to nonhuman primates, we conducted a similar experiment with human participants. Naive participants viewed a random dot motion (RDM) stimulus that was difficult to categorize. After a delay period following the RDM, two choice targets were presented and participants decided which target lay closer to the perceived motion direction. Decision accuracy varied across participants, reflecting individual differences in ability to integrate motion evidence. Notably, participants with higher decision accuracy showed prolonged deliberation after choice-target presentation. Furthermore, the time they took to report their decisions depended on the strength of the motion evidence. This pattern of accuracy and decision reporting time could be accounted for by a bounded diffusion model in which participants sequentially sample stored sensory information from memory during the target selection phase. When the RDM was challenging to categorize, the subsequent appearance of the targets provided a framework to interrogate stored evidence and render a decision. Our results reveal a strategic feature of working memory of retaining information based on its future utility. This observation opens new avenues for investigating how memory and decision-making interact.

### Trust Modulates Speech Entrainment: Enhanced Cortical Tracking for Low-trust Speakers
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hannah JA, Di Liberto GM | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Trust is a critical component of human communication, providing a foundation for understanding, information exchange, and social coordination. Much of the research on trust in speech communication has focused on how vocal characteristics impact perceived trustworthiness. However, little is known about how trust in a speaker affects the neural processing of speech. Here, we demonstrate a two-stage experimental framework to study that question using noninvasive EEG. First, participants engage in a trust-building stage, where they play an investment game with fictional characters, each paired with a distinctive voice and trustworthiness level (i.e., frequency and magnitude of lies). Next, participants engage in a story-listening stage, in which they are presented with stories from the same characters. Data acquired from 20 young adults confirm a statistically significant correlation between the perceived and actual trustworthiness of the fictional characters. Cortical speech tracking was quantified using a temporal response function analysis on the EEG data. We found that the trustworthiness established during the trust-building stage influenced the cortical tracking of speech in the subsequent story-listening stage, with lower trustworthiness corresponding to a stronger cortical tracking of speech. Interestingly, trustworthiness selectively modulated tracking strength, with no statistically significant changes in the spatial and temporal temporal response function dynamics.

### Conflict between representational formats and procedures constrains visual tracking
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognitive Psychology (Cogn Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wu Q, Flombaum JI | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Computer-assisted design software, such as PowerPoint, groups separate elements to create structured objects whose parts can be manipulated relative to the whole. Might visual perception perform a similar operation, grouping scene elements and representing them with internal reference to the emergent structure? The proposal is central yet perennially controversial in cognitive neuroscience and philosophy of mind. In a series of seven preregistered tracking experiments, we identify internally referenced representations in human vision by relying on the logic that, as in computer-assisted design, a representation will make certain procedures easy to access while limiting others. Participants tracked a target among identical nontargets organized into geometric shapes. When the shapes rotated, performance was high, suggesting the use of internally referenced, shape-centered representations that naturally support rotational transformations. In contrast, performance declined in translation conditions, where constituent items moved independently, revealing a mismatch between the internally structured representation and the requisite, externally oriented tracking procedure. This effect replicated across diverse stimuli, including occluded, rounded, and irregular shapes, and was reduced when structure-inducing cues were disrupted. These findings identify representational format and its compatibility with task procedures as an important determinant of visual performance.

### Visual and interoceptive factors shape affective and aesthetic evaluations of body postures
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Orlandi A, Cross ES | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

The influence of internal body rhythms, such as heartbeats, on affective and cognitive processes remains an area of research, informing how bodily states shape perception of the world. Studies of facial expression evaluation show that emotional (negative vs. neutral) faces are detected more easily and perceived as more intense when presented during systole rather than diastole, two phases of the cardiac cycle. Whether similar cardiac phase effects extend to affective and aesthetic evaluations of human bodies remains unclear. In this preregistered study, participants without dance training rated upright and inverted body postures from motion capture recordings of contemporary dancers for emotional intensity and likability. Each image was presented during either the participant's ventricular systole or diastole. We assessed interoceptive accuracy (via the Heartbeat Counting Task; HBCT) and collected emotional valence and bodily arousal ratings for each stimulus. Contrary to predictions, emotional intensity and likability did not increase during systole. Instead, we found that cardiac-related effects were conditional. Emotional intensity was positively associated with bodily arousal, except for inverted postures during systole. How this relationship depended on orientation was modulated by HBCT performance. Likability was positively associated with valence, with this relationship becoming stronger as HBCT performance increased. These findings suggest cardiac-related factors may contribute to affective and aesthetic evaluations of dance-like body postures, as a function of body orientation, stimulus affective characteristics, and individual differences in heartbeat perception. They indicate that ongoing physiological states may shape how others are evaluated beyond facial information, extending to both affective and aesthetic domains.

### Computational complexity improves recognition memory for reward-relevant attributes
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kapounek A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 4

Computational complexity in optimisation tasks is often seen as impairing optimal choice. We test whether it could have beneficial effects on memory. In a preregistered lab experiment (N = 131) using the NP-hard 0-1 knapsack problem, we manipulated knapsack capacity to create instances differing in two objective complexity metrics: phase transition proximity and Sahni-k. As predicted, solving a more complex instance resulted in a 44% (d = 0.45) increase in above-chance recognition memory relative to a lower-complexity instance. Participants spent longer on the complex instance and clicked on items more often. A plausible interpretation of these findings is that the increased processing requirements of search algorithms under complexity may have resulted in enhanced encoding through frequent revisiting of reward-relevant information. These findings provide a formal framework to generate hypotheses about the memory consequences of complexity and the effects of cognitive offloading in real-world decisions.

### The role of bilingualism and executive functions in the comprehension of which-questions by typically developing and autistic children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Solaimani E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

Bilingualism has been associated with enhanced Executive Functions (EFs) in typically developing (TD) children and autistic children, although findings remain mixed. Furthermore, while studies investigating the effect of bilingualism on complex syntax in autistic children are still scarce, they tend to show that bilingualism does not exacerbate performance. The main aim of this study is to examine whether bilingualism enhances particular EFs which, in turn, has a cascade effect contributing to the improved comprehension of which-questions. We tested 134 verbal autistic and 234 TD children aged 3 to 13. Bilingualism was examined both categorically (monolingual vs. bilingual) and continuously (proficiency, length of exposure, length of use, balance of exposure, and balance of use) using the parental Quantifying Bilingual Experience questionnaire (De Cat et al., 2025). Executive functions were assessed using performance-based tasks measuring working memory, inhibition and shifting, alongside a parent-report measure of attention. Furthermore, all children completed a character selection task to assess comprehension of subject and object which-questions. Results showed specific bilingual experiences (e.g., balance and length of language exposure and use) indirectly supported comprehension of which-questions through their influence on EFs. The mediating EF domains differed for TD and autistic children, highlighting the need to consider cognitive diversity in bilingualism research. These findings suggest that bilingual experience may serve as a cognitive-linguistic resource, especially for autistic children, by enhancing domain-specific EFs that underpin complex language processing.
