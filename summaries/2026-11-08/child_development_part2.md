### Caregiving-related early adversities and parental context effects on adolescents' momentary emotion differentiation
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Abramson L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

Emotion differentiation - the ability to identify specific experienced emotions - correlates with psychological health. Although theorized to develop through caregiving experiences, contextual effects on emotion differentiation in developmental populations have rarely been investigated, particularly among youth who have experienced disruptions in early caregiving. Here, we examined how adolescents' momentary emotion differentiation varies across socio-emotional contexts and whether these patterns differ following exposure to severe caregiving-related early adversities (crEAs). Adolescents aged 10-17 years with or without crEAs ( N  = 81; mean age = 13.49, SD = 1.97; 53% male/47% female; 9%-Asian American, 28%-Black/African American, 34%-White/European American, 28%-Other) completed a 7-day ecological momentary assessment protocol (4-5 surveys/day), reporting emotions and social contexts. At the trait level (spanning all contexts), after controlling for covariates, there were no significant group differences in emotion differentiation. At the momentary level, negative momentary emotion differentiation decreased following negative parent-related contexts compared to other contexts. However, the momentary emotions' standard deviation indicated more differentiated phasic intensification of a few negative emotions. Importantly, exposure to crEAs attenuated these contextual effects,  F (3, 2,317) = 3.06/3.75,  p  = .027/.011. These findings provide initial evidence linking severe crEAs to affective dynamic processes and raise the hypothesis that early caregiving disruptions attenuate momentary parental modulation effects on emotion differentiation.

### Preadolescent psychosocial maladjustment in children who exhibited different developmental patterns of co-occurring externalizing behaviors during the preschool years
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Carbonneau R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

The co-occurrence of externalizing behaviors (EBs) in school-aged children is associated with long-term homotypic (similar) and heterotypic (diverse) psychosocial difficulties. It is unknown whether this developmental process begins with the first manifestations of EBs in the early preschool years. This population-based cohort study examined psychosocial maladjustment at ages 10-12-including externalizing problems, addictive behaviors, internalizing problems, and socialization difficulties-among children who followed distinct patterns of single or co-occurring EBs across the preschool years. EBs included parent-rated hyperactivity-impulsivity, noncompliance, and physical aggression. Six high-trajectory classes, identified with latent growth modeling, were used as longitudinal indicators of EBs and compared to a reference class of children with low or moderate trajectories across all EBs, controlling for internalizing problems and other confounders. Results indicated that all trajectories characterized by high levels of single or co-occurring EBs showed homotypic continuity, and most exhibited heterotypic continuity, at ages 10-12. Preschoolers with a high co-occurrence pattern encompassing all three EBs showed the poorest psychosocial adjustment across all 14 outcomes in the four maladjustment domains. Differences between the other trajectories with high levels of single-EB and co-occurring EBs, relative to the reference class, were more mixed. Results suggest that high and stable levels of a single EB or co-occurring EBs in preschool are associated with significant homotypic and heterotypic continuity into preadolescence. They underscore the importance of early manifestations of EBs and highlight that the multiple co-occurring EBs observed in preadolescents originate from similar patterns observable during the preschool years. ### Top-Down Self-Regulation: Bidirectional Associations Between Vagal Regulation and Cool and Hot Executive Functions in Adolescents
**כתב עת:** 📄 Developmental Science (Dev Sci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Huang Y, Lü W | **תאריך:** 2027 Jan

Vagal regulation and executive functions both reflect top-down self-regulation. However, how vagal regulation and executive functions are interconnected with each other during adolescence remains an open question. This study investigated bidirectional associations between vagal regulation and cool and hot executive functions in adolescents using a two-wave data collection (T1: November 2021, T2: May 2023). At both waves, 163 Chinese adolescents (M age  = 12.92, SD age  = 0.82; 47.24% females) underwent a speech task during which their baseline respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) and RSA suppression were obtained, assessing vagal regulation, and completed a Flanker task and an Emotional Stroop task, assessing cool and hot executive functions, respectively, indexed by intra-individual reaction time variability. Results showed that baseline RSA was not significantly associated with either cool or hot executive functions at T1 or T2. Additionally, RSA suppression to the impromptu speech task was bidirectionally associated with hot executive functions across timepoints, while unidirectionally associated with cool executive functions, with only the predicted direction from RSA suppression to cool executive functions being found. Findings revealed distinct prospective associations between vagal regulation and hot and cool EFs, providing a possibility for further investigation of longitudinal co-development of different aspects of top-down self-regulation during adolescence. SUMMARY: This study is the first to explore the bidirectional associations between vagal regulation and executive functions (EFs) as two aspects of top-down self-regulation. No significant associations between baseline respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) and cool and hot EFs were found. RSA suppression to the impromptu speech task was bidirectionally associated with hot EFs and unidirectionally associated with cool EFs during adolescence. Findings revealed distinct prospective associations between vagal regulation and hot and cool EFs.

### Adverse Childhood Experiences and Disordered Eating Behaviors in Adolescence: Differences by Gender
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Child Adolesc Trauma | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Helton JJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

Eating disorders often develop during adolescence. Disordered eating behaviors (DEBs) such as skipping meals, binging, and purging are less severe but still lead to significant health problems. Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are linked to various maladaptive behaviors, including DEBs, but gender-specific relationships are less understood. The purpose of this study is to explore the link between various types and measurements of ACEs and DEBs among adolescents, examining gender-specific differences. Data from the 2022 National Survey of Children’s Health (NSCH), involving a nationally representative sample of 21,573 adolescents aged 11 to 17, was used. DEBs were assessed based on parent reports of binge eating, purging, and use of diet pills. Fourteen ACEs were measured in a variety of ways, including categorized into economic hardships, violence exposure, family hardships, and discrimination. Logistic regressions modeled DEBs by ACE measures, controlling for covariates. Bivariate and multivariate analyses were stratified by gender. Each additional ACE increased odds of DEBs by 33% (OR = 1.33, p < .01), with no significant gender differences in cumulative effects. However, specific ACEs had gender-differentiated impacts: food insecurity increased odds among females (OR = 2.55, p < .01), while violence increased odds for males (OR = 3.14, p < .01). Body image concerns and obesity were more strongly associated with DEBs in females, whereas higher family income was significant for males. Findings highlight the need for gender-sensitive prevention and intervention strategies, addressing household stressors and promoting healthy family dynamics for females, and focusing on coping mechanisms and emotional expression for males.

Atypical eating patterns commonly develop during adolescence (Volpe et al., 2016 ). Eating disorders such as anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, and binge eating share key characteristics: severe disruptions in eating behaviors, distressing thoughts and emotions, and significant physical, psychological, and social impairments (Vanderlinden & Palmisano, 2018 ). Disordered eating behaviors (DEB), though less severe, can include meal skipping, binging, purging, restrictive dieting, and the use of diet pills to control weight (Dunn et al., 2022 ). Globally, around 1 in 5 children and adolescents engage in DEBs (Hart et al., 2020 ; Lopez-Gile et al., 2023 ). While not everyone with DEBs will develop an eating disorder, DEBs share risk factors and outcomes with more severe disorders, including long-term consequences like health problems, self-harm, poor mental health, and impaired functioning (Kärkkäinen et al., 2018 ; Micali et al., 2016 ; Warne et al., 2021 ). Both males and females engage in DEBs, but prevalence, symptoms, and impacts vary by gender (Chen et al., 2022 ; Massaldjieva et al., 2017 ). Females are more likely to restrict diets, binge eat, and purge (López-Gile et al., 2023 ), while DEBs in males have been increasing, especially related to body dissatisfaction, muscularity concerns, and pursuit of a lean physique (Gorrell & Murray, 2019 ; Mitchison & Mond, 2015 ). Outcomes also differ in that females may experience low body weight, amenorrhea, and osteoporosis (Barrack et al., 2021 ), while males are at risk of muscle dysmorphia, steroid use, and excessive exercise (Badenes-Ribera et al., 2019 ; Nagata et al., 2019 ). Gender differences in DEB etiology and consequences highlight the need for further research into how these behaviors manifest in adolescents across genders. Various factors contribute to DEB onset and maintenance, including psychological, interpersonal, sociocultural, and biological factors. Psychological traits such as low self-esteem, negative affect, perfectionism, neuroticism, poor body image, and difficulty regulating emotions are linked to DEBs (Russon et al., 2019 ). These vulnerabilities often interact with environmental stressors, leading to maladaptive coping mechanisms, such as using food to manage distress. Additionally, interpersonal relationships, especially family and peer dynamics, shape attitudes toward food and body image, influencing DEB development (Haynos et al., 2016 ; Keel & Forney, 2013 ). DEBs also share risk factors with obesity, including sociocultural pressures and interpersonal dynamics (Barakat et al., 2023 ; Jabeile et al., 2021 ). Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) have emerged as significant risk factors for various adverse outcomes, including mental health disorders and DEBs. First studied by Felitti et al. ( 1998 ), ACEs encompass abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction, including parental incarceration, domestic violence, household mental illness or suicide, and substance abuse. Additional adversities such as neighborhood violence, bullying, and parental death are also commonly included in ACE measures. ACEs are often studied using a cumulative risk framework, with research showing a dose-response relationship: the more ACEs, the higher the likelihood of adverse outcomes (Grummitt et al., 2022 ; Liming et al., 2018 ). Some studies suggest not all ACEs are equal, with certain ACEs forming natural clusters (Lee et al., 2020 ), while others highlight gender differences in the association between ACEs and adverse outcomes (Grigsby et al., 2020 ; Jones et al., 2022 ; Machado et al., 2024 ). ACEs are consistently linked to the development of eating disorders. Studies have shown that individuals who experience childhood adversities such as household violence, household mental illness, or neglect are more likely to develop eating disorders (Chu et al., 2022 ; Kovács-Tóth et al., 2022 ; Pop et al., 2020 ). The cumulative impact of ACEs also increases the likelihood of an eating disorder diagnosis (Chu et al., 2022 ; Guillaume et al., 2016 ; Kovács-Tóth et al., 2022 ). Specific ACEs, such as household mental illness, household violence, and criminal household members, have been found to have particularly strong associations with binge-eating disorder in younger age groups (Chu et al., 2022 ). Thus, childhood adversities increase the risk of developing eating disorders, and the type and number of adversities are important considerations in understanding these risks. Fewer studies have investigated the connection between ACEs and the broader spectrum of DEBs, but some research has begun to explore these relationships. Weight-based teasing, for instance, has been linked to dietary restraint and bulimic behaviors (Menzel et al., 2010 ), while individual studies have identified links between physical, sexual, and emotional abuse, bullying, discrimination, and various DEBs (Dunn et al., 2022 ; Fuemmeler et al., 2009 ; Isohookanaa et al., 2016 ; Nelson et al., 2023 ). However, Morrison ( 2021 ) emphasizes the need for more research to understand how different types and numbers of ACEs relate to DEBs. While much research has focused on the association between ACEs and eating disorders, especially in adult populations, the relationship between specific ACEs and DEBs in adolescents remains underexplored. Given that DEBs can severely impact physical and psychological well-being and increase the risk of developing clinically diagnosed eating disorders, this study aims to fill a critical gap in the literature. We will examine the relationship between multiple types of ACEs and DEBs, focusing on both individual ACE items and cumulative measures of ACEs by domain clusters. Additionally, given gender differences in DEB prevalence and emotional outcomes, the study will stratify results by gender to assess whether certain ACE items or clusters disproportionately affect adolescent females compared to males. ### Adverse Childhood Experiences and Associated Health-Risk Behaviors Among University Students in Turkey: A Cross-Sectional Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Child Adolesc Trauma | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Çam HH, Ustuner Top F | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are potentially traumatic events occurring before the age of 18 that may have long-term consequences for health and well-being. This study aimed to examine the association between ACEs and health-risk behaviors (HRBs) among university students in Turkey. A cross-sectional web-based survey was conducted between April and June 2024 with 1,115 university students aged 18–27 years. Data were collected using a structured self-report questionnaire assessing exposure to ACEs, including child maltreatment and household dysfunction, as well as a range of HRBs in young adulthood. Overall, 97.7% of participants reported experiencing at least one ACE, while 47.8% reported exposure to four or more ACEs. The most frequently reported ACEs were community violence (81.1%), emotional neglect (74.0%), emotional abuse (49.9%), witnessing violence against household members (39.2%), bullying (33.2%), and physical abuse (27.9%). A higher number of ACEs was significantly associated with several HRBs, including tobacco use, alcohol consumption, sedentary behavior, overweight and obesity, suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, interpersonal violence, and early sexual initiation (before age 16). These findings indicate a strong association between cumulative exposure to childhood adversity and multiple health-risk behaviors in young adulthood. The results highlight the need for trauma-informed public health strategies aimed at preventing ACEs and promoting resilience to reduce the long-term health consequences of childhood adversity.
