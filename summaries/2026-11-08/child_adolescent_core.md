### Exposure-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy Increases Connectivity Between the Central Executive and Salience Networks in Pediatric Anxiety
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry (J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 10.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Díaz DE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

OBJECTIVE: Exposure is considered the most active element of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) for pediatric anxiety. Yet many youth do not respond to CBT, and exposure is the component patients most often find difficult to tolerate. Cognitive control and its supporting neural substrates may enhance exposure efficacy by helping youth remain engaged with feared stimuli despite urges to avoid. However, little work has identified how exposure-based CBT modulates intrinsic connectivity of tripartite networks that support cognitive control (e.g., central executive [CEN], salience [SN], and default mode networks [DMN]), limiting efforts to develop strategies to improve and personalize treatment. Progress may be limited by individual variability in network connectivity in anxiety, which may obscure treatment-related effects in group-averaged analyses. This randomized clinical trial (RCT) leverages person-specific network modeling to test how exposure versus relaxation components of CBT influence resting-state connectivity of cognitive control networks in pediatric anxiety.

METHOD: Youth aged 7-18 years with an anxiety disorder (N = 100) or low/no anxiety (L/NA; N = 35) completed resting-state fMRI scans at baseline. Patients were randomized to exposure-focused CBT (EF-CBT) or relaxation mentorship training (RMT). Resting-state connectivity was reassessed following treatment (or after a commensurate interval in L/NA youth) in 112 participants. Changes in within-CEN, CEN-SN, and CEN-DMN density were examined using Group Iterative Multiple Model Estimation, which yields sparse, person-specific networks capturing both shared and individual connectivity structure.

RESULTS: At baseline, anxious youth exhibited lower density within-CEN and between CEN-DMN than L/NA youth. Treatment effects differed by intervention: EF-CBT selectively increased CEN-SN density, whereas RMT increased within-CEN density.

CONCLUSION: These findings demonstrate dissociable effects of exposure and relaxation on cognitive control network organization in pediatric anxiety. These results indicate that exposure-focused CBT is uniquely associated with increased connectivity between control and salience systems, consistent with a mechanism supporting top-down control of threat-related signals during exposure. Person-specific measures of tripartite network connectivity may inform future efforts to identify candidate neural targets for optimizing and personalizing treatment.

### Menarche and ADHD: associations between puberty, ADHD traits and mental health in female adolescents
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Friedel E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 2

BACKGROUND: Adolescents with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) may be more vulnerable to cooccurring mental health challenges during puberty, and some evidence suggests that puberty impacts ADHD traits. However, pubertal influences on psychological outcomes in female adolescents with ADHD are poorly understood. We investigated how menarche relates to ADHD traits and its potential moderation of associations between ADHD status or traits and mental health.

METHODS: Analyses used Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development SM  Study data from baseline to wave 3 (N = 5,318; 100% female; baseline age M = 9.9, SD = 0.6; 52.3% non-Hispanic White). ADHD traits, internalising difficulties and externalising difficulties were measured using the Child Behavior Checklist. Bayesian generalised linear mixed models examined cross-sectional and longitudinal associations between menarche status and ADHD traits. Interactions between menarche status and ADHD status or traits as predictors of mental health outcomes were also investigated. In comparative analyses, breast development replaced menarche status.

RESULTS: ADHD traits did not differ between pre- and post-menarche adolescents in cross-sectional analyses, but post-menarche adolescents had higher ADHD traits the following year. Complete breast development (vs. not started) also predicted higher ADHD traits the next year. Interactions between menarche and ADHD status were not associated with mental health outcomes cross-sectionally or longitudinally. Positive associations between ADHD traits and mental health difficulties were marginally stronger in post-menarche adolescents. The association between higher wave 2 ADHD traits and greater wave 3 externalising difficulties was substantially stronger with more advanced breast development at wave 2.

CONCLUSIONS: Longitudinal findings provide preliminary evidence that more advanced gonadarche in early adolescence is prospectively associated with elevated ADHD trait expression in females when controlling for individual differences in ADHD trait levels. More advanced breast development may reflect physiological and psychosocial changes that could contribute to a stronger relationship between higher ADHD traits and greater subsequent externalising difficulties.

### Hormonal effects on ADHD symptoms in adolescents across the menstrual cycle
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Eng AG et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

Female-specific risk factors for ADHD in adolescence have been vastly understudied despite increased impairment and comorbidity during this period. In adults, the premenstrual phase and rapid decreases in estradiol levels are associated with increased risk for ADHD symptoms. The current project aims to evaluate the effects of day-to-day changes in estradiol and progesterone on ADHD symptoms across the menstrual cycle in adolescents. Participants were 13 adolescents aged 12 to 17 years over-recruited for ADHD. Participants collected saliva samples and completed measures of ADHD symptoms daily. Generalized additive mixed models were used to explore symptom trajectories across the menstrual cycle and examine the relationships between hormones and ADHD symptoms. Inattention and hyperactivity severity exhibited significant menstrual cycle-related variation, but average symptom trajectories across the cycle were largely flat. Significant person-specific cycle effects and individual differences in estradiol-symptom associations suggest substantial heterogeneity in menstrual cycle influence on ADHD symptoms. These findings suggest that menstrual cycle-related changes in ADHD symptoms are not characterized by a uniform pattern across adolescents and instead may reflect individual differences in hormone sensitivity. Future research should prioritize identifying distinct hormone-symptom response profiles and determine their clinical relevance for personalized ADHD management.

### Suicide risk in first- and second-generation young migrants: a 12-month cohort study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Peñuelas-Calvo I et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

PURPOSE: Evidence on the impact of migration on suicidal behavior among adolescents remains limited. Particularly, there is scarce knowledge concerning clinical populations and second generations of migrants. This cohort study examined differences in clinical, psychosocial and behavioral profiles associated with suicidal behavior among non-migrant, first-generation and second-generation migrant adolescents presenting to a Child and Adolescent Psychiatry Emergency Department (ED) in Madrid.

METHODS: Adolescents aged 8-17 years (N = 1,281) presenting with suicidal ideation or behavior were identified at an index ED visit, and their electronic medical records were retrospectively reviewed over a 12-month follow-up period. Sociodemographic, clinical and psychosocial risk factors-including socioeconomic adversity, family psychiatric history, maltreatment, family conflict, victim of bullying and eating problems-were systematically recorded. Reasons for consultation, methods and severity of suicidal behavior were compared across migration groups. Cox proportional hazards regression models, including age- and sex-adjusted and two multivariable models, examined whether migration status independently predicted time to repeat suicidal behavior during follow-up.

RESULTS: Migrant adolescents-both first- and second-generation-presented with a higher burden of psychosocial adversity and more severe self-harm methods at index ED visit compared to non-migrants. Migrant adolescents, particularly first-generation, were less likely to have had prior outpatient mental health follow-up. In fully adjusted multivariable models accounting for sociodemographic, psychosocial and clinical covariates, including previous suicidal behavior, second-generation migrant background remained an independent predictor of repeat suicidal behavior, whereas first-generation background did not show a statistically significant effect.

CONCLUSIONS: Second‑generation migrant background was associated with a more severe clinical presentation and repeat suicidal behavior, even after adjusting for psychosocial and clinical covariates. These findings suggest that generational migration status is a relevant factor to consider when assessing and following up on suicide risk for adolescents of migrant origin, particularly those from a second-generation background.

### Longitudinal trajectories of cognitive reserve in youth offspring of patients with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder and the role of obstetric complications
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Camprodon-Boadas P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 5

Cognitive reserve (CR) is a construct reflecting the brain's capacity to flexibly use neural resources to cope with pathology. This study aimed to examine the longitudinal development of CR in offspring of patients with schizophrenia (SzO) or bipolar disorder (BpO) compared to community controls (CcO), and whether a history of obstetric complications (OCs) plays a role in CR trajectories during youth. The study involved 515 datapoints (99 SzO, 205 BpO, 211 CcO) from 50 SzO, 87 BpO, and 91 CcO with assessments at different time points over a 12-year period. CR was assessed using a composite score including intelligence quotient, sociability, academic performance, and parental socioeconomic status. History of OCs was assessed and grouped according to complications during pregnancy, and complications in delivery. Although CR increased over time in all groups, levels of CR remained consistently lowest in SzO, followed by BpO A significant age-by-complications during pregnancy interaction was found in the whole sample, and in SzO and BpO, indicating reduced CR accumulation over time in those with a history of OCs. Having three or more OCs was associated with lower age-related gains in CR in the entire sample. SzO and BpO showed lower CR levels from an early age, which persisted over time, and a history of complications during pregnancy was associated with less CR accumulation. These findings highlight the impact of early-life factors on CR during youth, suggesting that early interventions in high-risk groups could help mitigate long-term complications.

### Supportive parenting moderates the association between neural synchrony and irritability: Evidence from mother-son dyads
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ma Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 3

While research on parent-child neural synchrony using fNIRS hyperscanning has expanded, its association with child psychopathology, especially transdiagnostic symptoms such as irritability, remains poorly understood. Moreover, no prior studies have explored the potential contextual factors that may moderate the link between parent-child neural synchrony and children's irritability. The present study therefore investigated whether and how neural synchrony would interact with maternal supportive parenting to shape irritability. Forty mother-son dyads participated, consisting of school-aged boys with varying levels of irritability (M age  = 7.53 years, SD = 1.25 years) and their mothers (M age  = 39.35 years, SD = 4.53 years). Neural activity was recorded via fNIRS during a frustration-eliciting task. Results indicated that neural synchrony was identified in the prefrontal cortex and the temporoparietal junction. Furthermore, moderation analysis revealed that in dyads with low maternal supportive parenting, higher neural synchrony was associated with higher child irritability, whereas in dyads with high maternal supportive parenting, neural synchrony was not significantly associated with child irritability. This study advances our understanding of irritability by dissecting neuro-parenting interactive mechanisms.

### Resting state functional connectivity associated with maternal anxiety: preliminary evidence that infant rsFC moderates the association between behavioral inhibition and anxiety/depression
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Massera A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

Postnatal maternal anxiety shapes caregiver and infant behavior. Yet, the relationship with infant brain function remains unclear. We examine the relation of maternal anxiety with infant resting state functional connectivity (rsFC). To evaluate the clinical significance of the rsFC networks identified, exploratory analyses tested whether the rsFC associated with maternal anxiety moderates risk trajectories from infant behavioral inhibition (BI) to childhood anxiety/depression symptoms. Four-month-old infants (N = 60) underwent resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), and mothers completed the Beck Anxiety Inventory (BAI). Analyses included 49 infants with high-quality imaging and maternal anxiety data. In the longitudinal subsample (n = 21), BI was observed at 14 months, and anxiety/depression symptoms were assessed at 4-6 years using the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL). Using whole-brain network enrichment analyses, we identified cortical network pairs that were significantly associated with maternal anxiety. Connectivity estimates from significant network pairs were averaged and tested as moderators of infant BI and childhood anxiety/depression association. Infants exposed to higher maternal anxiety showed an altered limbic-dorsal attention network (DAN) and default mode (DMN)-DAN. In both network pairs, negative and positive node-to-node pairs were identified, indicating that some regional connections exhibited stronger coupling, while others exhibited weaker coupling. Results demonstrated that DMN-DAN rsFC moderated developmental pathways: greater BI was associated with higher early-childhood anxiety/depression among infants with stronger DMN-DAN connectivity. Maternal anxiety is related to infant brain connectivity, and these neural differences moderate BI-related risk for anxiety/depression. DMN-DAN coupling may serve as an early marker of infants at risk for anxiety/depression. Given the small longitudinal sample, the moderation finding should be considered exploratory and requires replication in a larger, independent cohort.
