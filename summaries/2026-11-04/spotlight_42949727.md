### Global Burden of Anorexia Nervosa and Bulimia Nervosa, 1990 to 2023, With Forecasts to 2050: A Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 Analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bai R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

This cross-sectional study estimates the global, regional, and national prevalence and disability burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—and mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa—from 1990 to 2023, and to forecast prevalence to 2050.

Key Points Question What is the global burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa? Findings In this Global Burden of Disease 2023 analysis, an estimated 18.1 million people worldwide were living with anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa in 2023, accounting for 3.83 million disability-adjusted life-years. Burden was highest in high-income regions, the largest increases in age-standardized prevalence occurred in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia, and prevalent cases increased by 71% since 1990 and are forecast to rise through 2050. Meaning The burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa is increasing globally, highlighting the need for strengthened surveillance, earlier detection, and better integration of care into mental health systems.

Importance Eating disorders are a major cause of nonfatal mental health burden, yet global evidence on their long-term trends, geographic distribution, and future trajectories remains limited. Objective To estimate the global, regional, and national prevalence and disability burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—and mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa—from 1990 to 2023, and to forecast prevalence to 2050. Design, Setting, and Participants Using Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 methods, data were synthesized from systematic literature reviews, population surveys, and vital registration systems. Estimates were produced for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, with forecasts through 2050. These data were analyzed from October 2025 to August 2026. Exposure Anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa diagnosis. Main Outcomes and Measures Counts and age-standardized rates with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) for anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa prevalence, years lived with disability (YLDs), and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), and anorexia nervosa mortality, reported by age, sex, and location. Results In 2023, an estimated 18.1 million people (95% UI, 13.9-24.0) globally were living with anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa, of whom 12.6 million (69.4%) were female, 5.5 million were male (30.6%), and 41.2% were aged 10 to 24 years. These cases accounted for 3.83 million DALYs (95% UI, 2.23-5.69). Since 1990, the global number of prevalent cases increased by 71.0%, while age-standardized prevalence and DALY rates rose by about 21%. Females experienced substantially higher prevalence and DALY rates than males. The highest age-standardized prevalence rates were observed in high-income regions, particularly Australasia and Western Europe, whereas the largest relative increases in age-standardized prevalence occurred in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. Forecasts indicate continued increases in prevalent cases through 2050. Conclusions and Relevance This GBD 2023 analysis found that anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa contribute substantially to global nonfatal health loss. These findings support the integration of eating disorders into mental health surveillance, prevention, and service planning worldwide.

Introduction Anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa are serious eating disorders characterized by substantial disturbances in eating or eating-related behavior. Anorexia nervosa is commonly characterized by restricted intake, low body weight, and fear of weight gain, whereas bulimia nervosa involves recurrent binge eating followed by compensatory behaviors and overconcern with body weight or shape. These disorders arise from both genetic and environmental risk factors, disproportionately affect adolescents and young adults, and often follow a chronic or relapsing course, with substantial medical, psychological, and social consequences. In the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2010, the health burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa was first quantified at the global, regional, and national levels, with the most recent major update conducted in GBD 2017 following the incorporation of additional data sources. Since then, no substantial new data have been added to the estimation framework, apart from limited gray literature included in GBD 2019. Updated estimates are needed to inform evidence-based health care strategies, particularly in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, which may have affected service demand and symptom burden in some settings. While other eating disorders, such as binge eating disorder and other specified feeding or eating disorder, account for a substantial proportion of eating disorders, the absence of survey-derived disability weights for these disorders remains a barrier to quantifying their nonfatal health burden within the GBD framework. Accordingly, this analysis focuses specifically on anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa and does not represent the total burden of eating disorders. We extend previous estimates of the disease burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa through 2023 using updated data sources and refined methodological approaches, and we forecast future prevalence through 2050. This article was produced and conducted as part of the GBD Collaborator Network and in accordance with GBD Protocol. Methods Study Overview We estimated incidence, prevalence, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) for anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, as well as mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa, by age and sex for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023. The GBD 2023 iteration incorporated newly identified data and methodological refinements, including changes to improve model fit in selected locations, the application of refined crosswalks for bulimia nervosa to harmonize diagnostic criteria, and adjustment for recall bias. This study follows the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting ( GATHER ) statement (eMethods 1 in Supplement 1 ). Deidentified data were used and the study was approved by the University of Washington Institutional Review Board (9060), which waived the requirement for informed consent. Case Definitions and Input Data Anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa were modeled separately using DSM or International Classification of Diseases ( ICD ) case definitions. Other eating disorders were not included in the estimation because there were no available disability weights for those conditions that had been derived by disability-weight surveys. Detailed case definitions are provided in eMethods 2 in Supplement 1 . Updated systematic literature reviews were conducted to identify studies reporting the prevalence, incidence, and remission of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, as well as excess mortality associated with anorexia nervosa. Peer-reviewed literature was identified through electronic database searches of PsycINFO, Embase, and PubMed. In addition, gray literature searches and expert consultation were undertaken. Detailed search strategies for the electronic databases are provided in eMethods 2 in Supplement 1 . In total, 59 new citations were incorporated compared with GBD 2017. Vital registration data used to estimate mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa were from the GBD Cause of Death database (eMethods 3 in Supplement 1 ). Data Processing and Modeling Epidemiological data were harmonized before modeling, including age-sex splitting and meta-regression–bayesian, regularized, trimmed (MR-BRT) adjustments where required. For bulimia nervosa, crosswalks were applied to account for differences in recall periods and diagnostic criteria. DisMod-MR version 2.1 was used to estimate incidence, prevalence, and remission by age, sex, location, and year.
