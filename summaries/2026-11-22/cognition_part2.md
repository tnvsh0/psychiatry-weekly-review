### Distinct Amygdala-Frontopolar and Amygdala-Medial Temporal Pathways Support Temporal Memory for Emotional Events
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

Growing evidence suggests that emotion shapes temporal aspects of memory-such as remembering when an event occurred-yet the neural bases of these effects remain unclear. Prior work indicates that temporal context representations in memory are supported by the functions of the medial temporal lobe (MTL) and prefrontal regions, including hippocampus, perirhinal cortex, and lateral frontopolar cortex (FPl). These regions have privileged access to amygdala-originated affective signals that may mediate emotional influences on temporal memory. To test whether these candidate pathways shape temporal memory for emotional events, participants encoded trial-unique negative and positive images in the scanner, followed by a surprise temporal memory task. We found that more negative stimuli were associated with greater amygdala activation and lower temporal memory error for when emotional images occurred. Replicating and extending prior work, higher stimulus-evoked multivoxel pattern similarity in the hippocampus and FPl was associated with higher temporal memory error. Notably, greater amygdala responding to emotional images predicted higher between-trial pattern similarity in the hippocampus but lower within- and between-trial similarity in FPl. Amygdala engagement was also correlated with greater perirhinal activation, which in turn predicted stronger item memory and a recency bias in temporal memory estimates. Collectively, these findings reveal distinct-and opposing-modulatory effects of amygdala-FPl and amygdala-MTL pathways on temporal memory, with the potential to sharpen and blur memory for when emotional events occurred.

### You process it, yet miss the change: a magic-based demonstration of a novel form of change blindness
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Thomas C, Didierjean A | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Previous research on change blindness has demonstrated that perceptual changes can sometimes go undetected despite preserved access to pre-change information. In the present study, we investigated whether change blindness can occur even when pre-change information is consciously and actively processed. Participants (N = 40) observed opaque cups filled with water (pre-change information) while mentally manipulating the liquid as part of an insight problem-solving task. After a memory digit-span task, the cups were turned upside down without any liquid spilling (post-change information). This magic trick relied on superabsorbent powder secretly placed in the cups beforehand. Strikingly, most participants did not spontaneously report surprise, and subsequent reports (e.g., explicitly acknowledging that they had failed to notice the water's disappearance) provided more direct evidence of change blindness for a substantial subset. This occurred despite participants having actively processed the initial state of the cups and encoded both the pre- and post-change information. These findings highlight fundamental limits in adults' ability to monitor the coherence of their environment. We discuss the mechanisms underlying this novel form of change blindness, notably in relation to the phenomenon of event boundaries.

### Development of the mental timeline and mental number line in preschool children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sridhar S, Starr A | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

Humans routinely use space to represent abstract concepts such as time and number, yet little is known about whether these spatial representations emerge through shared or independent developmental processes. The present study investigated the development of the mental timeline and mental number line in English-speaking preschoolers (N = 71; M age  = 4.37, 38 female). Children completed two card arrangement tasks in which they spatially organized sequences of temporal events and numerical magnitudes, along with an assessment of early literacy skills. Across both domains, children predominantly created linear spatial arrangements, demonstrating an emerging tendency to organize abstract concepts sequentially in space. Children produced left-to-right arrangements more frequently for temporal sequences than for numerical magnitudes, suggesting that the mental timeline may become systematically organized earlier than the mental number line. However, individual differences in directional preferences were related across domains: children who more consistently represented temporal order from left to right were also more likely to represent numerical magnitudes from left to right. In addition, older children showed a greater tendency to produce left-to-right arrangements, and though early literacy scores also predicted this tendency, the relation was no longer significant after accounting for age. These findings suggest that the mental timeline and mental number line become increasingly aligned with culturally dominant spatial conventions during the preschool years. Our results support the view that experience with culturally determined directional information contributes to the development of spatial representations of abstract concepts.

### Does the mirror have magic? The moderating role of self-awareness intensity on sharing behavior in 3- to 6-year-old children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liang T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 14

This study examined whether theintensityof self-awareness induction moderates sharing behavior in 3- to 6-year-old children. Two experiments employed distinct sharing paradigms. Experiment 1 used a dictator-game sticker-sharing task. Experiment 2 used a balloon-sharing task. Both adopted a between-subjects design that crossed three self-awareness conditions with two age groups. The three conditions were: anenhanced mirror-selfcondition (mirror plus self-relevant cues), amirror-selfcondition (mirror only), and a control condition (no mirror). The two age groups were 3-4 years and 5-6 years. The results showed that, in both tasks, children in theenhanced mirror-selfcondition shared significantly more than those in the control condition. In the balloon task, they also shared significantly more than those in themirror-selfcondition, whereas themirror-selfand control conditions did not differ. Meanwhile, children aged 5-6 years shared significantly more than those aged 3-4 years across both tasks. Among the 3- to 6-year-old preschoolers studied here, these findings indicate that the reliability of the mirror-self effect may depend on thestrengthof self-awareness induction. Higher-intensity self-awareness induction appears to more reliably promote prosocial behavior in preschoolers. The results support the continuity hypothesis of objective self-awareness theory, help resolve prior inconsistencies regarding the stability of mirror effects, and offer empirically grounded implications for fostering prosocial development.
