### A state-dependent glymphatic dimension in major depressive disorder: implications for treatment resistance
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Barlattani T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 19

The glymphatic system refers to cerebrospinal fluid-interstitial fluid exchange and proposed drainage pathways shaped by sleep, arousal, vascular dynamics, and astrocytic organization. This physiology offers a way to connect disturbances that often coexist in major depressive disorder (MDD): disrupted sleep, fatigue, cognitive slowing, and stress-related abnormalities. We propose that difficulty establishing or maintaining a coordinated sleep-astrocyte-vascular state may contribute to illness persistence in some patients. Treatment-resistant depression (TRD) provides a setting in which this possibility can be examined across the course of treatment. Preclinical studies link stress and depressive-like behavior to astrocytic injury, aquaporin-4 dysregulation, and altered fluid transport. Human imaging findings vary across depressive phenotypes. In a dedicated TRD study, a higher diffusion tensor imaging along the perivascular space (DTI-ALPS) ratio was partly driven by reduced denominator diffusivity, illustrating the importance of interpreting this indirect index at component level. Whole-brain spatiotemporal models provide a complementary account of the neural activity accompanying these physiological states. Ketamine/esketamine, transcranial magnetic stimulation, and chronobiological interventions offer different ways to investigate treatment-related change; serotonergic psychedelics provide an exploratory extension. Longitudinal studies can establish how changes in sleep, fluid-sensitive imaging, and neural dynamics relate temporally to clinical recovery. This Perspective therefore develops a potentially modifiable physiological dimension of persistent depressive illness and defines how it can be tested.

### Assortative mating can mask non-additive genetic contributions to individual differences
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bignardi G et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 19

Assortative mating impacts trait variation and covariation among individuals within populations. Here, we examine how direct and indirect assortative mating influence additive and non-additive genetic contributions to individual differences. In an extended twin design, we show by simulations that correlations between mating partners can mask non-additive genetic influences. Accounting for assortative mating can unmask non-additive genetic contributions, revealing upward biases in additive genetic estimates. We applied our approach to anthropometric (height), personality (openness to experience), and psychiatric (borderline personality disorder features) traits in a large dataset of 9000 Dutch families of twins, siblings, and spouses of twins. Our results demonstrate that accounting for partner correlations (r = 0.21-0.29) unmasks non-additive genetic influences (13-19% of trait variation), leading to additive genetic estimates that are 14-39% smaller than those obtained from models ignoring assortment. Our findings clarify the impacts of assortative mating on both additive and non-additive genetic variance components, contributing to a better understanding of the heritability gap between twin and molecular-based studies, and highlight a refined framework for modelling the genetic transmission of behavioural and psychiatric traits.

### Remodeling stress-responsive networks following early life adversity: Insights from preclinical models
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** O'Neill OS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

Early life adversity (ELA) is a leading risk factor for the development of mental illness worldwide. While the association between experiencing developmental stress and psychopathology is robust, the underlying brain mechanisms remain unclear. Evidence from clinical and preclinical studies suggests that ELA may hijack typical developmental plasticity mechanisms, leading to maladaptive functional network changes. Understanding the nature and magnitude of these changes is complicated by several key dimensions, including the timing, type, and severity of ELA, as well as biological factors such as sex and genetic makeup. Preclinical models using primates and rodents have allowed for fine control over these variables, providing substantial evidence of pathways and related biomarkers impacted by ELA. The present review aims to bridge findings from human clinical data with preclinical research, highlighting well-supported ELA-induced changes in the functional connectivity of stress-sensitive pathways, as well as alterations in additional cortical and subcortical pathways which are susceptible to disturbances during development. We also present key neuromodulatory mechanisms contributing to connectivity changes observed in preclinical models, and sex-specific nuances. Addressing these critical gaps could enhance the potential of connectivity-based therapeutic approaches. Lastly, we consider the translatability of functional connectivity analyses using various preclinical techniques for studying the influence of ELA on the developing brain.

### Working memory capacity moderates stress-related drinking in alcohol use disorder: An intensive one-year ecological assessment study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zech H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

BACKGROUND: Stress is a trigger for alcohol consumption, with working memory capacity (WMC) proposed as a protective factor. While several laboratory-based and cross-sectional studies point toward an interaction between WMC, stress, and alcohol consumption, ecological momentary assessments (EMAs) are needed to establish this relationship in real-life contexts, where alcohol consumption and stress experiences actually take place.

METHODS: In this one-year longitudinal EMA study, we collected data from a gamified, smartphone-based WMC task (every 4 weeks) and EMAs of stress and alcohol consumption (every two days) from n=603 participants diagnosed with alcohol use disorder (AUD). We then integrated this data with structural brain imaging data to explore the neural correlates of the hypothesized moderating effect of WMC.

RESULTS: As preregistered, we found that WMC moderated the within-subject association between stress and alcohol consumption. Participants with low WMC, but not participants with high WMC, consumed more alcohol in more stressful compared to less stressful months. In further analysis, within-subject fluctuations in WMC also moderated the within-subject association between stress and drinking. Although effects were in the expected direction, the moderating effect of WMC was not mediated by neural correlates of interest (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and insula).

CONCLUSIONS: We provide first real-life evidence that greater WMC might increase resilience to stress-related drinking in individuals with AUD. This advances our neurocognitive understanding of individual susceptibility to stress-related drinking, which could inform the development of future, personalized interventions.

### Mapping the shared genetic architecture between anorexia nervosa and metabolomic biomarkers
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Makowski C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

BACKGROUND: Anorexia Nervosa (AN) has the highest mortality rate of any psychiatric disorder yet lacks effective treatments. While recent genomic efforts have reframed AN as a metabo-psychiatric condition that overlaps with cardiometabolic traits, AN's relationship to circulating metabolomic markers remains unknown.

METHODS: We examined shared genetic architecture between AN and 249 metabolomic biomarkers, and compared profiles with related mental health, anthropometric and cardiometabolic traits. Genetic and biological overlap was assessed with global genetic correlations, conjunctional false discovery rate, bivariate Gaussian mixture modeling, and gene set enrichment analysis across body tissues. We also explored putative causal relationships between AN, body mass index (BMI), and metabolomic biomarkers.

RESULTS: Significant genetic correlations emerged between AN and 142 metabolomic biomarkers, which were opposite in direction to cardiometabolic traits such as BMI and type 2 diabetes (r s <-0.94), and stronger than correlations with anxiety. Shared variants between AN and metabolomic biomarkers, particularly lipid-based metabolites, exhibited a mixture of positive and negative effects. These were mapped to genes involved in lipid-related cell signals, developmental growth, and inflammation, and were widely expressed in the brain, most internal organs and female reproductive organs. Bidirectional associations between AN and metabolomic biomarkers were found to act through their respective influence on BMI.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings reveal a complex, bidirectional mixture of positive and negative genetic relationships between AN and systemic metabolism, which would be obscured by examination of broader cardiometabolic traits. Uncovering specific shared lipid and developmental pathways provides a genetically-anchored mechanistic understanding of the illness, while highlighting promising metabolomic targets for weight restoration approaches.
