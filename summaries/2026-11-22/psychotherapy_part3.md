### The Session-specific Risk Index (SRI) in psychotherapy: Developing longitudinal session-by-session machine learning algorithms to predict the likelihood of non-response to treatment
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Gomez Penedo JM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

INTRODUCTION: This study aimed to develop and introduce an implementation-oriented, session-specific metric called the Session-specific Risk Index (SRI), which defines the likelihood of treatment non-response in psychotherapy. The SRI integrates baseline information with real-time clinical data from each session, providing a continuously updated probability of non-response to treatment.

METHODS: We used observational data from an outpatient clinic, including 1,010 psychotherapy patients. We compared the performance of elastic net regressions and gradient boosting machines for the prediction of non-response by session 20 using patients' initial sociodemographic, diagnostic and clinical information, combined with data from their symptoms' clinical evolution. Finally, we used the best performing algorithms to define the SRIs.

RESULTS: Elastic net regressions without interaction effects performed the best across the first ten sessions (accuracy = .65-.70; area-under-the-curve = .70 [.70, .71] -.77 [.76, .78]). The use of maximum Youden indices provided optimal SRI thresholds, balancing sensitivity and specificity, to identify patients at high-risk of non-response. Midpoints of SRI groups strongly correlated with proportion of patients not responding to treatment within those groups (weighted correlation= .85, 95%CI [.83-.86]).

CONCLUSION: These findings positioned the SRI as an implementation-oriented, timely-sensitive index to determine the risk of non-response to therapy session-by-session. If implemented in clinical practice, SRIs might enhance precision in mental health care going beyond baseline models of treatment personalization.

### Long-term acquisition and maintenance of cognitive behavioral therapy skills through a smartphone-based intervention: A secondary analysis of the RESiLIENT trial
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tsujino R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

BACKGROUND: Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is assumed to confer longer-term benefit partly through the acquisition and maintenance of therapeutic skills, but evidence on these processes after smartphone-based CBT remains limited in subthreshold depression. This exploratory secondary analysis of the RESiLIENT randomized controlled trial examined 50-week changes in five CBT skill domains: behavioral activation (BA), cognitive restructuring (CR), problem solving (PS), assertion training (AT), and behavioral therapy for insomnia (BI).

METHODS: The reported comparisons involved 3280 participants with baseline PHQ-9 scores of 5 or higher assigned to nine CBT intervention arms or the weekly self-check control arm. Self-reported skill enactment was assessed at baseline and weeks 6, 26, and 50. Standardized mean differences versus self-check were reported for 135 CBT contrasts. P-values and confidence intervals used a Dunnett approximation within each domain and visit.

RESULTS: Estimated changes in CR skill scores favored several intervention arms over self-check, whereas changes in BA skill scores favored the intervention in some comparisons. Estimated between-group differences in PS, AT, and BI skill scores were generally small. At week 50, standardized mean differences for BA + CR versus self-check were 0.26 (95% CI 0.07 to 0.46) for BA and 0.27 (0.07 to 0.47) for CR. The corresponding CR estimate in the CR arm was 0.21 (0.01 to 0.41). These estimates illustrate exploratory patterns and do not establish superiority of one intervention or skill domain.

CONCLUSIONS: This exploratory study describes varying patterns of change across self-reported CBT skill domains over 50 weeks following a brief smartphone-based intervention. These findings may inform future research on the persistence of CBT skill use and its relationship with longer-term clinical outcomes.

### Perspectives on temporality in psychoanalysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 The International Journal of Psychoanalysis (Int J Psychoanal) *(IF: 1.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Pola A | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

The text presents three complementary approaches to temporality in psychoanalysis-pedagogical, metapsychological, and clinical-all conceiving psychic time as multiple, non-linear, and structured through Nachträglichkeit oraprès-coup. Wang Chenyang examines Lacanian temporality through logical time, anticipation, the signifier and scansion. The past gains meaning retrospectively, while transference allows the subject's relation to time and desire to unfold and change. Bernard Chervet traces psychic time to discontinuity, loss, and drive extinction, distinguishing the repressed's preservative timelessness from traumatic atemporality. Because time has no direct representation, it is figured through rhythms, movement, and measuring devices. Temporalisation uses unconscious preservation to create continuity, work through loss and open future possibilities. Rosine Perelberg explores how coexisting temporalities are transformed in clinical practice, integrating Freudian, British, and French perspectives. She emphasises Green's heterochrony, Birksted-Breen's reverberation time and the organising role of après-coup. Analysis can represent and reorganise experiences frozen by trauma. Together, the papers unite conceptual rigour and clinical sensitivity, framing psychic change as a transformation in the experience of time.

### Unseen mothers: a psychoanalytic community responds to perinatal mood distress
**כתב עת:** 📄 The International Journal of Psychoanalysis (Int J Psychoanal) *(IF: 1.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fritsch EB | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

Perinatal mood disturbances are prevalent yet often not seen. Internal psychic pressures contribute to women downplaying such disturbances. Additionally, stressed maternal health providers frequently fail to provide these women with support in getting to the mental health care they need. These distressed mothers can fall through the cracks of the maternal health care system. This paper details the successful development of a psychoanalytic society's community outreach program that offers affordable and accessible therapy for pregnant women and new mothers. The SPRING Project has become a valued resource in the community and significantly altered the sponsoring psychoanalytic society's relationship to the external world. The Project has been life changing for many in the community and, in turn, has established a portal for psychoanalysts to become community psychoanalysts. With case examples, the author highlights the receptivity of pregnant women and new mothers to psychoanalytic treatment at this transformative time of life. It is posited that unconscious social defenses in medical practices may contribute to overcompartmentalization and obstacles to promoting access to necessary mental health care and unwittingly intertwine with a new mother's wish for self-sufficiency.

### Motor imagery-based brain-computer interface training for upper-limb rehabilitation after subacute stroke: A randomized sham-controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 PLoS One | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Tong Q et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

OBJECTIVE: To determine whether motor imagery (MI) based brain-computer interface (BCI) training improves upper-limb motor function in patients with subacute stroke compared with a high-fidelity sham-BCI control, and to explore accompanying structural and resting-state functional MRI changes.

METHOD: In this participant-blinded randomized controlled trial, 32 patients with subacute stroke were randomized to a MI-based BCI group or a high-fidelity BCI sham-control group (n = 16 each). Both groups received dose-matched training (30 min/session, 5 sessions/week for 4 weeks) in addition to standard rehabilitation. Upper-limb motor outcomes were assessed using the Fugl-Meyer Assessment-Upper Extremity (FMA-UE; primary outcome), Wolf Motor Function Test (WMFT), Motor Assessment Scale (MAS), and Modified Barthel Index (MBI). Structural MRI and resting-state functional MRI were acquired at baseline and post-intervention to assess gray matter volume (GMV) and functional connectivity (FC).

RESULTS: Both groups showed significant improvement after the intervention. Compared with the sham-control group, the MI-based BCI group demonstrated greater gains in FMA-UE and secondary motor outcomes (WMFT and MAS), whereas the difference in MBI was not statistically significant. Voxel-based morphometry revealed a within-group increase in GMV in the contralesional supplementary motor area (SMA) in the MI-based BCI group; however, no significant between-group GMV difference was observed. The MI-based BCI group also exhibited increased resting-state FC between the SMA and the ipsilesional cerebellar lobule IX and fusiform gyrus. Changes in FC were associated with improvements in FMA-UE, WMFT, and MBI, but not MAS.

CONCLUSION: Compared with a high-fidelity sham-control group, MI-based BCI group training was associated with greater upper-limb motor recovery in patients with subacute stroke, accompanied by changes in structural and functional neuroimaging measures involving motor-related regions and connections. Larger randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up are required to confirm durability and clinical relevance.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2100052452.
