### Unreliability of Biomarker Research in Psychiatry
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vandekar SN et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

IMPORTANCE: Biomarker discovery is a key research area in psychiatry, with the goals of identifying susceptibility to conditions, improving diagnostic accuracy, and predicting treatment response. Most biomarkers in psychiatry fail to be externally validated.

OBJECTIVE: To discuss 5 key principles used in unreliable biomarker discovery and provide alternative strategies for rigorous internal validation of biomarkers that can improve rigor and replicability.

EVIDENCE REVIEW: Six articles were reviewed for biomarker discovery in major depressive disorder and their use of the 5 principles.

FINDINGS: Well-documented unreliable practices have contributed to failed biomarker research. Modern methods are subject to the same unreliable principles.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Rigorous biomarker discovery is rare and remains a challenge with complex data augmentation, synthetic data, and cross-validation strategies.

### Effects of specialised and centralised versus generalised and decentralised outpatient treatment for progressed bipolar disorder: Results from the CAG Bipolar randomised controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychol Med *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Kessing LV et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

BACKGROUND: Clinical Academic Group (CAG) is a new organization of treatment bringing together clinical services, research, education, and training to provide evidence-based care. We aimed, for the first time, to investigate whether specialized and centralized outpatient treatment (CAG Bipolar) versus decentralized community-based treatment improves patient outcomes and satisfaction with care in patients with progressed bipolar disorder.

METHODS: A pragmatic randomized controlled trial was undertaken with individual randomization to 1) specialized and centralized outpatient treatment in CAG Bipolar or 2) generalized and decentralized community-based outpatient treatment. The 12-month outcomes were 1) cumulated number and duration of psychiatric hospitalizations, and 2) psychotropic medication, coercion, self-rated depressive and manic symptoms, quality of life, perceived stress, and satisfaction with care.

RESULTS: There was no difference among 1,134 included patients in cumulated number (RR: 0.94 (95% CI: 0.74-1.14),  p  = 0.6) or duration (0.16 days, (95% CI: -4.10-4.42;  p  = 0.9) of psychiatric hospitalizations. Lithium (RR: 1.19 (95% CI: 1.05-1.33) and antipsychotics (RR: 1.09 (95% CI: 1.01-1.17), specifically quetiapine (RR: 1.17 (95% CI: 1.03-1.30), were prescribed more in the CAG Bipolar group, and CAG Bipolar patients were overall more satisfied with care including the skills of the professionals, information and the efficacy of the service. There was no difference in coercion, self-rated depressive or manic symptoms, quality of life or perceived stress.

CONCLUSIONS: Specialized and centralized outpatient treatment for progressed bipolar disorder does not result in improvement in robust outcome measures such as hospitalization and coercion, but in the use of psychotropic medication concordant with international guidelines, and increased satisfaction with care.

### ALE meta-analysis of cognitive control, motivation, and effort in schizophrenia and mood disorders: implications for multidimensional apathy
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Psychiatry Neurosci | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Lafond-Brina G et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jan 1

BACKGROUND: Apathy is present in about 50% of patients suffering from schizophrenia (SZ) and mood disorders (MD). Even though these two disorders are different, in psychiatry, apathy refers typically to the same unidimensional clinical entity, sharing the same pathophysiological processes and symptoms regardless of the underlying diagnosis. Neurology proposes another perspective: three forms of apathy-emotional, executive, and initiative-are related to a disturbance in motivation, cognitive control, and effort-based decision-making, respectively. We explored whether this latter model can be applied in psychiatry by identifying differences between SZ and MD through a PRISMA meta-analysis of imaging studies on reward processing, cognitive control, and effort-based decision-making networks.

METHODS: We searched the Sleuth BrainMap and PubMed databases for studies on SZ, MD, and/or healthy controls using tasks probing reward processing, cognitive control, and effort-based decision-making. Twenty-three functional MRI studies were identified and included for a coordinate-based activation likelihood estimation analysis.

RESULTS: In SZ, hypoactivity in reward-processing structures, a specific hyperactivity in the right cerebellar vermis that has previously been linked to emotional blunting and apathy, and a lack of activation during effort-based decision-making that could imply an impaired reward processing, suggest a dominant form of emotional apathy. In MD, hypoactivity in both cognitive control and reward-processing structures, which has previously been linked to the co-occurrence of executive difficulty and amotivation, suggests a mixed emotional/executive apathy profile.

LIMITATIONS: The main limitations include the use of two databases, a small number of existing studies, a lack of control over treatment, age, and disorder severity, as well as the inherent heterogeneity of each diagnosis.

CONCLUSION: By highlighting different dominant forms of apathy in SZ and MD, these results could help target new individualized treatment strategies in precision psychiatry based on a multidimensional conceptualization of apathy.

REGISTRATION: This review was not prospectively registered.

### [Child suicide: a developmental perspective]
**כתב עת:** 📄 Soins Pediatr Pueric | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Torne Celer A | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov-Dec

Suicide before puberty is rare but can affect children as young as 6. Suicidal behaviour should be viewed from a developmental perspective, at the intersection of individual vulnerabilities and family, school and social factors. Abuse, trauma, family conflicts, mental health problems, bullying, rejection and isolation increase the risk. Conversely, parental support, a sense of belonging at school and the presence of reassuring adults act as protective factors. Any expression of suicidal thoughts must be taken seriously and form the subject of a comprehensive assessment. Prevention involves a combination of child and adolescent psychiatry, family support, cooperation with schools and public policy.
