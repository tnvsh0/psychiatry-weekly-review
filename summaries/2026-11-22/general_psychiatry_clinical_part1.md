### Lithium for Suicide Prevention: Umbrella Review and Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Yano OL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

OBJECTIVE: Lithium has anchored suicide-prevention guidelines for five decades, yet quantitative syntheses of the randomized evidence have produced divergent estimates. The authors evaluated the contemporary randomized evidence on lithium for suicide-related outcomes in patients with psychiatric disorders.

METHODS: Five databases were searched from inception to April 6, 2026, for systematic reviews with meta-analyses of randomized trials comparing lithium with placebo, treatment as usual, or active comparators; all eligible trials were re-extracted, with an updated six-source primary-trial search from January 2024. Prespecified outcomes were completed suicide, suicide attempt, deliberate self-harm, all-cause mortality, composite suicide-related events, and treatment discontinuation. The primary estimator was the Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio without continuity correction, and certainty of evidence was rated using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) framework.

RESULTS: Ten systematic reviews, one reanalysis, and 20 trial publications (22 strata; 3,675 participants) were included. For completed suicide (11 informative comparisons, 2,292 participants; six events with lithium vs. 16 with comparators), the pooled estimate favored lithium but included the null (odds ratio [OR]=0.39, 95% CI=0.15, 1.00; I²=0%). A Peto sensitivity estimate reached statistical significance (OR=0.42, 95% CI=0.18, 0.98), whereas excluding the most influential comparison attenuated the estimate (OR=0.43, 95% CI=0.13, 1.40). In exploratory subgroup analysis, the signal concentrated in trials enrolling mixed mood-disorder populations (four trials; OR=0.25, 95% CI=0.07, 0.90). Treatment discontinuation favored lithium over placebo but active comparators over lithium. No significant effects emerged for suicide attempt, deliberate self-harm, all-cause mortality, or composite suicide-related events. GRADE certainty was low to very low.

CONCLUSIONS: Current evidence indicates greater uncertainty than suggested by earlier syntheses, with a sparse completed-suicide signal that was estimator-sensitive and with no consistent evidence across related outcomes. Lithium remains a treatment option when clinically indicated, but adequately powered pragmatic trials that prespecify suicidal behavior and balance clinical contact between arms are needed.

### Growth Differentiation Factor 15 as a Body-Brain Stress Signal in Major Depression and Insulin Resistance
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Aichholzer M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

OBJECTIVE: Major depressive disorder (MDD) is linked to insulin resistance, which is associated with increased risk, chronicity, and poor treatment response in depression. Growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15), a stress-induced cytokine linking peripheral to central energy metabolism, may mediate this link, but its role in MDD is unclear. The authors assessed associations of MDD and insulin resistance with GDF15; links between GDF15, symptoms, and stress; and whether baseline GDF15 levels predict symptom persistence.

METHODS: In this preregistered secondary analysis, 190 participants with MDD and 183 control participants provided blood, underwent interviews, and completed questionnaires. GDF15 values were log-transformed and analyzed with linear models. The association between GDF15 and depression severity was validated in an independent cohort of medication-free MDD patients (N=168).

RESULTS: Both MDD diagnosis and insulin resistance were significantly associated with elevated GDF15 (adjusted for age and sex). Across all participants, elevated GDF15 was significantly associated with greater depressive severity, childhood trauma, and perceived stress. In stratified analyses, the association with depression severity and perceived stress was significant only in healthy control subjects. In MDD patients, baseline GDF15 was associated with persistence of symptoms across time. In the second independent sample of medication-free MDD patients, depression severity was significantly associated with GDF15.

CONCLUSIONS: GDF15 is elevated in MDD and insulin resistance, indicating convergent stress-energy pathway activation, and in MDD higher GDF15 predicted symptom persistence. These findings position GDF15 as a mechanistic biomarker at the interface of psychiatric and metabolic health and reinforce the need for integrated care of metabolic and mental health.

### Implication of the ADCY1 Gene in Lithium Response in Bipolar Disorder by Genome-Wide Association Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Bipolar Disord | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Yao K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

OBJECTIVES: Efforts to examine the influence of genetics in the efficacy of lithium using genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have identified several loci. Here, we report new cases and meta-analyses to combine previous efforts.

METHODS: We report data from 1259 participants with bipolar disorder (BD) recruited at University College London who had been treated with lithium. The data come from three waves of genotyping on different arrays. The GWAS data from each array was analysed separately and then meta-analysed with two published lithium response GWAS datasets. Post-GWAS analyses were conducted to examine lithium response heritability and its genetic correlations with other traits. We also attempted to replicate past polygenic risk scores (PRS) results.

RESULTS: SNP rs116927879 (A/G) was associated with lithium response at a genome-wide significance level (p = 4.509 × 10 -08 ) with a consistent effect across all cohorts. rs116927879 is located on chromosome 7 and maps to the protein-coding gene ADCY1 and two pseudo-genes, GTF2IP13 and SEPT7P2. The sQTL results suggested that rs116927879 genotypes may influence the splicing of ADCY1 across different brain regions. We estimated the SNP heritability (h 2 ) for good lithium response as 20.3% and 15.6% for subjective/objective response definitions, respectively. We did not observe any genetic correlation or PRS association between the lithium response and schizophrenia or major depression disorder. However, we found weak evidence to suggest that males are more likely to be good responders.

CONCLUSIONS: Our GWAS identifies a genome-wide significant finding and provides updated heritability estimates for lithium efficacy.

### The efficacy of ketogenic interventions on neuropsychological & metabolic outcomes in serious mental illness & neurocognitive disorders: a systematic review & meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Transl Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Maksyutynska K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 27

INTRODUCTION: Ketogenic interventions have garnered attention for their potential benefits in individuals with psychiatric and neurocognitive disorders. This review analyzed findings from studies investigating the impact of ketogenic interventions on clinical outcomes in individuals with serious mental illness (SMI) and neurocognitive disorders.

METHODS: A comprehensive search was conducted to identify studies reporting on psychopathology or cognitive performance in individuals with schizophrenia spectrum, major mood, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), or major neurocognitive disorders/dementia treated with a ketogenic intervention. Changes in body composition, metabolic parameters, adherence, and safety were also assessed. Evidence was organized by population and outcome of interest, and supplemented by a quantitative meta-analysis.

RESULTS: Seventy-three studies (SMI k = 31; neurocognitive disorders k = 42) were included, with treatment durations ranging from single-dose interventions up to 12 years. Evidence in SMI was largely limited to case reports and single-arm trials reporting symptom remission, reductions in positive and negative symptoms, and mood improvements. Controlled trials in neurocognitive disorders provided more substantiated evidence amenable to meta-analysis. Ketogenic interventions improved cognitive performance (SMD 0.24, 95% CI [0.06, 0.43], p = 0.01, n = 587, k = 14, I² = 61%) and reduced body weight (MD - 3.13 kg, 95% CI [-5.91, -0.35], p = 0.03, n = 80, k = 3, I² = 6%) in this population. Gastrointestinal symptoms were the most frequently reported adverse event.

CONCLUSION: While the evidence for ketogenic interventions differs by population, promising findings emerge. In neurocognitive disorders, controlled trial data support modest cognitive and metabolic benefits. In SMI, preliminary evidence highlights the evolving nature of this field and the need for more rigorous investigation, including RCTs, to establish clinical safety and efficacy.
