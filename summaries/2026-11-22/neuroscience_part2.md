### Neotenic expansion of adult-born dentate granule cells reconfigures GABAergic inhibition to enhance social memory consolidation
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chung A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

Immature adult-born dentate granule cells (abDGCs) show heightened synaptic and structural plasticity and preferentially contribute to hippocampal-dependent memory. Protracted maturation (neoteny) of abDGCs in higher mammals is posited to offset the decline in adult hippocampal neurogenesis by supporting increased plasticity. Here we found that selective, synchronous expansion of 4-week-old immature, but not 8-week-old mature, mouse abDGCs promotes recruitment of fast-spiking parvalbumin-expressing inhibitory interneurons (INs) in hippocampal CA3 and CA2, increases feed-forward inhibition, decreases social memory interference and enhances social memory consolidation. In vivo local field potential recordings in dorsal CA1 and CA2 revealed increased baseline power, amplitude and duration of sharp-wave ripples, as well as enhanced rate modulation by social experience. Inhibitory neuron-targeted chemogenetic manipulations implicate dorsal CA3 and CA2 INs, including parvalbumin-expressing INs, as necessary and sufficient for social memory consolidation following and sans neotenic expansion of abDGCs, respectively. Our findings suggest that neoteny of abDGCs supports cognition by increasing feed-forward inhibition in DG-CA3 and DG-CA2 and promoting network oscillations that underlie memory consolidation.

### The neural mechanisms of fast versus slow decision-making
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Nashaat M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 19

One hallmark of intelligent behavior is the ability to optimize how much time is devoted to decisions. Yet the principles governing decision timing remain unclear. Here, we developed a novel paradigm for directly comparing decision-making between mice and humans. This approach allowed us to combine widefield and 2-photon imaging in the mouse frontal cortex with optogenetic inhibition to examine the underlying mechanisms. We found that history-dependent refinement of sequences in the medial frontal cortex (MFC), versus a thresholding mechanism in the lateral frontal cortex (LFC), provides a unified logic for decision strategy on a trial-by-trial basis. Notably, behavioral performance and strategy were profoundly disrupted by optogenetic inhibition of the MFC compared with that of the LFC, revealing a dominant cognitive role for MFC in decision selection and timing. These dynamics reflect reinforcement-learning updates that explain timing variability better than sensory evidence. Our findings provide a computational framework that unifies voluntary decision-making across species.

### Cortical and meningeal neuroimmune interactions contribute to the generation of migraine headaches
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Erdener ŞE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

Migraine is a prevalent disorder that ranks as the second leading cause of disability among all diseases. Recent advancements have significantly enhanced our comprehension of its underlying pathophysiology. Growing evidence highlights complex neuroimmune signaling networks-particularly interactions between meningeal nociceptors and resident immune cells-as key contributors to headache generation. The clinical success of anti-calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) antibodies underscores the importance of intercellular communication between the dural nociceptive nerves and adjacent resident immune cells such as macrophages in migraine headache. Notably, the intercellular inflammatory interplay is not confined to the meninges. Disturbances within the brain parenchyma potentially act as initiating triggers, as suggested by the presence of aura and premonitory symptoms preceding the headache. Recent experimental discoveries indicate that neuronal stress triggers an inflammatory signaling cascade that, through interactions with astrocytes and microglia, propagates to the meninges, ultimately inducing meningeal neurogenic inflammation and headache. This model is supported by converging experimental evidence and human imaging studies employing PET and quantitative MRI, which document the presence of changes compatible with inflammatory activity within the brain parenchyma and overlying meninges of patients with migraine with aura. Genetic studies identifying several inflammation-related genes and pathways as risk factors for migraine further corroborate this concept. Furthermore, cell-specific transcriptomic analyses of mouse brains have revealed astrocyte-selective activation of nuclear factor kappa B -regulated inflammatory transcriptional pathways after cortical spreading depolarization, the neurophysiological correlate of migraine aura. Owing to their extensive interface with the cerebrospinal fluid, astrocytes are uniquely positioned to release inflammatory mediators to activate meningeal nociceptors and immune cells. Insights from pain research suggest that neuroimmune interactions may be responsible for the widespread parenchymal increases in 18 kDa translocator protein (TSPO) PET signals observed in migraine with aura patients. This discovery also identifies TSPO as a potential player in migraine pathophysiology and novel drug target. In this review, by integrating cellular and molecular findings from peripheral pain and inflammation research, we examine the dynamic interactions between neurons and glia, as well as between meningeal nociceptors and immune cells in the context of migraine headache. This perspective provides a conceptual framework for understanding how nociceptors and glial cells coordinate to signal non-homeostatic brain states-initiating protective, tightly regulated responses without progressing to overt neuroinflammatory disease and provides novel insight into the findings of PET studies involving TSPO.

### Sour Suppresses Sweet, Salty, and Bitter: Implications for Signal Processing in Taste Buds
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fleites IR et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 15

Taste is typically encountered as complex blends, yet how peripheral taste organs process heterogeneous mixtures remains poorly understood. Here we asked how sour stimuli influence the encoding of sweet, salty, and bitter tastes in primary gustatory sensory neurons. Using in vivo calcium imaging in mice of either sex to record neurons in geniculate ganglia during oral delivery of tastants, we compared responses to naturally occurring taste stimuli presented alone versus in binary mixtures that activate acid (sour)-sensing (Type III) taste bud cells. Adding citric acid or HCl robustly suppressed sucrose-evoked responses in geniculate ganglion neurons, even though acid itself did not activate those neurons. Acid-induced suppression generalized to bitter and salty tastes, which, like sucrose, are believed to activate Type II taste cells. Additional stimuli that activate acid-sensing taste cells (NH 4 Cl, KCl) also reduced sucrose responses, supporting a common mechanism linked to Type III cells. Manipulating levels of serotonin (a paracrine transmitter released by Type III cells) in taste buds altered taste suppression: increasing serotonin with 5-hydroxytryptophan enhanced acid-induced suppression whereas depleting serotonin with para-chlorophenylalanine weakened it. Finally, population analyses, including UMAP dimensionality reduction, indicate that taste suppression is not limited to a particular subset of gustatory sensory neurons. The findings are consistent with serotonin-dependent paracrine inhibition within taste buds as a contributor to sour-driven suppression in taste mixtures and reveal a compression of gustatory information into hedonic categories as early as at the level of primary sensory neurons. Significance Statement  Foods typically present a complex mixture of tastes. However, understanding how the nervous system processes gustatory signals has traditionally relied on investigations that analyze responses to individual tastes. Our study investigates how mixtures of naturally occurring food tastes activate sensory neurons and how emergent signals arise when combinations of gustatory stimuli excite taste buds. Using in vivo imaging of primary gustatory neurons in mice, we show that sour stimuli suppress neural responses to sweet, bitter, and salty tastes. The findings reveal an early, peripheral stage of neural computation that helps explain how complex flavors are encoded.
