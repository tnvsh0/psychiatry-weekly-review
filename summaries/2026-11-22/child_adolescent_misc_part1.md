### Clinico-demographic Profile and Associated Factors of Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 Kathmandu Univ Med J (KUMJ) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sharma A  et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Apr-Jun

BACKGROUND: Autism Spectrum Disorder is a neurodevelopmental disorder with symptoms manifesting in early childhood.

OBJECTIVE: To determine the clinical and sociodemographic profile of children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and identify associated factors.

METHOD: A descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted among children diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder. Children were recruited consecutively at the Child and Adolescent Psychiatry Unit, Kanti Children's Hospital, Nepal. The study duration was from 1st August 2023 to 31st January 2024. The study was approved by Institutional Review Board of Kanti Children's Hospital. A semi-structured proforma and Childhood Autism Rating Scale, Second Edition were used to assess the children. Data were entered and analysed using IBM SPSS version 16.0.

RESULT: The sample included 145 children. Mean age of diagnosis was 44.1 months (SD ± 18.7). Majority of them were 119 (82.1%) male, 85 (58.6%) belonged to nuclear family, and 69 (47.5%) were single child. Male to female ratio was 4.5:1. The mean paternal and maternal ages at the time of conception were 32.1 years (SD ± 6.5) and 27.7 years (SD ± 5.2), respectively. Most common source of referral was doctors/ health workers 66 (45.5%), parents themselves 45 (31%) and relatives 15 (10.3%). The main reason for visit was speech delay 135 (93.1%) and poor socio emotional reciprocity 76 (52.4%). Mean childhood autism rating score was 34.9 (SD ± 4.3), with 108 (74.4%) having mild-to-moderate autism severity. Sibling status was significantly associated with autism severity (p= 0.042) as children without siblings were more likely to exhibit mild to moderate symptoms compared to those with one or more siblings.

CONCLUSION: The study shows higher rate of Autism Spectrum Disorder among males and diagnosis at an early age. Presence of a sibling may affect autism severity.

### Cross-sectional analysis of the association between exclusive breastfeeding duration and Autism Spectrum Disorder odds in U.S. preschool children: a nationally representative study
**כתב עת:** 📄 Int Breastfeed J | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yuan H, Yang Z | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 25

BACKGROUND: The association between breastfeeding and Autism Spectrum Disorder remains unclear, with existing evidence showing inconsistency. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between breastfeeding patterns and ASD in a national sample of preschool children.

METHODS: Data from the 2018-2023 National Survey of Children's Health (N = 48,158) were analyzed. Breastfeeding was categorized into four mutually exclusive groups based on official NSCH methodology: never breastfed, breastfed < 6 months, breastfed ≥ 6 months non-exclusively, and exclusively breastfed for 6 months. Multivariable weighted logistic regression models examined the association between breastfeeding patterns and ASD, adjusting for confounders. Dose-response was evaluated by modeling breastfeeding category as an ordinal variable (0-3) in fully adjusted models, with polynomial contrasts assessing linearity of trends. Subgroup analyses stratified by age, sex, race/ethnicity, parental education, household size, poverty level, low birth weight, and preterm status tested effect heterogeneity via likelihood ratio tests. Robustness to unmeasured confounding was quantified using E-values for the primary exposure-outcome association.

RESULTS: The weighted prevalence of ASD was 3.0%. Compared to never breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months was associated with significantly lower ASD odds in fully adjusted models (OR = 0.61, 95% CI:0.44-0.86). A significant linear trend was observed, with ASD odds decreasing by 16% for each increase in breastfeeding category (OR = 0.84, 95% CI: 0.76-0.93). Polynomial contrasts further indicated the steepest reduction in odds occurred at the transition to exclusive breastfeeding. Subgroup analyses revealed significant effect modification (P for interaction < 0.05) and stronger inverse associations with lower ASD odds among 3-year-olds, non-Hispanic White children, children of college-educated parents, and those from single-child households.

CONCLUSION: Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months was associated with lower odds of ASD in U.S. preschool children, with a graded inverse association across increasing breastfeeding exposure categories. This association was modified by sociodemographic and biological factors, underscoring the importance of contextualizing feeding practices within broader family and social environments in neurodevelopmental research.

CLINICAL TRIAL NUMBER: Not applicable.

### [Native language rejection and foreign language dominance in autism: empirical evidence and clinical and educational policy recommendations]
**כתב עת:** 📄 Psychiatr Hung | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Németh D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) may be associated with a wide range of atypical language patterns. Over the past two decades, an increasing number of scientific publications have documented a phenomenon in which autistic children acquire a foreign language - most commonly English - instead of their native language as their primary means of communication, often exclusively through exposure to media content (YouTube videos, cartoons, computer games). The literature refers to this phenomenon as "unexpected bilingualism", "paradoxical language dominance", or "non-interactive language acquisition". The present review article summarizes the available empirical and case-based evidence, analyzes possible explanations for the phenomenon, and discusses its clinical and educational implications. We argue that autistic children's preference for a foreign language is not an independent pathological symptom, but rather a possible consequence of the cognitive and autonomic nervous system profile characteristic of autism; a phenomenon that educational systems should recognize and actively support rather than attempt to suppress. Keywords: autism; autism spectrum disorder; non-interactive language acquisition; unexpected bilingualism; paradoxical language dominance; native language rejection; interpersonal distance; homeschooling; online education; educational policy.
