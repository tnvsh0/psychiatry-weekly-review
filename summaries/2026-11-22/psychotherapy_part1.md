### Concomitant Psychotropic Medication Is Associated with Reduced Outcomes of Trauma-Focused Psychotherapy for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Steenen SA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

INTRODUCTION: Psychotropic medications are frequently prescribed alongside trauma-focused psychotherapy for post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), yet their impact on treatment response remains uncertain. This study emulated target trials to examine the association between psychotropic co-medication at treatment onset and psychotherapy outcomes in a real-world PTSD cohort.

METHODS: A prospective cohort of 6,125 adults with chronic or delayed-onset PTSD received a standardized 2-8 day trauma-focused psychotherapy program, including daily prolonged exposure and eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) therapy, at a Dutch psychotrauma center (2021-2024). Target trial emulation with double machine learning with inverse probability of treatment weighting estimated the effects of specific psychotropic co-medications versus non-use on changes in Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5 (CAPS-5) scores (range, 0-80) from pre-to post-treatment.

RESULTS: Mean CAPS-5 scores improved by 25.7 points (Cohen's d = -2.30). Psychotropic co-medication (n = 1,382) was associated with reduced symptom improvement compared with non-use (model-estimated difference = -2.52 points; relative reduction = -9.4%, 95% CI = -10.0 to -8.9; d = -1.11, E = 10.0). This effect persisted at 6-month follow-up (d = -0.42). Antidepressants overall (d = -0.28; follow-up d = -0.56), amitriptyline (d = -0.51), and mirtazapine (d = -0.29) were consistently associated with poorer outcomes across sensitivity analyses. Similar patterns were observed for anticonvulsants, mood-stabilizing anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, fluoxetine, zolpidem, and zopiclone. Sensitivity analyses and E-values indicated robustness to unmeasured confounding.

CONCLUSION: Several psychotropic co-medications were associated with reduced outcomes of evidence-based trauma-focused psychotherapy for PTSD. By identifying this as a potentially modifiable factor, psychotherapy outcomes may be optimized. Trials are warranted to evaluate whether tapering or substituting these agents improves outcomes.

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is often treated with trauma-focused psychotherapy, which includes structured methods such as prolonged exposure and eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR) therapy. These treatments are highly effective for many people. However, many patients also take psychotropic medications, such as antidepressants or sleeping pills, at the time of therapy. It has been unclear whether these medications change how well psychotherapy works. In this study, we followed more than 6,000 adults with PTSD who all received the same intensive psychotherapy program at a specialized treatment center. We compared the treatment results of patients who were using different medications with those who were not using them. To ensure fair comparisons, we applied advanced statistical methods that take into account many individual differences, such as severity of symptoms, past treatment history, and other health problems. Overall, patients showed large improvements after therapy. However, patients who used certain psychotropic medications (such as some antidepressants, antipsychotics, anti-epileptics, and sleeping medications) experienced less symptom improvement compared to those who did not use these drugs. This difference remained visible even 6 months after treatment ended. These findings suggest that certain medications may reduce the benefits of psychotherapy. More research is needed to understand why this happens, and whether temporarily adjusting medication use before therapy could improve results. Such decisions should always be carefully weighed against the possible risks of medication withdrawal or symptom return.

### Placebo Effect of Verbal Instructions Coupled with Pharmacological Treatment: Results from a Randomized Controlled Trial in Migraine Patients
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Romanazzo S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

INTRODUCTION: Verbal instructions modulate the expectation concerning a given drug. Knowing that the prescribing clinician is positive and enthusiastic toward the prescribed treatment may lead to positive expectations. We tested whether administering strongly positive verbal instructions together with a pharmacological prescription might trigger a placebo effect in migraine patients.

METHODS: Outpatients (n = 102) were randomly assigned to: (a) an experimental condition: the pharmacological prescription was coupled to strongly positive verbal instructions; (b) a control condition: the prescription was coupled to positive verbal instructions. Subjects were assessed before the prescription (T0), 7 days (T1), 15 days (T2), 1 month (T3), 2 months (T4), and 3 months after it (T5). At each assessment point, Headache Impact Test (HIT-6) and two Numeric Rating Scales (NRSs) were proposed. Kellner's scale of change was administered at T1-T5.

RESULTS: Patients who received strongly positive instructions had no HIT decrease (p = 0.177; η2 = 0.034). Patients who received positive instructions had HIT decrease (p = 0.006; η2 = 0.770). No difference was found between the groups. No significant across timepoints change of expectations was observed in the group who received strongly positive instructions (p = 0.433; η2 = 0.021) or in the group who received positive instructions (p = 0.151; η2 = 0.037). Since the first group had a decrease across timepoints of the score while the second group had a peak at T2, difference between them was statistically significant (p = 0.029; η2 = 0.049).

CONCLUSION: Strongly positive verbal instructions did not produce placebo effect.

Verbal instructions can induce positive expectations and, if coupled to a medication prescription, can positively influence the response to it. We tested whether administering strongly positive instructions together with an antimigraine prescription may influence the response to the medication (the so-called placebo effect) in migraine patients. To test such hypothesis, 102 outpatients were assigned to: (a) an experimental condition in which the antimigraine prescription was coupled to strongly positive verbal instructions; (b) a control condition in which the antimigraine prescription was coupled to positive verbal instructions. Subjects were assessed before the prescription, 7 days, 15 days, 1 month, 2 months, and 3 months after the prescription using specific tools which measure migraine severity and expectations related to the medication prescribed. The results showed that patients who received strongly positive instructions had less expectations than those who received positive instructions. In brief, strongly positive instructions did not produce placebo effect and, consequently, did not produce benefit if told to migraine patients.

### Can we leverage scalable barrier free single session interventions developed for adolescents (13-18 years) for use in young adults? A mixed methods single arm pilot study in the UK
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Perry G et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 12

INTRODUCTION: Many young adults (YA) experience depression symptoms, yet few access appropriate support. Single session interventions (SSIs) offer brief, evidence-based support and can be delivered online, potentially increasing accessibility. SSIs appear acceptable, useful, and efficacious for adolescents which may extend to YA. However, this has not yet been established. This study aimed to explore whether an SSI developed for adolescents would be acceptable and useful to YA, and to identify opportunities for further adaptation.

METHODS: We used a single-arm pre-post intervention within subjects' design. YA in the UK (age 19-25) completed demographics, outcome measures, a behaviour activation-based SSI and gave feedback about the intervention. Demographics and recruitment source were described, and qualitative feedback was analysed using conventional content analysis. Pre-post changes were analysed using linear mixed effects models.

RESULTS: Two-hundred and thirty-two eligible participants consented, 161 started the SSI, and 73 (45.3%) completed it. Most were recruited through a third-party mental health organisation. Participants were predominately White British, assigned female at birth, and heterosexual. Post-intervention programme feedback scale scores indicated that most YA found the SSI acceptable. Participants highlighted both positive and negative aspects in relation to usability, and value. Analyses indicated significant pre-post changes in hope, hopelessness, and perceived control.

CONCLUSIONS: SSIs developed for adolescents appear acceptable and useful, and may produce short term changes in hope, hopelessness and perceived control. Further adaptations could improve age-appropriateness and suitability for YA. Future research could include intervention codesign with YA, and examine whether SSIs influence short- and longer-term clinical outcomes including depression symptoms.

### Non-pharmacological augmentation of trauma-focused psychotherapy for PTSD: a systematic review and meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eur J Psychotraumatol | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Langhammer T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

BACKGROUND: Trauma-focused psychotherapies (TFPs) effectively treat post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), yet patients show variable response or discontinue treatment. Numerous augmentation strategies have been developed to address these barriers, but the field lacks a framework that extends beyond mechanistic categories as well as a meta-analytic synthesis of their effects.

OBJECTIVE: To develop a classification framework for non-pharmacological augmentation strategies of first-line TFP for PTSD and to meta-analytically evaluate whether these strategies improve treatment outcomes.

METHOD: We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) comparing TFP augmented with a non-pharmacological intervention versus TFP alone or an active control (i.e. sham intervention or dose increase). Eight databases were searched to July 2026. Augmentation strategies were qualitatively categorised. Primary outcomes were PTSD symptom severity and response rate; dropout was a secondary outcome.

RESULTS: Fifty RCTs met inclusion criteria. Their augmentations were categorised within a three-level framework comprising targets, mechanisms, and implementation forms. Of these, 39 trials (40 comparisons;  N  = 2,853) were meta-analysed and showed no significant overall effects (symptom severity:  g  = -0.10,  p  = .062; response:  RR  = 1.04,  p  = .111; dropout:  RR  = 0.94,  p  = .489). One subgroup targeting sleep disturbance showed an add-on effect.

CONCLUSIONS: The present findings suggest that augmenting TFP with non-pharmacological interventions does not necessarily lead to incremental efficacy at the overall level, even though small benefits were observed for sleep-focused strategies. Our three-level framework provides a structured approach for conceptualising this heterogeneous field. The absence of a robust overall effect should not be mistaken for evidence that augmentation cannot be effective.

We meta-analysed 39 RCTs ( N  = 2,853) evaluating augmentation of trauma-focused therapy for PTSD and developed a framework classifying add-ons by target, mechanism, and implementationAugmentation showed no overall benefit for PTSD symptoms, response, or dropout.Sleep-focused add-ons were the exception, suggesting that augmentation may be most effective when matched to a specific treatment barrier rather than applied universally.

### Recruiting a geographically, ethnically and socioeconomically diverse population of postpartum women into a behavioural weight management intervention trial: an evaluation of strategies used in the Supporting MumS (SMS) randomised controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Trials | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Gallagher D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 28

BACKGROUND: Engaging women in behavioural weight management interventions in the postpartum period has proved challenging. Previous trials of such interventions have frequently experienced issues with participant recruitment and retention and recruited samples have been skewed towards inclusion of women from white ethnicities and more affluent backgrounds. In this paper, we evaluate strategies used to recruit a geographically, ethnically and socioeconomically diverse sample of postpartum women to the Supporting MumS randomised controlled trial (RCT).

METHODS: Two-arm, parallel group, multi-centre RCT. Recruitment took place in five sites across the United Kingdom (UK), selected with consideration of geographic, ethnic and socioeconomic population diversity. Participants were women (including those who gave birth but who do not identify as women), within 6 weeks to 2 years postpartum, with a Body Mass Index of ≥ 25 kg/m 2 . The range of recruitment strategies employed spanned healthcare settings, community settings and social media. Strategies used to recruit over a 12-month period were recorded, and outcomes including numbers expressing interest, screened and randomised, were assessed by site, ethnicity and Index of Multiple Deprivation (IMD) data.

RESULTS: Over 12 months, 2457 women expressed an interest, 1227 (49.9%) were screened for eligibility and 892 (36.3%) were randomised. The sample included 33.6% of women from minoritised ethnic groups and 53.5% of women who lived in the two most deprived IMD quintiles. Most participants learnt about the study through social media (45.5%), followed by community groups and settings aimed at parents (26.0%), the Born in Bradford's Better Start (BiBBS) cohort (12.5%), friends/family (7.2%), general community settings (5.3%) and healthcare professionals (5.4%). Success of recruitment strategies varied across sites and participant characteristics.

CONCLUSIONS: An ethnically and socioeconomically diverse sample of postpartum women across the four UK countries was recruited within the planned 12-month timeframe. This was achieved by selection of recruitment sites (considering geographical spread and population characteristics), using a range of strategies and tailoring these to local populations, and iteratively adapting strategies employed based on success.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: ISRCTN16299220 ( https://doi.org/10.1186/ISRCTN16299220 ). Prospectively registered on February 10, 2022.
