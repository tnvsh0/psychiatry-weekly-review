### When resilience breaks down: Erosion of the protective role of intelligence in PTSD under extreme combat stress
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zalmenson T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

Resilience factors play an important role in shaping posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) trajectories, yet their protective effects may depend on the severity of trauma exposure. Prior research has linked higher premorbid IQ to lower PTSD risk, with longitudinal evidence from deploying soldiers showing more favorable symptom trajectories among those with higher IQ during routine combat deployment. The present study examined whether this protective effect persisted under conditions of extreme and protracted combat exposure during the Israel-Hamas war. Male Israel Defense Force infantry reservists ( N  = 192; Mean age at enrollment = 19.60 years, SD = 0.78; range = 19-23) were assessed for IQ at age 17 and followed across four time points: three during mandatory service with routine deployments and one during active reserve duty in the war. Ethnicity data were not collected. A mixed-design ANCOVA revealed an interaction ( η 2 p  = 0.017) indicating that higher IQ was associated with greater resilience under routine combat deployment conditions. However, this advantage disappeared under the extreme and protracted combat conditions of the war. Findings highlight that the protective role of cognitive ability is context-dependent, supporting diathesis-stress and threshold models of PTSD risk and resilience. Developmental implications associated with war-related trauma exposure during the critical period of emerging adulthood are discussed.

### Hands-on or screen-on? The role of training approach in spatial skill development and transfer to mathematics
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Child Development (Child Dev) *(IF: 5.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Leung CO et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 19

Growing evidence suggests that spatial skills are crucial for learning and everyday functioning and can be improved through training. However, which approaches are most effective remains unclear. The present study compared improvement in children's spatial skills and math performance across four carefully matched spatial training approaches and examined whether individual differences in working memory, spatial anxiety, and gender moderated these changes. Fifth- and sixth-graders from Dutch schools (N = 349; Mage = 11.5 years; 57% girls) were randomly assigned to four training approaches: (1) physical manipulatives (hands-on), (2) virtual manipulatives (hands-on), (3) video demonstration (hands-off), or (4) practice without materials (baseline). Children completed a pretest 1-2 weeks before training, an immediate posttest, and a follow-up assessment 1-2 weeks later (data collected January-March 2024). Results indicated that the two hands-on conditions and the practice-without-materials condition produced comparable gains across all spatial and math outcomes, whereas the video demonstration condition generally showed smaller gains. Additionally, children higher in working memory improved more, regardless of training approach. Interestingly, while girls and children with higher spatial anxiety performed lower overall, they improved to a similar extent as boys and low-anxiety children. Finally, we found that gains were sustained at follow-up, indicating durability.

Spatial skills are fundamental for academic achievement and everyday life, and research shows they can be improved with training. This study compared children's improvements in spatial and math outcomes following one of four spatial training approaches: physical manipulatives, digital manipulatives, video demonstrations, or practice without materials. In total, 349 fifth- and sixth-graders completed tasks 1–2 weeks before training, immediately after training, and 1–2 weeks later. Children using physical and digital manipulatives and those practicing without materials showed similar improvements, whereas those watching video demonstrations showed smaller improvement. Gains were maintained at follow-up, suggesting durability. Overall, these findings suggest that hands-on materials are not always necessary for spatial training, and simpler approaches may be sufficient when hands-on methods are not feasible.

### Adverse childhood experiences and later overweight and obesity: Findings from an 11-year longitudinal follow-up study-The HUNT Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 Scand J Public Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fredriksen I et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

Aims:  Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are associated with unfavorable health consequences. This study aimed to examine the longitudinal associations between ACEs and overweight/obesity in young adulthood.  Methods:  Up to 1942 participants aged 13-19 years in Young-HUNT3 (2006-08) were followed into adulthood 11 years later in HUNT4 (2017-2019). Associations between ACEs (violence/threats, sexual abuse, death/serious illness of someone close, and disaster/serious accidents) reported in adolescence as either a total sum score or sub-scores and adult body mass index (BMI) or waist circumference were estimated by linear regression, stratified by sex and adjusted for age and socioeconomic position.  Results:  Among the participants, 80% (82.6% females and 76.7% males) reported at least one ACE. However, the highest cumulative ACE burden (7-10 ACEs) was slightly more common among males (2.3%) than females (1.7%). Among females, but not males, a positive association was found between the total ACE score and BMI and waist circumference.  Conclusions:  Female adolescents reporting ACEs are at risk of increased overweight and obesity in adulthood. This finding underscores the importance of early interventions targeting ACEs in obesity prevention strategies.

### Associations between adverse childhood experiences and later-life multiple functional limitation in older adults across four cohorts
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Med | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jiang F et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 24

Background The aim of this study is to evaluate the association between Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) and multiple functional limitation in older adults across various international settings. Methods We used longitudinal data from 78,417 adults aged 50 years or older in four ageing cohorts: the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), the English Longitudinal Study of Ageing (ELSA), the Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe (SHARE), and the Health and Retirement Study (HRS). Six ACE indicators were assessed retrospectively (physical abuse, emotional neglect, parental death, foster care placement, financial hardship, and parental unemployment) and analyzed in two ways: as the cumulative number of ACEs (categorized as 0, 1, 2, or ≥ 3), and as four ACE types (physical abuse, emotional neglect, adverse family environment, and economic adversity). At each survey wave, multiple functional limitation was measured as the number of impairments across six domains (hearing, vision, physical functioning, mobility, mental health, and cognition), giving a score from 0 to 6. Within each cohort, we used survey-weighted generalized estimating equations with a Poisson distribution to estimate associations with functional limitation, adjusting for age, sex, and survey wave. Cohort-specific risk ratios (RRs) were then combined using random-effects meta-analysis. Results Across all cohorts, the proportion reporting at least one ACE ranged from 32.6% in ELSA to 66.9% in CHARLS. Compared with those reporting no ACE, the RRs (95% CI) for functional limitations were 1.17 (1.09–1.26) for one ACE, 1.29 (1.17–1.43) for two ACEs, and 1.39 (1.20–1.61) for three or more ACEs. Among the four ACE types, physical abuse (RR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.08–1.29), economic adversity (RR, 1.16; 95% CI, 1.10–1.23), and emotional neglect (RR, 1.11; 95% CI, 1.02–1.20) were each associated with higher functional limitation. Increased risks were observed across all six domains, with the strongest associations for mental disorders among individuals with three or more ACEs (RR, 1.82). The findings were consistent across cohorts and robust in sensitivity analyses. Conclusions ACEs were consistently associated with a greater risk of experiencing multiple functional limitations in later life, emphasizing the importance of mitigating childhood adversity to promote healthier ageing.

Background The global population is aging at an unprecedented rate, presenting significant challenges to public health systems worldwide [ 1 ]. Healthy aging prioritizes maintaining functional independence and quality of life, rather than merely extending lifespan. Multiple functional limitation, characterized by a decline in sensory, physical, cognitive, and psychological function, poses a major obstacle to this goal and places a significant burden on individuals, families, and society [ 2 ]. The life-course perspective suggests that health trajectories in adulthood and later life are influenced by experiences in early life. One such critical early-life factor is Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs). ACEs encompass potentially traumatic events that occur before the age of 18, such as physical abuse, emotional neglect, and household dysfunction [ 3 ]. These experiences are recognized as key stressors associated with long-term health outcomes. Previous studies have consistently demonstrated an association between ACEs and a range of adverse health outcomes in adulthood and later life. Individuals who have experienced multiple ACEs are at a significantly higher risk of developing depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders, as well as cardiovascular disease, dementia, and multiple chronic conditions [ 4 – 8 ]. Beyond these clinical conditions, ACEs have also been linked to impairments in specific functional domains, including poorer cognitive function, greater difficulty with activities of daily living and mobility, and accelerated decline in hearing, vision, and dual sensory function [ 9 – 12 ]. These associations may arise through neuroendocrine and inflammatory dysregulation, accelerated biological ageing, and adverse socioeconomic pathways that contribute to decline across multiple domains jointly. Despite the growing body of evidence on ACEs and specific health issues, there are still several research gaps. First, most studies have examined the association between ACEs and individual diseases or health dimensions, but the comprehensive impact of ACEs on overall functional status, particularly multiple functional limitation that better reflects general health, remains poorly understood [ 9 , 13 , 14 ]. Second, existing evidence predominantly derives from single-country or culturally specific contexts [ 10 , 15 ]. Large-scale, cross-national comparative evidence is lacking to determine whether findings can be generalized across populations with diverse socioeconomic backgrounds. Third, while some research has explored the effects of cumulative ACEs effects, the relative and independent contributions of different types of adversity to later-life functional limitation requires further examination [ 16 , 17 ]. To address these limitations, we investigated the prospective association between ACEs (both cumulative number and specific types) and multiple functional limitation (including hearing, vision, physical activity, mobility, mental health, and cognition) in a comprehensive study using data from 78,417 adults aged 50 years and older in the United States, England, Europe, and China. We hypothesize that there is a dose-response association between the cumulative number of ACEs and functional limitation in later life. Furthermore, we hypothesize that each sub-type of ACE (physical abuse, emotional neglect, adverse family environment, and economic adversity) is also associated with an increased risk of functional limitation in each domain. These studies recruited nationally representative samples of middle-aged and older adults using stratified multistage probability sampling and conducted follow-up surveys every two or three years according to comparable study protocols (Additional file: Additional Methods ). Participants were followed longitudinally across survey waves, and refreshment samples of adults were periodically introduced to compensate for attrition due to mortality and loss to follow-up. Individuals were excluded if they (1) had no information on ACEs; (2) were aged below 50 years across all waves; (3) had more than 20% missing values across all covariates and outcomes in each wave, as imputation may be less reliable when a large proportion of information required for modeling is missing; [ 26 ] (4) without sample weights information. As no participants from the Netherlands or Ireland remained after applying these criteria, both countries were excluded from the SHARE analytic sample. Furthermore, survey waves were excluded if any outcome variable was missing for > 50% of participants, as extensive imputation of outcome variables in waves with limited observed information may reduce the reliability of the estimates. ### Adverse childhood experiences, social isolation and loneliness across adulthood: A nationwide cross-sectional study of Chinese adults
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Affect Disord | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fan S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

BACKGROUND: Loneliness and social isolation are distinct risk factors for depression and other affective disorders but are often conflated. Whether adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) relate differently to the two, and whether this varies across adulthood, is unclear.

METHODS: We analysed 24,219 adults from the nationwide 2024 Psychology and Behaviour Investigation of Chinese Residents. Seven ACE domains were assessed with items adapted from the WHO ACE-IQ. Social isolation (three-item structural index, score ≥ 2) and loneliness (two-item functional index, score 2) were defined using indices adapted from the UK Biobank. Adjusted prevalence ratios (PRs) were estimated by modified Poisson regression with city-clustered standard errors; a stacked model compared the two outcomes.

RESULTS: Social isolation (30.0%) and loneliness (11.0%) overlapped weakly (kappa 0.17). Each additional ACE was associated with higher prevalence of loneliness (PR 1.24, 95% CI 1.20-1.28) and, more weakly, isolation (PR 1.04, 1.01-1.08; ratio of PRs 1.19, 1.17-1.22). All seven domains were associated with loneliness; only emotional abuse, witnessed violence and, marginally, physical abuse were associated with isolation; sexual abuse, household substance use, household mental illness and parental incarceration were not. The association of ≥4 ACEs with loneliness strengthened with age (P for interaction <0.001; PR 2.71, 1.98-3.72, at ≥60 years).

LIMITATIONS: The cross-sectional design and retrospectively self-reported ACEs preclude causal inference.

CONCLUSIONS: ACEs were more strongly associated with loneliness than with social isolation, particularly in later life. Among adults with ACE histories, asking about loneliness may capture disconnection that structural indicators such as living arrangements or contact frequency miss.

### Adverse childhood experiences and oral health outcomes among adults in the United States: 2022 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Am Dent Assoc | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vu GT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

BACKGROUND: Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) can have lasting effects on oral health. The authors examined the relationships between ACEs and dental outcomes among adults in the United States.

METHODS: The authors used the 2022 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System data (with an overall response rate of 45.0%). The study included 73,537 adults from 12 states. The authors used SAS Release 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc) to conduct multiple logistic regression analyses to assess the association of ACEs with tooth loss and past-year dental visits, and they examined the interactions between ACEs and covariates (age, sex, race, education, income, cigarette smoking status, general health, diabetes, health insurance).

RESULTS: Less than one-half (48.5%) of the study population was male; 62.5% were White, 38.0% had no college education, and 51.5% reported at least 1 ACE. Those with ACEs had higher odds of missing 6 or more teeth than those without ACEs after adjusting for covariates. The adjusted odds ratios of participants with 1, 2 through 3, and 4 or more ACEs were 1.22 (95% CI, 1.06 to 1.40), 1.20 (95% CI, 1.04 to 1.38), and 1.45 (95% CI, 1.26 to 1.68), respectively, compared with those without ACEs. Adults with 4 or more ACEs were less likely to have had a past-year dental visit than those without ACEs (adjusted odds ratio, 0.74 [95% CI, 0.67 to 0.82]).

CONCLUSIONS: Having 1 or more ACEs was significantly associated with a higher risk of tooth loss, and having 4 or more ACEs was significantly associated with a lower likelihood of a past-year dental visit among adults in the United States.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS: Dental care providers should be encouraged to incorporate trauma-informed care into their practice through patient-centered communication, shared decision making, and strategies that promote safety and trust for patients with ACEs.

### Adverse and Positive Childhood Experiences among US Bereaved Youth (2022-2024)
**כתב עת:** 📄 Acad Pediatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Woodward KP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

OBJECTIVES: To describe associations of adverse and positive childhood experiences (ACEs and PCEs) and social demographic characteristics with exposure to parent or guardian death among a nationally representative sample of US youth (6-17 years).

METHODS: We pooled cross-sectional data from the 2022-2024 National Survey of Children's Health (N=101,617). We estimated stepwise Poisson and Negative Binomial regression models testing the association of parent or guardian death with ACEs and PCEs accumulation in both the full sample and racial and ethnic stratified subsamples.

RESULTS: Approximately 4% of youth experienced parent or guardian death and were disproportionately non-Hispanic Black, Native American/Other Race, or from low-income households. Bereaved youth were more likely to be exposed to all ACEs and less likely to be exposed to the majority of PCEs relative to their non-bereaved counterparts. Youth exposed to parent or guardian death exhibited significantly more ACEs (β = 0.40, IRR = 1.49, SE = 0.03), yet a similar number of PCEs (β = -0.02, IRR = 0.98, SE = 0.01), relative to their non-bereaved peers. The association between parent and guardian death and ACEs was no longer significant among non-Hispanic Black (β = 0.15, IRR = 1.16, SE = 0.08) and Native American/Other Race (β = 0.12, IRR = 1.12, SE = 0.11) youth relative to other racial and ethnic groups.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings underscore the elevated and unequal exposure of adversity among youth exposed to parent and guardian death. Implications for targeted pediatric supports that address heightened adversities and inequities within this population are discussed.
