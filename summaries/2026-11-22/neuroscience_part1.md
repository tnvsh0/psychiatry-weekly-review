### Microglia mediate motor memory decay through activity-dependent phagocytosis of myelin sheaths
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hu Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

Experience-dependent changes in myelination are important for motor learning, with newly generated oligodendrocytes contributing to the formation of new myelin. However, motor memories decay over time and the underlying mechanisms remain largely unexplored. Here we show that motor memory decay is linked to changes in memory-associated myelin sheaths resulting from microglial phagocytosis in mouse brain. Mechanistically, reduced activity of motor memory engram cells lowers oligodendrocyte expression of the 'don't eat me' signal from Cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47), facilitating microglial phagocytosis and driving myelin loss. Knockout of CD47 from motor memory-associated oligodendrocytes increased microglial phagocytosis of myelin sheaths and accelerated the decay of motor memory, whereas overexpression of CD47 showed opposite results. Moreover, microglia phagocytose myelin sheaths and synapses independently. Our findings reveal that microglial phagocytosis of motor memory-associated myelin sheaths leads to the decay of motor memory. Since various types of memory require myelination in the brain, this mechanism may contribute to the decay of other memory types.

### Noncoding functions of FMR1 transcript in riboregulation of human neurodevelopment
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sandoval SO et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

SUMMARY The non-coding functions of protein-coding messenger RNAs (mRNAs) are largely ignored. Here, we demonstrate an unexpected non-coding role of FMR1 mRNA in human neurodevelopment. A loss of FMR1 and its protein product, fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein (FMRP) leads to neurodevelopmental disorder fragile X syndrome (FXS). Neurons derived from FXS patients display hyperexcitability and immature morphology, whereas FMRP knockout (FMRP-KO) neurons that retain FMR1 transcript expression do not. Multiomics analyses reveal key metabolic differences between FXS and FMRP-KO neurons. While both FXS and FMRP-KO neurons exhibit reduced mitochondrial respiration, only FXS neurons exhibit elevated glycolysis. We further discover that the FMR1 transcript riboregulates the levels of glycolytic enzyme LDHA by inhibiting binding of LDHA mRNA to insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding protein 2 (IGF2BP2). Finally, FMR1 transcript, independent of FMRP, rescues glycolytic and cellular deficits of FXS neurons. Together, these results suggest that FMR1 is a dual-function RNA in human neurodevelopment.

INTRODUCTION The central dogma of molecular biology places messenger RNAs (mRNAs) as the intermediate step of directional information flow from genes to proteins 1 . The discovery of dual-function RNAs that have both protein coding and non-coding capabilities challenges this doctrine. First discovered in bacteria and plants, then in animals, and recently in human cancer cells, dual-function RNAs have been shown to play important roles in maintaining developmental processes and in regulating biological functions such as RNA splicing, RNA stability, protein synthesis, epigenetic regulation, and gene silencing 2 – 5 . However, most dual-function RNAs discovered to date are non-coding RNAs that produce short peptides with biological functions 2 , 3 . Limited studies have demonstrated non-coding functions of canonical mRNAs during normal cellular processes 6 , 7 or cancer pathogenesis 8 , 9 . Neurons are morphologically and functionally complex and must adapt to external stimuli for the survival of the organism. As such, neurons in the mammalian brain have the highest number and diversity and the most complex regulatory mechanisms of RNAs 10 . Yet few, if any, dual-function mRNAs have been studied in neurons. It has been shown that while Tp53inp2 protein is expressed in skeletal muscle, Tp53inp2 mRNA, without its protein product regulates axonal growth in rat peripheral sympathetic neurons 6 . Therefore, Tp53inp2 is either a protein coding or a non-coding RNA depending on cell types. However, whether mRNA can have both coding and non-coding roles in the same neurons and whether dual-function mRNAs regulate neurodevelopment, especially in humans, remain largely unexplored. Fragile X syndrome (FXS), the most common form of inherited intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorder, affecting about 1 in 4000 males and 1 in 7000 females, is typically caused by full mutational CGG expansion (>200 CGG repeats) in the 5’ untranslated region (UTR) leading to epigenetic silencing of the fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein 1 gene ( FMR1 ) and loss of its protein product, fragile X messenger ribonucleoprotein (FMRP) 11 – 13 . FXS research has been focusing on FMRP, an RNA binding protein important for neurons through regulation of synaptic protein translation, mRNA stability, RNA transport, and ion channel activities 13 . We have found that FMRP-deficient mouse and human neurons exhibit mitochondrial fragmentation, metabolic deficits, morphological impairment, and altered neuronal activities 14 , 15 . In contrast to FXS, fragile X-associated tremor/ataxia syndrome (FXTAS) and fragile X-associated primary ovarian insufficiency (FXPOI) are caused by the premutation CGG expansion (55–200 CGG repeats) of the FMR1 gene, leading to increased transcription of CGG-repeat containing FMR1 mRNA 16 . It has been shown that premutation CGG- FMR1 mRNA is able to sequester RNA binding proteins and participate in Repeat Associated Non-AUG (RAN) translation 17 – 20 . Because FXTAS and FXPOI patients retain FMRP protein and do not exhibit intellectual disability, the toxic effects of CGG- FMR1 mRNA has been the focus for pathogenic mechanisms. However, whether FMR1 mRNA, without pathogenic CGG expansion, has regulatory roles in normal cellular functions and neurodevelopment remains unknown. Here, we tested the hypothesis that FMR1 is a dual-function mRNA and FMR1 transcript riboregulates metabolic pathways in human neurodevelopment. We show that FMRP knockout (FMRP-KO) neurons retaining FMR1 mRNA expression do not exhibit the same immature morphology and hyperexcitability deficits found in FXS neurons. Although both FMRP-KO and FXS neurons have reduced mitochondrial function, only FXS neurons have elevated glycolysis. Integrative transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic analyses unveiled significant differences between FXS and FMRP-KO neurons. We discovered that mRNA and protein levels of lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA), a key glycolytic enzyme, are upregulated in FXS, but not in FMRP-KO, neurons. The FMR1 transcript binds RNA-binding protein insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA binding protein 2 (IGF2BP2) and inhibits IGF2BP2 binding to LDHA mRNA. The absence of FMR1 transcript in FXS neurons leads to increased binding of LDHA mRNA by IGF2BP2, resulting in elevated LDHA mRNA stability. Finally, exogenous FMR1 transcript alone reduces glycolysis and partially rescues morphological and electrophysiological deficits of FXS neurons. Our data reveals a non-coding function for FMR1 in neurodevelopment through riboregulation of another RNA binding protein and FMR1 transcript alone is sufficient to rescue certain neuronal deficits in FXS. ### A cell-type-resolved proteomic atlas reveals intra- and intercellular signaling in Alzheimer's disease
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhang X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

Summary Alzheimer’s disease (AD) arises from pathological interactions among diverse brain cell types, but cell-specific proteomic changes remain underexplored. Here, we present deep proteomic profiling of sorted or proximity-labeled brain cells from AD mouse models (5xFAD and App NL-G-F ) at multiple ages, quantifying 13,411 proteins in microglia, astrocytes, oligodendrocyte precursor cells, and neurons, with additional spatial information. We identified 3,028 differentially abundant proteins, most of which were absent in bulk proteomic dataset and constructed cell type-specific networks to define functional modules and hub proteins. Comparison with transcriptomic data revealed that ~30% of proteomic changes are RNA-discordant. Further analyses uncovered cross-cell type signaling proteins conserved in human AD brains, such as pleiotrophin (Ptn), which is transcriptionally enriched in astrocytes but accumulates in microglia. Importantly, recombinant PTN activates induced microglia-like (iMG) human cells. Thus, these findings provide a comprehensive cell type-resolved proteomic atlas of AD models, highlighting novel intra- and intercellular signaling events.

Introduction Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the leading cause of dementia worldwide 1 , but the molecular mechanisms underlying its cellular heterogeneity are incompletely understood. Single-cell RNA sequencing has substantially advanced AD research by uncovering disease-associated cell subtypes and cellular mechanisms 2 – 6 . RNA-level changes do not capture the full extent of protein-level alterations, particularly in brain tissue composed of postmitotic neurons 7 – 10 . Mass spectrometry (MS) has enabled extensive bulk proteome profiling of AD brain tissue, revealing large-scale protein alterations that appear to be enriched in distinct brain cell types 11 – 13 . However, direct cell type-specific proteomic studies in Alzheimer’s disease remain limited. Proteomic profiling of distinct brain cell types is challenging due to difficulties in isolating specific cell types and limited protein yield from small populations 12 , 14 . To overcome these challenges, several strategies have been developed, including laser capture microdissection (LCM) 15 – 17 , fluorescence- or magnetic-activated cell sorting (FACS or MACS) 18 , translational labeling methods such as BONCAT 19 , and proximity labeling approaches such as TurboID 20 or antibody-mediated biotinylation 21 to capture cell type-specific proteomes. For instance, an early proteomic study employing MACS-MS achieved high proteome coverage of different brain cell types in mouse brain 18 . Compared with MACS, FACS provides higher purity and better resolution, particularly for microglial populations 14 . Alternatively, TurboID-based biotin labeling enabled in vivo neuronal proteome profiling through adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated expression 22 or by Cre-lox transgenic mice 23 , whereas antibody-mediated biotinylation also allowed in situ cell type-specific proteome analysis in mouse brain 24 . The mouse brain is composed of approximately 46% neurons, 20% oligodendrocytes and oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPC), 15% vascular cells, 12% astrocytes, 4% immune cells (mainly microglia), and 3% other cell types 25 . Despite their low abundance, microglia are particularly enriched for the expression of AD risk genes 26 . Distinct microglial subtypes are proposed by markers such as CD63 and CD74, reflecting functional reprogramming relevant to pathogenesis 27 , 28 . But microglial subtype-specific proteomic alterations are largely masked in current bulk tissue analyses. Advancing single-cell-type proteomics is critical for elucidating the molecular pathways that govern cell-specific responses in Alzheimer’s disease. In this study, we generated a cell type-resolved proteomic atlas of two commonly used AD mouse models. Using FACS-sorted microglia (three subtypes), astrocytes, and OPC, along with AAV-based neuronal labeling, we profiled 13,411 proteins at multiple ages in 5xFAD and App NL-G-F mice. We also compared our proteomics data with the results from single cell RNA-sequencing of 5xFAD brain tissue. Hundreds of differentially abundant proteins (DAPs) per cell type were identified, revealing cell type-specific pathways, RNA-protein discordance, and conserved intercellular signaling between mouse and human datasets. Pleiotrophin (Ptn) was identified as a candidate mediator of astrocyte-microglia communication. Finally, the cell type-resolved findings were further supported by LCM-based proteomics and spatial transcriptomic analyses, providing additional spatial context for disease-associated proteomic changes. This atlas highlights AD-related cellular heterogeneity and cell-cell interactions and is available at https://penglab.shinyapps.io/pannda . ### Motor system inefficiency as an activity-dependent amplifier of fatigue in multiple sclerosis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kavanagh JJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

Multiple sclerosis (MS) fatigue is clinically obvious but mechanistically elusive. It can dominate daily life despite modest disability, stable structural MRI findings, or preserved strength on examination. This Update reviews evidence that an important component of MS fatigue may arise from increased motor-control cost and incomplete recovery after activity. Voluntary muscle activation studies show that some people with MS have incomplete central access to available force, particularly after exertion. Functional imaging studies indicate that apparently preserved movement often depends on expanded recruitment of premotor, cerebellar, striatal, prefrontal, and cingulo-insular systems involved in motor compensation, effort allocation, and body-state monitoring. Metabolic and pathological studies further indicate energetic vulnerability in demyelinated and chronically injured networks, providing biological plausibility for impaired restoration after repeated signalling demands. These findings support a systems framework in which expanded motor-control recruitment may be compensatory when it preserves performance and remains flexible and recoverable, but may amplify MS fatigue when ordinary activity begins from incompletely restored physiological reserve. We outline testable predictions for identifying a motor-cost fatigue phenotype and for designing repeated-measures studies of activity-related fatigue in MS.

### Heterogeneous Encoding Patterns of Dynamic Audiovisual Social Stimuli in Autism Spectrum Disorder (Heterogeneous Encoding Patterns in Autism)
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Van De Water AL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 15

The neural basis of social difficulties in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) remains uncertain. Prior studies typically used static stimuli or reduced complex stimuli to only a few dimensions, obscuring real-world complexity. Here, we used videos with social content to engage brain networks and trained encoding models to predict brain activity from high-dimensional audio, visual, and more abstract socially relevant features (e.g., valence). We hypothesized that brain activity in autistic adults would be less accurately predicted and more heterogeneous within and across individuals than in neurotypical (NT) participants, particularly for socially relevant features. Participants (48 ASD [13 female], 90 NT [25 female]) viewed six videos (5-20 minutes). Brain activity was measured using functional magnetic resonance imaging. Cross-validated encoding models were assessed on prediction accuracy, variance, and pattern similarity. Brain activity was predicted significantly above chance across brain networks and videos with striking consistency between groups. Auditory and language networks were most accurately predicted. Within-participant predictions were equally variable between groups; however, autistic participants exhibited lower within-group encoding pattern similarity than NT participants and greater across-group than within-group similarity. Analyses using only socially relevant features showed the largest across-group differences in encoding pattern similarity. The novel application of encoding models to multiple videos in ASD suggests greater heterogeneity in neural representations of social information in ASD. Although group-averaged encoding accuracies were comparable across groups, ASD participants showed more heterogenous regional encoding patterns. These idiosyncratic patterns underscore the need to incorporate individual variation in brain representations when developing translational neuroimaging approaches in autism.
