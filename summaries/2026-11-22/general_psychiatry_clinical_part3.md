### Intolerance of Uncertainty Among US and Canadian Young Adults Attending College 1992-2023: A Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** van Baar JM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

This meta-analysis investigates the evolution of intolerance of uncertainty (IU), a transdiagnostic risk factor for anxiety, in US and Canadian young adults attending college between 1992 and 2023.

Key Points Question How did intolerance of uncertainty, a transdiagnostic risk factor for anxiety, evolve in US and Canadian young adults attending college between 1992 and 2023? Findings In this meta-analysis including 137 studies and 37 298 participants, average intolerance of uncertainty grew between 1992 and 2023. The annual rate of change was 3-fold higher between 2012 and 2023 than between 1992 and 2011. Meaning Study results suggest that intervening on intolerance of uncertainty may curb the growth of anxiety prevalence in young adults.

Importance Rising anxiety prevalence among young adults may relate to intolerance of uncertainty (IU), difficulty enduring the perceived absence of important information. A prior meta-analysis found increasing long-form Intolerance of Uncertainty Scale (IUS) scores among US and Canadian undergraduates from 1992 to 2014, but recent evidence is lacking. Objective To estimate IU trends among US and Canadian young adults attending college 1992-2023 by extending the prior meta-analysis and including the short-form IUS. Data Sources Web of Science was used to track English-language articles from Canada or the US citing the source articles for the long-form or short-form IUS across 13 databases. Study Selection Eligible studies were peer-reviewed empirical articles with nonclinical undergraduate samples from the US or Canada that reported sample size and mean long-form or short-form IUS. Data Extraction and Synthesis Following MOOSE guidelines, 2 investigators extracted study characteristics and mean IU, resolving discrepancies jointly. Studies were merged with the prior meta-analysis and analyzed using fixed-effect meta-regression. To assess IU across scale forms, mean IU was converted into SD units and regressed onto data collection year, weighted by sample size, and adjusted for mean age and sex or gender distribution. Study data were analyzed from September 2025 to July 2026. Main Outcome and Measure Mean IU. The hypothesis was preregistered before data collection. Results Of 799 abstracts screened and 294 full texts reviewed independently by 2 investigators, 92 articles (11%) met selection criteria. After merger with prior meta-analysis and removal of duplicates, this meta-analysis included 137 studies (62 long-form IUS and 75 short-form IUS) comprising 37 298 participants (unweighted median [IQR] of sample mean age, 20.1 [19.4-21.3] years; 69.5% female [unweighted mean of sample sex/gender distribution]). Mean long-form IU increased from 1992 to 2023 by 0.66 points annually (95% CI, 0.47-0.84 points) on a scale from 27 to 135. Mean short-form IU increased from 2005 (first sample) to 2023 by 0.60 points annually (95% CI, 0.43-0.76 points) on a scale from 12 to 60. In the combined sample, mean IU grew by 0.04 SD per year from 1992 to 2023 (95% CI, 0.03-0.05 SD per year), with 3-fold faster growth during 2012-2023 (0.06 SD per year; 95% CI, 0.04-0.08 SD per year) than during 1992-2011 (0.02 SD per year; 95% CI, 0-0.04 SD per year). Conclusions and Relevance Results of this meta-analysis suggest that IU increased among US and Canadian young adults attending college 1992-2023, with a steeper increase after 2012. This supports further investigating the role of uncertainty in the epidemiology of anxiety.

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists in Psychotic Disorders: Mechanistic Convergence and Therapeutic Implications for Brain and Metabolic Health
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Le TDV et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 18

OBJECTIVE: The objective of this study was to review and synthesize the mechanistic rationale and emerging clinical evidence for the use of glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) in psychotic disorders.

METHODS: The authors conducted a systematic review of the clinical literature relevant to GLP-1RA use in people with psychotic disorders. Searches of PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, APA PsycInfo, and Cochrane CENTRAL were conducted from database inception through March 7, 2026, and were supplemented by searches of ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform and reference-list screening. The findings were integrated into a narrative synthesis of preclinical and translational evidence relevant to novel metabolic mechanisms in the pathophysiology of psychotic disorders.

RESULTS: Preclinical and early human mechanistic studies suggest that GLP-1RAs may influence several pathways relevant to psychotic disorders, including systemic and central glucose homeostasis, gut-brain signaling, inflammatory processes, redox alterations, mitochondrial function, and reward-related signaling. Clinically, the clearest evidence of benefit is for antipsychotic-associated weight gain and cardiometabolic risk, with most randomized clinical trials reporting meaningful improvements in body weight and metabolic outcomes. Relevant to comorbidities in psychotic disorders, the evidence for GLP-1RA effects on alcohol and nicotine use is suggestive but inconclusive. Available studies have not yet demonstrated consistent improvements in psychotic symptoms, cognition, or functioning. Limitations to the clinical evidence include small samples; short follow-up; heterogeneity in agents, dosing, and endpoints; and minimal incorporation of mechanistic endpoints.

CONCLUSIONS: GLP-1RAs represent a clinically and mechanistically grounded strategy to address the substantial cardiometabolic burden in psychotic disorders, with the potential for reduced substance use and other neuropsychiatric benefits.

### Eight-Week Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled 2-Site Trial Assessing the Efficacy and Safety of Mixed Amphetamine Salt as an Adjunctive Therapy for Bipolar Depression
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Clin Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Frye MA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

Objective:  This study examined the efficacy and safety of adjunctive mixed  d -amphetamine/ l -amphetamine salt (MAS; Mydayis) in adults with  DSM-IV-TR -confirmed bipolar I or II depression.  Methods:  In this 8-week, randomized, placebo-controlled 2-site study of adjunctive MAS, the primary outcome was baseline-to-end point change in the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS). Secondary outcomes included, in part, treatment response and remission (≥50% MADRS reduction, MADRS <10) and illness improvement as measured by the Clinical Global Impression for Bipolar Disorder (CGI-BP). The primary safety assessment was the Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS). The 8-week controlled trial was followed by an 8-week open-label extension.  Results:  Thirty-two (14 with bipolar I disorder, 18 with bipolar II disorder; 10 male, 22 female; mean age=38.2 y) patients enrolled in the study. Baseline symptoms (MADRS = 27.0±6.3, Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology-Clinician-Rated = 15.3±3.2, Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology-Self-Report = 14.8±4.7, and YMRS = 4.0) were consistent with moderate depression and absence of hypomania. Baseline demographics showed no significant group differences. In age-adjusted analyses, participants receiving adjunctive MAS (n = 15) showed significantly greater improvement on MADRS compared to placebo (n = 17, mean percent reduction: 62.3% vs 15.4%;  P = .002), with higher rates of remission (63.3% vs 8.5%;  P = .002) and response (79.5% vs 7.3%;  P <. 001). CGI-BP ratings of "much improved" or "very much improved" were higher with MAS (67.0% vs 10.2%;  P = .001). No significant differences were observed in YMRS (last observation 2.9 vs 3.3;  P = .72). The open-label extension (n = 24) showed maintenance of response, with 1 participant experiencing a switch to mania and 2 participants with drug misuse.  Conclusions:  Adjunctive MAS was associated with effective reduction in depression symptoms with broad safety profile for most participants. Secondary analyses are encouraged to examine differential response rates by mood stabilizer and item analyses of attention and eating behavior.  Trial Registration:  ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04235686.

### Mitochondrial Bioenergetics in Schizophrenia: a Systematic Review, Meta-analysis, and GRADE-Based Certainty Assessment
**כתב עת:** 📄 Schizophr Bull | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Tran HA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

BACKGROUND AND HYPOTHESIS: Mitochondrial dysfunction has been linked to schizophrenia, but findings remain inconsistent. No previous systematic review has quantitatively synthesized mitochondrial function and biomarker outcomes while also evaluating risk of bias and certainty of the evidence in schizophrenia.

STUDY DESIGN: We conducted a PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis of functional outcomes and mitochondrial biomarkers in schizophrenia, including human studies and animal models published through December 2025. Meta-analyses were performed separately by experimental system. Risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-E/ROBINS-I and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, and certainty of evidence was evaluated using GRADE.

STUDY RESULTS: Forty-seven studies met the inclusion criteria. Human studies showed reduced ATP-related respiratory capacity. Mitochondrial Complex activity was reduced in schizophrenia for Complexes III and IV, but not for Complex II, which lacks mitochondrial DNA-encoded subunits. Human meta-analyses showed high heterogeneity (I2 > 90%), effect estimates varied with study quality, and pooled estimates were unstable. Animal studies showed similar trends but were imprecise. Overall, the certainty of the evidence was very low due to the risks of bias, inconsistency, imprecision, and indirectness.

CONCLUSIONS: The available evidence supports altered mitochondrial function in schizophrenia, particularly impaired respiratory capacity. However, substantial methodological heterogeneity, risk of bias, and indirectness limit confidence in the magnitude and biomarker specificity of these findings, highlighting the need for larger, standardized, and independently replicated studies.
