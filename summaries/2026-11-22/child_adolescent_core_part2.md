### Prenatal alcohol exposure and adolescent sleep in clinical pathways: a commentary on Devine et al
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lin CC, Wei LC | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

Devine et al. provide timely evidence that parent-reported adolescent sleep disturbances are associated with prenatal alcohol exposure (PAE) in the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development cohort, even when most exposure was low to moderate. This commentary argues that the central contribution of the study is not only the replication of a sleep signal after PAE, but the identification of a clinically recognisable phenotype: sleep-wake transition problems, excessive somnolence, and possible sleep breathing symptoms. We highlight three implications: sleep should be treated as a developmental mediator and modifiable target, future studies should triangulate retrospective PAE and parent-reported sleep with objective measures, and prevention messages about alcohol in pregnancy should be paired with non-stigmatising family support. Building clinical pathways from screening to intervention may transform small epidemiologic effects into meaningful benefits for affected adolescents.

### Childhood competence, adverse childhood experiences, and sociocultural context: Understanding resilience processes from childhood to young adulthood
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Gissandaner TD et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 17

BACKGROUND: Childhood competencies in mental health, social, and academic domains could support (or promote) low levels of stress later in life. These competencies could also protect those highly exposed to ACEs from experiencing elevated perceived stress (PS).

METHODS: Participants (N = 2,004, ages 5-17 at Waves 1-3; ages 15-29 at Wave 4) were from the Boricua Youth Study, a sample of Puerto Rican children recruited from the South Bronx, New York, and the metropolitan area in San Juan and Caguas, Puerto Rico. The current study examined longitudinal patterns of childhood competence (stable, unstable, not reaching) across three domains (mental health, social adjustment, and academic achievement) in relation to PS in young adulthood. We also investigated the role of childhood competencies as a protective factor, buffering the association between ACEs and PS. We explored whether associations varied across two sociocultural contexts with varying levels of ACEs (South Bronx vs. Puerto Rico).

RESULTS: Childhood mental health competence, in relation to never reaching competence, was the only type of competence that promoted (b = -1.72, p < .001) lower PS in young adulthood. Childhood mental health competence also modified the association between ACEs and PS among those recruited in the South Bronx (F (2, 776) = 4.45, p = .01), such that among those with stable childhood mental health competence, higher ACEs were associated with more PS (b = 0.56, SE = 0.22, p = .01) while the same was not observed in the other two trajectories (Unstable: b = 0.44, SE = 0.23, p = .053; Not reaching competence: b = -0.59, SE = 0.33, p = .08).

CONCLUSIONS: Experiencing competence in mental health during childhood may alleviate PS in young adulthood. For those growing up in a sociocultural context where adversities are frequent experiences and who have stable levels of mental health competence, ACEs were related to high PS in young adulthood.

### Treatment patterns among US children and adolescents with recorded depressive disorders
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang F et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 20

OBJECTIVE: To characterize depression-linked treatment, annual trends, and factors associated with receipt of both psychotherapy and antidepressants among youths in the United States.

METHODS: This repeated cross-sectional study used 2016-2023 Medical Expenditure Panel Survey data. The population included children and adolescents aged 6-17 years with depressive disorders. We linked condition records to psychotherapy visits and antidepressant fills, estimated survey-weighted treatment percentages, and used survey-weighted logistic regression to examine factors associated with receipt of both treatments during the same survey year.

RESULTS: The analysis included 865 person-year observations, with adolescents comprising 90.3% of the weighted sample. Any treatment was recorded for 82.7% (95% confidence interval [CI], 79.6-85.7%), psychotherapy for 62.7%, and antidepressant treatment for 50.1%. Psychotherapy alone accounted for 32.5%, both treatments for 30.2%, and antidepressants alone for 20.0%. Neither treatment was recorded for 17.3%. The percentage receiving any treatment increased from 58.9% in 2016 to 89.8% in 2023 (P for trend < 0.001). Compared with non-Hispanic White youths, Hispanic and non-Hispanic Black youths had lower adjusted odds of receiving both modalities (0.44 [95% CI, 0.26-0.76] and 0.35 [95% CI, 0.13-0.89], respectively). Comorbid anxiety disorder and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder were associated with higher adjusted odds (1.54 [95% CI, 1.04-2.29] and 1.60 [95% CI, 1.07-2.41], respectively).

CONCLUSIONS: More than one in six participants had neither depression-linked treatment recorded, and receipt of both treatments differed by race and ethnicity. Small subgroup sizes limited precision, particularly for younger children. The linked records measured treatment receipt without establishing clinical indication or adequacy.

### Neurocognitive profiles structure heterogeneous treatment response to bumetanide in neurodevelopmental disorders: secondary analysis of a multiple baseline single-case experimental design
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Geertjens L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

We performed a secondary analysis of the Bumetanide for Developmental Disorders (BUDDI;EUDRA-CT-2020-002196-35) trial to examine whether baseline neurocognitive profiles are associated with differential behavioral and cognitive treatment response. Participants (7-19 years) with atypical sensory reactivity and ASD, ADHD, epilepsy, or TSC diagnoses received 6-months of bumetanide treatment (max 3.0 mg/day) within a multiple-baseline SCED. Neurocognitive functioning was assessed across multiple domains and used to derive subgroups via cluster analysis. Behavioral outcomes were captured using a parent-reported PROMset and standardized questionnaires. Linear mixed models and subgroup comparisons were used to evaluate change over time. Of 119 participants enrolled, 105 completed baseline and 85 follow-up assessments At the group level, memory improved after six months (B = 0.65, 95%CI = 0.45to0.85, pFDR < .001, d = 1.07), while processing speed decreased (B = - 0.25, 95%CI = - 0.42to - 0.07, pFDR < .001, d = - 0.49). Cluster analysis identified four neurocognitive subgroups with distinct baseline profiles and differential treatment responses: individuals with weaker functioning showed the largest memory improvements, whereas those with preserved functioning showed greater behavioral improvement alongside declines in processing speed and visuomotor integration. Across subgroups, neurocognitive and behavioral changes did not align, and correlations were small (r < 0.30). In this study, neurocognitive profiling based on a standardized test battery was associated with distinct patterns of change during treatment. The dissociation between neurocognitive and behavioral outcomes indicated domain-specific and non-linear treatment effects. These findings provide a basis for linking variability in real-world change to individual neurocognitive characteristics within a defined clinical context.Trial registration: EudraCT (2020-002196-35) registration date 24-04-2020. Protocol: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-022-04033-8.

### Analysis of the placebo response model and its influencing factors in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a model-based meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 16

PURPOSE: A robust placebo response continuously challenges the evaluation of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) therapies. This study utilized model-based meta-analysis (MBMA) to quantify the longitudinal trajectory of the placebo response, identify trial- and patient- level predictors, and compare response magnitudes between pediatric and adult populations to optimize future trial designs.

METHODS: A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science identified placebo-controlled randomized clinical trials (RCTs) for ADHD. Longitudinal changes in placebo groups were mathematically modeled based on the ADHD Rating Scale (ADHD-RS) and Clinical Global Impression-Severity (CGI-S). Covariate modeling and subgroup analyses were applied to evaluate the impact of demographic and trial design factors.

RESULTS: Data from 79 RCTs were analyzed. Placebo responses exhibited non-linear dynamics, plateauing at approximately 6 weeks for the ADHD-RS and 10 weeks for the CGI-S. Larger trial sample sizes significantly predicted increased ADHD-RS placebo scores. Placebo magnitudes were also more pronounced in recent, multicenter, three more trial arms, industry-sponsored trials, trials using a stimulant as the active comparator, as well as in cohorts with fewer male or White participants and more severe baseline disease. Crucially, The time-course trajectories and amplitudes of the placebo response were similar in pediatric and adult populations.

CONCLUSION: This study provides a quantitative description of the placebo response trajectory in ADHD trials, together with the trial-level factors that modify it. The concordance between pediatric and adult placebo trajectories is supportive. These findings offer parameters for baseline-adjusted sample size estimation and endpoint selection in future trials. PROSPERO registration CRD420251171116.
