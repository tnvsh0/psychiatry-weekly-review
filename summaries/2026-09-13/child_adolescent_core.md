### Early adversity, executive function, and the development of cognitive disengagement syndrome: a sex-specific mediating pathway
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yousefkhani MP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 12

BACKGROUND: Despite growing recognition of cognitive disengagement syndrome (CDS), the developmental mechanisms linking early adversity to CDS symptoms remain largely unknown. This study addresses this gap by examining whether executive function (EF) serves as a cognitive mechanism linking cumulative adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) to later CDS symptoms and whether this mediational chain differs by adolescent sex.

METHODS: Data were drawn from the prospective longitudinal Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study®. The final analytic sample included 4,678 adolescents (52.5% male) identifying primarily as White (72.5%), Hispanic/Latino (13.6%), and Black (13.1%). A moderated mediation model was tested using cumulative ACEs score (ages 9-11) as the predictor, EF deficits (ages 12-13) as the mediator, CDS symptoms (ages 13-14) as the outcome, and adolescent sex as moderator.

RESULTS: Results indicate cumulative ACEs predict later CDS symptoms above and beyond baseline levels. EF deficits partially mediated the ACEs-CDS link (accounting for 30% of the effect). Notably, adolescent sex moderated the EF-CDS pathway; while adversity impacted EF similarly across sexes, the association between EF deficits and CDS symptoms was significantly stronger for female adolescents.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings provide the first prospective evidence that early adversity accelerates CDS development via disruptions in executive functioning. The results support a developmental psychopathology model of CDS and identify EF as a potentially modifiable intervention target, particularly for female adolescents exposed to early adversity.

### Adolescent mental health outcomes following early regulatory problems
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kadiš M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 8

Early regulatory problems (RPs), including excessive crying, sleeping difficulties, and feeding issues in infancy, are associated with later mental health difficulties. However, existing studies used broad mental health outcomes and inconsistent outcome measures. They have also overlooked adolescence, which is a crucial and sensitive developmental period. Using harmonized RP classifications, measured from 6 to 36 months of age, and item-level harmonized mental health dimensions, we examined emotional, social, hyperactivity / inattention, and conduct problems at ages 10, 14, and 18 years in two population-based cohorts (ALSPAC, N = 3870; Generation R, N = 1798). Longitudinal mixed models showed RP-related differences across all mental health dimensions. Specifically, children with multiple (two or more at one timepoint) or multiple-persistent (two or more RPs present at three or more timepoints in the first 36 months) RPs had consistently higher emotional, social, attention, and conduct problems, compared with participants without RPs. These differences remained stable across adolescence, with no evidence of RP-by-assessment wave interactions and were comparable for both sexes. Our cross-cohort findings support early RPs as early indicators of later mental health vulnerability. They additionally show that these associations can be identified reliably using harmonized exposure and outcome constructs across cohorts. Screening for these behaviors in routine pediatric care offers a practical method to identify at-risk children and guide early preventive interventions.

### Risk and protective factors for psychological distress, anxiety, depression, and PTSD in unaccompanied minor migrants in Spain
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Franch-Roca M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

Unaccompanied minor migrants represent a rapidly growing and vulnerable population in Southern Europe, yet no comprehensive quantitative data have examined the multiple factors shaping their mental health outcomes in Spain. This study aimed to identify risk and protective factors linked to the migration process that are associated with psychological distress (K10), anxiety (GAD-7), depression (CIDI), and PTSD (PCL-5) among unaccompanied minor migrants in Spain. We examined 230 male participants (M age = 16.79, SD = 0.45). Data were collected through face-to-face, culturally adapted structured interviews and analysed using multivariable Tobit and logistic regression models. The analysis encompassed a broad range of factors. Mean scores were 4.75 (3.60) for psychological distress, 1.92 (2.01) for anxiety, and 3.51 (2.70) for PTSD, while 21.3% of participants reported depressive symptoms. Risk factors included being from Gambia, Pakistan, or other countries (compared with Morocco), associated with higher anxiety; additionally, being from other countries was linked with higher psychological distress; childhood abuse and perceived discrimination both associated with higher psychological distress and anxiety; longer duration of residence in Spain linked to higher psychological distress, anxiety and depression. Protective factors included higher resilience linked to lower psychological distress; and having migration expectations met, which was related to lower anxiety and PTSD. Neglect experiences were associated with less anxiety. Overall, these findings underscore the importance of contextual stressors and protective mechanisms in guiding tailored interventions for unaccompanied minor migrants.

### An intersectional analysis of the socio-structural factors associated with anxiety and mood disorder diagnoses in young people in Catalonia: a primary care cohort study (2008-2022)
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bennett M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 8

Mental health inequities among young people are often examined through single social dimensions, potentially obscuring heterogeneity across intersecting social positions. This study examined how anxiety and mood disorder diagnoses varied according to intersections of sex, socioeconomic status (SES), and nationality in Catalonia. Population-based cohort included 2,086,641 individuals aged 10-24 years (2008-2022) using anonymized primary care records. Sex-stratified regression models examined associations between SES-nationality intersections and incident anxiety and mood disorder diagnoses. Among young people with Spanish nationality, the likelihood of receiving anxiety and mood disorder diagnoses increased as SES decreased, with the highest risks among lower SES groups (e.g., anxiety among girls: HR = 1.31, 95% CI 1.29-1.33). This gradient was weaker among those with Latin American nationality and absent among the Other nationality group, who showed consistently lower diagnosis risks across SES strata (e.g., mood disorder HRs 0.54-0.83). Girls with Latin American nationality in middle- and high-SES groups had a higher likelihood of receiving a mood disorder diagnosis than the reference group (HR = 1.22, 95% CI 1.11-1.34; HR = 1.17, 95% CI 1.07-1.28, respectively). Diagnostic inequalities varied across intersections of nationality, SES, and gender, with migration-related social positioning emerging as a key dimension shaping the likelihood of receiving a diagnosis. These differences may reflect unequal exposure to distress and inequitable processes of diagnostic recognition. Intersectional analyses can reveal inequities obscured by single-axis approaches and support more equitable mental healthcare.

### Global, regional and national burdens of schizophrenia in adolescents and young adults: a trend analysis and forecast from 1990 to 2021
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhang B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 9

Schizophrenia is a serious psychological illness that tends to occur during the teens or early adulthood years. It causes major disability and social impairment. However, few studies have examined its global burden among adolescents and young adults. This study utilised data from the 2021 Global Burden of Disease Study to evaluates long-term trends and forecasts the future burden in this age group. The global rate for age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), Aprevalence rate (ASPR), and rate of disability-adjusted life years (ASDR) from 1990 to 2021 reduced gradually (AAPC < 0, p < 0.05). However, the actual number rose for incident from 0.41 million to 0.48 million, for prevalence from 1.71 million to 2.00 million, and for Disability-Adjusted Life Years(DALYs) from 1.14 million to 1.33 million. The rate for males was higher for both ASIR and ASDR for both sexes, and for both, the highest burden was in the group aged 20-24 years. Regionally, Australasia and East Asia showed the highest rates, while Eastern Europe reported the lowest. High-middle the Sociodemographic Index (SDI) countries carried the greatest burden. Inequality analyses indicated widening disparities, with greater concentration of burden in high-SDI regions. Decomposition analysis showed population growth as the main driver of increasing burden. Under the historical-trend continuation scenario, point estimates of ASIR, ASPR, and ASDR are projected to increase modestly through 2040, although prediction intervals widen substantially. Although age-standardized rates of schizophrenia among youth declined, the absolute burden increased due to demographic growth. The highest risks were observed in late adolescence and early adulthood, with males more affected than females. Regional and SDI disparities persist and may widen. Early detection and targeted interventions are needed to reduce the future burden of schizophrenia in young populations.

### Disparities in treatment initiation following diagnosis of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a systematic review
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Wickham P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

Early intervention improves long-term outcomes in children with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD). However, disparities may exist in the timing of treatment initiation following diagnosis. The objective of this systematic review was to examine associations between sociodemographic factors and time to treatment initiation after ADHD diagnosis in children and adolescents. We searched MEDLINE, EMBASE, PsycINFO, and CINAHL from inception to October 8, 2025. We included quantitative studies comparing time to ADHD treatment initiation after a diagnosis across sociodemographic groups, including sex, race and ethnicity, socioeconomic status, insurance type, and geographic factors. Two reviewers independently screened studies, extracted data, and assessed risk of bias using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale. Heterogeneity between studies prevented meta-analysis. We narratively synthesized findings. Twelve out of 6,223 studies initially identified were included in the review. Seven studies reported on pharmacological treatment alone while four studies examined both pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatments; one study reported on psychosocial treatment alone. Sex was the most frequently assessed factor (N = 10 studies), with most studies reporting earlier treatment initiation among males compared with females (effect estimates ranged from 1.02 (95% CI 0.82-1.26) to 1.36 (95% CI 1.21-1.54)). Children from racial and ethnic minority groups, lower-income families, and rural areas generally experienced longer delays in treatment initiation. Findings varied by geographic region, and no consistent differences were observed by insurance type. Sociodemographic disparities persist in the timing of ADHD treatment initiation. These findings may inform strategies to promote equitable and timely ADHD care following diagnosis.
