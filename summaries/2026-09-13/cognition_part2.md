### Suboptimal but intact integration of reward information during perceptual decision-making in autism
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fazioli L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 10

Alterations in reward processing have been proposed as a contributing factor to social and communication symptoms in autism. However, the nature of these alterations remains unclear, and it is debated whether autism is characterized by reduced sensitivity to reward. Evidence for general reduced reward processing in autism primarily comes from neurobiological studies, yet it remains uncertain how these findings translate to autistic behavior. A key challenge in addressing this question lies in the quantitative assessment of behavioral responses to reward. Here, we addressed this issue by investigating the integration of monetary reward information into behavior through the framework of Bayesian perceptual decision-making, enabling a quantitative evaluation of the direct contribution of reward to decision-making. Autistic (n = 32) and non-autistic (n = 48) participants performed an orientation categorization task, while the monetary reward given per correct answer varied across categories. Using signal-detection theory, we estimated decision boundaries while accounting for sensory uncertainty and prior expectation. Both groups comprehended the reward contingencies similarly and showed comparable perceptual sensitivity. Importantly, autistic individuals adjusted their decision boundaries in response to monetary reward in a suboptimal yet similar manner to that of non-autistic individuals. Integration of monetary reward during perceptual decision-making is intact in autism. These findings challenge the view of general reduced reward processing in autism and are inconsistent with accounts proposing enhanced rationality in autism.

### Visual perspective-taking and image-like representations: we can see it
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Aldrich L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

Visual perspective taking describes the ability to represent how others see the world. It is presumed to be crucial for social reasoning and may underpin the development of theory of mind. Yet, a recent series of experiments by Samuel et al. (2021) purports that people are entirely incapable of representing another person's view. In these experiments, participants were asked to judge which of two parallel and vertical lines would appear larger from another person's perspective. Results indicated a lack of basic awareness that the line closer to the other person would appear larger to them. Due to potential issues with key features of the task however, Samuel et al.'s (2021) conclusion of perspective taking inability may be premature. Specifically, features of how the critical question was phrased, how the stimuli were created, and the way in which participants responded, likely obscured an underlying capacity for visual perspective taking. Over two experiments, we first demonstrate that minor adjustments to the original paradigm enables participants to reliably report another person's perspective. Second, by using a novel multi-trial adaptation of the paradigm, we show a large proportion of participants can make highly accurate judgements that closely track how another person's visual experience changes with location.

### Outcome severity increases verbal probabilities used in dyadic communications
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Harris AJL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 7

Verbal probability expressions (VPEs) are interpreted as denoting higher numerical probabilities where they describe severe events, as compared to more neutral events (all else equal). Previous research suggests that this effect represents the correction of a perceived tendency for communicators to downplay the likelihood of more severe events, consistent with a rational speech act. We tested whether communicators really do downplay the likelihood of more severe events in four studies (total N = 2210). In each study, participants read four to six scenarios including a numerical probability of either a severe or non-severe negative outcome, and were asked to communicate this probability 'to a friend' with a VPE. Our results did not show that communicators downplayed the likelihood of more severe events. Participants typically provided higher VPEs to communicate the same numerical probability when that probability referred to a severe versus a non-severe event. This result was observed when participants communicated about events for which explicit actionable protective actions were highlighted (Study 1), where such actions were stated not to be possible (Studies 2 & 3), and where this was not specified (Study 4). Similar results were observed when participants chose a VPE from a pre-specified list (Studies 1, 2, & 4), and where they chose their communication freely (Study 3). The finding, coupled with those of past research, suggests that severity has a two-fold effect on subjective probability: VPEs are increased, and numerical interpretations of these VPEs are also subsequently increased.

### Vowel duration spelling in children with developmental language disorder: More errors, but affected by similar cues
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bliekendaal W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

Relatively little is known about the specific characteristics of the spelling abilities of children with developmental language disorder (DLD). This concerns knowledge about spelling patterns and the item characteristics contributing to spelling performance. It has been found that vowel duration spelling in multisyllabic words is challenging for typically developing (TD) children and that this performance is influenced by implicit cues, including semantic familiarity, orthographic frequency, and morphology. We investigated outcomes on long and short vowel duration spelling in Dutch and the potential impact of implicit cues in Dutch Grade 5 children with DLD (n = 74). We did so by comparing spelling dictation outcomes of this group of children to that of Grade 3 TD children (n = 47) and Grade 5 TD children (n = 63). The dictation task consisted of items differing in semantic familiarity, orthographic frequency, and morphology. We found that children with DLD made more errors on long and short vowel duration patterns than both TD peers and younger TD children, indicating severe difficulties. The influence of implicit cues was similar for children with DLD and younger TD children, though results varied slightly per pattern: In the consonant doubling pattern, morphology affected the spelling performance of younger TD children, but not that of children with DLD, whereas morphology affected the performance of all groups in vowel degemination. Semantic familiarity also affected the spelling accuracy of children with and without DLD, while frequency did not. The contributions of semantic familiarity and morphology can help shape spelling instruction.

### Effects of orthographic consistency on spoken word recognition and production in L1-Spanish children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Furgoni A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 10

Learning to read reshapes spoken language processing by establishing systematic mappings between phonemes and graphemes. One robust manifestation of this interaction is the Orthographic Consistency Effect (OCE), whereby spoken words containing phonemes with a single possible spelling (consistent spellings) are processed more efficiently than those with multiple possible spellings (inconsistent spellings). The present study investigates whether early L1-Spanish readers show phoneme-level OCEs in spoken word perception and production. L1-Spanish second graders of around seven years of age completed three tasks: an auditory lexical decision task, a picture naming task, and a reading aloud task. Stimuli could contain either consistent phonemes or inconsistent phonemes. Results revealed OCEs in both perception and production. In the auditory lexical decision task, children responded more accurately to items with consistent than inconsistent phoneme-to-grapheme mappings. In reading aloud, consistent items were named both faster and more accurately than inconsistent items. In contrast, no consistency effects were observed in picture naming, a task that does not overtly involve orthographic input. These findings demonstrate that, even in a highly transparent orthography, early readers are sensitive to phoneme-level orthographic inconsistencies during spoken word recognition and production. L1-Spanish early readers appear to rely on phonological decoding strategically, suggesting that the impact of literacy on speech perception and production in Spanish is present but task-dependent at the onset of reading acquisition.

### Recognition of individual angry versus happy faces in 6- and 9-month-old infants
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Quinn PC et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

With mixed evidence from adults, children, and infants suggesting either anger or happiness superiority in emotion detection, emotion recognition, and recognition of individual faces posing emotional expressions, the current research was undertaken to determine if one or the other emotion would result in higher recognition of individual faces by 6-month-old and 9-month-old infants. The infants were familiarized with an angry or happy face and then given a novelty preference test pairing the familiarized face with a novel face posing the same emotion. Recognition performance of both age groups in each emotion condition was above chance; however, recognition of angry faces was reliably higher than for happy faces. A simple exposure-frequency account of the findings is unlikely because infants experience more happy than angry faces. In addition, lower-level interpretations based on differences in salience, complexity, and discriminability were not supported by image analyses, infant looking times during familiarization, or adult ratings. An account based on the processing of facial expressions as social or communicative signals by infants is discussed, with a possible role for an early negativity bias.
