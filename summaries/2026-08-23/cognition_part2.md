### Enhanced Episodic Memory Encoding Following Modulation of the Amygdala via Transcranial Low-intensity Focused Ultrasound
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Doss MK et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 4

The amygdala is considered crucial to the formation of emotional episodic memories, yet causal evidence in humans is limited due to challenges in noninvasive neuromodulation of deep brain structures. In a double-blind, sham-controlled, repeated-measures study, we tested whether transcranial low-intensity focused ultrasound (tFUS) targeting the left amygdala before encoding emotional and neutral pictures impacted memory for these pictures 24 hr later (n = 23). Using a putative inhibitory tFUS protocol previously shown to attenuate amygdala BOLD-dependent signal, we hypothesized that preencoding amygdala inhibition would impair emotional memory. Contrary to this hypothesis, active (vs. sham) sonication enhanced-or tended to enhance-memory for both neutral and emotional memory across two memory tests. As a secondary measure of amygdala modulation, participants completed a dynamic emotional facial recognition task (n = 20). Active tFUS selectively enhanced recognition of subtle fearful expressions. Computational modeling further supported tFUS-mediated memory and emotion recognition enhancements. Assuming that this tFUS protocol did indeed inhibit amygdala function, these findings suggest that rather than solely amplifying emotional salience, the amygdala may act as a filter that suppresses weaker, ambiguous signals. Inhibiting the amygdala may thus attenuate this gating function, thereby unmasking subtle affective cues and enhancing their processing into memory. Nevertheless, the purported inhibitory nature of this tFUS protocol requires further investigation. Should these findings replicate, they highlight the potential for tFUS as a translational tool to modulate affective processes for disorders such as depression and posttraumatic disorder characterized by impairments in emotional processing and memory.

### Age-related differences in the role of explicit encoding instructions on verbal, visual and spatial Hebb learning
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Leach H, Guitard D | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 20

Hebb repetition learning - the improved recall performance for repeated sequences compared to non-repeated sequences - is widely used as a laboratory measure of learning. Recent research has demonstrated that awareness of the repeated sequence enhances learning, although there is reason to believe that older adults may not benefit from such foreknowledge in the same way as younger adults. In three experiments, we systematically investigated the role of foreknowledge in younger and older adults, across verbal, visual and spatial Hebb repetition learning. Each experiment employed a serial recall task (of letters, Chinese characters or dot locations), in which a repeated sequence was presented on every third trial. Half of the participants received foreknowledge - they were informed that a repeated sequence would occur and were signalled the presence prior to its presentation. The remaining participants received no foreknowledge and were not explicitly made aware of the repeated sequence. We compared learning rates between younger and older adults, in both foreknowledge and no foreknowledge conditions. Across all experiments, we found comparable Hebb learning between younger and older adults in the no foreknowledge condition. However, the foreknowledge condition revealed that the additional cue provided substantially greater benefit for learning the repeated sequence in younger adults compared to older adults. We suggest this pattern reflects older adults' difficulties in forming associations, in this case, between the foreknowledge cue and the repeated sequence, which limits their ability to use the cue to enhance learning.

### Social priming enhances young children's trust in robots
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Magnini M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 18

This study examined preschoolers' selective trust in a robot versus a human informant, and whether social priming may increase trust by making the robot's social characteristics more salient, using the word-learning paradigm. Children also completed the Theory of Mind (ToM) scale, along with an adapted naïve biology task and an Attribution of Mental States questionnaire assessing their beliefs about robots. In Experiment 1, eighty-six Italian children (aged 3 and 5) observed a competent robot (NAO) correctly labeling familiar objects and an incompetent human mislabeling them. The two informants then provided novel labels for unfamiliar objects. Three-year-olds endorsed labels from both informants equally, while five-year-olds preferentially endorsed the labels provided by the robot. ToM related to trust only for judgment scores (which informant was correct), an association largely explained by age. Experiment 2 investigated whether exposure to social priming would increase children's trust. Eighty-five Canadian children (aged 3 and 5) completed the same selective trust procedure, except that they first viewed a social priming video designed to increase the robot's perceived social agency. Both age groups preferred learning new labels from the robot, suggesting that social characteristics support children's trust in robot informants, particularly among younger preschoolers. ToM predicted children's endorsement of the robot's labels, although this effect was largely explained by age. Together, the findings suggest that, by age 5, preschoolers exhibit robust sensitivity to the epistemic characteristics of informants, regardless of their agency. Younger children, in contrast, appear more likely to trust a robot displaying salient social characteristics.

### Children's thinking about asymptomatic carriers of disease
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** DeJesus JM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 18

Prior research has examined children's understanding of disease transmission between people who demonstrate illness symptoms or are labeled as sick (Blacker & LoBue, 2016; DeJesus et al., 2021). The present studies examine North American children's thinking about illness carriers with varying symptoms in terms of the likelihood and severity of disease transmission (Study 1) and moral judgments about disease transmission (Study 2). In Study 1, we tested 6- to 12-year-old children on Zoom (n = 108, 42 % female, 59 % White). Children were introduced to one character that was symptomatic, asymptomatic, or did not have a novel germ ("Germ X") inside them (the carrier). After the carrier interacted with their friend (the recipient), participants were asked if the recipient caught Germ X and how they would feel later. Children rated the recipient as feeling worse if the carrier had Germ X and was symptomatic. In Study 2, we tested 5- to 10-year-olds at a children's museum (n = 96, 53 % female, 54 % White). Here, the carrier was symptomatic or asymptomatic and shared an apple with the recipient, who later became symptomatic or asymptomatic. Children were asked before and after learning about the recipient's symptoms if the interaction was okay. Children provided worse ratings if the carrier felt sick, and adjusted their ratings based on recipient status after the interaction. Across studies, we observed limited age effects. These findings suggest that children consider the severity of illness as part of its transmissibility and by age 5 negatively view interacting while sick.

### Little time travelers: Future thinking and temporal language in the second year of life
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Remiszewska A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 19

Episodic future thinking-the ability to anticipate and prepare for future events-is a central component of mental time travel. Although most research on episodic future thinking has focused on preschool-aged children, much less is known about its earliest emergence in toddlerhood. The present study investigated whether precursors of episodic future thinking, such as future-oriented object selection, can be observed in the second year of life and whether this ability is associated with temporal language use beyond general vocabulary. Eighty-one typically developing Polish toddlers (M = 22.04 months, SD = 2.24; range = 17-26 months) participated in an adapted Rooms Test. The task required children to select an object needed to solve a previously encountered problem after a 3-minute delay and a change of context. Parent-report measures assessed children's temporal term use and general vocabulary. Among children included in the main analyses (N = 74), 55% selected the target object without prompting, significantly exceeding chance performance (33%). Children who passed the task used more temporal terms and had larger general vocabularies than children who failed. Temporal term use was strongly correlated with general vocabulary and did not explain additional variance beyond age and general vocabulary. These findings indicate that precursors of episodic future thinking may already be observable during the second year of life when experimental paradigms are developmentally appropriate and employ salient, motivating task structures. They also link future-oriented task performance to emerging language abilities, while leaving the specific association between temporal language and early future-oriented behavior open for future research.
