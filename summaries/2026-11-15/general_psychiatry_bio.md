### Vagus-Hepatic-Creatine axis modulates manic-like behaviors via hippocampal MMP19 upregulation in bipolar disorder
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yang J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Bipolar disorder (BD) is associated with pronounced alterations in peripheral creatine kinase levels; however, the mechanistic basis of this phenomenon remains poorly understood. To address this gap, we conducted a translational study combining clinical assessments in 55 patients with major depressive disorder, 57 BD patients, and 55 healthy controls, alongside sleep deprivation-induced manic-like mouse models. Our findings revealed significantly elevated peripheral creatine concentrations in both BD patients and model mice, which correlated with increased hepatic expression of guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT), a rate-limiting enzyme in creatine biosynthesis. Behavioral experiments in mice showed that exogenous creatine supplementation recapitulated manic-like phenotypes, whereas administration of the creatine analogue β-guanidinopropionic acid (β-GPA) or surgical subdiaphragmatic vagotomy effectively ameliorated these aberrant behaviors. Mechanistically, peripherally derived creatine was found to modulate neural activity independently of blood-brain barrier penetration, acting instead through interleukin-1α (IL-1α)-mediated inflammatory signaling. Transcriptomic analyses further identified hippocampal matrix metalloproteinase-19 (MMP19) as a critical downstream effector, and genetic knockdown experiments confirmed its indispensability in the expression of manic-like behaviors. Collectively, these results delineate a previously unrecognized vagus-liver-creatine-hippocampal axis in BD pathophysiology, highlighting peripheral creatine metabolic routes as actionable targets for therapeutic intervention. Overall, this work provides compelling evidence that peripheral metabolic disturbances can drive central nervous system dysfunction via neuroimmune crosstalk, offering a new conceptual framework for understanding mood disorder etiology.

### GluD1 modulates GluN2B-containing NMDAR function and plasticity at subicular synapses
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Purisic E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 6

Dysregulation of the delta-type glutamate receptor GluD1 and N-methyl-D-aspartate receptors (NMDARs) is implicated in neuropsychiatric disorders including schizophrenia and intellectual disability, and GluD1 modulates NMDAR responses in hippocampal neurons. However, the precise mechanisms by which GluD1 influences specific NMDAR subtypes remain undefined, representing a critical gap given the reliance of synaptic plasticity and cognition on NMDAR composition. GluN2A- and GluN2B-containing NMDARs are essential for synaptic long-term potentiation (LTP) and contextual learning and memory. Here, we used CRISPR/Cas9 to generate GluD1 knockout (KO) in cultured hippocampal neurons and observed a selective decrease in GluN2B-containing NMDAR responses. In acute hippocampal slices, GluD1 KO similarly reduced GluN2B-containing NMDAR currents at ventral CA1→subiculum synapses and impaired LTP at these synapses. In vivo, region-specific GluD1 deficiency in the ventral subiculum disrupted long-term contextual memory, indicating a critical role for GluD1 in cognitive processes. These findings demonstrate that GluD1 is indispensable for preserving GluN2B-containing NMDAR function, synaptic plasticity, and memory, providing molecular insight into how GluD1 regulates NMDAR subtypes implicated in synaptic dysfunction in neuropsychiatric disorders. Understanding this mechanism will guide the development of therapeutic strategies that selectively target GluD1-dependent modulation of NMDAR subtypes in brain disease.

### Comparative effects of various exercise types and doses to improve executive function in children and adolescents with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: a systematic review with pairwise, network, and dose-response meta-analyses
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Hou X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 12

Exercise interventions have been demonstrated to effectively improve executive function (EF) in children and adolescents with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). However, evidence comparing effects of different exercise types and doses is limited, and the optimal exercise type and dose that improve EF remain unclear. This study aims to compare the effects of different exercise types and doses on EF improvement in children and adolescents with ADHD. Seven databases (PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cochrane Library, SPORT Discus via EBSCO, PsycINFO, and CNKI) were systematically searched up to February 2026. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing the effects of exercise interventions on EF in children and adolescents with ADHD were included. The outcomes included working memory (WM), inhibitory control (IC), and cognitive flexibility (CF). Pairwise, network, and dose-response meta-analyses using random-effects models were conducted to assess the effects of exercise on EF. Forty RCTs (n = 1563) were included. Network meta-analysis indicated that, among children and adolescents with ADHD, mind-body exercise had the highest probability (SUCRA = 94.9) of being the most effective intervention for improving WM. Combat sports demonstrated the highest probability of being the most effective intervention for enhancing IC (SUCRA = 93.4) and CF (SUCRA = 92.5) in this population. Furthermore, a significant positive non-linear dose-response relationship was identified between total exercise and all EF domains among children and adolescents with ADHD. The minimum effective dose was estimated at approximately 1200 METs-min/week for WM, 340 METs-min/week for IC, and 280 METs-min/week for CF, respectively.

### Greater Translocator Protein Distribution Volume in Neurocircuitry of Anorexia Nervosa
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lepra M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

BACKGROUND: Inflammatory stimuli are associated with onset of anorexia nervosa (AN) but whether inflammation occurs in the brain of AN is unknown. The primary objective is to determine whether [ 18 F]FEPPA total distribution volume ([ 18 F]FEPPA V T ), PET index of translocator protein density and inflammatory marker of gliosis, is elevated in AN in anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), ventral striatum (VS), dorsal caudate (DC), dorsal putamen (DP), thalamus, orbitofrontal cortex and insula. Secondary objectives are to determine the relationship of [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  in ACC to rumination severity and [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  to body mass index (BMI).

METHODS: [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  was measured in 20 medication free AN participants and 20 healthy controls. Correlations of [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  with rumination severity, and BMI, were determined.

RESULTS: [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  was elevated in AN in all ROI (linear mixed model (LMM), group effect, P = 4×10 -7 ), particularly ACC, VS, DC and thalamus (mean 52% elevation; LMM, region by group interaction, P = 4×10 -4 ). ACC [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  correlated positively with eating concerns (Eating Disorder Examination, r = 0.58, P = .007; Inventory of Depression and Anxiety Symptoms-II, r = 0.66, P = .002). There were no significant correlations between [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  and BMI.

CONCLUSIONS: Greater [ 18 F]FEPPA V T  is strong evidence for neuroinflammation and gliosis, particularly in ACC, VS, DC and thalamus in AN. Given such, it may be of interest to assess interventions that influence gliosis towards curative roles in future clinical trials in AN.

### Personalized Functional Network Mapping Across the Lifespan
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Xu X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Functional brain networks vary substantially across individuals, yet this variability is obscured by conventional group-average atlases. Personalized functional network mapping addresses this limitation by characterizing individual-specific network organization, including the spatial topography of network territories, boundaries, and variants. Personalized mapping can be applied across large developmental and aging cohorts to study lifespan changes in such organization. Precision functional mapping (PFM), a specialized form of personalized mapping, uses extensive functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data per individual to achieve highly reliable and fine-grained characterization of individual functional organization. Here, we review individual-specific functional network organization from infancy through older adulthood. Evidence across life stages suggests a lifespan trajectory in which individual-specific network organization is detectable near birth, continues to refine through childhood and adolescence, and exhibits marked trait-like stability in adulthood, while its evolution in later life remains less well characterized. Across these stages, inter-individual topographic variability is greater in higher-order association than primary sensorimotor systems, positioning the sensorimotor-association axis as a unifying framework for understanding individual-specific network organization across the lifespan. We further review how individualized network features relate to cognition and psychopathology and how they are altered in psychiatric and neurodegenerative disorders. Finally, we discuss advances in MRI acquisition and analysis that facilitate personalized functional network mapping and opportunities for precision clinical translation.

### PTSD is Associated with Advanced Epigenetic Age in 43,155 US Military Veterans
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wolf EJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 7

BACKGROUND: Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) has been associated with advanced biological age in DNA methylation data, but results have been inconsistent. This study evaluated PTSD in association with epigenetic age in the largest cohort to date (by ∼20 fold).

METHODS: Participants were 43,155 US Veterans (92.96% male) enrolled in the VA's Million Veteran Program, with VA electronic health record (EHR), self-report PTSD severity (n = 21,999), and DNA methylation and genotype data. PTSD diagnoses predated the blood draw for obtaining DNA by ≥ one year.

RESULTS: PTSD diagnosis and symptom severity were associated with age-adjusted metrics of epigenetic age ("age residuals") per the Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, GrimAge, and DunedinPACE algorithms after multiple testing adjustment. PTSD severity showed the most consistent associations with epigenetic age residuals, ranging from up to ∼7 months (Horvath) to ∼2 years (GrimAge) advanced age and up to 16 days faster pace per year (DunedinPACE). The relationships between PTSD severity and GrimAge, PhenoAge, and DunedinPACE residuals were stronger among younger vs. older Veterans. PTSD was associated with all epigenetic age residuals in the European ancestry subgroup (n = 26,630), but fewer significant associations emerged in the African ancestry participants (n = 11,119). Associations were similar across male and female Veterans.

CONCLUSIONS: PTSD was associated with advanced epigenetic aging in the largest study to date to evaluate this question. Effects were generally small in magnitude, though meaningful when considering the personal and healthcare system impact of advanced aging in the large population of VA users with PTSD.
