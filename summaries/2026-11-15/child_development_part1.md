### Identity-linked peer stress and internalizing distress: Heterogeneous joint trajectories across two years in adolescence
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jian B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 9

Bias-based victimization may function as identity-linked peer stress, yet little is known about how it co-develops with internalizing distress across two years in adolescence. We analyzed four waves of data from  N  = 639 US adolescents (M = 15.23 years, SD = 1.30, range 13-17; 51.3% identified as female; 51.5% White, 23.6% Hispanic/Latino, 19.6% Black, 4.1% Asian, 1.3% multiracial/other) from the AmeriSpeak Teen Panel. Multidimensional growth mixture modeling identified joint trajectories of victimization, depression, and anxiety. A three-class solution emerged (entropy = 0.94): Co-Occurring High-Decreasing (7.5%), High Internalizing, Low Victimization (19.7%), and Low-Stable (72.8%). Higher parenting stress predicted membership in both higher-risk classes relative to Low-Stable (OR = 2.44 and 1.67); the female gender indicator (OR = 2.85,  d  ≈ 0.58) and lower parental education predicted the High Internalizing, Low Victimization class. Both higher-risk classes reported lower school safety and higher peer rejection at Wave 4 than the Low-Stable class but did not differ significantly from each other; classes did not differ in grades. Identity-linked peer stress and internalizing distress followed heterogeneous developmental pathways: comparably elevated internalizing distress emerged with and without frequent victimization.

### Early adversity, poverty, and positive social development show distinct associations across epigenetic aging measures in childhood
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mastromatteo LY, Miller JG | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 9

Adversity and poverty are implicated in biological aging, but epigenetic age research has produced inconsistent findings, and few studies have considered positive developmental factors. Leveraging the Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study ( N  = 863 with epigenetic data; 51.14% males; 45.67% Non-Hispanic Black), we examined how early adversity, poverty, and social skills trajectories in childhood relate to multiple epigenetic clocks at age 9 ( M   age  = 9.33 ± 0.32), including age-trained (Horvath, Hannum, Skin & Blood, PedBE), health-related (PhenoAge, GrimAge), and pace of aging (DunedinPACE) measures. Early adversity was associated with accelerated GrimAge ( β  = 0.12) and PedBE ( β  = 0.12), but decelerated age on Horvath ( β  = -0.08) and Skin & Blood clocks ( β  = -0.12). Poverty showed more consistent associations with accelerated age on mortality-related clocks. Children with decreasing social skills across childhood had higher GrimAge ( β  = 0.20), and this pattern of social development partially mediated the effect of poverty. This pattern was specific to social skills and not psychopathology symptoms. Together, these findings suggest that early adversity and poverty are implicated in similar and distinct alterations in epigenetic clocks indexing maturation versus disease risk, while social development may play a role specifically in epigenetic aging related to disease risk.

### Day-to-day peer and family stress as proximal correlates of self-injurious thoughts during adolescence
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** James KM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Theory and research both highlight interpersonal stressors as precipitants to self-injurious thoughts and behaviors (SITBs) during adolescence. Yet, little is known about near-term risk or which types of interpersonal stressors (e.g., peer and/or family) are most likely to precipitate adolescent SITBs. This study used an intensive longitudinal design to examine whether peer- and family-related stress were associated with adolescents' same- and next-day self-injurious thoughts. Participants were 154 female adolescents aged 12-17 years (77.3% White). Youth were oversampled for risk such that 73.4% ( n  = 113) were at high risk for future SITBs. Youth completed a 30-day ecological momentary assessment procedure assessing interpersonal experiences and SITBs. Multilevel models showed that peer stress predicted same-day nonsuicidal self-injury urges and suicidal ideation (SI), whereas family stress predicted same-day nonsuicidal self-injury urges only. Neither peer nor family stress predicted next-day self-injurious thoughts. Exploratory models examining bidirectional effects showed that non-suicidal self-injury urges predicted next-day peer and family stress while SI predicted next-day family, but not peer, stress. Findings suggest that daily interpersonal stress is proximally associated with self-injurious thoughts during adolescence and underscore the importance of studying these processes in real time. Results may have implications for ecological momentary interventions to interrupt interpersonal risks for SITBs.

### Children's and adults' metamotivational beliefs about how cognitive reframing shapes motivation
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hu L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 9

The ability to overcome motivational challenges depends, in part, on individuals' beliefs about how motivation can be regulated-that is, metamotivational beliefs. Although research with adults shows that such beliefs support more effective motivation regulation and goal pursuit, little is known about how they develop during childhood. The present research examined the development of a foundational aspect of metamotivational beliefs in 4- to 9-year-old North American children ( N  = 379, 58% White, 55% girls) and adults ( N  = 135, 65% White, 54% women): the belief that motivation can be shaped by how one interprets the situation (i.e., cognitive reframing). In Study 1, children as young as 4-5 years attributed stronger motivation to positive than to negative reframing for the same situation, with increases in effects through age 9. In Study 2, children and adults rated reframing as more effective than distracting thoughts for motivation, but the reverse pattern held for emotion. These findings suggest that judgments about reframing cannot be reduced to valence reasoning and that participants recognize that regulating motivation is not identical to regulating emotion. Using a between-subjects design, Study 3 conceptually replicated Study 2's pattern that reframing was considered more effective than distraction for motivation but not for emotion and revealed that, unlike adults, children did not prefer reframing over distraction when selecting a strategy for themselves. Together, the research demonstrates a developing recognition of reframing effects on motivation-a process central to many motivation regulation strategies-with implications for the development of metamotivational beliefs and self-regulation. ### Maternal responses to infant positive affect and toddler regulation of positive affect: A longitudinal pilot study
**כתב עת:** 📄 Infant Mental Health Journal (Infant Ment Health J) *(IF: 2.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Schmitt G et al. | **תאריך:** 2027 Jan

Children's ability to regulate positive affect (PA) is a crucial yet understudied aspect of early socioemotional development. This pilot study investigated whether maternal responses to infant PA longitudinally predict toddlers' regulation of PA. Participants were 32 mother-infant dyads from a larger longitudinal study in Toronto, Canada. At 11-12 months, dyads were video recorded during naturalistic play at home. Maternal responses to infant PA were assessed using an observational coding system that distinguishes supportive (e.g., elaborative, matching) from nonsupportive (e.g., intrusive, ignoring) responses. At 18-20 months, toddlers completed the gift delay task, which measures regulation of high-arousal PA through latency to touch a desirable toy. Multiple regression analysis controlling for age and family income showed that a greater proportion of supportive responses in infancy predicted better regulation of PA in toddlerhood (β = .37, p = .027). Findings provide preliminary evidence that supportive maternal responses to PA in infancy are associated with capacities to regulate PA in toddlerhood. Results highlight the importance of examining positive as well as negative affect in models of early emotion regulation and suggest potential targets for early intervention.
