### Effects of Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation on Core Symptoms and Sleep Disturbances in Children With Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain Behav *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Wang M, Yang M | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

ABSTRACT Objective This meta‐analysis was designed to determine whether transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) confers measurable benefits for selected behavioral features of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and for sleep problems in pediatric ASD populations. Methods A comprehensive literature retrieval was conducted across eight electronic databases—PubMed, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Embase, CNKI, CBM, VIP, and Wanfang—covering the period from each database's inception through June 1, 2026. Eligible studies were randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) in patients with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). The methodological quality of the included trials was appraised using the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment instrument. Quantitative synthesis was performed with RevMan 5.4 and Stata 17.0, following the PRISMA statement for systematic reviews and meta‐analyses. Results Ultimately, seven studies qualified for inclusion in this systematic review. Pooled analyses showed that tDCS was associated with lower ATEC scores (mean difference [MD] = −8.81, 95% confidence interval [CI]: −12.06 to −5.56, p < 0.00001), ABC scores (MD = −12.08, 95% CI: −21.75 to −2.41, p = 0.01), and CSHQ scores (MD = −4.95, 95% CI: −8.66 to −1.24, p = 0.009). However, no statistically significant difference was observed for CARS scores (MD = −1.12, 95% CI: −4.15 to 1.91, p = 0.47). Conclusion tDCS, most commonly delivered at 1 mA over the DLPFC, was associated with improvements in some ASD‐related behavioral outcomes; preliminary evidence also suggested a potential benefit for sleep disturbances, although this finding was based on only two low‐certainty studies and warrants cautious interpretation.

1 Introduction Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) represents a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by core impairments encompassing persistent deficits in social communication and reciprocal social interaction, alongside constrained or stereotyped patterns of behavior and interests. Nevertheless, these manifestations often remain undetected until environmental or social expectations surpass the individual's adaptive capacity, and ASD‐related symptoms generally lead to substantial functional impairment spanning several domains. According to surveillance estimates from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) via the Autism and Developmental Disabilities Monitoring (ADDM) Network, roughly 1 in 31 children at 8 years of age (3.2%) are diagnosed with ASD. At present, the prevalence of ASD among boys (49.2 per 1,000) is 3.4 times that observed among girls (14.3 per 1,000) (Shaw et al. 2025 ). Such an upward trend in prevalence could signify not merely an actual growth in ASD incidence but may equally be attributable to refinements in diagnostic criteria or advances in assessment instruments (Sharma et al. 2018 ). The etiology of ASD is multifactorial, with genetic and environmental determinants each contributing critically to the pathogenesis of the disorder (Sharma et al. 2018 ). Pathological studies on ASD have found differences in brain development, structure, and connectivity in individuals with the condition. The mechanism of action is complex, and effective clinical treatments are still lacking. Recent studies have shown promising results in treating ASD using non‐invasive brain stimulation techniques (Khaleghi et al. 2020 ), with one of the main techniques being tDCS. This method applies a current through electrodes placed on the scalp (Fernández‐Corazza et al. 2020 ). During tDCS, a low‐intensity electrical current is applied via electrodes positioned on the scalp, which modifies neuronal membrane potentials and consequently changes spontaneous firing rates (Sandrini et al. 2020 ). In a two‐electrode configuration, one electrode functions as the anode while the other acts as the cathode. Anodal polarization enhances neuronal excitability through depolarization, whereas cathodal polarization reduces excitability via hyperpolarization. This approach is capable of modulating the excitability of distinct cortical regions, and its influence on neuronal excitability may persist for minutes to hours following extended stimulation (Dalong et al. 2020 ; Li et al. 2019 ). Typically, a single tDCS session lasts no more than 20 min, employing a current strength of 1–2 mA. Under such parameters, tDCS has demonstrated a favorable safety profile and good tolerability (Zewdie et al. 2020 ). This form of brain stimulation has been tested as a therapeutic option in various psychiatric disorders, and tDCS appears to have positive effects on depression and certain symptoms of schizophrenia (Borrione et al. 2022 ; Narita et al. 2020 ), particularly in areas such as inhibitory control and working memory (Salehinejad et al. 2019 ). Increasing evidence suggests that social, cognitive, and sensory dysfunctions in ASD are associated with abnormalities in distributed networks rather than isolated brain regions. Therefore, tDCS may produce favorable effects due to its ability to modulate functional connectivity in the brain. Nevertheless, although tDCS has attracted increasing attention in autism research, its effects on sleep problems remain poorly characterized. Sleep disturbances are very common in children with autism and can have a big impact on their quality of life. A lot of people have sleep problems, and this makes them act in a worse way and makes them find it harder to think. Some studies have looked at how tDCS can help with the main symptoms of ASD, but there is not much evidence that it can improve sleep problems in children with ASD. Against this backdrop, the present meta‐analysis seeks to fill this gap by systematically assessing the efficacy of tDCS for alleviating ASD symptoms, with particular emphasis on its influence on sleep disturbances within this population. This will help clinicians and researchers understand better how tDCS could be used to treat not only core behavioral symptoms but also sleep problems, which are a clinical challenge that hasn't been fully addressed yet. Although some reviews have explored this area, there is a lack of objective, evidence‐based conclusions (Osório and Brunoni 2019 ). ### The effect of a psycho-motor rehabilitation on executive functions and theory of mind in children with autism spectrum disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 Appl Neuropsychol Child | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** A Alsamiri Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct-Dec

The aim of the current research was to investigate the effect of a psycho-motor rehabilitation on executive functions and theory of mind in children with autism spectrum disorder. The sample size was 50 children with an average age of 4-6 years. A quasi-experimental experiment was designed and implemented, specifically a pre test post test- control-group design with a quantitative approach, because the researcher want to established possible causes and effects between dependent and independent variables. After checking the normality of the data using the Shapiro-Wilk test, the covariance analysis test was used to test the hypotheses. All data were analyzed using SPSS software version 23, and the significance level was considered as  p  ≤ 0.05. The comparison of the scores in the two experimental and control groups indicates that the Dohsa-hou method was successful in improving the executive functions and theory of mind in children with autism spectrum disorder.

### The diagnostic accuracy of Wisconsin Card Sorting Test-the Short Computerized Version (WCST-64:CV2) for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 Appl Neuropsychol Child | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Darandari E, Alqahtani R | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct-Dec

This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of Wisconsin Card Sorting Test-The Short Computerized Version (WCST-64:CV2) for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), and differences based on gender (males and females) and classification (typically developing-TD and with ASD). The sample consisted of ( N  = 96) TD and ( N  = 48) ASD, Saudi children (aged 7-9 years). The diagnostic accuracy results showed that most (WCST-64:CV2) indicators have discrimination ability between TD and ASD children, for males and females. Accuracy differences based on gender were on three indicators (3, 4 & 5). There were no significant differences ( p  >.05) on indicators based on gender, however, there were significant differences ( p  < .01) based on classification on all indicators, except indicators (3 & 4). Interaction between gender and classification was significant ( p  < .05) for one indicator (5). It is recommended to use (WCST-64:CV2) to diagnose ASD children (7-9 years), based on eight indicators for males (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, & 9) and seven indicators for females (1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, & 9), and to take differences based on gender and classification into account, when using (WCST-64:CV2). The (WCST-64:CV2) could be relied on for clinical/neuropsychological assessment of ASD children.

### Epileptiform Discharges in Seizure-Free Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder and the Role of Antiseizure Medications
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Coll Physicians Surg Pak | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Al-Mosawi VS, Gargary KH | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

OBJECTIVE: To determine the frequency of epileptiform discharges (EPDs) in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) without clinical seizures, assess autism severity using the Childhood Autism Rating Scale, Second Edition (CARS-2), and reassess the children after treatment with antiseizure medications (ASMs).

STUDY DESIGN: A prospective cohort study. Place and Duration of the Study: Four autism centres in Duhok City, Kurdistan Region, Iraq, from August 2024 to August 2025.

METHODOLOGY: Fifty children with seizure-free ASD (aged 2.5-18 years) underwent clinical evaluation, electroencephalography (EEG) recording, and severity assessment using CARS-2. Children with EPDs were offered ASMs according to EEG pattern and clinical profile. EEG and CARS-2 assessments were repeated after 6 months. Data were analysed using SPSS version 31.0. Descriptive statistics were presented as means ± standard deviations for continuous variables and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. For group comparisons, independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for small samples were used. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS: EPDs were detected in 24 participants (48%). EPDs were focal in 19 cases (79.16) and generalised in 5 cases (20.83). Adherence to ASMs after 6 months was observed in 9 cases (37.5 %). All adherent participants demonstrated EEG improvement and significant reductions in total CARS-2 scores (p = 0.001). Improvements were also observed in the following domains: emotional response (p = 0.023), body use (p = 0.024), activity level (p = 0.026), visual response (p = 0.040), taste, smell and touch (p = 0.057), and general impression of ASD (p = 0.026). Adverse effects were reported in 6/15 (40%) participants whom started ASMs, primarily behavioural.

CONCLUSION: EPDs are common in children with ASD, even in the absence of clinical seizures. When ASMs were used consistently, EEG improvements were observed, and ASD severity scores on the CARS-2 were reduced.   Key Words: Anti-seizure medications, Autism spectrum disorder, Electroencephalography, Epileptiform discharges.

### Global Statistics of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Autism Spectrum Disorder, and Conduct Disorder among School-Age Children from 1990-2021, with Forecasts to 2040
**כתב עת:** 📄 Neuroepidemiology | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

BACKGROUND: Mental disorders among school-age children represent a significant global health issue and substantially contribute to the global disease burden. This study aimed to examine the patterns and trends of attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), autism spectrum disorder (ASD), and conduct disorder (CD), which are commonly observed in school-age children, from 1990 to 2021, offering valuable insights for health policy formulation, medical resource allocation, and optimization of patient management strategies.

METHODS: We analyzed data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study for 21 regions and 204 countries, focusing on prevalence, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), gender, and different time periods to gain a deeper understanding of the health burden. We assessed correlations with the Socio-demographic Index (SDI) and employed autoregressive integrated moving average modeling to forecast developments up to 2040, enabling us to better comprehend the burden and trends of these mental disorders.

RESULTS: In 2021, the prevalence rates of ADHD, ASD, and CD per 100,000 population among children aged 5-14 years were 2,374.8, 847.13, and 2,146.48, respectively; DALY rates were 29.17, 162.85, and 262.53, respectively. Compared to 1990, prevalence and DALY rates exhibited changes of -0.06, 0.03, and 0.04, respectively. Australia, Japan, and the UK exhibited particularly notable burdens, with the UK continuing to show rapid growth in ASD, while ADHD and CD increased rapidly in China and India, respectively. Gender and age analyses revealed a higher burden among males. Regions with high SDI displayed higher epidemiological indicators. For the 2040 forecast, the indicators for ADHD in terms of prevalence and DALYs are projected to be 3,796.65 and 44.33 for males, and 1,343.18 and 16.23 for females. For ASD, they are projected to be 1,103.04 and 215.23 for males, and 552.01 and 106.40 for females. For CD, they are projected to be 2,650.69 and 325.64 for males, and 1,593.57 and 200.41 for females.

CONCLUSION: This study underscores the complex epidemiological landscape of ADHD, ASD, and CD, revealing variations in burden across gender and geographical regions. It emphasizes the urgent need for healthcare professionals and policymakers to devise innovative prevention and healthcare strategies based on the current and evolving burden of these mental disorders, with the aim of alleviating the global disease burden.
