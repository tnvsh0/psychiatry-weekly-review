### Comparative efficacy and tolerability of pharmacological interventions for the acute treatment of obsessive-compulsive disorder across the lifespan: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Cohen S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

BACKGROUND: Pharmacological treatment is recommended for obsessive-compulsive disorder. Several options are available, yet little is known on comparative effects of individual medications. We aimed to elucidate the efficacy and tolerability of pharmacotherapy for people with obsessive-compulsive disorder, across the lifespan.

METHODS: We did a systematic review and network meta-analysis comparing short-term pharmacological interventions for obsessive-compulsive disorder. We searched multiple databases, including MEDLINE, Embase, PsycInfo, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, for literature on licensed or recommended pharmacological treatments for obsessive-compulsive disorder, without age restrictions, from database inception up to March 11, 2026. We included double-blind, randomised controlled trials with outcomes between 8 and 16 weeks. Two reviewers independently and in duplicate extracted outcomes, with a quality check done by a third reviewer. Primary outcomes were efficacy, defined as change from baseline score, and tolerability, defined as the proportion of participants discontinuing treatment due to adverse events. Separately for children and adolescents (aged 0-17 years) and adults (aged ≥18 years), we calculated standardised mean differences (SMDs) and odds ratios (ORs) with random-effects pairwise and network meta-analysis. The certainty of evidence was assessed using the Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis (CINeMA) framework. People with lived experience of obsessive-compulsive disorder were involved in the entire review process, including protocol development, writing, and interpretation of the findings. The study was preregistered in Open Science Framework (identifier 4V3GS).

FINDINGS: We included 52 randomised controlled trials with 7316 participants. Nine trials were included for children and adolescents (N=822; median age 12·5 years [IQR 11·6-13·2]; 352 [55%] male and 285 [45%] female among 637 participants with reported sex distribution). 43 trials were included for adults (N=6494; median age 36·4 years [35·0-37·9]; 3105 [49%] male and 3200 [51%] female among 6305 participants with reported sex distribution). Data on ethnicity were reported in 15 (29%) of 52 randomised controlled trials, using inconsistent definitions. In children and adolescents, clomipramine was more efficacious than placebo (SMD -1·19, 95% CI -1·73 to -0·64, CINeMA low), as were sertraline (-0·52, -0·80 to -0·02, low), fluoxetine (-0·43, -0·76 to -0·10, low), and fluvoxamine (-0·39, -0·71 to -0·07, low). Clomipramine was more efficacious than fluoxetine (SMD -0·75, 95% CI -1·39 to -0·11; very low), fluvoxamine (-0·79, -1·43 to -0·16; very low), paroxetine (-0·87, -1·51 to -0·22; very low), and sertraline (-0·87, -1·51 to -0·22; very low). Only sertraline was less tolerable than placebo (OR 4·51, 95% CI 1·36 to 14·96; low). In adults, clomipramine (SMD -0·71, 95% CI -0·88 to -0·54; high), fluvoxamine (-0·63, -0·83 to -0·43; high), paroxetine (-0·51, -0·73 to -0·29; moderate), sertraline (-0·49, -0·70 to -0·28; moderate), escitalopram (-0·43, -0·85 to -0·02; moderate), and fluoxetine (-0·37, -0·6 to -0·08; moderate) were more efficacious than placebo, and clomipramine was more efficacious than fluoxetine (-0·34, -0·65 to -0·04; low). Clomipramine (OR 4·65, 95% CI 3·27 to 6·60; moderate), fluvoxamine (2·73, 1·77 to 4·20; moderate), fluoxetine (2·52, 1·27 to 4·98; moderate), sertraline (2·23, 1·49 to 3·34; moderate), and paroxetine (1·99, 1·36 to 2·93; moderate) were less tolerable than placebo. Clomipramine was less tolerable than all active comparators (OR range 1·70 to 3·50; moderate) except fluoxetine (moderate) and venlafaxine (low).

INTERPRETATION: Clomipramine showed the largest estimated efficacy for obsessive-compulsive disorder but had a lower tolerability than SSRIs. The evidence of its superiority over individual SSRIs was more pronounced in children and adolescents. Our findings suggest that the trade-off between efficacy and tolerability should be discussed within an individualised, shared, decision-making framework.

### Intervening on Therapy-Interfering Behavior
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Psychiatr Pract | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Borges LM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 1

Therapy-interfering behavior (TIB), although commonly encountered in clinical practice, often goes unaddressed. But if behaviors preventing treatment engagement are not addressed, patients may struggle to achieve their treatment goals, remain at a high chronic risk for suicide, and drop out of treatment. Therefore, to truly help our patients approach their stated goals and target suicide risk, it is critical to address TIB. In this second column of a 2-part series, we discuss common contexts in which TIBs emerge, how to explore the function of TIBs, and methods for addressing them.

### Deaths due to elderly suicide in Turkey: a retrospective study
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Geriatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hösükler E, Erkol ZZ | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 13

Background Understanding the dynamics of elderly suicides is of great importance for the development of effective prevention strategies. This study aims to identify the characteristics of elderly suicides and to assess the impact of the pandemic on these incidents. Methods In this study, we retrospectively analysed the suicides of adults aged 65 years and over who died by suicide in Turkey between 01/01/2014 and 31/12/2023 using the data of the Turkish Statistical Institute. Results A total of 4066 elderly suicides were identified. 75% of elderly suicide deaths occurred in males. Suicide rates were significantly higher among individuals aged ≥ 75 compared with those aged 65–69 (RR = 1.40, 95% CI: 1.30–1.50) and 70–74 (RR = 1.36, 95% CI: 1.26–1.47). Elderly males had a 3.83-fold higher suicide rate than females (RR = 3.83, 95% CI: 3.56–4.11). Population-adjusted Poisson regression showed that suicide rates increased significantly during the pandemic period compared to the pre-pandemic period (IRR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.02–1.18). This increase was significant among males (IRR = 1.11, 95% CI: 1.02–1.22) but not females (IRR = 1.02, 95% CI: 0.88–1.19). In the post-pandemic period, suicide rates declined marginally in both males (IRR = 0.91) and females (IRR = 0.84). Hanging was the most common method of suicide overall, whereas suicides involving firearms and those attributed to financial difficulties occurred predominantly among males. Conclusion Elderly suicide rates in Turkey were consistently higher among males and individuals aged ≥ 75 years. Higher population-adjusted suicide rates were observed during the COVID-19 period, particularly among elderly males, followed by a decline in the post-pandemic period. These findings highlight the importance of age- and sex-specific suicide prevention strategies and strengthening mental health and social support services for elderly adults. Background The elderly population is increasing every year and suicide remains an important public health problem in this very vulnerable population. Although elderly suicides declined before the pandemic, they still cause thousands of preventable deaths each year [ 1 ]. Elderly people remain at the top of the risk of death from suicide worldwide [ 2 ]. Compared to young people, elderly people are less likely to attempt suicide but more likely to die [ 3 ]. With ageing, losses in health, social roles, and relationships may lead to serious psychological distress [ 4 ] The rise of nuclear families has increased loneliness and social isolation among the elderly [ 5 ]. Among adults who commit suicide, it is estimated that the elderly are nine times more likely to live alone than other age groups [ 6 ]. Lack of social interaction, isolation and loneliness are the most important risk factors for suicidal behaviour in the elderly [ 7 ]. Especially depression, physical illnesses, previous suicide attempts, substance abuse, loneliness, marital status, financial stress, history of psychiatric disease or suicide in first-degree relatives and low social support are the factors that most frequently increase suicidal tendencies in elderly adults [ 1 , 8 ]. As the global elderly population grows, elderly suicide cases are expected to rise [ 9 ]. COVID-19, declared as an epidemic by World Health Organization (WHO) on 11 March 2020, was first seen in Turkey in March 2020. As most COVID-19 deaths occurred in those over 65, strict curfews and isolation were imposed on the elderly. These strategies have deepened social isolation and the decline in quality of life, which are among the risk factors for suicide in the elderly [ 10 ]. In addition, common psychiatric diseases and disorders, which are important risk factors for suicide, may be seen in individuals due to reasons such as quarantine practices during pandemic periods, fear of infection, insufficient information, and support [ 11 ]. In our study, we aimed to determine the suicide characteristics of adults over 65 years old who died due to suicide in the 10-year period between 2014 and 2023 and the change in suicide characteristics over the years by using the data of the Turkish Statistical Institute. Materials and methods This retrospective study examined suicides among adults aged 65 and older in Turkey between 2014 and 2023. Data were retrieved from the Turkish Statistical Institute’s (TSI) publicly available online reports. As the study used anonymised, retrospective TSI data, informed consent was not required. The study was approved by Abant Izzet Baysal University Clinical Research Ethics Committee (September 09, 2024 date and 2024/236 desicion no). Our research adhered to the ethical criteria outlined in the Helsinki Declaration of 1964, as revised in 2013. The evaluation focused on several aspects, including gender, the crude suicide rate, changes in suicide rates over the years, the impact of the pandemic, methods of suicide, and factors associated with suicide. Suicide deaths were identified from the Turkish Statistical Institute (TSI) national mortality database, which is compiled from official death registration records completed by law enforcement authorities and coded according to the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10). Deaths classified as intentional self-harm (ICD-10 codes X60–X84) were included in the present study. Information on suicide methods and reported reasons for suicide was obtained directly from the annual “Death and Cause of Death Statistics” published by TSI. Suicide methods (e.g., hanging, firearm, jumping from height, chemical intoxication, drowning, sharp instruments, and other methods) and reported reasons (e.g., illness, family conflict, economic difficulties, emotional relationship problems, educational failure, and unknown) were analysed using the predefined administrative categories provided by TSI without modification. The reported reason for suicide was recorded by the Turkish Statistical Institute (TSI) using information obtained from official death investigation records completed by law enforcement authorities. Each suicide death was assigned to a single predefined administrative category in the TSI database. The present study analysed these classifications as provided and did not independently verify or reclassify the reported reasons for suicide. The study period was categorised into three phases based on major changes in public health measures implemented in Turkey rather than the WHO declaration of the end of the Public Health Emergency of International Concern. The pre-pandemic period spanned from 2014 to 2019. The pandemic period included 2020–2021, corresponding to the years during which strict public health measures, including lockdowns, age-specific curfews, and social distancing restrictions, were implemented nationwide. The post-pandemic period was defined as 2022–2023 because most COVID-19-related restrictions had been lifted in Turkey by early 2022, allowing a return to routine social and healthcare activities. Statistical analysis R 4.4.0 version (R Core Team (2024). _R: A Language and Environment for Statistical Computing_. R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria. < https://www.R-project.org/ >) was used for data analysis of the study. The primary outcome variable was the annual number of suicide deaths among elderly adults (≥ 65 years). The corresponding annual elderly population obtained from the Turkish Statistical Institute (TSI) was used as the population at risk. Independent variables included calendar year, age group (65–69, 70–74, and ≥ 75 years), sex (male/female), pandemic period (pre-pandemic, pandemic, and post-pandemic), suicide method, and reported reason for suicide.
