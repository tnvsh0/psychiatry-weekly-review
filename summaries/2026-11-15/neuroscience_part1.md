### Gene discovery and pleiotropic architecture of multidimensional pain in a genome-wide association study of >1.2 million individuals
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Toikumo S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 12

Chronic pain is highly prevalent worldwide, yet its genetic architecture remains to be fully elucidated. Here we leveraged data from 1,235,695 European-ancestry individuals across three biobanks. In a meta-analytic genome-wide association study of multidimensional pain (MP), we identified 343 independent loci, 92 of which were new pain associations. Sex-specific meta-analyses revealed 115 independent loci (12 of which were new) among males (n = 583,066) and 12 loci (2 of which were new) among females (n = 241,266). Multi-omic gene prioritization analyses highlighted 490 genes associated with MP through their effects on brain-specific and blood-specific regulation. Mendelian randomization analyses showed causal associations between MP and psychiatric disorders, substance use disorders and C-reactive protein levels. MP variants also showed pleiotropic associations with several brain imaging-derived phenotypes. This study expands our knowledge of the genetics of pain and its pathogenesis, highlighting the importance of its pleiotropy with multiple disorders and elucidating its multi-omic pathophysiology.

In neuroscience, the most relevant human biology has often been the least accessible. Stem-cell-based models, such as organoids and assembloids, are opening ways to generate, assemble, probe, and perturb neural cells and circuits, helping define "stem cell neuroscience." In this issue of Neuron, this and other reviews and commentaries discuss the current state of this emerging field.

### Toward functional oversight of neural organoid research: A report from the 2025 Asilomar Conference

In November 2025, an international multistakeholder group convened at Asilomar, California, to address oversight of rapidly advancing neural organoid and assembloid research. This process report documents the convening's design, outcomes, and lessons, and it asks what it will take to turn this momentum into ongoing monitoring and practical guidelines that ensure responsible research without constraining scientific progress.

### Hypersensitivity to adrenomedullin in post-traumatic headache: a randomized crossover study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Al-Khazali HM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Adrenomedullin (ADM), a vasoactive member of the calcitonin peptide family with receptor-signalling overlap with calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), has emerged as a candidate therapeutic target in migraine. Given the striking clinical parallels between migraine and persistent post-traumatic headache (PTH), we investigated whether ADM-responsive signalling contributes to migraine-like headache in adults with persistent PTH attributed to mild traumatic brain injury. We conducted a single-centre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, two-way crossover study in adults with persistent PTH and no pre-trauma history of migraine. On two experimental days separated by at least 7 days, participants received 20-minute intravenous infusions of ADM (19.9 pmol kg⁻¹ min⁻¹) and isotonic saline as the matched inactive comparator (placebo) in randomised sequence. Headache outcomes were recorded for 12 hours. The primary outcome was the within-participant incidence of experimentally induced migraine-like headache. The secondary outcome was the within-participant difference in baseline-corrected area under the curve (AUC) for headache intensity from 0 to 12 hours. Of 158 individuals assessed for eligibility, 21 were randomised, completed both periods and were analysed. Participants had a mean age of 34.3 years, 19 (90%) were female, and 18 (86%) reported a migraine-like phenotype. Migraine-like headache occurred in 17 of 21 participants (81%) after ADM and four of 21 (19%) following placebo. Fourteen participants developed migraine-like only after ADM and one only following placebo, yielding a paired absolute risk difference of 61.9 percentage points (95% CI 29.7-80.6; one-sided P < 0.001; two-sided P = 0.001). Baseline-corrected headache-intensity AUC was higher after ADM than placebo (median 1,110 [IQR 315-2205] versus 420 [-160 to 565]; P = 0.006). Median time to onset of migraine-like headache was 30 minutes after ADM and 390 minutes following placebo. Fifteen participants reported that the ADM-induced headache resembled their usual migraine-like headache episodes, and rescue medication was used by 10 participants after ADM and two after placebo. These findings identify pronounced responsiveness to ADM-linked signalling in persistent PTH and support the ADM pathway as a candidate target for mechanism-based therapeutic development, pending confirmation in independent cohorts and complementary preclinical studies.

### Mosaic CDKL5 deficiency drives progressive inhibitory circuit remodelling
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mottolese N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

CDKL5 deficiency disorder predominantly affects heterozygous females, in whom random X-chromosome inactivation generates cellular CDKL5 mosaicism. Although mosaicism is a defining feature of the disease, whether it actively drives disease progression or simply reflects the genetic mode of inheritance remains unknown. Here, we combined age-comparative electrophysiology, quantitative synaptic imaging, transcriptomic profiling and postmortem human brain analyses to determine how mosaic CDKL5 deficiency shapes inhibitory circuit remodeling during disease progression. Heterozygous Cdkl5 female mice exhibited striking age- and region-specific remodeling of inhibitory neurotransmission. Hippocampal inhibition was initially enhanced but progressively declined with age, whereas cortical inhibitory transmission remained preserved at 3 months and increased in middle-aged animals. These functional changes were accompanied by dynamic remodeling of inhibitory presynaptic terminals, persistent disruption of inhibitory synapse organization and reduced hippocampal gephyrin-positive puncta despite preservation of the major inhibitory interneuron populations. At 12 months, heterozygous females also exhibited a higher density of IBA1/CD68 double-positive microglia in both regions compared with age-matched wild-type controls. Importantly, complete Cdkl5 deficiency failed to reproduce the progressive loss of inhibitory presynaptic terminals observed in heterozygous females, indicating that this phenotype is specific to the mosaic condition rather than a simple consequence of complete Cdkl5 loss. Transcriptomic profiling revealed a temporal transition from alterations detected at 3 months in immune-synaptic regulatory pathways to activity-dependent transcriptional programs associated with reduced structural and homeostatic support, consistent with progressive hippocampal circuit reorganization. Exploratory analyses of postmortem cortical tissue from individuals with CDD revealed differences in inhibitory presynaptic labeling between the available young and adult cases, suggesting that inhibitory alterations may also vary with age in human CDD. Together, these findings identify mosaic CDKL5 deficiency as a biologically distinct circuit state characterized by progressive, region-specific inhibitory circuit remodeling and provide a mechanistic framework for disease progression in CDKL5 deficiency disorder.

### The Neural Correlates of Sickness
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Lanovoi H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

The brain protects animals during infection by detecting immune activity, coordinating behavioral and physiological defenses, anticipating potential infectious threats, and limiting excessive inflammation. Collectively termed sickness, these centrally organized responses include changes in body temperature, activity, energy balance, appetite, fluid intake, social behavior, neuroendocrine function, and autonomic output. Rather than constituting a single uniform state, sickness is generated by distributed and partially dissociable neural pathways that translate immune signals into responses appropriate to the nature and stage of an infection. Work presented at a symposium at the 2026 Society for Neuroscience meeting highlights how humoral and cranial sensory pathways detect ongoing immune responses; how social cues, expectations, and prior experience prepare the organism for anticipated threats; and how endocrine and autonomic circuits constrain inflammation and promote recovery. Together, these findings establish the brain as an active regulator of immunity across the full course of illness.

### Arousal state fluctuations destabilize speech intelligibility in older adults
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Smith SS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Understanding speech in noisy, multi-talker environments is fundamental for social communication but becomes increasingly challenging as we age. Age-related hearing loss reduces audibility and degrades speech encoding, yet these deficits do not fully explain the momentary lapses in speech understanding that frustrate real-time communication. Here, we tested whether brain state fluctuations immediately before speech onset predict forthcoming speech recognition errors in older listeners. We measured the accuracy, speed, and consistency of speech recognition in 183 adults aged 18-76 years (103 female) using a brief, trial-based multi-talker listening task while tracking pupil dynamics before and during speech presentation. Prestimulus pupil-linked arousal predicted trial-level speech recognition in older, but not younger, adults, independent of hearing status, cognitive load, and task accuracy. In adults over 50 years of age, lower and higher extremes of prestimulus pupil size were associated with less accurate and slower responses, producing a striking inverted-U relationship between prestimulus arousal and forthcoming behavioral performance. Older adults exhibited lower extremes of pupil-linked arousal that were specifically associated with greater self-reported difficulty listening in everyday life. By contrast, stimulus-evoked pupil dilation during speech encoding, a measure of listening effort, did not predict trial-level performance at any age. These findings identify prestimulus pupil-linked arousal as a hidden marker of forthcoming lapses in speech intelligibility in older adults. When considered alongside conventional measures of peripheral hearing status in mixed-effects regression models, these measures provide a more complete accounting of biological changes underlying speech intelligibility deficits that frustrate social communication in older adults .   Significance statement  As we age, following a conversation in crowded environments becomes more difficult and frustrating, even when hearing tests appear normal. This study shows that fluctuations in arousal level (measured through prestimulus pupil size) are tightly linked to fluctuations in the speed and accuracy of speech processing in adults over 50 years of age. Changes in arousal state regulation uniquely accounted for the moment-to-moment variations in speech intelligibility and were more closely associated with self-reported listening challenges than other conventional measures. These findings highlight the contribution of brain-wide arousal systems to real-world listening challenges and identify a straightforward measure that could be used to more comprehensively assess and potentially improve speech understanding in social settings.
