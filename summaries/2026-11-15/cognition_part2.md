### How explanations evolve: Social learning and the explore-exploit trade-off
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognitive Psychology (Cogn Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kedrick K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Explanations are social objects, and when people try to explain something, they usually have at hand the explanations that others have tried before. We present a theory of how people use the explanations they encounter as clues to the broader landscape of possible explanations for the phenomenon to hand, which in turn informs their decisions about how and when to forage further for the explanations they wish to adopt. The challenge of coming up with novel explanations draws people to exploit or imitate appealing ones they have already encountered; this draw increases as less appealing alternatives become more distant (the "strawman" effect). Conversely, when presented with distinct, high-quality explanations, people can synthesize them, recombining the appealing features in new ways. We test and confirm these predictions using a two-stage transmission chain experiment. In Stage One, 101 participants were asked to explain unusual scenarios. In Stage Two, a separate group of 995 participants were asked to explain the same scenarios after reading pairs of explanations drawn from Stage One. We found that participants in Stage Two were more likely to exploit, or imitate, explanations when at least one was high in quality and particularly when it was paired with a weaker alternative. Participants were also more likely to synthesize perspectives from prior explanations when the two explanations were both high-quality and conceptually distinct. In contrast, participants were more likely to explore, or diverge, from prior explanations when the two explanations were both of low quality and distinct. Our work provides new insight into how collective exploration can be promoted, or stalled, by implicit information about what is yet to be discovered.

### Metarepresentation and the puzzle of desire-attribution
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Westra E, Foster GR | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 9

Among theory-of-mind researchers, it's widely agreed that children acquire the capacity to attribute desires well before they acquire the capacity to attribute beliefs. This suggests that there is something about the concept of desire that makes it less cognitively challenging than the concept of belief, but what could that be? One possibility is that the difference has something to do with the development of the capacity for metarepresentation, but this just raises more questions: according to the standard philosophical model of the propositional attitudes, the concepts of belief and desire share the same basic metarepresentational structure. First, we review three potential solutions to this developmental puzzle and discuss their limitations. Then we propose a novel non-metarepresentational account of young children's concept of desire that grounds the contents of desire attributions in a type of representation that we call modal affordances.

### How do US children and adults make sense of unequal compensation in division of labor?
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Aldan P, Dunham Y | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Division of labor is a ubiquitous feature of social life, as it allows people to specialize and reach desired outcomes efficiently. However, it also creates pay disparities, as some roles are compensated more than others. Here we examined how U.S. children (105 6-12-year-olds [52 girls, 52 boys, 1 other; 37 Asian, 51 other, 17 race unknown]) and adults (N = 108 [Mage = 43.39; 70 women, 36 men, 2 other; 91 White, 17 other]) make sense of why some roles receive higher pay. Children, like adults, are more likely to perceive unequal compensation as fair in division of labor compared to cooperative arrangements where all participants perform the same tasks (i.e., "parallel cooperation"). Also, while older children are more likely to see unequal pay as unfair in parallel cooperation, they continue to see unequal compensation as fair in division-of-labor cases. Participant explanations provide a potential explanation: a circular meritocratic logic, in which participants assume that higher pay signals greater difficulty, and then use perceived difficulty to justify those same wage disparities. This tendency does not depend on actual job characteristics, and increases with age in the child sample. We discuss how this may help rationalize lower pay for certain occupations-such as assuming female-coded roles like caregiving are easy simply because they offer lower pay.

### Heterogeneity in holistic face processing disruptions in developmental prosopagnosia
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chapman AF et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Developmental prosopagnosia (DP) is a striking disorder characterised by lifelong deficits in face recognition. Research into the nature of DP has broad implications for understanding interrelated theoretical issues in vision, neural organisation, and development. One long-standing but unresolved question is whether holistic face processing, the integration of individual face features into a unified whole, is compromised in DP. Here, we test a large sample of DPs (N = 113), using the three signature effects of holistic processing: the inversion, composite, and part-whole effects. This approach allows us to characterise the extent and pattern of holistic reductions across multiple effects within the same sample and to assess relationships between them. We found that holistic processing is reduced across all three effects in DP. The magnitude of reductions varied across the different effects; however, follow-up analyses indicated that reductions in the composite effect were slightly less robust. We found little evidence linking these reductions to DP severity, though limited reliability in the holistic measures may constrain these conclusions. While we observed reductions in holistic processing at the group level, few individual DPs met the thresholds for impairment. Our findings suggest that DP is associated with weaker holistic effects, but do not support the notion that impairments in holistic processing are necessary for DP.

### Early self-regulation in context: Multidimensional organization of neonatal non-nutritive sucking and its association with subclinical maternal depressive traits
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Faré O et al. | **תאריך:** 2027 Mar

From the very first days of life, infants' affective regulation unfolds within a dyadic co-regulatory system shaped by caregiver responsivity. In contexts of reduced maternal availability, such as maternal depressive states, infants may be required to compensate for diminished interpersonal regulation by increasingly relying on their internal resources. Neonatal non-nutritive sucking (NNS) represents one of the earliest spontaneously available regulatory behaviors, contributing to physiological and behavioral homeostasis from birth. However, it remains unclear whether its spontaneous organization is associated with early relational vulnerability indexed by maternal depressive symptoms. The present study aimed to characterize the spontaneous organization of NNS in a sample of newborns (N = 42) and to examine whether individual differences in NNS are associated with maternal postnatal depressive subclinical traits measured using the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS). Results from an exploratory principal component analysis indicated that neonatal sucking behavior was organized along two functional dimensions, reflecting overall sucking effort and behavioral activation, and the temporal organization of sucking bursts. Importantly, higher maternal depressive traits, though subclinical, were selectively associated with increased sucking effort, with infants of mothers reporting higher depressive traits showing more frequent and intense sucking, while no differences emerged for the organizational dimension. Overall, these findings suggest that individual differences in maternal depressive traits within the subclinical range may be associated with neonatal NNS effort, a precocious compensatory mobilization of regulatory resources, potentially serving both to support autonomous regulation and to elicit maternal engagement.

### Gesture use in autobiographical memory narration: The comparison of blind and sighted early teenagers
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Güneş-Acar N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Gestures are an integral component of speech. During autobiographical memory narration, gestures can be linked to the episodic quality of recalled events, facilitating speech production or supporting visuospatial processing. Gestures' role in children's autobiographical memory, particularly in blindness, remains unclear. The present study examined how cue modality (visually known, haptically known, abstract) related to episodic details of autobiographical memories and gesture use in blind and sighted early teenagers, and whether gesture use was associated with the episodic richness of recalled memories. Thirty-three blind and thirty-six sighted early teenagers were asked to recall autobiographical memories using a cue-word methodology. The recalled memories were subsequently coded for episodic and non-episodic details, as well as for representational and non-representational gesture use. Results showed that memory content varied as a function of cue modality in a similar manner across groups, with haptically-known cues eliciting fewer episodic details than visually-known and abstract cues. This cue-modality effect was attenuated when controlling for age, suggesting it may be partly age-driven. Blind teenagers produced more non-episodic details than sighted ones despite comparable levels of episodic content. Sighted teenagers gestured more frequently than blind teenagers, although gesture use did not vary by cue modality. Crucially, gesture frequency was not related to episodic quality of autobiographical memories in either group. These findings suggest that while gestures may support autobiographical retrieval in adults capable of producing extended, coherent narratives, their role may be more limited in sighted and blind teenagers, potentially due to developmental differences in scene construction and narrative ability.
