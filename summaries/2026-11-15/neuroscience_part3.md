### Enhancer-targeted CRISPR-A rescues haploinsufficiency and mutant phenotypes in organoid models of autism
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chen GT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a highly heritable condition with diverse clinical presentations. Approximately 15% of the genetic susceptibility of ASD is imparted by de novo haploinsufficient mutations of major effect. We mapped enhancers of two high-confidence autism genes, CHD8 and SCN2A, and used CRISPR-based gene activation targeting enhancers (eCRISPR-A) in human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived excitatory neurons and cerebral forebrain organoids to correct the effects of haploinsufficiency, taking advantage of the presence of a wild-type allele of each gene and endogenous gene regulation. Here we show that eCRISPR-A induced a sustained increase in CHD8 and SCN2A expression specifically in mutant neurons and cortical organoids, with rescue of gene expression levels and mutation-associated phenotypes, including gene expression and physiology. These data support gene activation via targeting enhancers of haploinsufficient genes as a therapeutic intervention in ASD and other neurodevelopmental disorders.

### Brain organoids: Experimental access to human-specific brain development
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Kastli R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Human cerebral cortical development includes species-divergent cellular programs and unusually prolonged trajectories that remain difficult to investigate experimentally. These processes support human-specific cognitive abilities and are thought to be associated with vulnerability to neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric disorders. Human brain organoids provide a tractable system for studying these processes across developmental time and under controlled perturbation. Here, we review how cortical organoids model human-specific features of progenitor biology and cell diversification, as well as progressive cellular maturation and circuit formation. We consider the limitations that constrain later-stage maturation and how approaches including the addition of missing cell types, the use of assembloids, and transplantation can extend the developmental processes accessible to study. Finally, we examine how patient-derived, isogenic, chimeric, and perturbation-based organoid systems are being used to investigate neurodevelopmental disorders and, in turn, reveal mechanisms of typical human development.

### Unbundling the ethical and social issues in human neural organoid research
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Greely HT, Farahany N | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

As human neural organoid research advances, some key concepts in its ethical analysis need to be unbundled to keep pace with scientific developments. These include interests involved in informed consent, distinct dimensions of consciousness and their implications for welfare, approaches to engaging public concerns, and governance and oversight of both organoids themselves and their uses.

### Insight discordance differentiates behavioural variant frontotemporal dementia from false positives
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Flavell J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Behavioural variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) can present with behavioural symptoms similar to psychiatric mimics of the disease (sometimes termed 'phenocopies'). Despite this overlap, loss of insight is a cardinal feature of bvFTD unlike most psychiatric disorders. This study tested whether insight could be quantified to improve diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing these conditions, and examined neuroimaging correlates of impaired insight. In this prospective cohort study, 27 patients with frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD) presenting as the bvFTD phenotype and 25 false-positives of bvFTD who ultimately had a psychiatric disorder underwent patient, informant, and clinician symptom ratings on a modified Frontal Behavioural Inventory (FBI). Discrepancy scores were calculated, diagnostic discrimination was evaluated by area under the curve (AUC) with leave-one-out cross-validation, alongside a neuroimaging analysis of network-level associations of insight in bvFTD. Patients with bvFTD endorsed fewer symptoms than false-positives (FBI 6.62 ± 8.80 vs 15.36 ± 8.42, P < 0.001), whereas clinician observations were markedly greater in bvFTD (27.67 ± 8.35 vs 5.64 ± 3.85, P < 0.001). Informant ratings did not differ between groups (25.15 ± 11.11 vs 21.32 ± 12.61, P = 0.14), even though bvFTD informants had more knowledge of the disease (FTD Knowledge Scale 16.20 ± 6.68 vs 9.41 ± 8.19, P = 0.003). A model incorporating patient-clinician and informant-clinician discrepancy showed excellent cross-validated discrimination (AUC 0.99, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.97-1.00). A model independent of expert clinician ratings, the patient-informant discrepancy, showed fair cross-validated discrimination across the full cohort (AUC 0.71, 95% CI 0.56-0.85) and good discrimination in those who did not develop another FTLD syndrome on follow-up ('pure bvFTD'; AUC 0.78, 95% CI 0.63-0.94). In bvFTD, reduced insight was associated with salience and semantic appraisal network degeneration but not control frontotemporal regions. BvFTD, false-positives, and their informants demonstrated opposing patterns of symptom endorsement. Symptom under-endorsement (lack of insight) by patients relative to clinician and informant observation supports neurodegeneration, whereas informant and patient over-endorsement without clinician-observed disturbance suggests a false-positive. Unfamiliarity with bvFTD may contribute to over-reporting. Though the clinician-dependent findings may not translate to non-expert settings, the patient-informant model retained good discrimination. Formal quantification of discrepancy patterns in behaviour may reduce misdiagnosis in bvFTD. The neuroimaging findings support salience and semantic appraisal networks as the neuroanatomical basis for insight in bvFTD. Future studies should validate the FBI clinician version and examine factors underlying over-reporting by false-positives and their informants.

### Btbd11 regulates glutamatergic synapse organization in GABAergic inhibitory interneurons
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Boyer MB et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

The mechanisms that underlie glutamatergic synapse organization and function in GABAergic inhibitory interneurons (INs) are not well described, despite evidence that impaired glutamatergic excitation of INs is implicated in psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia and anxiety. Glutamatergic synapses received by INs have unique basal transmission properties and exhibit distinct synaptic plasticity compared to those received by excitatory neurons, likely due to cell-type specific differences in postsynaptic density (PSD) composition and maintenance mechanisms. In the present study, we show that the interneuron-specific protein Btbd11 regulates excitatory synapse transmission in mixed-sex primary cultured rat and mouse hippocampal interneurons through promotion of phase separation and support of postsynaptic nanoarchitecture. Btbd11 forms a phase separated protein complex with Psd-95 and TARPγ2 and impacts the stability of TARPγ2 and GluA1 within glutamatergic IN synapses in an expression- and phase separation-dependent manner. Using super resolution imaging, we show that Btbd11 displays nanoscale clustering properties within IN synapses that correlate with Psd-95 nanostructure. Furthermore, genetic deletion of Btbd11 decreases PSD protein expression, reduces synapse size, and disrupts Psd-95 nanocluster organization. These effects manifest as a drastic reduction in glutamatergic synaptic transmission onto INs when Btbd11 is deleted. Together, these data provide insights into a novel cell type-specific synaptic regulatory mechanism in an understudied synapse population. Significance Statement  Although glutamatergic excitation of GABAergic inhibitory interneurons (INs) is essential for maintenance of balanced signaling in the brain and dysregulation has been linked to neuropsychiatric disorders, the molecular mechanisms underlying IN-specific glutamatergic transmission are not well characterized. Using protein modeling, molecular biology, live-cell and super-resolution imaging, we describe a unique mechanism of glutamatergic synapse organization in INs by Btbd11, an IN-specific synaptic protein. Btbd11 promotes liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) and regulates PSD protein mobility, the abundance of key synaptic proteins, and nanoscale synaptic organization. Collectively, this is critical for glutamatergic synaptic transmission within INs. Understanding Btbd11's role at the synapse highlights the importance of synaptic LLPS, providing insight into the regulatory mechanisms in an understudied synapse with psychiatric disease relevance.
