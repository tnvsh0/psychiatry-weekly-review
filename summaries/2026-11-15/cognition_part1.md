### Older yet faster: Representational development explains accelerated semantic processing in older adults
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Review (Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 9

Slower responses in older age are often taken as evidence for cognitive decline, yet they may also stem from representational changes shaped by lifelong learning. Building on the information-accumulation perspective on aging, we argue that behavioral change across the lifespan can arise from representational systems continuously updated through learning from the environment. This view predicts that aging may bring both benefits and costs: Performance improves when task demands are congruent with accumulated knowledge but declines when they conflict. We provide convergent evidence for this account across three studies, each comprising the following components. First, in tasks requiring participants to access word meanings (i.e., when task demands were congruent with representation), we found that older adults (70-80 years old) exhibited faster semantic processing speed than younger adults (18-25 years old), a finding that cannot be explained by decline-based accounts. Second, we traced this processing advantage to systematic changes in older adults' conceptual representations inferred from semantic rating tasks. Third, using learning simulation models, we demonstrated how such representational changes could emerge naturally from cumulative language experience. Replications across different samples, task requirements, and word stimuli strengthen the robustness of these findings. Finally, we identified a boundary condition: When learned representations contain information incongruent with task demands, older adults' greater learning experience not only attenuates their representational changes but also reduces processing-speed advantage. Altogether, our research challenges the explanatory framework of cognitive aging that emphasizes decline and compensation, advancing a more balanced view grounded in representational development and lifelong learning. ### Repetition bias in self-directed learning
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ten A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 8

Self-directed learning is a major part of individual knowledge formation. People often autonomously organize their own learning into "blocks": they practice a certain task for some continuous stretch of time, then switch to another task for another period of time, and so on. Yet current models of intrinsic motivation highlighting epistemic factors such as novelty, competence, and learning progress make various predictions about the extent to which learners block, as opposed to interleaving, their practice. In this study, we demonstrate that people tend to block in a multi-task setting where existing models of intrinsic motivation based on epistemic factors make ambiguous predictions. We show that while epistemic factors can partially explain blocking, a simple repetition bias substantially improves our ability to predict choices.

### Communication about color over the life span
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Brown AM, Lindsey DT | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 12

Color communication is more than a naming game where the informant names each color: the color terms must also be understood by the listener. Here, we studied how participants' productive and receptive vocabularies affect communication about color over the life span. Human participants, 3-72 years old, played a color communication game. As "senders", participants named 15 or 30 color samples presented singly. Then, as "receivers", they selected from their palette of 15 or 30 colors the sample that corresponded to color term "messages," which were either their own color terms (self-communication) or another participant's color terms (interpersonal communication). The number of color terms in participants' vocabularies, their fraction correct performance for self-communication, and their fraction correct for interpersonal communication, all matured continuously until at least age 21, and were highly correlated with each other. Simulations using a quantitative model of the communication task showed that performance for self-communication was determined by the number of color terms the participant provided in the color-naming phase (color term production), with some forgetting of how they had previously named the samples. The simulation predicted no association between participant age and performance on the interpersonal communication task. Contrary to that prediction, interpersonal communication improved throughout the age range. Further analysis showed that performance for interpersonal communication was independently controlled by life-long improvement in participants' ability to understand terms provided by others (color term comprehension). Therefore, color term production and color term comprehension are distinct abilities, which mature independently across the life span.

### Testing a secondary motor integration account of confidence computation
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Garnier-Allain A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 13

A growing body of empirical evidence suggests that confidence judgments are modulated by action-related cues, yet the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Recent decision-making models propose that the evidence accumulation variable is continuously transmitted to the motor system, allowing motor plans to evolve in parallel with decision formation. At the motor level, a secondary leaky integration process acts as a filter that regulates the speed-accuracy tradeoff: the stronger the filter, the slower and more accurate the responses. Here, we tested whether action-related effects on confidence are mediated by this secondary motor integration process. We manipulated sensory noise using a doubly stochastic random dot motion task and varied the motor-level filter via speed-accuracy instructions. We reasoned that the positive effect of sensory noise on confidence observed in previous studies should be attenuated by stronger filtering in the accuracy condition compared to the speed condition. However, we found strong empirical evidence against this qualitative prediction. Computational modeling further revealed that the predicted attenuation was extremely small and that confidence predictions based on secondary motor integration offered no advantage over those based on evidence accumulation. Together, these results show that confidence need not be computed from secondary motor integration to account for the observed pattern.

### Young children engage in choice-relative moral reasoning
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Waltzer T et al. | **תאריך:** 2027 Mar

A central challenge in moral reasoning is evaluating actions not only by their outcomes, but also by the alternatives that were available at the time of choice. The present study examined whether preschool-age children engage in this form of choice-relative moral reasoning. Using a child-friendly version of the trolley problem, 3- to 5-year-old children (N = 124, 51% girls) judged a protagonist who moved a barrier to protect three children's cookies from being eaten by a mean squirrel. Children typically judged this action as morally good when no better alternative was available, even when it caused harm to another child. However, they judged the same action as morally bad when a non-harmful alternative was available but not chosen. These findings suggest that preschool-age children evaluate actions in light of available alternatives rather than solely on the basis of outcomes. Thus, even young children appear to consider what else could have been done when making moral judgments.

### Do parental questions and gestures support executive functions in toddlers born preterm and full-term?
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yılmaz B, Göksun T | **תאריך:** 2027 Mar

This study examined parental questions and gestures in the development of executive functions (EF) in preterm (PT) and full-term (FT) toddlers. Sixty-four Turkish-speaking parent-child dyads (32 girls, Mage = 26.1 months, SD = 1.33, 25 PT) were observed in free-play sessions. The form (i.e., WH, yes/no) and function (i.e., pedagogical, information-seeking, rhetorical) of parental questions and gestures accompanying them were coded. Children's working memory and cognitive flexibility skills were measured using Hide the Pots and Reverse Categorization (pre- and post-switch) tasks, respectively. Results showed that PT and FT children had similar EF skills. Parental WH questions were associated with PT children's working memory skills. Parental WH questions, not pedagogical ones, were related to children's cognitive flexibility skills, regardless of neonatal status. When accompanied by gestures, both WH and pedagogical questions were associated with PT children's working memory and cognitive flexibility skills. These results highlight the importance of specific parental input as a function of children's neonatal status in the development of EFs.
