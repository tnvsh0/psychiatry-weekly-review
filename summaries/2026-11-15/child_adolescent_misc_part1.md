### Altered dynamic brain states and potential transcriptomic correlates in autism spectrum disorder
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain Imaging Behav *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 11

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is associated with widespread alterations in functional brain networks, but its transient dynamic brain states and potential transcriptomic correlates remain unclear. We applied Leading Eigenvector Dynamics Analysis (LEiDA) to resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging data from 110 individuals with ASD and 110 healthy controls (HCs) from the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange I (ABIDE I) dataset. Five recurrent phase-locking states were identified, and dynamic brain state metrics were compared between groups. Compared with HCs, individuals with ASD showed reduced fractional occupancy (FO) of State 5, which was predominantly characterized by the visual network with additional involvement of the sensorimotor network. Exploratory analyses within the ASD group revealed nominal negative associations between State 4 FO and ADOS total and social scores. To investigate potential transcriptomic correlates, we derived an ROI-wise ASD-HC State 5 weighted beta map and related it to Allen Human Brain Atlas (AHBA) gene expression data and SFARI ASD risk gene sets. SFARI ASD risk gene sets showed a consistent pattern of negative spatial associations with the ASD-related State 5 imaging phenotype, but these associations did not reach statistical significance after BrainSMASH testing and FDR correction. Exploratory rank-based Gene Ontology (GO) analysis implicated biological functions related to neuronal structure, synaptic function, and the regulation of neuronal excitability. These findings identify alterations in dynamic brain state organization in ASD and provide exploratory transcriptomic clues to their potential biological context.

### Understanding verbal and numerical probability information and decision making among individuals with and without autism spectrum disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 Psychon Bull Rev | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Honda H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 12

This study examined how individuals with and without autism spectrum disorder (ASD) interpret probabilistic information expressed verbally or numerically, and how such interpretations influence decision-making. Two experiments were conducted: Experiment 1 included 48 participants with ASD and 41 participants without ASD in the final analyses, and Experiment 2 included an independent sample of 49 participants without ASD. Participants translated verbal probability expressions (e.g., likely, doubtful) into numerical estimates and made hypothetical treatment recommendations based on either verbal or numerical probabilities. Compared with non-ASD individuals, participants with ASD showed less differentiated numerical translations across verbal probability expressions, indicating greater similarity in the numerical meanings assigned to different expressions. A hierarchical Bayesian analysis followed by cluster analysis identified three decision-making profiles. Notably, individuals with ASD were more likely to belong to a cluster characterized by stronger sensitivity to the directionality (positive vs. negative framing) of verbal probability expressions and weaker reliance on numerical translation. In contrast, non-ASD individuals were more frequently represented in a cluster that relied primarily on numerical information. These findings suggest that individuals with and without ASD differ in how verbal probability expressions are interpreted and incorporated into decision making, highlighting the importance of considering individual differences in the communication of uncertainty.

### Neuregulin 2 and tubulin protein levels in children with autism spectrum disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Neurol | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Alayadhi L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 19

Background Multiple lines of evidence suggest that neuregulin 2 (NRG2) and tubulin play important roles in the neuron cytoskeleton, neuronal development, synaptic plasticity, and downstream signaling pathways implicated in autism spectrum disorder (ASD). This study aimed to investigate plasma levels of NRG2 and tubulin in ASD and to examine their association with the ASD severity and social impairment. Method Plasma levels of NRG2 and tubulin were measured using a sandwich enzyme immunoassay in 40 children with ASD and 40 age-matched healthy controls. Autism severity and social impairment were evaluated by the childhood autism rating scale (CARS) and Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS) respectively. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to determine the area under the curve (AUC), and Spearman’s correlation coefficients (r) were calculated to examine the relationships between NRG2 and tubulin levels and CARS, SRS, and age using SPSS. Results Significantly decreased plasma levels of NRG2 were observed in children with ASD ( n = 40, 4.27 ± 0.26 ng/ml [mean ± SEM]) compared with healthy controls ( n = 40, 5.44 ± 0.42 ng/ml, [mean ± SEM], p < 0.05). Similarly, plasma tubulin levels were significantly lower in children with ASD ( n = 37, 3.65 ± 0.42 ng /ml) than in healthy controls ( n = 38, 6.05 ± 0.48 ng /ml), [mean ± SEM], p < 0.05). Segregation of children with ASD into ASD level 2 and ASD level 3 autism revealed that both the ASD level 2 ( n = 17, 3.03 ± 0.40 ng /ml, p = 0.001) and ASD level 3 group ( n = 20, 4.18 ± 0.57 ng /ml p = 0.01) had significantly lower tubulin levels ( p < 0.05), compared with control children. NRG2 was significantly positively correlated with SRS scores (r = 0.434, p = 0.005), whereas tubulin exhibited significant diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.743, 95% CI [0.146–0.369], p < 0.001). Conclusion Our findings highlight the potential involvement of NRG2 and tubulin in the pathophysiology of ASD, particularly in relation to neuronal development and synaptic function. Their association with social ability of children with ASD and diagnostic sensitivity suggests that they may serve as promising candidate biomarkers. Further large-scale studies are needed to confirm these findings and clarify their roles in ASD pathogenesis.

Introduction Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a group of neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by impairments in social communication, as well as restricted and repetitive behaviors and interests, as defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders 5 (DSM-5) [ 1 ]. The worldwide prevalence of ASD is estimated to be approximately 1 in 127 individuals [ 2 ]. A study from the United States involving 11 communities in the Autism and Developmental Disabilities Monitoring Network reported a prevalence of 1 in 36 children (2.8%) [ 3 ]. In Saudi Arabia, the prevalence of autism varies, however, a recent study reported a prevalence of 2.51% among children aged 2–4 years, with a male to female ratio of 3:1, while other studies have reported lower prevalence rates [ 4 ]. Neuregulins are soluble proteins that function as growth and differentiation factors and are structurally related to the epidermal growth factor (EGF) family. The neuregulin family includes four isoforms, NRG1, NRG2, NRG3 and NRG4, which are involved in the development and homeostasis of the nervous system, heart, and other organs. The interaction of neuregulins with ErbB receptors plays an essential role in several biological processes in the nervous system, including neuronal migration and development, synaptic plasticity, and the regulation of neuromuscular receptor expression [ 5 ]. NRG1 plays a critical role in nervous system development and is highly expressed in the embryonic and fetal brain. In contrast, NRG2 transcript levels increase markedly after birth, suggesting a role in adulthood. NRG2 is persistently expressed in the cerebellum, olfactory bulb and dentate gyrus, highlighting its potential involvement in neurodevelopment disorders such as ASD [ 6 , 7 ]. NRG2 has been shown to regulate N-methyle-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor function through ErbB4 signalling in the postnatal brain and has been associated with cognitive impairment in psychiatric disorders [ 8 ]. Furthermore, NRG2 knockout animal studies have revealed dopamine dysregulation in the dorsal striatum and medial prefrontal cortex, accompanied by behavioral abnormalities resembling those observed in psychiatric disorders [ 9 ]. Tubulin is a globular protein composed of α- and β- tubulin heterodimers that polymerize to form protofilaments, which subsequently assemble into microtubules, the primary components of the cytoskeleton in all eukaryotic cells [ 10 ]. Mutations in α- and β tubulin genes have been associated with a range of neurodevelopmental disorders [ 11 ]. Microtubules are essential for maintaining cytoskeleton structure, supporting synaptic plasticity, and facilitating axonal growth and maintenance. They also serve as intracellular transport tracks for the movement of specific proteins and organelles [ 12 ]. Disruptions in microtubule organization can impair neuronal proliferation, migration and synaptogenesis. Myelination, the process of insulating nerve fibers, is essential for efficient neuronal communication and overall brain function. Abnormal myelination has been reported in individuals with ASD, further supporting the involvement of cytoskeletal and microtubule-related pathways in the pathophysiology of the disorder [ 13 ]. Thus, NRG2 and tubulin are involved in neuronal development, function and signaling pathways that are critical for normal brain function, and their impairment or dysregulation may contribute to the etiology of psychiatric disorders such as autism. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to investigate the plasma levels of NRG2 and tubulin proteins in patients with ASD compared with age-matched healthy control children. Patients were diagnosed with ASD by certified consultant neurologist according to the criteria of the 5th edition of the diagnostic and statistical manual of mental disorders criteria (American Psychiatric Association, 2013). The ASD group comprised 40 male children aged 3–12 years (mean ± SD = 6.82 ± 2.37 years). Patients with neurological disorders (such as palsy or tuberous sclerosis), metabolic disorders (e.g., phenylketonuria or diabetes), or autoimmune diseases were excluded from the study. ### The role of bilateral temporal poles activity mediating the association between anxiety symptoms and sleep problems in children with autism spectrum disorder
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 19

BACKGROUND: Sleep problems and anxiety symptoms frequently occur in children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). This investigation sought to examine the shared neural mechanisms that underly the co-occurrence of anxiety symptoms and sleep problems in this population.

METHODS: A total of 163 children with ASD were recruited. The anxiety symptoms and sleep problems were assessed through the Child Behavior Checklist and Children's Sleep Habits Questionnaire, respectively. Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging(rs-fMRI) was utilized to compute the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF). Mediation analyses were conducted to examine the interrelationships among ALFF, anxiety symptoms, and sleep problems.

RESULTS: Sleep problems scores were positively linked to anxiety scores. ALFF in bilateral temporal poles were positively linked to anxiety scores. Mediation analyses revealed the right superior temporal pole mediated the pathway from sleep problems to anxiety symptoms. The left middle temporal pole exhibited a significant mediating effect in both directions of anxiety-sleep relationship.

CONCLUSION: These findings enhance comprehension of neurobiological processes that underlie the co-occurrence of anxiety symptoms and sleep problems, while identifying promising targets for subsequent clinical therapeutic approaches. As all rs-fMRI scans were performed under sedation, our results cannot be generalized to unsedated children or those scanned with different sedative protocols.

### The association between adult attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and lifestyle factors: findings from the UK Biobank
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kozin Porat M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 14

Background Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a prevalent neurodevelopmental disorder, with diagnosis persisting in adulthood in up to 55% of cases. The association of ADHD with health-related behaviors in middle-aged and older adults remains relatively underexplored. We examined the relationship between ADHD in later adulthood and nutritional and lifestyle factors. Methods This cross-sectional study included UK Biobank participants aged 40 years and older. Individuals reporting ADHD diagnosis were compared to age- and sex-matched individuals with a ratio of 1:10. Multivariate linear, logistic and multinominal regression models were used to assess the relationship of ADHD diagnosis with lifestyle factors, adjusting for sex, age, education, BMI, sleeplessness, alcohol intake, smoking and physical activity. Interaction with age and sex were additionally tested. Results The sample included 133 individuals with ADHD diagnosis and 1330 in the comparison group (mean age 56.0 ± 7.9 years, 42.8% men). After adjusting for potential confounders, ADHD was significantly associated with higher likelihood of being a former or current smoker (OR [95% CI], 2.2 [1.4, 3.3]; p < .001; 2.2 [1.2, 4]; p = .02 respectively), increased alcohol intake (OR [95% CI] 1.8 [1.1, 3]; p =.01), and higher energy intake (β [SE], 0.7 [0.4]; p < .001). The association between ADHD and smoking was stronger among men compared to women (p-for interaction=0.006). No significant associations were found between ADHD diagnosis and adherence to the Mediterranean diet or physical activity. Conclusion Our findings, which demonstrate associations between adult ADHD diagnosis and health-related behaviors, underscore the need for tailored health promotion strategies to mitigate long-term health risks in this population. Introduction Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a highly heritable neurodevelopmental disorder associated with significant functional impairments, elevated health risks and societal costs, underscoring the importance of evidence-based interventions and lifestyle considerations [ 1 ]. ADHD’s core features, such as inattention, impulsivity, hyperactivity and executive dysfunction, can interfere with functioning and development [ 2 ], undermine self-regulation and consistency in daily routines [ 3 ]. These influences may explain why individuals with an ADHD diagnosis often struggle with consistent routines and health-promoting behaviors [ 4 , 5 ], specifically influencing dietary habits, physical activity, smoking rates and alcohol use. The Mediterranean diet, high in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, and healthy fats like olive oil, along with moderate consumption of meat, dairy products, and alcohol intake [ 6 ], is associated with lower risks of cardiovascular disease [ 7 ], metabolic disorders, and cognitive decline in the general population [ 8 , 9 ], with well-documented anti-inflammatory [ 10 , 11 ] and neuroprotective properties [ 12 ]. These features may be particularly relevant for individuals with ADHD, as this diet may support weight regulation, neuronal functioning and cognitive performance [ 13 , 14 ]. Indeed, several studies have shown that individuals with ADHD are less likely to adhere to the Mediterranean diet compared to those without this condition [ 15 – 17 ]. However, most of this evidence derives from studies in children and younger populations, leaving adherence patterns in older adults with ADHD largely unexplored. Beyond unhealthy diet, lower physical activity is also commonly observed among individuals with ADHD [ 18 , 19 ]. In addition, ADHD is strongly associated with increased risk of smoking, with childhood ADHD predicting nicotine dependence in later life and earlier smoking initiation in young adults [ 20 – 22 ]. Similarly, impulsivity, a core ADHD characteristic, is linked to alcohol use disorders, including increased binge drinking and earlier onset of alcohol use [ 23 , 24 ], with young adults with ADHD reporting higher alcohol consumption than controls [ 25 , 26 ]. Because most existing studies concentrate on children and young adults, a critical gap remains in our understanding of how ADHD is associated with health behaviors in later adulthood, limiting the evidence available to inform lifestyle interventions in this population. Yet, although ADHD is one of the most common neurodevelopmental disorders of childhood [ 27 ], symptoms persist into adulthood in up to 55% of cases [ 28 ], and the prevalence among adults is estimated at 1–2.5%. The current study aimed to assess the relationship between ADHD and lifestyle factors in middle-aged and older adults. Considering age and sex differences in ADHD health effects [ 29 , 30 ], we also tested for effect-modification by these factors. The current findings may improve understanding of ADHD associations with nutritional and life-style factors during older adulthood and help address the limited evidence regarding lifestyle interventions for middle-aged and older adults with ADHD. Participants voluntarily completed a touchscreen questionnaire and underwent an online interview, providing self-reported data on mental health, health behaviors, and lifestyle factors. Of them, 345,036 individuals did not complete a mental health questionnaire. From the remaining 157,331 who did, we identified 133 individuals who reported a diagnosis of ADHD as one of their mental health diagnoses in response to the relevant item in the mental health questionnaire. From the remaining 157,201 participants who did not report an ADHD diagnosis, 43,422 were excluded for not completing the diet questionnaire and 3,185 for implausible or extreme values in energy intake and BMI, defined as more than three standard deviations from the mean of the z-score value. Among the remaining 110,594 individuals with no report of ADHD, we randomly selected 1,330 participants at a 1:10 ratio to maximize statistical efficiency and precision of estimates, given the limited number of cases and the abundance of eligible controls [ 32 ]. Comparison group was matched to ADHD participants based on age and sex using the matchit function from the MatchIt package in R (version 4.5.0). (tick all that apply).” ADHD was included as one of the mentioned disorders [ 33 ]. A binary variable was created indicating ADHD diagnosis presence or absence.
