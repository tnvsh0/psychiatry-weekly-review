### A Single Dimension of Memory Strength Underlies Semantic and Phonological False Memories
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Science (Psychol Sci) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhang L, Osth AF | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 25

False memory can arise from semantically or perceptually similar experiences, as demonstrated in the Deese-Roediger-McDermott (DRM) paradigm. Although semantic and phonological DRM tasks yield parallel false-recognition patterns, researchers have argued that they reflect distinct memory processes, based in part on findings that semantic associates elicit more false "remember" (R) judgments, whereas phonological associates elicit more false "know" (K) judgments. We directly tested this claim using semantic and phonological DRM tasks with old-new recognition followed by RK ratings ( N  = 227 English-speaking students from an Australian university). The R/K dissociation was replicated, yet both parametric and nonparametric analyses revealed a single underlying dimension. A signal-detection-theory model fitted the data nearly perfectly, where R judgments reflected a stricter response criterion than K. A state-trace analysis likewise showed a monotonic relation between old/new and R false alarms across tasks. These results challenge dual-process interpretations, instead suggesting that semantic materials simply produce higher rates of false recognition.

### Action Framing Modulates the Pavlovian Bias in Go and No-Go Learning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Science (Psychol Sci) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chen Z, Braem S | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 28

Reward- and punishment-associated (i.e., Pavlovian) cues can trigger specific responses that interfere with learning appropriate actions. Prior work has shown that reward impedes the learning of "no-go" responses, and punishment impedes the learning of "go" responses. This  Pavlovian bias  is theorized to arise at the motor level and may reflect a rigid coupling between reward and action activation and between punishment and action suppression. Contrary to this view, across two experiments testing adults (one preregistered, total  N  = 370), using behavioral analyses and computational modeling, we show that action framing strongly modulates Pavlovian bias. When go was framed as approach and no-go as avoidance, we replicated the typical asymmetry in learning. However, when go was framed as avoidance and no-go as approach, this Pavlovian bias disappeared in choice and was attenuated in response times. Pavlovian bias thus operates partly on a semantic level and is more flexible than often theorized.

### What Survivors Remembered: Cognitive and Experiential Themes in Trauma Narratives From the Deadliest Mass Shooting in Modern American History
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Science (Psychol Sci) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ridgway WB et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 26

This study examined cognitive and experiential patterns in the trauma narratives of 42 survivors (74% women;  M age  = 42.1) of the Route 91 Harvest Festival shooting, the deadliest mass shooting in modern American history. We conducted a codebook thematic analysis of written narratives collected within one month of the event. We developed four themes: contextual memory (sensory-perceptual features, time, and space), physical injury, heroism, and contemplating mortality. Survivors recalled vivid sensory impressions, detailed spatial awareness organized around escape, and temporal distortions, including both subjective slowing and sequence gaps. Accounts of graphic injury, prosocial action under fire, and direct confrontation with death further structured the narratives. Limitations include prompted rather than free recall, written self-report allowing revision, and a predominantly female sample. These findings add to the scant literature on mass-shooting survivors' memories.

### Attentional engagement enhances encoding-based discrimination in aging
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Experimental Psychology: General (J Exp Psychol Gen) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Amer T, Wynn JS | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep

The present study investigated how goal-directed attention modulates age-related declines in the ability to behaviorally differentiate overlapping stimuli in memory. Participants completed a perceptual discrimination task involving relevant (attention-prioritized) and irrelevant items, followed by a surprise memory test. Using a sensitive gaze-based measure, we found that directing attention to prioritized stimuli enabled older adults to discriminate among similar items at levels comparable to young adults. Gaze similarity also predicted memory performance, with age differences emerging only during explicit testing. These findings demonstrate that attentional engagement and assessment method critically shape age-related changes in discriminating overlapping stimuli. ### Children's understanding of COVID-19 and the flu in three demographically distinct U.S. communities
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Experimental Psychology: General (J Exp Psychol Gen) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Menendez D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 24

Children's understanding of illness reflects their developing views of the biological world and predicts the protective measures they engage in. However, most of the research to date has focused on children from the communities surrounding research universities and as such might not provide a generalizable view of how children across the United States think about illnesses and viruses. This is of particular concern with regard to the COVID-19 pandemic, as Black and Latinx communities experienced more hospitalizations and deaths, and rural conservative communities were the targets of misinformation. We examined beliefs about two illnesses (COVID-19 and the flu) in 5- to 12-year-old children ( N  = 313) and their parents ( N  = 194), from three different types of communities across the United States: university communities ( N children  = 104;  N parents  = 64), low-socioeconomic-status Black and Latinx communities ( N children  = 101;  N parents  = 64), and low-socioeconomic-status White rural conservative communities ( N children  = 108;  N parents  = 65). We found that children had a good understanding of the external processes involved in disease transmission but showed gaps and misconceptions about the internal biological processes; accuracy increased with age. The patterns of responding were largely similar across the three types of communities. Nonetheless, sociodemographic variables related to children's and parents' knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors toward illness. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive view of how children in different communities across the United States think about illness and viruses.
