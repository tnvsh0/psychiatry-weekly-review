### The development of a parental identity during the transition to parenthood: A psychological resource for first-time parents' well-being
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Schrooyen C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 27

The transition to parenthood is a momentous life event that brings about significant changes and profound adjustments in individuals' lives. This period poses challenges for new parents and can increase their vulnerability to mental health problems. Although this transition is an identity-defining episode, surprisingly little research has focused on the development of parental identity during this crucial phase. To address this gap, the present study examined the development of parental identity among first-time mothers and fathers and its longitudinal associations with their own and their partners' psychological well-being. A four-wave longitudinal study was conducted, covering the period from pregnancy to 2 years into parenthood. A total of 288 mother-father couples participated, completing measurements on parental identity commitment, exploration, ruminative exploration, and mental health at each time point. Results showed that parents with high levels or stronger increases in parental identity commitment and with lower levels or stronger decreases in ruminative exploration reported higher levels and stronger increases in life satisfaction and lower levels and stronger decreases in depressive symptoms. The associations between parental identity exploration and mental health outcomes were less pronounced and more inconsistent. Additionally, the study revealed partner effects, indicating that one partner's mental health was also influenced by the other partner's parental identity, with notable differences between mothers and fathers. Overall, these findings underscore the importance of parental identity processes in the well-being of first-time parents. Targeting parental identity in prevention and intervention efforts could prove valuable in enhancing mental health of (future) parents during the transition to parenthood. ### A Model of Intergenerational Adverse Childhood Experiences in a Sample of Irish Families
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Interpers Violence | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Maiorano N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 26

A recent line of inquiry in the study of Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) has considered their intergenerational impact, providing evidence that the number of adversities reported by parents correlates with the number experienced by their children. However, research approaches using cumulative ACE scores may not capture the complexity or nuance of different experiences of adversity. There is also a need to consider the interaction between the family and the historical and social context within which they live their lives. The current study builds upon previous findings by querying if intergenerational ACEs are best represented by descriptively different typologies or cumulative scores while considering the specific context of Ireland. The study includes 565 families who received therapeutic or educational support services with a non-governmental organization in Dublin, Ireland. Parents completed sociodemographic measures and ACE questionnaires (indicating the number of ACEs experienced) on behalf of themselves and their child engaged with services. A latent class analysis was conducted, which found parental ACEs were best described by three classes, distinguished based on a high, moderate, and low ACE total scores. Based on this, parental ACEs as a continuous variable was carried forward to a regression model. Parental ACEs, child age, and parent nationality all significantly predicted child ACEs and, together, the model predicted 23.0% of the variance in child ACEs ( F [5, 533] = 31.86,  p  < .001). This finding supports the theory of intergenerational trauma and demonstrates a need for support services to consider families holistically. Further, the findings indicate a need for greater investment in context-specific understandings of trauma. The implications for both research and practice are discussed.
