This study analyses the differential profile of minors treated for suicide attempts in a large provincial cohort to identify specific vulnerabilities and risk patterns.

### Longitudinal Motor Trajectories and 2-Year Cognitive and Functional Outcomes in First-Episode Psychosis

BACKGROUND AND HYPOTHESIS: Motor abnormalities (MAs) are intrinsic features of the psychosis spectrum, but their longitudinal associations with cognition and functioning remain unclear. We hypothesized that Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS)-derived hypokinetic and hyperkinetic motor dimensions would be associated with cognitive and functional outcomes and that distinct longitudinal motor trajectories would capture clinically relevant heterogeneity in first-episode psychosis (FEP).

STUDY DESIGN: In a multicenter naturalistic FEP cohort, 159 patients completed clinical, functional, and neurocognitive assessments at 2 months and 2 years. PANSS-derived motor dimensions were assessed longitudinally. Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations with cognition and functioning were examined using correlational, mixed-effects, and trajectory-based analyses, including growth mixture modeling.

STUDY RESULTS: At 2 months, hypokinetic severity was associated with poorer verbal memory (r = -0.252, p = .002), whereas hyperkinetic severity was associated with poorer vocabulary (r = -0.260, p = .001) and verbal memory (r = -0.237, P = .003). Prospectively, higher 2-month hyperkinetic severity was associated with poorer vocabulary (r = -0.282, P < .001), verbal memory (r = -0.366, P < .001), and global cognition (r = -0.309, P = .001) at 2 years. Mixed-effects analyses confirmed associations between hyperkinetic severity and poorer longitudinal verbal memory trajectory (β = -0.295, P = .019). Higher-severity hyperkinetic trajectories remained associated with poorer 2-year verbal memory (β = -0.858, P = .003) and functional outcome (β = 10.209, P = .005).
