### A machine learning approach to predicting high-risk irritability trajectories across the transition to adolescence
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jordan LS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Youth irritability, characterized as proneness to anger/frustration, is associated with negative mental health outcomes. This study identified irritability trajectories across the transition to early adolescence and applied machine learning to identify preadolescent predictors of irritability trajectories. Data are from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study, a large longitudinal study that tracks youth development from preadolescence ( n  = 11,862, ages 8.92-11.08 years,  M  = 9.91,  SD  = 0.62). The baseline sample was 47.83% female ( n  = 5,680), 63.35% White, 15.74% Black, 12.07% Biracial or Multiracial, 2.32% Asian, and 20.30% Hispanic. Using parent-report youth irritability scores across four years, latent class growth analysis (LCGA) identified four distinct irritability trajectories: low stable ( n  = 8,692, 73.28%), high decreasing ( n  = 1,083, 9.13%), low increasing ( n  = 1,449, 12.22%), and high chronic ( n  = 638, 5.38%). Machine learning approaches generated predictive models for irritability trajectories, including baseline youth psychopathology, physical health, neurocognitive abilities, psychosocial environment, demographics, parent psychopathology, and brain structure metrics. The models demonstrated strong performance in detecting probability of being in the high chronic and low stable trajectories but poorer performance in classifying the irritability trajectories characterized by change. The most prominent predictors included youth externalizing and internalizing problems, parent psychopathology, and sleep problems. Findings provide insight into preadolescent predictors of irritability across a critical developmental period.

### A systematic review and meta-analysis of fear conditioning in children who have experienced childhood trauma or maltreatment
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Whalen ML et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 8

Childhood trauma has been identified as a major risk factor for the development of fear-related disorders in adulthood. Exposure to trauma might impact fear learning and extinction processes, which in turn may increase risk for these disorders. A PubMed, PsycInfo, and Web of Science search was conducted through May 6, 2025. Studies that used classical fear conditioning paradigms, measured indicators of fear response, examined childhood trauma or maltreatment in populations aged 18 or younger were included. Effect sizes were calculated for response to threat cues, response to safety cues, and differential fear response during acquisition and extinction, if included. Ultimately, 14 studies met the criteria for the qualitative review and 12 were quantitatively meta-analyzed (aggregate n = 1094). Results showed that trauma exposure was significantly associated with blunted responses to the threat cue during early acquisition when compared to children with little or no trauma exposure (g = -0.40; p < 0.01; k = 4). When assessing trauma exposure continuously, greater trauma exposure was significantly associated with lower responding to the safety signal (r = -0.10; p < 0.05; k = 8) and more pronounced discrimination (r = 0.15; p < 0.001; k = 8) in early acquisition. No significant effects were observed for extinction albeit studies were limited (k = 4). Findings suggest that trauma-exposed children exhibit altered fear learning patterns during acquisition. Future research should examine the impact of early life trauma on the development of fear learning and potential for acquiring fear-related disorders into adulthood.

### Headache disorders among Indigenous communities: A scoping review undertaken with knowledge keeper guidance
**כתב עת:** 📄 Headache | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Kelly M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

OBJECTIVE: We performed a scoping review of literature on headache etiology, classification, prevalence, and treatment in Indigenous communities in Canada, the United States, Australia, and New Zealand.

BACKGROUND: In the United States, analysis of national prevalence indicates that Indigenous Americans lead all other racial/ethnic groups in headache prevalence. Risk factors associated with headache burden, including diabetes, head injury, psychological trauma, environmental exposures, and lack of access to appropriate diagnosis and treatment, have been documented in Indigenous communities across the geographies studied.

METHODS: A systematic search of literature in PubMed, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature, Scopus, and Informit Indigenous Collection was conducted through January 2026 using descriptors of headache, migraine, and Indigenous populations across Canada, the United States, Australia, and New Zealand. Of the 616 citations identified, 30 publications from 1981 to 2024 were included in the present review and analyzed under the following areas of inquiry: (1) headache classification, (2) headache etiology, (3) headache prevalence, (4) headache treatment, and (5) barriers to headache care.

RESULTS: Analysis of national data in the United States indicates a higher prevalence of headache in Indigenous communities in comparison to all other communities. In Canada, evidence from smaller studies suggests a similar pattern. Prevalence data for Indigenous communities in Australia and New Zealand remains limited. Ethnographic data and expert informant data from Indigenous communities demonstrate conceptualization and treatment of headaches within biopsychosocial models that relate individuals to the life of their community and land. There were no data from medical headache treatment trials or intervention studies specific to or inclusive of Indigenous patients with headache. Barriers to headache care for Indigenous peoples were identified prior to diagnosis, at the point of assessment, and in treatment. Addressing these barriers demands system level changes as well as development of individual relations of respect and care for Indigenous patients.

CONCLUSIONS: Included studies from Canada, the United States, Australia, and New Zealand reported a high prevalence of headache in some Indigenous communities and suggest that this burden may result from or be associated with a combination of intergenerational trauma, adverse childhood experiences, lower socioeconomic status, head injury, environmental contaminants, and systemic racism resulting in barriers to diagnosis and care. Despite this apparent burden, the literature reveals a paucity of quality, headache-specific research conducted with Indigenous peoples across all four regions of study. To close gaps in headache diagnosis, treatment, and prevention, research and program development must be led by, or conducted in meaningful collaboration with, Indigenous communities in a manner that recognizes and uplifts Indigenous knowledge systems, healing, and conceptualizations of headache.

Headache burden is disproportionately high in Indigenous communities in Canada, the United States, Australia, and New Zealand, yet headache‐specific research conducted with Indigenous peoples remains limited. Our review found that Indigenous understandings and treatments of headache are often connected to land and community; however, systemic racism, barriers to care, gaps in communication, and lack of Indigenous‐led research limit effective diagnosis and treatment. To close these gaps, future work must be carried out by, or in meaningful collaboration with, Indigenous communities in a manner that uplifts diverse Indigenous knowledge and healing systems.

### Food insecurity, adverse childhood experiences, and eating disordered behavior in college students
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Am Coll Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Canady BE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Objective:  Food insecurity (FIS) is a substantial concern for many college students. Evidence suggests that FIS and adverse childhood events (ACEs) are separately linked to higher rates of eating disordered behavior.  Method:  The present study examines food insecurity and ACEs together as predictors of eating disordered behavior in a sample of students ( n  = 346) from an Appalachian university.  Results : indicate that rates of food insecurity were fairly high in this sample, with approximately 70% of the sample experiencing childhood and current food insecurity. Current food insecurity and ACEs were each associated with eating disordered behavior, with a model including both variables accounting for more of the variance in eating disordered behavior.  Conclusions:  Implications for these findings within a college campus are discussed, including consideration of trauma-informed approaches to better understand and address the combination of food insecurity, ACEs, and eating disordered behavior.

### Trajectories of suicidal ideation and attempts across the academic year: longitudinal study of UK university students
**כתב עת:** 📄 Br J Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Morse KF et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 10

BACKGROUND: Suicide is a leading cause of death among young adults, yet few studies have examined longitudinal trajectories of suicidal ideation and attempts across the academic year in UK university students.

AIMS: The study aimed to identify demographic and psychosocial factors associated with lifetime suicide risk, examine factors associated with changes in risk trajectories, and evaluate the relationship between suicidal ideation and attempts over an academic year.

METHOD: Participants comprised 3295 students from 5 UK universities who completed longitudinal surveys in autumn and spring terms 2021-2024 in the Nurture-U study. Suicidal ideation and attempts were assessed with Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale items. Demographic, psychosocial and mental health measures were examined as potential risk and protective factors. Regression models with multiple imputation were used, with case-control analyses for smaller subgroups.

RESULTS: Approximately 45.5% of students reported baseline lifetime suicidal ideation and 9.5% reported a past attempt. Factors associated with lifetime suicidal ideation included minoritised sexual orientation or ethnic background, male or gender-diverse identity, previous mental health treatment and adverse childhood experiences. Among students with lifetime ideation at baseline, 61.1% did not report ideation in the spring term, whereas new-onset ideation occurred in 3.5% of participants. Among students with prior ideation only, 1.3% made a suicide attempt during the academic year, whereas 10.5% with prior attempt history made a suicide attempt. Increases in depressive symptoms and rumination were associated with new attempts at follow-up.

CONCLUSIONS: Most students experiencing suicidal ideation during university have risk factors before university entry. Suicidal ideation alone showed limited specificity for identifying those most at risk of future attempts, highlighting the importance of targeted support for high-risk groups and ongoing monitoring of symptoms throughout the academic year.
