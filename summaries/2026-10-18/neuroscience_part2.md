### Symmetries and continuous attractors in disordered neural circuits
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Clark DG et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

A core challenge in neuroscience is reconciling idealized models with experimental data. Classical continuous-attractor models describe neural representations of continuous variables such as head direction. Symmetry in the weights generates a manifold of stable states, with tuning curves identical up to shifts. Rodent head-direction cells, by contrast, are markedly heterogeneous. Such heterogeneity, we show, is nevertheless compatible with these models. Here we build, directly from head-direction cell data, recurrent networks that behave as quasicontinuous attractors. A generative process then reproduces the heterogeneous tuning of these cells while making connectivity and dynamics tractable at large N. Although the weights appear disordered, they carry a circular symmetry whose eigenvalue degeneracies could signify continuous-attractor organization in connectome data. Dynamical mean-field theory recovers the ingredients of classical ring attractors, namely, Mexican hat interactions and a spontaneously broken symmetry producing bump states. Continuous-attractor mechanisms may thus operate in complex mammalian circuits.

### Raphe nuclei in cognition: A network-level framework for behavioral-state control
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Khalighinejad N, Rushworth MFS | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 16

Adaptive behavior requires organisms to maintain, update, and switch between behavioral states as environmental conditions change. The raphe nuclei (RN), best known as a major source of forebrain serotonin, have long been linked to arousal, inhibition, patience, affect, and flexibility, but these functions have often been considered separately. Here, we argue that recent advances in circuit tracing, cell-type-specific manipulation, high-resolution neuroimaging and neurostimulation, and computational modeling support a more integrated view in which RN are embedded within distributed cortico-subcortical and neuromodulatory networks. Across species, RN activity tracks environmental statistics such as value, uncertainty, threat, and reward context and contributes to transitions between motivational and cognitive states. We propose a network-level framework in which RN regulate when behavioral states should persist, update, or switch.

### Highly replicable multisite patterns of adolescent white matter maturation
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Meisler SL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Adolescent brain development is difficult to study at scale because diffusion MRI (dMRI) measures are sensitive to technical variability across scanning environments. The Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study is the largest US-based longitudinal neuroimaging study of adolescent brain maturation. We present ABCD-Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) Community Collection (ABCC) release 3.1.0, a curated resource of more than 24,000 fully processed human ABCD dMRI datasets from participants aged 8-17 years. ABCC provides processed images, nuanced image quality metrics, advanced microstructural measures, and person-specific white matter bundle tractography. Across 62 bundles, measures of diffusion restriction and non-Gaussianity-including intracellular volume fraction from neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI), mean kurtosis tensor from diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI), and return-to-origin probability from mean apparent propagator (MAP)-MRI-were more sensitive to development and more robust to variation in image quality than tensor-derived measures. Longitudinal harmonization of microstructural features with ComBat-GAMM markedly improved the cross-vendor generalizability of developmental effects. Together, ABCC accelerates reproducible, rigorous research on adolescent white matter development.

### Target engagement and efficacy in disease-modifying trials of synuclein-related disorders
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Artusi CA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Disease-modifying trials in Parkinson's disease (PD), multiple system atrophy (MSA), and dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) increasingly incorporate fluid and imaging biomarkers to demonstrate target engagement, de-risk subsequent phases of drug development and provide mechanistic evidence of biological activity. Whether biomarker modulation predicts clinical benefit remains unclear. We conducted a PRISMA-compliant review of phase II and III disease-modifying trials (2020-2025) that included at least one biomarker intended to assess target engagement. Twenty-six trials met inclusion criteria (PD=19, MSA=4, DLB=3; n=2,195 participants). Target engagement was reported in 14 of 19 PD trials (12 phase II/2 phase III), 1 of 4 MSA trials, and 2 of 3 DLB trials (all phase II). Concordance between target engagement and clinical improvement was uncommon, as only 8/26 studies (7 phase II/1 phase III) demonstrated both target engagement and statistically significant clinical benefit, and just two of these (both phase II, one in PD and one in MSA) were rigorously controlled with low or moderate risk of bias. Nine trials showed target engagement without clinical benefit, whereas two reported clinical improvement without measurable target engagement. Baseline biomarker-guided patient selection was uncommon and rarely aligned with the compound's mechanism of action. Patient-reported outcomes were widely used but rarely improved. Across recent disease-modifying trials in PD, DLB, and MSA, target engagement was frequently demonstrated, yet clinical benefit rarely followed. These findings question the predictive value of currently used target-engagement biomarkers and highlight the need to biologically align trial populations with the mechanisms targeted by experimental molecular interventions.

### Extracellular vesicle α-synuclein topology reveals disease-associated conformers in brain and blood
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Dutta S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Parkinson's disease (PD), dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), and multiple system atrophy (MSA) are unified by pathological α-synuclein (αSyn) aggregation, yet diverge in terms of the cellular distribution of pathology and clinical phenotype. Extracellular vesicles (EVs) have emerged as potential mediators or biomarkers of αSyn pathology, but whether their molecular constituents in the brain reflect disease-specific processes or conformations remains unknown. Here, we combined EV isolation, electrochemiluminescence, quantitative mass spectrometry and nanoscale single-vesicle imaging by direct stochastic optical reconstruction microscopy (dSTORM) to map the topology, epitope exposure and cellular origin of αSyn in EVs isolated from post-mortem PD, DLB, MSA, and healthy control (HC) brains and assessed the tractability of these molecular signatures in blood. Proteomics and immunoblotting of EV lysates isolated from human brains confirmed that they were highly purified and derived from neurons and glial cell subtypes. Across diseases, αSyn association with EVs exhibited distinct patterns: In PD and DLB, both total αSyn levels normalised to CD81 and the percentage of neuronal (L1CAM+) EVs with aggregated αSyn as detected by aggregate-preferring antibodies, were increased, whereas in MSA, aggregated αSyn was detected primarily in neuronal and to a lesser extent glial EV subpopulations without an increase in total EV-associated αSyn. Further epitope mapping by dSTORM identified a distinct αSyn conformational arrangement, as detected by antibodies against an epitope spanning αSyn residues 34-45, that was enriched on the surface of L1CAM+ EVs in MSA compared to PD. This N-terminal immunoreactive conformer was tractable in serum and enriched on the surface of L1CAM+ EVs from MSA compared to PD patients or controls. Collectively, our findings suggest that the epitope accessibility and topology of EV-associated αSyn proteoforms, rather than their abundance alone, discriminate synucleinopathy subtypes, offering a framework for a blood-based conformational stratification of these disorders.

### Voluntary movement initiation is associated with cardiac input in Libet's task
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Germanova K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

​​The relationship between motor intention and initiation of voluntary movement remains a fundamental topic in neuroscience, originating from the seminal framework of B. Libet, introduced in 1983. Libet's paradigm significantly influenced discussions on intentionality, motor control, and free will. However, methodological critiques continue to challenge its interpretations, specifically the accuracy and validity of the 'urge to move' phenomenon. One understudied factor in this debate is the potential influence of interoceptive signals-such as cardiac activity-in shaping the experience of motor intention and movement initiation. In our study, we addressed this gap by examining whether cardiac signals modulate participants' experience of the 'urge to move', using behavioural and electrophysiological measures in 39 healthy human participants (17 males and 22 females) performing Libet's task. Crucially, when participants were asked to follow the 'urge to move', their button press timings were predominantly aligned with the diastolic phase of the cardiac cycle, indicating cardiac modulation of movement initiation. However, analysing heart evoked potential (HEP) amplitudes as a measure of cardiac input perception, we observed no differences in HEP amplitudes associated with changes in introspective demands during the task in both source and sensor spaces. Our results suggest that implicit perception of cardiac signals biases voluntary action initiation in Libet's task, independently of cortical interoceptive markers. These findings have implications for models of motor preparation, intentionality, and the bodily basis of voluntary action, challenging conventional interpretations of motor intention and informing debates on volition and interoception. Significance Statement  Our study provides evidence that implicit perception of cardiac signals influences movement initiation in Libet's task. We demonstrate, for the first time, that when attending to the 'urge to move', participants tended to initiate voluntary movements during the diastolic phase of the cardiac cycle. These findings challenge traditional views on factors affecting motor initiation in Libet's task, suggesting the relevance of interoceptive processing. By highlighting the role of cardiac input in experiencing motor intention, our findings expand on existing debates on volition, agency, and free will, further underscoring the importance of integrating bodily signals into these theoretical frameworks.
