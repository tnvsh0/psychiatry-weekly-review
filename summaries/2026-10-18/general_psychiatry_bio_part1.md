### Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: transition to psychotic disorders
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Thapar A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

It is well recognized that Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is associated with heightened risk for common psychiatric disorders; early identification and treatment of ADHD may help reduce some of this risk. However, the substantially increased risk of bipolar disorder, psychosis, and schizophrenia (hereafter psychotic disorders) is often overlooked (odds ratios 4.7-8.9). Given how common ADHD is, there is growing pressure for greater involvement of primary care services, especially for less complex forms of ADHD. However, those at highest risk for transition to a psychotic disorder need to be prioritised for secondary care follow up. Currently it is unknown how to stratify ADHD according to risk or indeed why only some develop a psychotic disorder. This perspective examines key hypotheses that may explain why transition occurs only in a subset of individuals. One hypothesis is that features suggestive of ADHD may represent early manifestations of psychotic disorders, although direct support for this is limited. The potential role of ADHD treatment is also considered; current data indicate that therapeutic use of methylphenidate is not associated with increased risk of psychotic disorders, while uncertainty remains for amphetamines. The possible mediating roles of ADHD-evoked environmental exposures are discussed, but evidence for mediation is lacking. We also consider the evidence that ADHD and psychotic disorders result from shared underlying neurodevelopmental processes. We highlight key clinical implications for screening and monitoring individuals with ADHD for psychotic disorders and consider what factors may help in weighting risk. Finally, we identify important evidence gaps for future research.

### The role of white matter microstructure in the association between childhood maltreatment and adult post-traumatic symptoms
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Berger L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 10

Childhood maltreatment (CM) is a risk factor for adult post-traumatic symptoms and white matter (WM) alterations. It remains unknown how WM microstructure is affected alongside two common military conditions, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI). Data from 299 participants (53% male, 35.69 ± 12.23 years of age) of the Injury and Traumatic Stress (INTRuST) clinical consortium was analyzed. The study included 56 individuals with PTSD (41 with comorbid mTBI), 107 with mTBI, and 136 controls (no PTSD and/or mTBI). Diffusion parameters (mean, axial, and radial diffusivity, MD, AD, RD; fractional anisotropy, FA) in major WM tracts were extracted from diffusion MRI. CM was assessed using the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ). Multiple regression models examined associations between CM severity and WM diffusion. Moderation analyses assessed the effect of PTSD and/or mTBI on these associations. Mediation analyses examined how WM diffusion affects the associations between CM and both PTSD and post-concussive symptom severity. Greater CM severity was associated with lower MD and AD, independent of PTSD and/or mTBI. WM diffusion, particularly in the corpus callosum and the superior longitudinal fasciculus, mediated the associations between CM severity and both PTSD (MD b ab  = 0.022, AD b ab  = 0.019-0.034) and post-concussive symptoms (MD b ab  = 0.028-0.040, AD b ab  = 0.032-0.047). Findings suggest that WM alterations are more strongly associated with CM itself than with either PTSD and/or mTBI. Mediating effects in regions critical for sensory integration highlight the role of WM microstructure as a neurobiological pathway through which CM might predispose to post-traumatic symptoms.

### Excitation-Inhibition Balance and Fronto-Limbic Connectivity Distinguish TMS Treatment Outcomes in Refractory Depression
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Momi D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

BACKGROUND: Intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) is effective for treatment-resistant depression, but outcomes remain variable. The neurophysiological mechanisms underlying response remain poorly understood, limiting precision neuromodulation.

METHODS: We combined transcranial magnetic stimulation with electroencephalography (TMS-EEG) and connectome-based Jansen-Rit neural mass modeling to investigate the mechanistic basis of treatment efficacy in 90 patients with treatment-resistant depression. Individualized whole-brain models were fitted to pre- and post-iTBS TMS-evoked potentials, yielding subject-specific estimates of synaptic connectivity, excitation-inhibition (E/I) balance, and cortical dynamics. Brain states were defined by singular value decomposition of the model's population activity, and partial least squares analysis with nested cross-validation identified the brain-state pattern distinguishing responders from non-responders.

RESULTS: We identified two neurophysiological signatures associated with treatment response. First, responders showed greater post-treatment reductions in 3-10 Hz oscillatory power, driven by shifts in E/I balance toward greater inhibitory control through reduced excitatory drive and increased inhibitory-to-pyramidal connectivity. Second, a pre-treatment brain state characterized by opposite-polarity dynamics between subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC) and left DLPFC distinguished responders from non-responders, consistent with prior fMRI findings on frontolimbic anticorrelation. Neural trajectory analysis further revealed greater dynamical reorganization and increased attractor stability in responders following treatment.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings provide a neurophysiologically-informed framework linking iTBS treatment efficacy to specific changes in cortical E/I balance and frontolimbic circuit dynamics, pointing toward candidate markers that, with subject-specific structural connectivity and independent validation, may inform personalized neuromodulation approaches in treatment-resistant depression.

### Genetic architecture and risk of suicide attempt across neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Starnawska A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 13

BACKGROUND: Suicide attempt (SA) is prevalent among individuals with neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions, yet similarities and differences in the genetic architecture of SA across these conditions remain unclear.

METHODS: In the iPSYCH cohort (N=98,069), SA was identified using ICD-10 codes among individuals diagnosed with attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (N=22,962), autism (N=17,670), schizophrenia (N=5,974), bipolar disorder (N=3,028), depression (N=28,844), and controls (N=34,049). We evaluated 21 polygenic scores (PGSs) in individuals with and without SA within each diagnosis, stratified individuals into PGS quantiles, estimated absolute SA risk, and conducted sex-stratified analyses.

RESULTS: SA was 1% among controls but ranged from 5% in autism to 31% in schizophrenia. Several PGSs were increased in individuals with SA across diagnoses, including PGS for ADHD, depression, and substance use disorders (SUD). SA-PGS stratification showed marked SA absolute risk differences, e.g., 40% in the top PGS tertile vs 29% in the lowest tertile in schizophrenia. PGS-loads did not differ between females and males with SA across diagnoses. However, absolute SA risk differed substantially. In the highest SA-PGS tertile in ADHD, the absolute SA risk reached 36% for females and 22% for males and was further increased to 48% in those with comorbid SUD.

CONCLUSIONS: SA genetic architecture is similar across neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions and individuals can be stratified into SA risk groups based on PGSs, and co-occuring SUD. Being a female or having comorbid SUD are strong risk factors for SA, however SA common variants, still affect SA risk in the presence of those factors.

### Sex differences in circulating endocannabinoid concentrations during adolescence: Associations with pubertal development
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Neuropsychopharmacology *(IF: 8.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Evanski JM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Endocannabinoids contribute to sex-related processes, including puberty. In 1,159 adolescents, females showed significantly lower concentrations than males across five endocannabinoids (AEA, 2-AG, PEA, OEA, and 2-OG). Sex-by-puberty interactions were significant for AEA, PEA, and OEA. Exploratory analyses revealed sex-specific associations between endocannabinoids and self-reported pain, stress, internalizing, and attentional problems.

### Testing neurobiological models of reward/incentive sensitization in the continuation of alcohol use disorder (AUD) symptoms in young adults with AUD: A four-year longitudinal study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Neuropsychopharmacology *(IF: 8.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** King AC et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Alcohol use disorder (AUD) remains an urgent public health problem. Alcohol response phenotype may be an important factor underlying vulnerability to and progression of AUD. This study examined the prospective association between acute alcohol responses and continued or remitted AUD over time in young adults with AUD. This sample was N = 101 AUD participants (M age =27.1 ± 4.0 SD yrs) comprising the third cohort of the Chicago Social Drinking Project. They completed two beverage challenge  laboratory sessions (0.8 g/kg fixed-dose alcohol vs. placebo) and four years of follow-up to ascertain future AUD symptoms, drinking behaviors, and consequences. Retention was high with an overall 99.2% follow-up rate. Analyses identified three AUD trajectory subgroups over follow-up including a high AUD trajectory subgroup (n = 37), a medium AUD trajectory subgroup (n = 48), and a low AUD trajectory subgroup (n = 16). Generalized estimating equations showed that greater sensitivity to alcohol's pleasurable effects, including stimulation and hedonic and motivational reward (liking and wanting, respectively) predicted more AUD symptoms over follow-up (ps<0.001) and were positively associated with AUD trajectory subgroups. However,  sedation, heart rate, and cortisol response to alcohol were not  significantly associated with AUD trajectory subgroups. In sum, greater alcohol stimulation, liking, and wanting prospectively predicted the continuation and increasing severity of AUD over a four-year period. The findings challenge theoretical models emphasizing reward deficit and global tolerance in addiction and support the role of positive reinforcement, indexed by sensitivity to alcohol's pleasurable effects, as an important factor in AUD  persistence.
