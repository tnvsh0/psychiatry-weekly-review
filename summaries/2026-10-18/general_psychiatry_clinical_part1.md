### Temporal, Geographic, and Polysubstance Patterns in Orphine Analogue Detections
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhu DT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 7

INTRODUCTION Benzimidazol-2-ones, also known as “orphine analogs” or simply “orphines,” are synthetic opioids traced to pharmaceutical discovery programs in the 1960s, beginning with compounds such as bezitramide and R-6890 (spirochlorphine). 1 – 3 These drugs are μ-opioid receptor agonists, with some analogs estimated to be approximately 10 times more potent than fentanyl, and can produce classic opioid toxicity, including respiratory depression. 2 – 5 Orphine analogs first appeared in the US illicit drug supply in the third quarter of 2020 (2020 Q3) with brorphine. 2 As nitazene-class benzimidazole opioids have proliferated, 6 scheduling actions targeting these substances may have increased market pressure toward new synthetic opioids, including N -propionitrile chlorphine, colloquially known as “cyclorphine.” 2 , 7 Given limited data on this emerging drug class, we examined temporal, geographic, and polysubstance patterns in orphine analog detections. METHODS This cross-sectional study used data on orphine-positive biospecimens from the Center for Forensic Science Research and Education’s NPS Discovery program, a drug early warning system. 8 Data from 2020 Q3 through 2026 Q2 were analyzed. NPS Discovery tests biospecimens (e.g., blood, urine, serum, plasma) submitted by forensic laboratories, medical examiners, coroners, and clinical and public health agency partners. Drug identifications were made using liquid chromatography quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometry (LC-QTOF-MS), with supplemental mass spectrometry platforms used as needed. This analysis focused on five orphine analogs: brorphine, chlorphine, spirochlorphine, N -propionitrile chlorphine, and SR-17018 (5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine). We described orphine-positive biospecimen counts by quarter and analog, US Census region, state, and co-detected psychoactive drug categories ( eMethods in Supplement 1 ). Co-positivity was defined as the presence of one or more additional psychoactive drug in an orphine-positive biospecimen. We also identified biospecimens in which naloxone was detected. This study was deemed exempt by the Virginia Commonwealth University Institutional Review Board under 45 CFR §46. The STROBE reporting guideline was followed. RESULTS From 2020 Q3 through 2026 Q2, we identified 142 orphine-positive biospecimens, representing 1.0% of total biospecimens (n=142/14,261), 0.6% of fentanyl-positive biospecimens (n=51/9,004), and 3.0% of nitazene-positive biospecimens tested during this period (n=13/430). The most commonly detected analog was N -propionitrile chlorphine (n=102; 71.8%), followed by brorphine (n=38; 26.8%), spirochlorphine (n=6; 4.2%), 5,6-dichloro desmethylchlorphine (n=3; 2.1%), and chlorphine (n=1; 0.7%) ( Figure 1A ). Quarterly counts showed two distinct waves: an early wave of 36 orphine-positive biospecimens in 2020 Q3, all involving brorphine, followed by a later wave from 2025 Q4 through 2026 Q2 dominated by N -propionitrile chlorphine, present in 100 of 102 biospecimens (98.0%) during this period. Orphine-positive biospecimens were concentrated in the South (n=75; 52.8%) and Midwest (n=53; 37.3%), with fewer in the Northeast (n=8; 5.6%) and West (n=6; 4.2%) ( Figure 1B ). Across 22 US states, detections were most common in Tennessee (n=50; 35.2%), Illinois (n=37; 26.1%), Texas (n=14; 9.9%), and Minnesota (n=10; 7.0%). Tennessee and Texas detections all involved N -propionitrile chlorphine, whereas Minnesota detections all involved brorphine. Illinois had a mixed pattern, with 24 brorphine-positive and 13 N -propionitrile chlorphine-positive biospecimens. Polysubstance involvement was common ( Figure 2 ). Orphine-positive biospecimens were most often co-detected with benzodiazepine-related compounds (n=53; 37.3%). Frequently co-detected synthetic opioids included fentanyl-related compounds (n=51; 35.9%) and nitazene-related compounds (n=13; 9.2%). Co-detection of stimulants was also common, including methamphetamine/other amphetamines (n=51; 35.9%) and cocaine-related compounds (n=29; 20.4%). Other co-detections included medetomidine-related compounds (n=16; 11.3%) and xylazine (n=11; 7.7%). Naloxone was detected in 33 orphine-positive biospecimens (23.2%). DISCUSSION This study documents the proliferation of orphine analogs across 22 US states, with a sharp rise since late 2025 driven by N -propionitrile chlorphine, frequently co-detected with fentanyl-related compounds. Naloxone appeared in nearly one-fourth of orphine-positive biospecimens, consistent with attempted reversal of opioid-induced respiratory depression. 4 These findings provide timely evidence of orphine analogs as an emerging drug threat in the US illicit drug supply. One possible explanation for this shift is China’s generic scheduling of nitazene analogs in July 2025, which may have created an opening for the introduction of alternative synthetic opioid classes, including orphine analogs. 2 , 6 , 7 Our study showed that N -propionitrile chlorphine-positive biospecimens rose rapidly immediately following this regulatory intervention, while only a minority of orphine-positive biospecimens also contained nitazene-related compounds, consistent with this market substitution hypothesis. This study has limitations. NPS Discovery data are based on both random and for-cause testing of submitted biospecimens. 8 Although biospecimens can be received from across all 50 states, submissions are not systematically received from all jurisdictions; thus, findings may not be nationally representative. 8 These findings have important implications for clinicians, public health agencies, and policymakers. Orphine analogs are not routinely included in forensic or clinical toxicology testing, fentanyl and nitazene test strips do not reliably detect orphine analogs, and no orphine-specific test strips are available. 2 , 7 Clinical guidance for managing orphine-involved overdoses, development of orphine-specific test strips, broader access to confirmatory testing, and naloxone distribution remain key priorities. As several internationally reported analogs were not observed in this dataset, including fluorphine and iodorphine, 7 proactive surveillance is critical to monitor their potential entry into the US illicit drug supply. ### Sociodemographic, Criminological and Clinical Determinants in Suicide After Release From Prison: A 23-Year National Cohort Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica (Acta Psychiatr Scand) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bukten A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 11

BACKGROUND: Suicide risk is elevated after prison release, but population-level evidence on temporal risk patterns and associated factors remains limited. We aimed to describe characteristics of individuals who die by suicide after release from Norwegian prisons, compare their suicide mortality with the general population, and examine risk factors over time.

MATERIAL AND METHODS: Data from the Norwegian Prison Release Study (nPRIS), including all people in prison in Norway (2000-2022), were linked to the Norwegian Cause of Death Registry, the Norwegian Prison Registry, the Norwegian Patient Registry, and Statistics Norway. We calculated crude mortality rates (CMR) per 100,000 person-years, standardized mortality ratios (SMR), annual age-standardized years of life lost rate (ASYR), and Cox proportional hazards models.

RESULTS: From 2000 to 2022, 1183 suicides (9.1% of all deaths; CMR 81; 95% CI 76-85) occurred after release (90% male). The SMR was higher in women (SMR 8.64; 95% CI 7.17-10.34) than men (SMR 3.98; 95% CI 3.76-4.22). In multivariable models (2009-2022; n = 57,034; 516 suicides), strong independent associations with suicide 6 months post-release included self-harm (adjusted HR [aHR] 4.76; 95% CI 2.60-8.72), severe mental illness (aHR 1.95; 95% CI 1.16-3.27), release from high-security prisons (aHR 1.93; 95% CI 1.12-3.33), and pretrial detention (aHR 1.82; 95% CI 1.06-3.13).

CONCLUSIONS: Suicide risk is substantially and persistently elevated relative to the general population, peaks immediately after release, and is concentrated among people with prior self-harm, severe mental illness, and those released from high-security settings.

### Universal suicide risk assessment for veterans and soldiers in all healthcare settings: A nursing imperative
**כתב עת:** 📄 Arch Psychiatr Nurs | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Clements PT, Marklund LA | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

PURPOSE: Suicide remains a major public health concern among U.S. veterans and active-duty military personnel. This narrative review examines evidence supporting routine suicide risk screening and considers its implementation as a clinically appropriate, trauma-informed process rather than an inflexible requirement at every encounter.

METHODS: A structured narrative review used targeted searches of PubMed, CINAHL, PsycINFO, and Scopus, supplemented by federal surveillance reports, clinical guidance, and policy documents. Evidence was synthesized across five domains: suicide prevalence and trends; clinical, cultural, and system-level risk factors; recognition gaps in general healthcare; routine screening and early intervention; and nursing leadership in implementation.

RESULTS: Veterans and active-duty personnel experience multifactorial suicide risk associated with post-traumatic stress disorder, depression, moral injury, substance use, chronic pain, traumatic brain injury, transition-related stressors, stigma, and access to lethal means. Missed opportunities for recognition also occur in nonpsychiatric settings. Validated screening can facilitate early identification but requires pathways for secondary assessment, safety planning, referral, and emergency response.

CONCLUSIONS: Routine suicide risk screening should be integrated into healthcare encounters when screening can be completed safely, privately, and with an established response pathway. Nurses are well positioned to lead standardized workflows, culturally responsive communication, staff education, and quality improvement. Broader implementation requires organizational policy, workforce preparation, and further evaluation across military and civilian healthcare systems.

### Suicide in People With Co-occurring Substance Use and Mental Health Conditions: An Integrative Literature Review to Inform Risk Formulation in Mental Health Nursing
**כתב עת:** 📄 Int J Ment Health Nurs | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Marshall R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

ABSTRACT People with co‐occurring substance use and mental health conditions experience disproportionately high levels of suicidal ideation, attempts and deaths compared with those with either condition alone, yet guidance for suicide risk formulation in this group is limited. This integrative literature review synthesised evidence on suicidality in adults with co‐occurring conditions, identified risk and protective factors, and refined risk formulation approaches for mental health nursing. Using Whittemore and Knafl's methodology and Toronto and Remmington's stepwise guidance, CINAHL, Embase, Medline and PsycInfo were searched without date limits. Empirical studies of adults with co‐occurring substance use and mental health conditions were included, alongside relevant grey literature; specialist populations (e.g., prison, veteran, neurological/developmental) were excluded. Thirty‐three quantitative studies and six grey literature sources (total n = 39; 35 883 participants across 19 countries) were appraised using Critical Appraisal Skills Programme tools and the AACODS checklist. Co‐occurring conditions were consistently associated with elevated suicidality, especially mood disorders, notably depression, combined with alcohol use disorder. Risk factors were mapped onto an extended 5Ps model: presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective domains, with an additional Patient Experience domain to capture barriers to care, service dissatisfaction and stigma. Protective factors were comparatively scarce. Applying the Integrated Motivational–Volitional model highlighted psychological processes, including mental pain, perceived burdensomeness, impulsivity and access to means, that may contribute to suicidal behaviour. For mental health nurses and other mental health professionals, incorporating patient experience and IMV‐informed processes into an extended 5Ps framework may support more thorough suicide risk formulation and the development of safety and treatment plans that promote safer, more equitable and integrated care for people with co‐occurring conditions.

1 Introduction Co‐occurring substance use and mental health conditions (previously termed ‘dual diagnosis’) are common in mental health services and are associated with elevated suicide risk. In England, nearly half of those who die by suicide have a history of problematic alcohol use and over one‐third have a history of problematic drug use (an unreported proportion will have been using both alcohol and drugs and will be accounted for in both percentages), with even higher proportions among mental health patients (National Confidential Inquiry into Suicide and Safety in Mental Health [NCISH] 2025 ). Despite this, service responses remain fragmented, and specific guidance for suicide risk assessment in this population is limited. Co‐occurring conditions encompass a wide spectrum of mental health diagnoses and symptoms, combined with diverse patterns of alcohol and drug use, different levels of dependence and states of intoxication (Public Health England 2017 ). Studies examining the prevalence of co‐occurring conditions suggest that 30%–50% of people with serious mental illness also have a co‐occurring drug or alcohol problem (Regier et al. 1990 ; Weaver et al. 2001 ). The Comorbidity of Substance Misuse and Mental Illness Collaborative study found that 44% of mental health service users reported hazardous or harmful alcohol or drug use within the previous year (Weaver et al. 2003 ). Recent articles cite these studies when providing prevalence estimates, further demonstrating the lack of current research on co‐occurring conditions. Globally, suicide is a leading cause of death, with an estimated 727 000 deaths in 2021 (World Health Organisation [WHO] 2025 ). In the United Kingdom, 26% of suicides between 2012 and 2022 occurred among people who had been in contact with mental health services in the preceding year (NCISH 2025 ). Alcohol and drug use are highly prevalent in this group, and the national suicide prevention strategy for England (Department of Health and Social Care [DHSC] 2023 ) identifies people in contact with mental health and substance use services as a key priority group. Historically, people with co‐occurring conditions have often been required to address their substance use before being accepted for mental health treatment (Care Quality Commission 2015 ; Making Every Adult Matter 2022 ), despite evidence that substances often function as coping mechanisms in the context of unaddressed mental distress (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence [NICE] 2016 ). NCISH ( 2024 ) reported that 70% of people who died by suicide whilst under the care of alcohol and drug services had not been in contact with mental health services in the preceding year, prompting recommendations for joint working between drug and alcohol and mental health services and routine assessment of substance use within mental health settings. Various models of risk assessment are used by organisations providing mental health care, and the forms used vary between services. NCISH ( 2018 ) examined the use of risk assessment tools across UK mental health services. All 85 providers of mental health care were asked to describe the tools they used, and clinicians, patients and carers were invited to complete an online survey. The study found little consistency in the length, content or use of risk tools. Most were checklists intended to predict future behaviour, with scores used to inform management decisions. However, risk scales have limited value in guiding risk management (Large et al. 2017 ; Oates 2018 ) and poor predictive validity (Chan et al. 2016 ; Quinlivan et al. 2014 , 2016 ; Steeg et al. 2018 ). NCISH ( 2018 ) therefore recommended an approach that incorporates a formulation of risk based on the person's current situation, history and social circumstances. It also recommended training staff to undertake formulation collaboratively, personally and individually with the person and, where appropriate, their family or carer. The 5Ps model of risk formulation is widely used in UK mental health services. Weerasekera ( 1993 ) initially proposed the model following a literature review that identified the need for a comprehensive and memorable approach to formulation. Macneil et al. ( 2012 ) subsequently developed the model, which considers presenting problems, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors (Table 1 ). In practice, substance use is frequently recorded in a single section, usually as a perpetuating factor that interferes with treatment.
