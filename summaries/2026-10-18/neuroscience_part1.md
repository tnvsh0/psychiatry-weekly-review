### A half-center oscillator regulates sleep pressure
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hasenhuetl PS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Changes in oscillatory neural dynamics reflect-but may not necessarily determine-transitions between sleep stages and variations in sleep pressure. Here we show that sleep-control neurons innervating the dorsal fan-shaped body of Drosophila (dFBNs) produce slow-wave activity (SWA) that is causally linked to sleep. The dFBN ensemble contains bistable cells whose membrane voltages oscillate between hyperpolarized DOWN and depolarized UP states. The oscillations rely on interhemispheric competition of two half-centers connected by inhibitory glutamatergic synapses. dFBN-restricted interference with glutamate release disrupts SWA and baseline as well as rebound sleep, whereas the optogenetic replay of SWA induces sleep. dFBNs generate SWA throughout the sleep-wake cycle (despite mutual antagonism with arousing dopaminergic neurons) and adjust its power to sleep need via sleep history-dependent increases in excitability and homeostatic synaptic depression. The oscillatory format permits a continuous encoding of sleep pressure but requires mechanisms that convert the amplitude-modulated signal into binary sleep-wake states.

### Investigating molecular and hemodynamic effects of LSD in the human brain
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** McCulloch DE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) is a potent psychedelic with increasing therapeutic interest, yet qualification of target engagement using in vivo neuroimaging has not yet been performed. Using simultaneous positron emission tomography-magnetic resonance imaging technology, we describe multiple brain actions of LSD in seven healthy volunteers blinded to LSD dose received. Here we show that LSD occupies 5-HT2AR with a high affinity and increases both global cerebral blood flow (CBF) and internal carotid artery flow. LSD also decreases global connectivity, which was negatively associated with changes in CBF. We observe an anticlockwise hysteresis loop between plasma drug levels and subjective effects, suggesting atypical pharmacodynamic mechanisms. We contrast our CBF and global connectivity findings and their relation in a separate cohort of 25 participants administered psilocybin, highlighting key consistencies and differences. By establishing the dose-occupancy relation of LSD in humans, our findings provide insights for the clinical development of psychedelic compounds and demonstrate unique neurophysiological effects that distinguish LSD from psilocybin.

### Parvalbumin cell state dynamics regulate gamma oscillations and are disrupted in Alzheimer's disease
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ma K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Gamma oscillations are fundamental for cognitive processing, but the cellular and circuit dynamics that generate them remain poorly defined. Establishing a method for simultaneous calcium imaging and electrocorticography recordings in awake mice, we found that spontaneous gamma oscillations are accompanied by dynamic transitions between parvalbumin (PV) interneuron states. Rather than acting as a uniformly synchronized population, PV interneurons showed sequential recruitment, transient inactivation, and frequent transitions between activity states, indicating the formation of dynamic functional networks. In J20 Alzheimer's disease mice, these state transitions became dysregulated, leading to abnormal PV recruitment, impaired coupling between PV activity and gamma oscillations, and network hyperexcitability. Increasing interneuron excitability through expression of the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.1 restored PV cell dynamics, gamma oscillations, and network stability. These findings reveal dynamic PV cell states as an organizing feature of gamma oscillations and identify their disruption as a circuit mechanism of network dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease.

### A cross-species AAV platform for precise microglial interrogation
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yu F et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 13

Targeting microglia with adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) remains challenging because of inefficient transduction, off-target expression, and inconsistent payload performance. Here, we develop an integrated, Cre-independent AAV platform that combines a compact 446-bp microglia-specific regulatory element (MSRE), a high-yield AAV2/9.m capsid, microRNA-mediated neuronal de-targeting, and a microglia-tailored codon-optimization strategy. Following direct intracranial delivery, the optimized vector achieves robust, long-lasting transgene expression with greater than 90% microglial specificity across multiple mouse brain regions, including in an Alzheimer's disease model. With doxorubicin-assisted delivery, the platform also enables efficient and selective microglial targeting in rats, rhesus macaques, and human ex vivo brain slices. Codon optimization supports the expression of otherwise challenging functional payloads, enabling calcium imaging, chemogenetic manipulation, and CRISPR-Cas9-mediated genome editing. This cross-species toolkit provides a versatile resource for investigating microglial biology and developing microglia-directed therapeutic strategies.

### The genetic landscape of myotonic dystrophy type 1: insights from nearly one million genomes
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Rahman RP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 10

Myotonic Dystrophy type 1 (DM1) is a multisystem disorder caused by CTG repeat expansions in the dystrophia myotonica protein kinase (DMPK) gene. Current prevalence estimates of 1 in 3,000-8,000 have traditionally relied on clinically ascertained and geographically restricted cohorts, underestimating population burden and failing to capture ancestry diversity. Using large-scale whole-genome sequencing (WGS) datasets, we aimed to identify DMPK repeat expansions across nearly one million participants, define their ancestry-specific distribution, and evaluate associated clinical phenotypes, with particular focus on cardiac manifestations and early-onset cataracts. We analysed WGS participants from three cohorts: Genomics England (n = 80,110), UK Biobank (n = 490,086), and the All of Us Research Program (n = 414,830). DMPK repeat lengths were estimated using ExpansionHunter. Alleles were classified as Normal (≤37 repeats), Intermediate (38-49 repeats), or Pathogenic (≥50 repeats). Genetic ancestry was assigned using cohort-specific methods. Linked health record data assessed cardiac phenotypes in UK Biobank and early-onset cataracts in Genomics England and UK Biobank. Across all cohorts (n = 985,026), pathogenic DMPK repeat expansions were identified in 645 individuals (0.07%; ∼1 in 1,527), a prevalence two-to-five times higher than previous estimates. Most expansions occurred in those of European ancestry, although expansions were detected across all groups, including up to 1 in 6,693 African and Asian ancestry. In UK Biobank, individuals with a pathogenic expansion had increased odds of any cardiac diagnosis (odds ratio [OR]: 2.06, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.58-2.67; p = 5.8×108) and greater cardiac diagnostic burden (risk ratio 2.46, CI: 1.92-3.15; p = 1.6×10-13). Conduction abnormalities-particularly atrioventricular and left bundle-branch block, atrial fibrillation, and flutter-showed the strongest association with expansion status (OR: 4.86, CI: 3.38-6.79; p = 2.7×10-18). No sex differences were observed among those with a DMPK expansion, although male sex was independently associated with conduction disease in the overall population. Approximately one-in-ten individuals with pathogenic expansions had a cataract diagnosis before age 55. Most individuals with an expansion had no prior DM1 diagnosis, highlighting the value of systematic genetic testing in these phenotypes to improve diagnosis, shorten the diagnostic interval, and mitigate harm. By integrating WGS data from nearly one million individuals, this study refines understanding of the population distribution and clinical consequences of DMPK repeat expansions, revealing a reservoir of undiagnosed and potentially untreated multisystemic disease. These findings support routine inclusion of DMPK analysis within specified cardiac and ophthalmic diagnostic pathways to enable earlier detection.

### Biallelic variants in PIKFYVE cause a hypomyelinating leukodystrophy with endolysosomal dysfunction
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Averdunk L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

The PIKfyve-FIG4-VAC14 lipid kinase complex generates phosphoinositide PI(3,5)P2. Pathogenic variants in VAC14 and FIG4 cause neurodevelopmental disorders and PIKFYVE has been linked to autosomal dominant fleck corneal dystrophy. We searched for the underlyging, genetic cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder in five affected individuals from three families using exome- or genome sequencing. Cranial MRI images and clinical phenotypes were analyzed. Size, number and acidity of cellular vacuoles were investigated in patient-derived fibroblasts from family 1 using LysoSensor acidophilic probes. Levels of PI(3,5)P2 were quantified in patient-derived fibroblasts from family 1 using high-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. Pikfyve-mutant zebrafish larvae were injected with wildtype-mRNA or mutant mRNA harboring the PIKFYVE missense variant identified in family 3. Pikfyve-mutant zebrafish larvae were treated with metformin, bafilomycin A1, or rapamycin. We identified biallelic PIKFYVE variants in five affected individuals with hypomyelinating leukodystrophy, brain hyperintensities of inferior olives, thalami, long tracts, and optic nerves. Clinically, they presented with intellectual disability, spasticity, muscular weakness, and nystagmus. Additional features included feeding difficulties, auditory dysfunction, insulin resistance, microcephaly, peripheral neuropathy, and mild creatine kinase elevations. We detected four distinct PIKFYVE variants: three loss-of-function variants (family 1 & 2), and one p.Arg1156Pro missense variant (family 3). The pathogenicity of the p.Arg1156Pro variant was confirmed by phenotypic rescue of pikfyve-mutant zebrafish larvae injected with wildtype-mRNA but not with p.Arg1156Pro mRNA. Consistent with the role of PIKfyve in maintaining endolysosomal homeostasis, patient-derived fibroblasts of family 1 accumulated enlarged, hyperacidic vacuoles and showed a reduced PI(3,5)P2 production upon hyperosmotic stimulation. Exposing pikfyve-mutant zebrafisch larvae to the mTORC1 inhibitor and TRPML1 activator rapamycin attenuated cellular vacuolization, highlighting potential therapeutic approaches. Our findings establish PIKFYVE deficiency as a novel cause of hypomyelinating leukodystrophy and endolysosomal dysfunction.

### Microglial Presenilin-1 modulates neuronal function and memory-associated behaviors
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Frost PDS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Microglial cells, the macrophages of the brain parenchyma, are increasingly recognized to play a key role in synaptic plasticity and function. Presenilin 1 (PS1) is an essential protein involved in learning and memory; the loss of neuronal Presenilin function causes cognitive deficits and impaired synapse plasticity in mice. Here we show memory enhancement in male mice lacking PS1 in microglia (PS1 ΔMG ). Leveraging high-throughput RNA sequencing with single-cell deconvolution-analysis, we estimate that PS1 ΔMG  increases Cd52-lo microglial population, a proliferative group enriched in AP-1 responsive genes; and increases chemokines  Ccl7  and  Ccl8  In silico prediction of binding partners showed that microglial CCL7 may act via inhibition of neuronal CCR5, a receptor known to negatively regulate learning and memory. By profiling active neuronal populations via PhosphoTRAP the data showed a non-autonomous cell effect of microglial PS1 by modulating genes involved in neuronal excitability and synaptic plasticity . Ex vivo  electrophysiology and in vivo calcium imaging in freely behaving mice confirm these effects with functional changes in CA1 hippocampus as seen by increased NMDA/AMPA ratios and increased amplitude of neuronal Ca2+ transients in freely behaving animals, respectively. Remarkably, PS1 ΔMG  mice exhibited increased memory retention in contextual fear conditioning and novel object recognition. Together, these data suggest a novel mechanism where microglial PS1 negatively regulates neuronal activity through modulation of NMDAR and memory-associated behaviors. Significance Statement  Microglia are increasingly recognized as active regulators of neuronal function, yet the molecular mechanisms that constrain their influence on synaptic activity remain incompletely defined. This study identifies microglial Presenilin-1 (PS1) as a key regulator of neuron-microglia signaling that shapes synaptic transmission and learning. We show that loss of microglial PS1 shifts microglia toward a transcriptional and signaling state that enhances NMDA receptor-dependent synaptic activity and improves memory, demonstrating that microglial molecular programs can directly modulate neuronal excitability. These findings establish microglial PS1 as a physiological brake on circuit plasticity and reveal that altering microglial signaling alone is sufficient to influence cognition. Targeting microglial regulatory pathways may therefore provide new strategies to enhance synaptic resilience and cognitive function in brain disorders.
