### The secondary school transition as a turning point for internalizing symptoms: Trajectories, correlates, and early adulthood outcomes
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Brittain H, Vaillancourt T | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Internalizing disorders are the most common adolescent mental disorders. Using complementary variable-centered and person-centered approaches, we examined whether internalizing symptoms across eight years of adolescence (Grades 5-12) followed a continuous developmental trend or were disrupted by the transition to secondary school, and if different subgroups followed different pathways. Participants were 701 Canadian youth (52.9% girls; 75.9% white). Annual self-reported internalizing symptoms (ages 11-18) were examined using piecewise latent growth curve analysis (LGCM), revealing significant curve and level discontinuity at the transition, with distinct developmental phases in elementary and secondary school. Girls, youth with lower household income, and those with higher bullying victimization and lower social support showed elevated symptoms and/or steeper growth. An LGCM yielded four classes: Low (51.8%), Moderate (32.5%), Increasing (10.6%), and Declining (5.1%). The Declining and Moderate classes had comparable risk for early adulthood outcomes, demonstrating equifinality. Although beginning with similar levels, the Increasing class was at elevated risk compared to the Moderate class, demonstrating multifinality. Findings underscore that the secondary school transition can be viewed as a transition-linked turning point for a subset of adolescents, with implications for targeted screening and intervention beginning in elementary school.

### Adverse Childhood Experiences and Unintended Pregnancy: Evidence From North Dakota PRAMS, 2017-2020
**כתב עת:** 📄 AJPM Focus | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Shyllon OF et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

HIGHLIGHTS • Study used 4 years of Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System data from North Dakota (2017–2020). • Higher cumulative adverse childhood experience (ACE) exposure showed a dose–response relationship with unintended pregnancy. • Women with ≥4 ACEs had ∼2 times higher odds of unintended pregnancy. • Cumulative ACE score outperformed factor-based and individual-item ACE models. • Two specific ACEs (household substance use and parental separation/divorce) showed independent associations.

Introduction Unintended pregnancy remains a significant public health issue with lasting consequences. Adverse childhood experiences are linked to risky behaviors and poor reproductive outcomes; however, limited research has examined optimal approaches to measuring adverse childhood experience exposure in rural populations. This study examines the relationship between adverse childhood experiences and pregnancy intendedness in North Dakota from 2017 to 2020. Methods This cross-sectional study used North Dakota Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System data (N=2,973). A cumulative adverse childhood experience score was calculated. Exploratory factor analysis identified 2 adverse childhood experience groupings (abuse/neglect and household dynamics). Pregnancy intendedness was dichotomized as intended or unintended; a sensitivity analysis supported this categorization. Multivariable logistic regression assessed the associations between adverse childhood experiences and unintended pregnancy, controlling for sociodemographic and behavioral factors. The models compared the cumulative adverse childhood experience score, factor-based groupings, and individual adverse childhood experience items. Results Unintended pregnancy was more common among women with ≥4 adverse childhood experiences (51%) than among women with no adverse childhood experiences (32%). A dose–response relationship was observed, with increasing adverse childhood experience exposure associated with increased odds of unintended pregnancy. In adjusted models, younger age, being unmarried, higher parity, marijuana use before pregnancy, and higher adverse childhood experience exposure were independently associated with unintended pregnancy, whereas no significant differences were observed by residence or maternal race. The cumulative adverse childhood experience score showed greater predictive value than factor-based or item-only models. Sensitivity analyses revealed consistent adverse childhood experience gradients across subcategories of unintended pregnancy. Conclusions Higher cumulative adverse childhood experience exposure is associated with a greater risk of unintended pregnancy. Findings support the use of cumulative adverse childhood experience measures in reproductive health research and highlight the importance of trauma-informed care and upstream strategies that address childhood adversity.

### Childhood trauma and cognitive decline in women: an empirical analysis of cognitive vulnerability in later life
**כתב עת:** 📄 Front Aging Neurosci | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Salem MA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Childhood trauma has lasted consequences for women’s cognitive health and this may contribute to memory decline and increased vulnerability to cognitive impairment in later life. A cross sectional study was carried out to assess the correlation between childhood trauma and cognitive status among 55 women aged 58–79 years were selected from Departments of Psychiatry and General Medicine, Tanta University Hospital, Egypt. The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) was used to evaluate cognitive performance, the Global Deterioration Scale (GDS) for global clinical staging, and the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) for childhood adversity. Overall, emotional abuse was exposed the most with a high mean score on the CTQ subscale. Pearson correlations and multiple linear regression models (controlling for age) showed that emotional neglect and physical neglect were the strongest predictors across both outcomes. For MoCA performance, emotional neglect (β = −0.0877, r = −0.85, p < 0.01) and physical neglect (β = −0.0100, r = −0.80, p < 0.01) showed the largest effects. GDS coefficients had an absolute value that was proportionately smaller (emotional neglect: β = 0.0112; physical neglect: β = 0.0013) and, consistent with the higher-is-worse nature of the GDS, were positive in direction. Additional findings show a greater amount of neglect predicted more advanced clinical staging, and that there was no change to the relative order of effects by predictor compared to the MoCA model. The regression model accounted for 72.5% of the variance (R 2 = 0.725) in MoCA. The results indicate that childhood neglect, especially emotional neglect, might be an important risk factor for cognitive vulnerability in adulthood and highlight the necessity to intervene early in childhood to promote cognitive health in women.

1 Introduction Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder that primarily affects older adults. This leads to declines in memory, cognitive abilities, and everyday functioning. It is the most common cause of dementia which has affected an estimated 50 million people worldwide. This figure expected to rise substantially as global populations age ( Alzheimer’s Association, 2022 ; Scheidt, 2023 ). Epidemiological evidence has consistently demonstrated that women are more prone to developing AD and that almost two thirds of cases are female ( Moustafa, 2021 ; Zhu et al., 2021 ). This sex difference has prompted an increased interest in sex-specific risk factors that might help to explain this difference ( Chiow and Cicero, 2025 ). The cumulative effects of adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) such as trauma and chronic stress on cognitive health across the life span is an emerging focus of research ( Anda et al., 2005 ; Heim and Nemeroff, 2001 ). Physical, emotional, sexual abuse, and multiple types of neglect and household dysfunction have been linked to a wide variety of health issues as an adult, including mental disorders, cardiovascular disease, and neurodegenerative diseases like AD ( Graves et al., 2021 ). Especially childhood trauma can have a significant impact on memory and cognitive capacity in later life ( De Marco et al., 2017 ; Prieto et al., 2023 ). Critically, women experience ACEs more than men and are thus potentially more vulnerable to the long-term cognitive effects of ACEs ( Madigan et al., 2023 ; Moustafa, 2021 ). In addition to childhood stressors, women are also at higher risk of experiencing chronic social stressors such as for example caregiving responsibilities, gendered social expectations, and gender based violence. These can add to early life trauma and can also further lower cognitive resilience ( Fliege et al., 2005 ; Haahr-Pedersen et al., 2020 ; Patel and Oremus, 2023 ). It is believed that these stressors act partially through the dys-regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and related stress-response mechanisms that play a role in depression and neurodegeneration ( Heim and Nemeroff, 2001 ; Lei et al., 2025 ; Graves et al., 2021 ; Prieto et al., 2023 ). This combination of biological and social vulnerabilities could render women a population in which early adversity may have lasting effects on cognitive aging. This is a premise that has been increasingly supported by social and structural determinants of brain health studies ( Livingston et al., 2020 ; Safiri et al., 2024 ). Women’s elevated AD risk thus reflects the combined influence of biological, psychological, and sociocultural factors. Gendered caregiving responsibilities and associated sociocultural stressors in various cultural contexts have been linked to heightened chronic stress and depressive symptoms, both of which are independent risk factors for accelerated cognitive decline ( Moustafa, 2021 ; Safiri et al., 2024 ). In Asia, especially in the India and China, cultural gender roles and family systems also play a role in the increased rate of emotional and physical neglect in women that can only have life-long consequences on cognitive abilities ( Jain et al., 2022 ; Xiang et al., 2022 ). In the Middle East, domestic violence is another trauma that women are likely to experience, because gender inequality and violence are more common in this region. Besides, gender inequality, domestic violence, and emotional neglect documented across European, Asian, and Middle Eastern settings may each contribute to neurodegenerative risk through accumulated adversity ( Haahr-Pedersen et al., 2020 ; Umberson and Karas Montez, 2010 ). Situating ACEs within broader theoretical frameworks offers additional explanatory power for understanding their long-term cognitive consequences. Similarly, the social and structural determinants of brain health framework highlights systemic inequities. (2020) , Röhr et al. (2022) , Adkins-Jackson et al. (2023) , Shaaban et al. (2026) . This is also supported with longitudinal and retrospective cohort studies across diverse populations ( Corney et al., 2022 ; He and Yang, 2024 ; Radford et al., 2017 ). Critically, this risk follows a dose-response pattern. ### Microglia sculpt dopamine receptor-mediated adolescent behaviors in a social experience dependent manner
**כתב עת:** 📄 bioRxiv | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Clark M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 30

Abstract Adverse childhood experiences like social isolation (SI) contribute to long term negative health outcomes and the development of severe psychiatric disorder. Adolescence is a unique period of coordinated social and motivational system development driven by dopamine (DA) signaling. Microglia, immune cells of the brain, may play a unique role in regulating SI-induced DA system changes and associated behavioral dysfunction. Male microglia refine dopamine D1 receptors (D1rs) in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) of adolescent male rats shaping play behavior. Here we characterized the molecular developmental impacts of postweaning SI from P21-P38, with a resocialization period to P54. We tested the role of microglial engulfment of D1rs in mediating behavioral consequences of SI using neutrophil inhibitory factor (NIF) to block complement-mediated engulfment required for D1r pruning. We found sex specific impacts of SI on brain development and the DA system. In SI males, microglia engulf more NAc D1rs than group housed (GH) controls. Within GH animals, NAc D1r density is positively correlated to play and allogrooming. In SI animals, NAc D1r density is negatively correlated to biting behavior, and is not correlated to play or allogrooming. Pharmacologically inhibiting microglial engulfment of D1r via bilateral NAc NIF injection prevents observed D1r:behavior correlations in GH and SI animals. NIF treatment interacts with SI, exaggerating aggressive-like behavior, showing a role for microglia in regulating aggressive-like behavior via complement pruning in adolescence. Microglia may play a unique role in regulating behavior that drive negative health outcomes in the face of developmental stress, including SI.

### Risk and Resilience Factors of Participants in Urban Community Gun Violence Intervention & Prevention: Insights from the Advance Peace Program
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Urban Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Corburn J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Urban community-based gun violence prevention programs engage those with the greatest risk of perpetrating violence and provide them with services and supports to help them change their behaviors. Empirical studies of this public-health-oriented strategy, called community violence intervention (CVI), have been mixed, and few have reported which risk factors are most prevalent among participants. We offer detailed descriptive data about the risks and protective factors that participants in one CVI program, called Advance Peace, displayed at the onset of the intervention. Advance Peace operates in 13 cities and had over 1000 participants, or "fellows," between January 2021 and December 2024. We found that almost 34% of fellows were under 18 years old, 70.6% were African American, and over 90% were male. More than half (52.4%) reported poor mental health, 76.7% entered with four or more Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs), and over 30% had over 7 ACEs; more than 56% had been shot at, and almost half had a prior firearm-related arrest and a family member killed by a gun. We found that 49.2% had both four or more ACEs and at least two-gun violence exposures. Among program participants, only 17% were receiving any social services at enrollment. We also found that more than 62% wanted to change their lifestyle, and more than half had assets and strengths that could be protective or buffering factors that might prevent future gun use. We conclude that CVI programs should capture both the risks and strengths of program participants to ensure violence prevention interventions are trauma informed and healing centered.
