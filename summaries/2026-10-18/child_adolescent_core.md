### Parental and early child skills outweigh home numeracy environment in predicting maths development
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Amland T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 13

BACKGROUND: Studies on the home numeracy environment (HNE) show mixed evidence of its link with children's mathematical skills. A rarely considered aspect is parents' dual role as providers of the home environment and transmitters of genetic predispositions relevant for learning. Parents' maths abilities may serve as a proxy for the genetic transmission that confounds the home-child association, as proposed in the Familial Control Method. We examined whether numeracy activities at home predict children's maths development, while accounting for parents' maths ability and children's initial skill level.

METHODS: We analysed data from 244 Norwegian school children, 201 mothers and 146 fathers. Children's maths fluency was assessed in Grades 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. Parents completed an HNE questionnaire and a maths assessment when children were in Grade 2. Relations among HNE, parental maths and children's maths development were tested using structural equation modelling.

RESULTS: Both formal and informal HNE predicted Grade 3 maths. After controlling for parental maths, only mothers' informal HNE remained associated with Grade 3 performance. When Grade 1 maths was included, only fathers' formal activities were related to Grade 3 skills, while both parents' maths still predicted Grade 3 performance. Only Grade 1 maths predicted Grade 3 as well as subsequent growth.

CONCLUSIONS: The finding that parental maths predicted children's maths beyond family activities underscores the importance of addressing familial confounding in research on the home environment. To better understand the role of the HNE in maths development, research should consider parents' skills, children's early skills and HNE as a dynamic process.

### ADHD symptoms and learning difficulties: co-occurrence and directionality from Grades 1 to 9
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Psyridou M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 11

BACKGROUND: Children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) are at increased risk of learning difficulties. This association may reflect shared risk factors, ADHD symptoms hindering learning, or learning difficulties increasing ADHD symptoms. We aimed to map rates of co-occurrence among ADHD, dyslexia, dyscalculia, and reading comprehension difficulties and to investigate directional influences between ADHD symptoms and reading fluency, arithmetic, and reading comprehension.

METHODS: Reading fluency, arithmetic, and reading comprehension were assessed longitudinally in approximately 2,000 Finnish children across Grades 1-9 (ages 7-16). Attention/hyperactivity symptoms were measured with teacher ratings (Grades 1-4) and self-reports (Grades 6-9). ADHD, dyslexia, dyscalculia, and reading comprehension difficulties were based on a 10th percentile cut-off. Directionality between ADHD symptoms and academic skills was examined using random intercept cross-lagged panel models (RI-CLPMs) to separate stable traits from time-specific states.

RESULTS: Children with a learning difficulty were 1.14-1.76 times more likely to have ADHD, whereas children with one learning difficulty were 4.32-4.62 times more likely to have another. The RI-CLPMs showed consistent autoregressive effects across all domains, indicating stable within-person variation over time. At the between-person level (i.e. trait), academic skills were negatively related to ADHD symptoms, especially those reported by teachers. At the within-person level (i.e. state), ADHD symptoms were unrelated to reading fluency and arithmetic. However, at multiple time points, cross-lagged effects emerged from ADHD symptoms to subsequent reading comprehension, indicating that elevated ADHD symptoms predicted subsequent declines in reading comprehension.

CONCLUSIONS: Learning difficulties co-occurred more frequently with each other (homotypic) than with ADHD (heterotypic co-occurrence). Both ADHD symptoms and learning difficulties showed trait-like stability. After accounting for these, ADHD symptoms were no longer linked to reading fluency or arithmetic. However, more ADHD symptoms predicted later declines in reading comprehension, likely because comprehension requires sustained attention - often impaired in children with hyperactivity and inattention.

### Impaired visual-motor integration function in children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: potential neural correlates from resting-state fNIRS
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Visual-motor integration (VMI) impairment is common in Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), yet its underlying neurobiological basis remains unclear. This study aimed to explore the characteristics of VMI in children with ADHD and its statistical role in the association between functional connectivity (FC) and core symptoms. A total of 332 children with ADHD and 103 healthy controls (HC) completed the Beery-Buktenica Developmental Test of Visual-Motor Integration (Beery VMI). Group comparisons and dimensional correlation analyses were performed to explore the VMI characteristics of children with ADHD. Subsequently, 127 children with ADHD and 91 HC children underwent resting-state functional near-infrared spectroscopy. Mediation analyses were performed to investigate the role of VMI in the relationship between atypical FC and core symptoms of ADHD. Children with ADHD exhibited deficits in VMI performance, with 28.92% showing abnormal VMI. VMI was negatively correlated with core ADHD symptoms. At the network level, VMI showed significant statistical indirect associations in the relationship between FC of DMN-DMN, DMN-DAN, and DMN-VAN networks and inattention symptoms, and between FC of the DMN-DAN networks and hyperactivity/impulsivity symptoms. At the channel level, VMI was also indirectly associated with FC of the left superior frontal gyrus (SFG)-right middle occipital gyrus (MOG) and ADHD core symptoms. Children with ADHD have deficits in VMI, and poorer VMI performance is associated with greater severity of core ADHD symptoms. Altered intra- and inter- network FC of the DMN may represent a potential neural correlate of VMI impairment in ADHD.

### Psychiatric and substance use disorder polygenic scores are associated with the exposome in youth
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hung IT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 16

Gene-environment correlations (rGE) present challenges for accurately estimating the individual and joint contributions of genetic and environmental influences on risk for psychiatric and substance use disorders. The present study examined rGE between polygenic risk scores (PGS) for psychiatric and substance use disorders and environmental exposures (i.e., exposome factors) using multi-level data spanning youth- and parent-reports, as well as objective measures of the built environment. Participants included 4,797 genetically similar to European reference populations, 1,585 genetically similar to African reference populations, and 2,012 genetically similar to Admixed American reference populations from the Adolescent Brain and Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study. The ABCD study was conducted at 21 sites across the U.S and used a stratified probability sampling design to ensure the cohort recruited was demographically diverse. Based on factor-analytic work conducted by Moore et al. (2022) in the ABCD study, the exposome included 6 factors: household adversity, neighborhood adversity, positive day-to-day experiences, state conservatism/ruralness, family values, and pregnancy/birth complications. Disorder-specific PGS for psychiatric (e.g., major depressive disorder, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) and substance use conditions (e.g., alcohol use disorders, cannabis use disorders), as well as PGS for common liability for externalizing and general substance use disorders, were used to examine genetic associations with the exposome factors. After adjusting for participant sex, age, log income-to-needs ratio, and genetic ancestry principal components, higher PGS for psychiatric (e.g., broad externalizing liability, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder) and substance use disorders (e.g., general substance use disorders, cannabis use disorder) were positively associated with household adversity and negatively associated with positive day-to-day experiences. Our results indicate that polygenic risk for psychiatric and substance use disorders correlate with environmental factors in adolescence at multiple levels, most notably household adversity and positive day-to-day experiences. Findings underscore the importance of investigating rGE when examining these environmental exposures with genetic risk for psychiatric and substance use disorders.

### From childhood to adulthood: an attempt to model long-term outcomes of ADHD using machine learning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** van der Plas N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

BACKGROUND: Childhood-onset attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) persists in 28% to 58% of individuals, and is associated with adverse long-term outcomes, like additional psychiatric diagnoses and lower educational attainment. Knowing early life predictors for adverse long-term outcomes might help develop preventive measures, but too few have been identified using conventional statistical models. The current study investigated the potential of machine learning in identifying predictors for ADHD persistence and long-term functioning of adults with childhood-onset ADHD.

METHOD: A total of 133 adults (M age  = 29.53, SD age  = 2.83, 75% males) from an 18-year prospective cohort study were assessed on ADHD persistence and long-term functioning. Machine learning models were developed using cross-validation, with and without additional resampling, and compared to a conventional logistic/linear regression model. With SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) we explored model behavior. Childhood predictors encompassed ADHD symptoms and treatment, other psychopathology, cognition, somatic characteristics, ADHD polygenic risk score, and parental demographics and psychopathology.

RESULTS: Performance of all models for ADHD persistence and functioning (quality of life) was low. Best performing models included the logistic regression model with resampling for ADHD persistence (AUC's all models  = .52-.58; AUC logistic regression, resampling  = .58), and the ridge regression model for long-term functioning (R 2 's all models  = -0.36-0.02; R 2 's ridge regression  = .02, RMSE's  all models  = 2.07-2.42; RMSE's ridge regression  = 2.07).

CONCLUSIONS: Findings highlight the complexity of predicting long-term outcomes of ADHD. Model improvement is crucial before models can inform clinical practice, e.g., by increasing sample size and investigating additional predictors.

### Context-dependent gaze patterns in children with social anxiety disorder during repeated peer-evaluative stress: A naturalistic eye-tracking study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vietmeier N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Childhood social anxiety disorder (SAD) has been linked to altered attention to socially relevant cues, but findings on gaze behavior in youth remain mixed, particularly in naturalistic social-evaluative contexts. We examined whether children with SAD differ from healthy controls (HC) in gaze to peer faces during repeated peer-evaluative speech tasks. Children aged 9-14 years with SAD (n = 37) and HC (n = 41) completed two peer-evaluative speech tasks while gaze behavior was recorded with mobile eye-tracking. Before the second speech, participants were randomly assigned either to parental support or self-instruction during the anticipation phase. Fixation probability and fixation duration on audience faces were analyzed across the first four minutes of each speech. During the first speech, no significant group differences were observed for fixation probability or fixation duration. During the second speech, children with SAD showed lower fixation probability than HC, whereas no group differences emerged for fixation duration. Exploratory analyses suggested that fixation probability during the second speech varied by support condition and age. These findings suggest that gaze differences in childhood SAD may be subtle and context-dependent rather than uniformly evident across social-evaluative situations. In this sample, reduced fixation probability was observed during the second speech task only. Further research with larger samples and designs that disentangle repeated exposure from task- or stimulus-specific factors is needed.
