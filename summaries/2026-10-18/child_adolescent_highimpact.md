### Adult Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The New England Journal of Medicine (N Engl J Med) *(IF: 96.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Buitelaar JK, Newcorn JH | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a medical condition that is present in approximately 2.5% of adults worldwide and manifests in a variety of different ways across the lifespan. Adults with ADHD often have coexisting psychiatric disorders such as anxiety, mood, sleep, substance use, and personality disorders and are at increased risk for coexisting somatic conditions such as obesity, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, type 1 and type 2 diabetes, and immune and inflammatory conditions. Hence, such adults may be seen by clinicians providing care for a wide range of psychiatric and somatic conditions. Nevertheless, ADHD remains unrecognized and undiagnosed in many persons. Assessment requires interviewing of the patient for core ADHD symptoms, coexisting conditions, and their functional effects, which can be facilitated by using standardized rating scales. Key components of treatment are psychoeducation and psychostimulant or nonstimulant medication, mostly in combination with psychosocial interventions and lifestyle guidance.

### Change in HbA(1c) and weight after initiation of antipsychotic treatment in people with severe mental illness in England: an individual-level interrupted time-series analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bazo-Alvarez JC et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

BACKGROUND: Second-generation antipsychotics contribute to excess cardiometabolic morbidity in people with severe mental illness, but the timing of early glycaemic change and whether it parallels weight gain remain unclear. We aimed to evaluate glycated haemoglobin A 1c  (HbA 1c ) and weight trajectories before and after initiation of antipsychotic treatment.

METHODS: We conducted an individual-level interrupted time-series analysis in England using electronic health records (EHR) from the Clinical Practice Research Datalink Aurum database between Jan 1, 2000, and June 7, 2023. Included participants were aged 18-59 years with severe mental illness (SMI) who had at least a second oral second-generation antipsychotic prescription issued within 90 days from their first antipsychotic prescription. Individuals were excluded if they had dementia, type 2 diabetes, or antipsychotic prescriptions before age 18 years. The exposure was the first recorded oral initiation of antipsychotic treatment. We used individual-level, interrupted time-series mixed models with linear splines to estimate rates of change in HbA 1c  and weight in people with SMI before treatment initiation, 0-90 days after initiation, and from 91 days to 2 years after initiation. An additional outcome was time to type 2 diabetes diagnosis or antidiabetic medication prescription. Analyses were stratified by sex, antipsychotic drug, and baseline glycaemic status. People with lived experience contributed to the research question, study approach, interpretation, and dissemination.

FINDINGS: Of the 96 849 eligible individuals, 53 558 (55·3%) were male and 43 291 (44·7%) were female. The mean age of participants was 37·2 years (SD 11·1; range 18-59); 62 683 (64·7%) reported their ethnicity as White, 5952 (6·1%) as south Asian, 6736 (7·0%) as Black, 3793 (3·9%) as other or mixed ethnicity, and 17 693 (18·3%) had missing ethnicity. 74 233 (76·6%) individuals had at least one recorded weight measurement and 24 084 (24·9%) had at least one HbA 1c  measurement. In the pre-treatment period, HbA 1c  increased at a rate of 0·01 mmol/mol per week (95% CI 0·00-0·01) and weight at a rate of 0·02 kg (0·02-0·02) per week. In the 0-90 days following antipsychotic treatment initiation, HbA 1c  increased at a rate of 0·12 mmol/mol per week (0·09-0·15) and weight at 0·21 kg (0·20-0·22) per week. From day 91 to 2 years, the rate of increase of HbA 1c  returned to pre-treatment levels (0·01 mmol/mol per week [0·00-0·01]) and weight increased at 0·03 kg (0·02-0·03) per week. Increases were largest for olanzapine (HbA 1c  0·18 mmol/mol per week [95% CI 0·12-0·25]; weight 0·28 kg [0·27-0·30]) and generally higher among women (HbA 1c  0·14 mmol/mol [0·09-0·18]; weight 0·22 kg [0·21-0·23]). Quetiapine was associated with weight gain (0·19 kg per week [95% CI 0·18 to 0·21]), but minimal HbA 1c  change (0·02 mmol/mol [-0·03 to 0·08]). Among 918 individuals with baseline prediabetes, 126 (13·7%)developed type 2 diabetes, corresponding to an estimated 2-year cumulative incidence of 19·0%, compared with 152 (1·7%) of 9171 individuals with normal baseline HbA 1c , corresponding to an estimated 2-year cumulative incidence of 3·0%.

INTERPRETATION: Antipsychotic treatment initiation was followed by rapid HbA 1c  and weight increases in the first 90 days followed by slower increases up to 2 years. Imperfect concordance between HbA 1c  and weight supports baseline and 12-week monitoring of both outcomes, followed by risk-stratified monitoring and intervention.

FUNDING: National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR) Three Research Schools Mental Health Programme, NIHR School for Primary Care Research, NIHR Applied Research Collaboration West Midlands, NIHR Dementia and Neurodegeneration Policy Research Unit, UK Research and Innovation, University College London Hospitals NIHR Biomedical Research Centre, and NIHR North Thames Applied Research Collaboration.

### Long-term health, criminal justice, and socioeconomic outcomes among people subject to compulsory mental health care in Norway: a nationwide registry cohort study
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hofstad T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

BACKGROUND: Compulsory mental health care aims to manage risk and treatment needs among people with severe mental illness, but evidence on long-term outcomes across health, justice, and socioeconomic domains is scarce. We aimed to compare long-term outcomes across adult groups defined by community treatment orders (CTOs), compulsory hospitalisation, compulsory observation, and voluntary mental health care, and the general population.

METHODS: In this nationwide registry cohort study, we identified all individuals aged 18-65 years with specialist mental health-care contact in Norway from Jan 1, 2015, to Dec 31, 2016 and general population comparators sampled via Statistics Norway based on age and sex. Follow-up was through 2021-23, depending on registry availability. Study groups were defined hierarchically: CTO, compulsory hospitalisation without CTO, compulsory observation without compulsory hospitalisation or CTO, voluntary mental health care, and no specialist mental health-care contact (ie, the general population). Outcomes included mortality and cause of death, self-harm treated in emergency departments or hospital, substance use disorder, victimisation, violent or sexual offence charges or imprisonment, subsequent compulsory care, acute admissions, and socioeconomic outcomes. Data for all groups were sourced from linked nationwide registries. Separate binomial regressions estimated sex-specific probabilities by group, adjusting for birth year. People with lived experience of mental health services advised study design and interpretation.

FINDINGS: The final study sample comprised 321 708 individuals (mean age 36·5 years, range 18-65) across five mutually exclusive groups: CTO (n=3331), compulsory hospitalisation (n=2907), compulsory observation (n=2554), voluntary care (n=177 396), and general population (n=135 520), 193 473 (60·1%) were female and 128 235 (39·9%) were male; ethnicity data were unavailable. Compulsory care groups were associated with substantially worse long-term outcomes across health, criminal justice, and socioeconomic domains than voluntary patients and the general population. Across compulsory care groups, female participants had higher adjusted probabilities of self-harm or suicide than male participants, whereas male participants had higher adjusted probabilities of substance use disorder and of being charged with or imprisoned for violent or sexual offences compared with female participants. The compulsory observation group had the highest adjusted probabilities of self-harm or suicide and substance use disorder across the five groups. Self-harm or suicide was recorded in 135 (12·1%) of 1115 female participants (adjusted probability 12% [95% CI 10-14]) and 75 (5·7%) of 1308 male participants (5·5% [4·4-6·9]). Substance use disorder was recorded in 407 (39·1%) of 1041 female participants (39% [36-42]) and 639 (53·2%) of 1202 male participants (53% [50-56]). Charges or imprisonment for violent or sexual offences were recorded in 146 (14·2%) of 1030 female participants (14% [12-16]) and 446 (37·4% [34-40]) of 1194 male participants. During 2015-21, including the inclusion period, suicide and other external causes accounted for 132 (55·7%) of 237 classified deaths in compulsory observation, compared with 1818 (38·2%) of 4754 in voluntary care and 174 (18·6%) of 938 in the general population. The CTO group had the highest adjusted probability of disability benefit receipt: 89% (95% CI 87-91) in female participants and 93% (92-94) in male participants; a substantial minority of disability-benefit recipients in the CTO group also had earned income.

INTERPRETATION: Compulsory care groups were associated with distinct long-term prognoses from the voluntary care and general population groups. These findings provide population-level benchmarks for service planning and policy evaluation and show that legally defined compulsory care groups should not be treated as a single prognostic population.

FUNDING: Research Council of Norway.

### Estimates of Anxiety Symptoms Among US Adolescents
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Pediatrics (JAMA Pediatr) *(IF: 27.6)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bommersbach TJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Key Points Question What is the prevalence of anxiety symptoms among US adolescents, and how is anxiety symptom severity associated with mental health treatment? Findings In this nationally representative cross-sectional study of 15 730 US adolescents, 2851 (16.8%) reported moderate or severe anxiety symptoms. Greater symptom severity was associated with depression, suicidal thoughts, and substance use; however, nearly half of adolescents with moderate (793 [48.3%]) or severe (512 [41.2%]) symptoms received no past-year mental health treatment. Meaning Significant anxiety symptoms are common among US adolescents, yet many affected adolescents do not receive treatment, pointing to the need to improve early identification and access to evidence-based care.

Importance Anxiety symptoms are common among US adolescents and associated with substantial psychiatric burden, yet contemporary nationally representative estimates of anxiety symptom severity, associated clinical conditions, and treatment utilization are lacking. Objectives To estimate the prevalence of anxiety symptoms and symptom severity among US adolescents and examine associated co-occurring psychiatric conditions and treatment utilization. Design, Setting, and Participants This cross-sectional study used data from the 2024 National Survey on Drug Use and Health, a nationally representative survey of the US civilian, noninstitutionalized population. Participants included adolescents aged 12 to 20 years. Data were analyzed in June 2026. Exposure(s) Anxiety symptoms were assessed using the 7-item Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD-7) questionnaire, a brief self-report screener designed to identify probable generalized anxiety disorder and measure symptom severity. Main Outcomes and Measures National estimates of anxiety symptom severity were generated overall and by sociodemographic characteristics. Co-occurring psychiatric and substance use conditions and past-year mental health treatment utilization were examined across anxiety symptom severity categories. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with severe anxiety and receipt of mental health treatment. Results Among 15 730 adolescents (7725 [48.9%] female), 3642 (23.7%) reported mild anxiety symptoms, 1599 (9.6%) reported moderate symptoms, and 1252 (7.2%) reported severe symptoms. Female adolescents had substantially higher rates of moderate (13.4% vs 6.0%) and severe (10.8% vs 3.7%) anxiety symptoms than males. Among adolescents with severe anxiety symptoms, 802 (65.8%) met criteria for a past-year major depressive episode, 536 (48.2%) reported past-year suicidal thoughts, and 392 (31.0%) reported past-month substance use. Despite this high burden of co-occurring mental health conditions, 512 adolescents (41.2%) with severe anxiety symptoms and 793 (48.3%) of those with moderate anxiety symptoms received no mental health treatment during the preceding year. In multivariable analyses, mental health treatment was less likely among male adolescents, racial and ethnic minoritized adolescents, and uninsured adolescents. Conclusions and Relevance Per the results of this cross-sectional study, approximately 1 in 6 US adolescents reported moderate or severe anxiety symptoms in 2024, yet a substantial proportion received no mental health treatment. The high prevalence of anxiety symptoms, frequent co-occurrence with depression and suicidal thoughts, and large unmet need underscore the importance of improving early identification and access to evidence-based treatment for adolescents with anxiety symptoms.

This cross-sectional study estimates the prevalence of anxiety symptoms and symptom severity among US adolescents and examines associated co-occurring psychiatric conditions and treatment utilization.
