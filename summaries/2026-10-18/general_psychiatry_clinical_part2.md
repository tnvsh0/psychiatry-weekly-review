### GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation and risk of psychiatric hospitalisation and emergency department presentation in adults with schizophrenia and type 2 diabetes in the USA: an observational cohort study using electronic health records data
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מחקר תצפיתי | **מחברים:** Davoodian N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

BACKGROUND: Many individuals with schizophrenia have persistent psychotic symptoms despite antipsychotic treatment, and the disorder is associated with a disproportionately high burden of metabolic dysregulation. Whether GLP-1 receptor agonists are associated with psychiatric benefit alongside their metabolic effects remains unknown. We aimed to examine associations between GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation and psychiatric hospitalisation and emergency department presentation in adults with schizophrenia and type 2 diabetes, compared with DPP-4 inhibitor initiation, SGLT2 inhibitor initiation, and non-initiation.

METHODS: In this observational cohort study, we used electronic health record (EHR) data from the US NeuroBlu Data repository to construct a cohort of adults (≥18 years) with documented diagnosis of schizophrenia and comorbid type 2 diabetes. The exposure was the initiation of GLP-1 receptor agonists, compared with non-initiation or initiation of DPP-4 inhibitors or SGLT2 inhibitors. The index date, from Jan 1, 2018, was defined as the date of the first recorded psychiatric visit at which both a diagnosis of schizophrenia and comorbid type 2 diabetes were present in the participant's record. Time zero (T 0 ) was the date of the first prescription of any of these agents between Jan 1, 2018, and Oct 1, 2025; for non-initiators, T 0  was the index date. Participants contributed person-time from T 0  until 2 years of follow-up, the last recorded clinical observation in the NeuroBlu Data repository, or the end of the observation period (March 24, 2026), whichever happened earliest. The main outcomes of interest included risk of psychiatric hospitalisation and psychiatric emergency department presentation. Inverse probability weighting was applied to balance demographic, clinical, and pharmacological covariates, and weighted regression models were used. Analyses were conducted using an intention-to-treat framework, whereby exposure status was defined according to the medication initiated at T 0 .

FINDINGS: A total of 70 257 individuals met the inclusion criteria; mean age was 53·1 years [SD 12·32] and 33 882 [48·2%] were women. Among the 70 257 eligible individuals, 9093 (12·9%) initiated GLP-1 receptor agonists, 4968 (7·1%) initiated DPP-4 inhibitors, 5328 (7·6%) initiated SGLT2 inhibitors, and 50 868 (72·4%) were non-initiators. In weighted regression analyses, GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation was associated with a lower risk of psychiatric hospitalisation compared with non-initiation (weighted hazard ratio [HR] 0·76 [95% CI 0·71-0·82]), DPP-4 inhibitor initiation (0·82 [0·75-0·90]), and SGLT2 inhibitor initiation (0·83 [0·78-0·88]). GLP-1 receptor agonist initiation was also associated with a lower risk of emergency department presentation than non-initiation (0·71 [0·65-0·77]), DPP-4 inhibitor initiation (0·87 [0·80-0·95]), and SGLT2 inhibitor initiation (0·80 [0·73-0·88]).

INTERPRETATION: GLP-1-based therapy initiation was associated with lower psychiatric acute-care use than non-initiation and initiation of DPP-4 or SGLT2 inhibitors in adults with schizophrenia and type 2 diabetes. Potential residual confounding and selection bias restrict generalisability of the results. The findings suggest that lower psychiatric acute-care use could be a favourable secondary benefit when GLP-1-based therapies are clinically indicated for metabolic management, although their use as primary psychiatric treatments require investigation in randomised and mechanistic studies.

FUNDING: Synergy for the Development of Innovative Clinical Treatments in Schizophrenia (SYNDICAT) project (GNT2035912).

### Online Sports Betting Legalization and Behavioral Health Care Use
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Westermeyer B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

This cohort study investigates the association between online sports betting legalization and behavioral health care use.

Key Points Question What is the association between online sports betting legalization and behavioral health care use? Findings In this quasi-experimental difference-in-differences study including almost 62 million enrollee-years, online sports betting legalization was associated with an increase in the probability of people with employer-sponsored health insurance having a prescription fill or outpatient visit related to depression or anxiety. Associations were more precise and robust for men than women, and legalization was not associated with changes in prescriptions or outpatient visits for a substance use disorder. Meaning Results suggest that state legalization of online sports betting was associated with measurable increases in mental health care use and may increase strain on the behavioral health system.

Importance The number of states with legal online sports betting has grown from 1 to 31 since 2018, expanding access to gambling. Despite the known behavioral health burdens associated with gambling, little is understood about legalization’s impact on mental health and substance use treatment use. Objective To evaluate the association between online sports betting legalization and treatment use for mental health conditions and substance use disorders (SUD). Design, Setting, and Participants This was a quasi-experimental staggered difference-in-differences study using claims data from January 2016 to December 2023. The setting was a large, national convenience sample of people with employer-sponsored health insurance. Included in the analysis were men and women aged 18 to 64 years living in states that legalized online sports betting during 2018-2022 or had not by the end of the study period. Exposure State legalization of online sports betting. Main Outcomes and Measures Outcomes were measured at the state-year level, weighted by number of enrollees as follows: proportions of enrollees with 1 or more prescription fills for antidepressant, antianxiety, or SUD medications and proportions of enrollees with 1 or more outpatient, emergency department, or inpatient visits with a diagnosis for depression, anxiety, or SUD. Results Among 61 951 305 enrollee-years, 31 914 852 (52%) were women, and the mean (SD) age was 41.9 (13.5) years. Online sports betting legalization was associated with increases in the probability of antidepressant use (men: 0.57 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.26-0.89 percentage points; women: 1.08 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.23-1.94 percentage points) and antianxiety use (men: 0.32 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.14-0.50 percentage points; women: 0.64 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.16-1.12 percentage points), but there was no detectable change in SUD medication use. Legalization was also associated with increases in the probability of an outpatient diagnosis for depression (men: 0.34 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.20-0.49 percentage points; women: 0.57 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.25-0.89 percentage points) and anxiety (men: 0.67 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.32-1.01 percentage points; women: 1.15 percentage points; 95% CI, 0.50-1.81 percentage points) but not SUD. Legalization was not associated with behavioral health diagnoses in the emergency department and inpatient settings. Conclusions and Relevance Results of this quasi-experimental difference-in-differences study suggest that online sports betting legalization was associated with an increase in outpatient and prescription mental health care use among people with employer-sponsored health insurance. These findings underscore the importance of prevention and treatment of potential adverse health consequences of legalized online sports betting.

### Evaluating the Effect of Positive Childhood Experiences on the Risk of Psychiatric Disorders in Early Adulthood
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Murphy J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

OBJECTIVE: While the negative impacts of traumatic childhood experiences are well established, less is known about whether positive childhood experiences (PCEs) contribute to long-term mental health. The aim of this study was to estimate the effect of PCEs on the risk of psychiatric disorders in early adulthood.

METHODS: Participants included 3,499 children from the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children. Eligible participants were nonrandomly exposed to PCEs (e.g., "school enjoyment" or "feeling safe in neighborhood") between ages 0 and 17, measured as a score ranging from 0 to 10. Inverse probability weighting (IPW) was used to account for the likelihood of PCE exposure conditional on confounders. The effect of PCE score on risk of psychiatric disorders at age 24, using IPW- and confounder-adjusted estimates, is reported. Subgroup analysis of individual disorders was subsequently conducted.

RESULTS: In unweighted analyses, each one-unit increase in the PCE score was associated with a 12% decrease in the odds of psychiatric disorder (odds ratio=0.88, 95% CI=0.82, 0.95). In the IPW- and confounder-adjusted model, this effect was retained (odds ratio=0.89, 95% CI=0.82, 0.95). Robust associations between PCEs and lower odds of depressive disorder (odds ratio=0.84, 95% CI=0.76, 0.93) and generalized anxiety disorder (odds ratio=0.91, 95% CI=0.84, 0.99) were found. The findings for PCE score and psychotic disorder were inconclusive.

CONCLUSIONS: The findings indicate that PCEs are associated with reduced odds of meeting criteria for psychiatric disorders in early adulthood, particularly depression. Further research should explore PCEs as a potential preventive strategy that might reduce the burden of psychiatric disorders in early adulthood.

### The effect of tele-nursing counseling on functioning and medication adherence in individuals with bipolar disorder: A randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Arch Psychiatr Nurs | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Ubiç N, Ayhan D | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

AIM: This study was conducted to determine the effect of tele-nursing counseling on functioning and medication adherence among individuals with bipolar disorder.

METHOD: The research was directed utilizing a randomized controlled experimental design with pretest and posttest control groups. The study included 70 individuals (experimental = 35, control = 35) who were followed at a Community Mental Health Center (CMHC). The experimental group received structured tele-nursing counseling consisting of weekly telephone interviews and supportive text messages for eight weeks. The control group continued to receive standard clinical care. Data were gathered utilizing the Introductory Information Form, the Functioning Assessment Short Test (FAST), and the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS).

RESULTS: At the end of the eight-week intervention, there was a statistically significant difference between the experimental and control groups in terms of functional levels (adjusted between-group difference [experimental minus control] = -6.8, 95% CI: -9.8 to -3.7; p < 0.001) and medication adherence (adjusted between-group difference = -2.11, 95% CI: -2.4 to -1.8; p < 0.001), adjusting for baseline scores.

CONCLUSION: The findings suggest that tele-nursing counseling may be an influential supportive intervention for cultivating functioning and medication adherence among individuals with bipolar disorder.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov, Identifier: NCT07404943 (Retrospectively registered, February 2026).

### Feasibility, Acceptability, and Preliminary Outcomes of a Group-Based Brief Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy Protocol for Suicide Prevention in a Brazilian Public Mental Health Service: A Mixed-Methods Pilot Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 Suicide Life Threat Behav | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** de Figueiredo LL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

ABSTRACT Introduction We evaluated the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary outcomes of group‐adapted brief cognitive‐behavioral therapy for suicide prevention (BCBT) in a Brazilian public psychiatric outpatient service. Methods This single‐arm, nonrandomized, mixed‐methods pilot enrolled 27 adults across two cohorts receiving 16 weekly group sessions. Feasibility and acceptability were assessed, and suicidal ideation was monitored weekly using the Beck Scale for Suicide Ideation (BSI). Results Enrollment targets were met; 70.4% had ≥ 4 recorded sessions, and no intervention‐related adverse events were recorded. All six questionnaire respondents rated recommendation at 8–10. Four provided qualitative responses, highlighting shared suffering recognition and an interpretive theme of hope instillation. The linear BSI slope was nonsignificant ( B = −0.29, p = 0.129); multiple imputation yielded B = −0.36 ( p = 0.018). An exploratory quadratic model indicated greater decline later in treatment ( p = 0.008). Among 18 participants with ≥ 4 recorded sessions and baseline BSI, scores decreased by 8.2 points from baseline to the last observation ( d = −0.67; p = 0.011). Conclusions Group‐adapted BCBT showed preliminary feasibility and acceptability, with uncontrolled signals of reduced suicidal ideation. Controlled evaluation is needed to establish efficacy.

1 Introduction Suicide constitutes a major public health burden, with an estimated 727,000 deaths worldwide in 2021 (World Health Organization 2025 ). In Brazil, the suicide mortality rate increased by 57% between 2000 and 2019 (Damiano et al. 2024 ). These trends highlight the need to expand access to evidence‐based, suicide‐specific psychotherapeutic interventions within the Brazilian public mental health system. However, a qualitative study of a Brazilian psychosocial care network identified barriers to psychotherapy access and reported that some group activities functioned primarily as opportunities for prescription renewal (Sampaio and Bispo Júnior 2021 ). Brief cognitive‐behavioral therapy for suicide prevention (BCBT), described in foundational work by Rudd ( 2012 ) and subsequently manualized by Bryan and Rudd ( 2018 ), has accumulated robust evidence of efficacy. In a landmark randomized clinical trial with military personnel, BCBT reduced suicide attempts by 60% over a two‐year follow‐up (Rudd et al. 2015 ). More recently, a telehealth‐delivered trial demonstrated a 41% reduced risk of attempts compared to present‐centered therapy (Baker et al. 2024 ), and a subsequent military trial confirmed that BCBT participants were less likely to attempt suicide during follow‐up (Bryan et al. 2025 ). BCBT has also shown promise in inpatient settings, reducing post‐discharge attempts by 60% (Diefenbach et al. 2024 ). However, these trials have been conducted almost exclusively in high‐income countries and in an individual‐therapy format. Critical gaps persist. First, evidence for group‐based delivery of BCBT remains limited. In an early randomized trial of a group‐based problem‐solving intervention among suicidal young adults in a military outpatient setting, both intervention and usual‐care participants improved, while the intervention retained high‐risk participants more effectively (Rudd et al. 1996 ). More recent work includes a randomized trial protocol comparing group BCBT with dialectical behavior therapy skills groups among military service members (Baker et al. 2023 ). Second, evidence on psychosocial interventions for self‐harm in LMICs remains limited, particularly regarding culturally adapted interventions and delivery models (Aggarwal et al. 2021 ). Resource constraints make scalable approaches essential (Patel et al. 2018 ). Third, routine session‐by‐session monitoring during naturalistic delivery warrants further investigation. Group delivery may extend access where specialist capacity is limited and provide opportunities for shared learning and peer support (Baker et al. 2023 ; Yalom and Leszcz 2020 ). These considerations motivate feasibility testing but do not establish equivalence with individual BCBT. The present pilot evaluated the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary clinical outcomes of group‐adapted BCBT in a Brazilian public psychiatric outpatient service using mixed methods. The study involved adults aged 18–65. Group sessions were co‐facilitated by two psychologists trained in cognitive‐behavioral therapy who had completed a BCBT workshop led by a psychologist with expertise in the approach. The therapists participated in weekly supervision with psychiatrists and psychologists. Trainee observers were present during some sessions. After both treatment cohorts had completed the intervention and approval had been obtained from the local ethics committee, outpatient clinic staff who were not involved in data collection explained the study to participants and obtained written informed consent from all participants for the use of their data in this study. 2.2 Intervention The intervention was adapted from the BCBT manual by Bryan and Rudd ( 2018 ). The manual and accompanying worksheets and intervention materials were translated into Brazilian Portuguese by one of the authors (LB), without back‐translation. Although individual BCBT and the published group protocol use approximately 12 treatment sessions (Baker et al. 2023 ; Bryan and Rudd 2018 ), the local adaptation was planned as a 16‐session group program to allow additional discussion, sharing of individual examples, in‐session practice, review, and consolidation of core BCBT components. Before the group intervention, each participant completed a separate 90‐min individual session focused on a narrative assessment of their most recent suicidal crisis. This was followed by 16 weekly group sessions lasting approximately 2 h each, including a 10‐min break. The intervention preserved the progression from crisis management and emotion regulation to work on suicide‐related beliefs and relapse prevention. Additional time was devoted to developing reasons for living and the survival kit, practicing cognitive strategies, and reviewing and integrating previously learned skills. Adaptations included a family session addressing psychoeducation, lethal‐means safety, and crisis support; a structured review session using a quiz; and a midpoint feedback session reviewing individual symptom trajectories. The final sessions emphasized skills integration and relapse prevention, culminating in rehearsal of coping responses to a previous suicidal crisis and a hypothetical future crisis. 2.3 Participants and Cohorts Two consecutive cohorts were enrolled. Exclusion criteria included acute psychosis or mania, substance intoxication or withdrawal requiring stabilization, severe cognitive impairment, or moderate‐to‐severe intellectual disability. ### A psychiatric nurse-led awareness and peer support intervention for suicide prevention among university students
**כתב עת:** 📄 Arch Psychiatr Nurs | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Öner K | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

OBJECTIVE: This study aimed to evaluate a suicide prevention program combining awareness training and peer support among university students.

METHODS: A quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group design with an embedded qualitative component was conducted with 63 university students. The intervention was structured within a psychiatric nursing framework informed by Peplau's Interpersonal Relations Theory. The seven-week program comprised a baseline assessment in week 1, five weekly group-based peer support sessions (weeks 2-6), and suicide prevention awareness training followed by post-intervention assessment in week 7. Quantitative data were collected using standardized measures of hopelessness, depression, suicide probability, peer support, and suicide-related knowledge. Qualitative data were analyzed using thematic analysis.

RESULTS: Compared with the control group, students in the intervention group demonstrated significant reductions in hopelessness, depression, and suicide probability scores, and significant increases in peer support and suicide-related knowledge (p < .05). Significant time×group interactions indicated that these changes were attributable to the intervention. Qualitative findings revealed enhanced awareness, empathy, supportive attitudes, and strengthened beliefs in the preventability of suicide.

CONCLUSION: Structured interventions integrating awareness training and peer support may effectively reduce suicide-related psychological risk factors and strengthen protective social mechanisms among university students. These findings support the implementation of nurse-led, peer-based preventive strategies within psychiatric nursing and campus mental health services.
