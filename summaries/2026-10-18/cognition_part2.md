### Further predictors of iconicity
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sidhu DM | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Iconicity refers to a perceived resemblance between the form of a signal and its meaning (e.g., "meow" resembling the sound of a cat). Iconicity has been operationalized by having participants rate words in terms of their iconicity. This has enabled researchers to examine the lexical and semantic properties of words high and low in iconicity, for example finding that iconic words tend to be rich in sensory experience and semantically unique. In the present study I examined a suite of novel predictors that have not been examined in English iconicity. I discovered that while iconic words are low in frequency they are generally well known. They also tend to have more specific meanings, though this was moderated by concreteness and part of speech. Iconic words are also more polysemous and emotional. With one exception, there was no evidence that iconic words were more systematic. Together these results extend previous work, and reinforce the finding that iconicity must be understood through a lens of word use and of words as depictions.

### Apathy is associated with lower perceptual curiosity
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tay L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 13

The hallmark of apathy is diminished goal-directed behaviour, yet its relationship to curiosity - the intrinsic drive to explore novel information - remains untested. Here, we examined whether trait apathy is associated with diminished perceptual curiosity. We presented online observers (N = 381) with images of either familiar, everyday objects; or novel, AI-generated objects, which appeared entirely realistic, but do not in fact exist. Observers used their mouse to interact with each stimulus before rating its familiarity, interest, and appeal, and then completed validated measures of trait apathy and perceptual curiosity. Our data yielded three convergent findings. First, trait apathy was associated with differences in how novel and familiar objects were subjectively rated - specifically, as trait apathy increased, observers rated novel objects as more familiar, and familiar objects as more interesting. Second, trait apathy was associated with changes in objective behaviour, such that, as trait apathy increased, observers spent less time viewing novel objects. Finally, scores on trait measures of apathy and curiosity were negatively correlated, such that those who reported higher levels of trait apathy had lower levels of perceptual curiosity. Emotional apathy in particular had the most consistent relationship across both task-based and trait measures of perceptual curiosity. Together, these findings demonstrate that lower perceptual curiosity is a key feature of the apathetic trait, and propose the existence of a previously unrecognised mechanism that links apathy to reduced engagement with perceptual novelty. These results have broad implications for current frameworks of motivation and curiosity in both healthy individuals and clinical populations.

### When textures meet: Relational evaluations explain overall pleasantness beyond component pleasantness
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yokosaka T | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

In everyday touch, the hand often contacts multiple surfaces simultaneously. Yet little is known about how pleasantness is formed when texture pairs are explored at the same time. Previous research has shown that tactile signals presented to adjacent fingers can be integrated through averaging or assimilation processes. These accounts primarily address the integration of stimulus attributes and do not consider whether relations between textures may also contribute to evaluative judgments. The present study examined this possibility by obtaining ratings on five relational dimensions (similarity, contrast, harmony, emergence, and modulation) and testing whether these ratings helped explain the overall pleasantness of texture pairs beyond the mean pleasantness of the component textures. Across three experiments, participants evaluated the pleasantness of texture pairs, judged relational qualities between textures, and rated the tactile attributes of individual textures. The results showed that overall pleasantness could not be fully explained by the average pleasantness of the component textures. Instead, ratings on these relational dimensions-particularly similarity and contrast-provided additional explanatory power for overall pleasantness. Together, these findings suggest that the texture combination itself contributes to pleasantness in paired touch beyond the pleasantness of the component textures alone.

### How social status shapes U.S. children's beliefs about social mobility
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mei J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Children grow up within social hierarchies that shape their everyday lives. Social mobility is a core mechanism through which societies either challenge or maintain existing inequalities. The present study examined how U.S. children's social status-both experimentally manipulated and measured at the neighborhood level-shapes their beliefs about social mobility for themselves and their high- and low-status peers. Children (N = 130; 3-8 years; 70 girls, 59 boys, 1 non-binary; 12.3% Asian, 6.9% Black, 11.6% Latine, 20.7% Multiracial, and 48.5% White) were randomly assigned to either experimentally advantaged or disadvantaged groups. They were then assessed on their (1) general expectations about social mobility, (2) effort-contingent expectations for mobility, and (3) post-hoc explanations for upward and downward mobility. We found that compared to experimentally-disadvantaged children, experimentally-advantaged children were more likely to have higher expectations for themselves and high-status peers, but lower expectations for low-status peers. With age, disadvantaged children (experimental and neighborhood) exhibited higher expectations for themselves and lower expectations for high-status peers, whereas advantaged children's expectations remained stable. Older children were more likely to believe working hard leads to upward mobility and attribute upward/downward mobility to individual effort. This tendency was especially pronounced for their own upward (vs. downward) mobility, but less so when evaluating the upward mobility of high-status peers (vs. themselves). These results suggest that children's beliefs about social mobility develop throughout childhood, differ by target, and are shaped by their own social status.

### Revising expectations in real time: processing speed constrains lexical form prediction in German 8-12 year-olds
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Haeuser KI, Kray J | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Do children predict the lexical form of upcoming linguistic input as readily as they predict semantic features? We investigated word form prediction using the prenominal mismatch paradigm, which tests whether children experience processing costs when encountering prediction-inconsistent gender-marked articles. We also examined individual differences and whether prenominal mismatch effects reflect revision of an anticipated noun. German-speaking 8-12-year-olds self-paced through sentences that either confirmed or disconfirmed expectations about a predictable noun. In half of the items, a prediction-consistent or -inconsistent gender-marked article served as an early cue signaling whether the upcoming noun would match expectations. At the group level, there was no evidence for a prenominal mismatch effect, suggesting that under time pressure, children do not reliably pre-activate word-form information based on gender. However, processing speed predicted the presence of mismatch effects: Faster child readers showed early predictability costs pre-nominally, whereas slower child readers did not. In addition, in faster children, mismatch effects were associated with reduced integration costs at the unpredictable noun, suggesting that prenominal mismatch effects reflect a revision process, whereby children dampen their expectations for the initially predictable noun. These findings suggest that lexical prediction in middle childhood is not consistently supported in time-compressed settings, and that prenominal mismatch effects reflect the revision of an initially predicted lexical representation.
