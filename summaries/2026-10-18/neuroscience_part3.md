### 5-HT(2A) receptors in layer 5 pyramidal neurons mediate the neuroplastic but not the hallucinogenic effects of psilocybin
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Baker JJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

It is unclear how serotonin 2A receptors (5-HT 2A Rs) in cortical layer 5 pyramidal neurons differentially contribute to psilocybin-induced hallucinations versus neuroplasticity. Here we show that psilocybin promotes synapse formation and maturation while accelerating the elimination of pre-existing synapses. Cell type-specific manipulation further demonstrated that 5-HT 2A R signaling in cortical layer 5 pyramidal neurons is necessary and sufficient for psilocybin-induced synaptic remodeling but dispensable for the head-twitch response, a rodent proxy of hallucination.

### Multi-omic profiling of 85 iPSC lines from familial dementia reveals cellular diversity and regulators of organoid development
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Qi L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Systematic molecular characterization of human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines carrying disease-associated mutations is needed to distinguish disease-related biology from variation in cell state, genomic integrity, and genome-editing history, yet such systematic characterization is uncommon. Here, we profiled 85 human iPSC lines carrying MAPT mutations and isogenic controls using whole-genome sequencing, long-read sequencing of the MAPT locus, bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing, and DNA methylation arrays. Single-cell analyses identified four reproducible iPSC states and candidate surface markers for their isolation. Whole-genome sequencing revealed unintended editing events and recurrent 20q11.21 duplications, which were associated with increased ID1 expression and impaired cortical organoid patterning. Transcriptomic analyses identified pathways associated with organoid quality and changes linked to clonal selection and editing, underscoring the need to use unconverted lines as isogenic controls. By providing an in-depth genomic characterization of MAPT iPSCs, this dataset serves as a proof of principle to enhance iPSC utility in biological research.

### Striatal astrocyte μ-crystallin (Crym) loss contributes to early-stage Huntington's disease pathophysiology
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ollivier M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

A striatal astrocyte subpopulation expressing μ-crystallin (gene: Crym) regulates perseverative behaviors, but its role in disease is unclear. We found that astrocytic Crym and μ-crystallin protein expression levels are reduced in human Huntington's disease (HD) tissue, cerebrospinal fluid, and HD mouse models. Selective astrocytic μ-crystallin restoration in a HD mouse model improved astrocyte and neuronal morphology, electrophysiology, gene expression, and early-stage compulsive and repetitive behaviors. Late-stage motor deficits remained mostly unchanged. Approximately 32% of the μ-crystallin rescued astrocytic genes overlapped with those dysregulated in human HD, indicating conserved molecular corrections. These results identify μ-crystallin-expressing astrocytes as modulators of HD pathophysiology and suggest that restoring μ-crystallin or targeting Crym-positive astrocytes could be beneficial in early disease stages.

### Neuronavigated dual-target transcranial magnetic stimulation for schizophrenia negative symptoms
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wei Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Negative symptoms in schizophrenia severely impair function and quality of life, yet respond poorly to current therapies. This multicenter, randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial aimed to investigate the efficacy of neuronavigated dual-target repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) over the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (rDLPFC) and cerebellum for improving negative symptoms in early-stage psychosis (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04853485). Participants with first-episode schizophrenia or clinical high-risk for psychosis were enrolled from Shanghai Mental Health Center and Suzhou Guangji Hospital. Participants were randomly assigned to either a real or sham group under MRI-guided neuronavigation, targeting rDLPFC with 1Hz at 100% motor threshold(MT), cerebellar with intermittent theta burst stimulation(iTBS) at 120% MT for 20 consecutive days. Clinical assessments were conducted at baseline(T0), 10-session(T1), 20-session(T2) and one month after the intervention(T3). Cognition and MRI were evaluated at T0 and T2. The primary outcome was change of negative subscale score of the Positive and Negative Symptoms Scale(PANSS), while secondary outcomes included alterations in other PANSS subscale scores and fronto-cerebellar circuit functional connectivity. In total, 75 participants were included in the intention-to-treat analysis (36 active, 39 sham). The pre-specified primary linear mixed-effects analysis revealed a non-significant group-by-time interaction for PANSS negative subscale scores (F(3,174.6)=1.45, p=0.230). At the 4-week endpoint (T2), the nominal unadjusted between-group p-value was 0.049; after Sidak correction for multiple comparisons, the adjusted mean difference was -2.48 points (95% CI -4.99 to 0.03, adjusted p=0.150, Cohen's d=-0.51). Descriptive uncorrected t-tests indicated greater negative-symptom reductions in the active group at T1 and T2, and the 4-week negative-symptom response rate (≥50% reduction) was higher under real rTMS (χ2=9.5, p=0.002). A significant group-by-time interaction was observed for PANSS positive subscale scores (F(3,172.0)=3.26, p=0.023). Both groups exhibited substantial within-group symptom improvement. Resting-state fMRI demonstrated group-by-time interactions within cerebello-temporal, parahippocampal, caudate, and parietal networks (FWE-corrected p<0.05), and negative-symptom change correlated with cerebello-caudate connectivity (r=0.33, p=0.026). These findings suggest neuronavigated dual-target rTMS over rDLPFC and cerebellum may be associated with numerical improvements in negative symptoms in early-stage psychosis.
