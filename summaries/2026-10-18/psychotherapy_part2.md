### Computerized Cognitive Training in People with Depression: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomized Clinical Trials
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Gefen E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

INTRODUCTION: Cognitive impairment and depression frequently co-occur, exacerbating disability. Identifying accessible interventions to enhance cognition in people with depression is imperative. Computerized cognitive training (CCT) is a potential strategy, but its clinical efficacy across settings remain unclear. We investigated the efficacy of CCT and potential moderators of cognitive and functional outcomes in individuals with depressive symptoms.

METHODS: In this systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO: CRD42020204209), we searched MEDLINE, Embase, and PsycINFO from inception to June 22, 2026 for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of CCT in adults (≥18 years) with clinically significant depressive symptoms. Primary outcome was change in overall cognition. Secondary outcomes included depressive symptoms, psychosocial and daily functioning.

RESULTS: Fifty-three 53 RCTs (3831 participants) were included. CCT was efficacious for overall cognition (49 RCTs, (g=0.23; 95% CI 0.14 to 0.32; τ2=0.095) and depressive symptoms (35 RCTs, g=0.22; 95% CI 0.08 to 0.37; τ2=0.066) with substantial heterogeneity across studies, and did not extend to psychiatric symptoms and daily function. Greater cognitive benefits were associated with multidomain training, higher doses, session durations >30 min, and ≤3 sessions per week. Efficacy was independent of baseline symptom severity, and comparable across delivery modes.

CONCLUSIONS: CCT can improve cognition, depression and subjective outcomes in people with major or comorbid depression, but efficacy varies across outcomes, settings and intervention design factors. Future work is needed to confirm these factors, and examine what approaches and dose is required to enhance cognitive and functional outcomes of CCT.

### The role of positive linguistic sentiment in extinction learning through exposure therapy
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Barnes-Horowitz NM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 9

Exposure therapy is derived from extinction learning principles. Evidence suggests that reward processes, including positive affect, may influence extinction learning. Few studies have explored the role of reward and positive affect in exposure. This study sought to address gaps by examining whether linguistic markers of positive affect expressed during exposure predicted learning and clinical outcomes. Adults (N = 47) with social anxiety or panic disorder completed nine sessions of exposure therapy. Participants rated expected likelihood of feared outcome occurrence before and after each exposure, and the difference (expectancy change) was calculated as a measure of learning. Natural language processing was used to analyze sentiment in written and spoken language during consolidation of extinction learning immediately after exposure, resulting in a score that reflected tendency towards positive over negative affect. Anxiety symptoms were assessed weekly at each exposure session and at end of treatment. Higher positive sentiment was associated with greater expectancy change at the between-person level for written and within-person level for spoken sentiment. Higher within-person positive sentiment predicted lower next-session anxiety for spoken sentiment only. Sentiment did not predict overall clinical outcomes. This is the first study to test the role of linguistic sentiment in exposure learning mechanisms and outcomes. More positive sentiment during exposure consolidation may support greater learning and reduced next-session symptoms, but may be insufficient in affecting overall clinical outcomes. It is possible that exposure learning may be maximized by directing attention towards more positive exposure elements; further study is needed to test this.

### Fear of positive evaluation impairs disengagement from positive and ambiguous dynamic social cues during late-stage attentional processing
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Song Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

Fear of Positive Evaluation (FPE) is a key cognitive feature of social anxiety, yet its neurodynamic impact on social attention remains unclear. This study used a cue-target paradigm with emotional biological motion cues (happy, angry, fear, neutral) and event-related potentials (ERPs) to systematically compare attention processing between individuals with high and low levels of FPE across distinct temporal stages. Behavioral results showed that individuals with high levels of FPE showed delayed responses specifically to neutral cues under valid conditions (i.e., the target appeared at the same location as the preceding cue). Electrophysiological results revealed that all participants showed consistent performance in early spatial orienting (N1); during the conflict monitoring stage (N2), neutral cues and valid targets generally enhanced the N2 amplitude. Critically, in late motivational evaluation stage (P3), the two groups displayed dissociated patterns: individuals with low levels of FPE demonstrated adaptive threat monitoring, with the strongest P3 response to angry cues under valid conditions and enhanced P3 to fear cues under invalid conditions (i.e., the target appeared at the opposite location). In contrast, individuals with high levels of FPE displayed maladaptive attentional engagement, with enhanced P3 amplitudes and impaired disengagement from happy and neutral cues under invalid conditions. Furthermore, they exhibited a reversed threat-sensitivity, as reflected in larger P3 amplitudes to fear cues compared to angry cues under valid conditions. These results demonstrate that FPE is characterized by stage-specific dysregulation, primarily marked by late-stage hyper-engagement with positive and ambiguous social signals rather than generalized threat sensitivity. This work provides direct neural evidence for the Bivalent Fear of Evaluation model and offers novel insights for developing intervention strategies targeting cognitive biases in the evaluation stage.

### Visual feedback increases expiratory muscle training effort in Parkinson's disease: a randomised crossover trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Neuroeng Rehabil | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Srp M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 22

Background Disorders of airway protection, including impaired cough and swallowing, are clinically important complications of Parkinson’s disease. Expiratory muscle strength training (EMST) is an established non-pharmacological intervention targeting these deficits. The aim of this study was to evaluate the acute effect of visual feedback provided by the SpiroGym mHealth app on expiratory muscle activation during EMST in people with Parkinson’s disease. Methods We conducted a single-center, randomised, two-period crossover trial in people with Parkinson’s disease. Participants performed EMST under two conditions, in randomised order: conventional EMST without visual feedback and EMST with real-time visual feedback provided by the SpiroGym app. Expiratory muscle activation was measured using surface electromyography of the abdominal muscles. The primary outcome was the within-participant difference in mean peak normalised internal oblique activity between conditions. Secondary outcomes included external oblique and rectus abdominis activity, associations with neuropsychiatric symptoms, and participant-reported usability ratings. Results Twenty participants were randomised, and 19 completed the study and were included in the final analysis. No study-related adverse events occurred. Visual feedback significantly increased mean peak normalised internal oblique activity compared with no feedback (118.2 ± 36.4% vs. 90.5 ± 23.0% of reference voluntary contraction; p < 0.001; Cohen d = 0.95). Without feedback, internal oblique activation significantly declined from 100.1 ± 22.2% in the first set to 84.4 ± 25.5% in the fifth set, whereas it remained stable with feedback (120.3 ± 29.4% to 119.9 ± 40.6%). External oblique activation was also significantly higher with feedback (122.2 ± 38.1% vs. 100.9 ± 32.4%; p = 0.002; Cohen d = 0.66), while rectus abdominis activation did not differ significantly between conditions (105.2 ± 41.3% vs. 90.2 ± 36.1%; p = 0.06; Cohen d = 0.43). No significant associations between feedback responsiveness and depression, apathy, or anxiety were detected. Participants rated the visual feedback as easy to understand and helpful for EMST performance. Conclusions Real-time visual feedback delivered by the SpiroGym app acutely increased and stabilized expiratory muscle activation during EMST in people with Parkinson’s disease. Background Parkinson’s disease (PD) is one of the fastest growing neurodegenerative disorders worldwide. This trend is partly driven by population ageing, as the incidence of PD rises with age [ 1 ]. However, age-adjusted prevalence is also increasing, suggesting additional contributors such as improved diagnostic accuracy and environmental factors [ 2 ]. Resistance training plays an important role in the non-pharmacological management of PD [ 3 ]. Such interventions may target several characteristic PD-related impairments, including bradykinesia, reduced muscle strength, impaired balance, gait difficulties and reduced functional mobility. In addition, resistance training may have beneficial effects on selected non-motor domains, including cognition and quality of life [ 4 , 5 ]. They have also been associated with slower disease progression [ 6 ] and improved quality of life [ 5 ]. One PD-specific application of resistance training aimed at supporting airway protection is expiratory muscle strength training (EMST) [ 7 ]. Airway protection is clinically relevant in PD, as both cough and swallowing are frequently impaired. These deficits are multifactorial and may involve impaired muscle control and peripheral muscle weakness [ 8 , 9 ]. This impairment increases the risk of aspiration and aspiration pneumonia is a leading cause of death in people with PD (PwP) [ 10 – 12 ]. EMST is performed using a calibrated device with a one-way, spring-loaded valve that provides resistance during forced expiration, thereby overloading the expiratory muscles involved in cough and the suprahyoid muscles involved in swallowing, and improving their force-generating capacity [ 9 , 13 ]. This provides a rationale for targeting the muscle weakness component that may contribute to cough and swallowing dysfunction. In addition, because the EMST maneuver mimics many of the movements required for cough production, it may also address the impaired coordination component [ 14 ]. Available detraining data indicate that EMST-related gains may be partly retained after intensive training [ 13 ]. Other research, by contrast, found that detraining was more pronounced in individuals with poorer baseline swallowing function, in whom a three-month detraining period returned swallowing function to baseline (pre-EMST) levels [ 15 ]. This suggests that, in PwP receiving EMST for clinically relevant cough or swallowing impairment, maintenance programmes are needed to sustain the functional gains [ 15 , 16 ]. However, the optimal duration, frequency, and intensity of long-term EMST in PwP remain insufficiently established in the current literature. In this context, SpiroGym, a mobile health (mHealth) application designed to support long term adherence to EMST by providing real-time visual feedback and longitudinal tracking of training progress, has been introduced [ 17 – 19 ]. In a previous randomised controlled trial, PwP were randomised to conventional EMST or the same EMST protocol enhanced with the SpiroGym app [ 20 ]. The study protocol consisted of 8 weeks of semisupervised intensive EMST followed by 16 weeks of unsupervised maintenance training. One key finding was that expiratory muscle strength, measured as maximal expiratory pressure (MEP), improved in both groups, but the gains were significantly larger in the SpiroGym group at the 8-week visit. Because the intensity of EMST was identical in both groups and adherence rates were comparable, the greater strength gains observed with SpiroGym may reflect more effective within-session training effort facilitated by real-time visual feedback [ 20 ]. This interpretation is consistent with evidence from resistance training research showing that visual feedback can enhance acute performance within a training session and promote greater long-term adaptations [ 21 ]. However, it remains unclear whether visual feedback increases acute expiratory effort during repeated EMST maneuvers in PwP. Therefore, the primary objective of this randomised crossover trial was to evaluate the acute effect of visual feedback provided by the SpiroGym app on expiratory muscle activation during EMST in PwP, as assessed by surface electromyography (sEMG). ### Effects of Mindfulness Therapy on Stress Biomarkers and Psychological Symptoms in Women With Recurrent Pregnancy Loss: A Randomized Clinical Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Stress Health | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Oliveira ECF et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

ABSTRACT Women with recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) exhibit high levels of depression, anxiety, and stress, which negatively impact their quality of life. This randomized clinical trial evaluated the effects of an eight‐week online Mindfulness‐Based Intervention (MBI), with weekly one‐hour sessions, on psychological and biological markers of stress in 35 women with RPL. Participants were randomly assigned to either the MBI or standard care (control group) and assessed before and after the intervention through salivary biomarkers (cortisol, alpha‐amylase, and Immunoglobulin A—IgA) and the validated questionnaires Depression, Anxiety and Stress Scale‐21 (DASS‐21) and Psychological General Well‐Being Index (PGWB‐I). No significant time × group interactions were observed for salivary biomarkers, including cortisol ( p = 0.672), alpha‐amylase ( p = 0.342), and IgA ( p = 0.148). Participants in the MBI group showed significant reductions in total DASS‐21 ( p = 0.018), depression ( p = 0.026), and stress ( p = 0.015) scores, whereas anxiety did not differ significantly between groups ( p = 0.121). They also showed robust improvements in overall well‐being ( p < 0.001) and multiple PGWB‐I domains. These findings suggest that MBI may improve self‐reported psychological well‐being in women with RPL; however, as no significant effects were observed in the primary biomarker outcomes, these secondary psychological findings should be considered exploratory and hypothesis‐generating. Trial Registration https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR‐4xc76cr

1 Introduction Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) is traditionally defined as the occurrence of three or more consecutive pregnancy losses before 20 weeks of gestation (Rai and Regan 2006 ), although more recent guidelines consider two or more losses sufficient for diagnosis (Tulandi and Al‐Fozan 2024 ). It affects approximately 1% of couples trying to conceive when defined by a minimum of three losses, and about 3% when the definition includes two or more losses (Rai and Regan 2006 ). Miscarriage is associated with an increased risk of mental health disorders, including depression and anxiety. It is estimated that up to two‐thirds of women with RPL experience some form of psychiatric illness (Klock et al. 1997 ). Psychological distress may persist for extended periods and often intensifies with successive pregnancy losses (Bergner et al. 2008 ; Carp 2014 ). Studies have confirmed that women with RPL tend to exhibit high levels of depression, anxiety, and stress, with a marked negative impact on their quality of life (He et al. 2019 ; Hedegaard et al. 2021 ; Kolte et al. 2015 ). In this context, mindfulness‐based interventions (MBIs) have emerged as promising strategies to promote emotional well‐being and improve quality of life in women affected by RPL (Jensen et al. 2021 ; D. N. Wang et al. 2021 ). Mindfulness is defined as a meditative practice that involves maintaining a moment‐by‐moment awareness of thoughts, emotions, and bodily sensations with a non‐judgmental attitude (Kabat‐Zinn 2003 ). It typically includes exercises that help individuals become more aware of their internal experiences, promoting emotional regulation through slow, conscious breathing (Gasnier et al. 2017 ). Over the past decades, MBIs have been extensively explored as tools for enhancing well‐being in various populations, including women with RPL (Jensen et al. 2021 ; D. N. Wang et al. 2021 ). The assessment of salivary biomarkers, such as cortisol, alpha‐amylase, and immunoglobulin A (IgA), offers non‐invasive, reliable methods to monitor physiological changes associated with stress‐related psychological conditions (Blair et al. 2017 ; Chojnowska et al. 2021 ; Gröschl 2008 ). The relationship between mindfulness and stress‐related biomarkers has been the focus of growing research (Lynch et al. 2011 ). These biomarkers reflect activity in both the immune system and the autonomic nervous system, and are regulated by the hypothalamic‐pituitary‐adrenal (HPA) axis, which plays a central role in the biological stress response (Chojnowoska et al. 2021 ; Engeland et al. 2019 ). Cortisol is a glucocorticoid hormone secreted by the adrenal cortex in response to activation of the HPA axis. Under stress, the hypothalamus releases corticotropin‐releasing hormone (CRH), which stimulates the pituitary gland to secrete adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), leading to cortisol production (Blair et al. 2017 ; Kirschbaum and Hellhammer 1989 ). Free cortisol diffuses passively through the membranes of the salivary glands and is secreted into the saliva. Thus, salivary cortisol concentration directly reflects its free and biologically active fraction, providing a reliable measure of the hormone levels available in the body (Blair et al. 2017 ; Gröschl 2008 ; Inder et al. 2012 ). Elevated salivary cortisol levels are often observed in individuals with chronic and acute stress and are commonly linked to depressive symptoms and anxiety (Blair et al. 2017 ; Gröschl 2008 ; Saraiva et al. 2005 ). According to meta‐analyses, MBIs have induced changes in salivary cortisol levels but the findings are inconsistent (Sanada et al. 2016 ; O'Leary et al. 2016 ; Vargas‐Uricoechea et al. 2024 ). In turn, salivary alpha‐amylase is an enzyme regulated by the sympathetic nervous system. Its levels increase in response to acute stress due to heightened sympathetic activation. As a result, salivary alpha‐amylase has been recognized as a sensitive and rapid biomarker of autonomic nervous system arousal, complementing cortisol measurements in stress research, particularly in studies focused on anxiety and acute stress responses (Engeland et al. 2019 ; Chojnowska et al. 2021 ; Nater and Rohleder 2009 ). Research indicates that MBIs can effectively reduce salivary alpha‐amylase levels, suggesting reduced sympathetic nervous system activation (Aguilar‐Raab et al. 2021 ; Hassanvandi et al. 2025 ). Secretory IgA is a key component of mucosal immune defense, especially in the oral and respiratory tracts (Chojnowska et al. 2021 ). Stress can modulate salivary IgA levels: acute stress typically increases its secretion, whereas chronic stress may have variable effects depending on the intensity and duration of exposure (Segerstrom and Miller 2004 ). ### The economic burden of Borderline Personality Disorder: direct and indirect costs, and evidence from evaluations of interventions
**כתב עת:** 📄 Front Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** סקירה שיטתית | **מחברים:** Ghoyal M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD) is characterized by longstanding patterns of difficulty in functioning, and when left untreated, BPD can impose a substantial financial burden on individuals and society, in terms of higher healthcare costs as well as broader societal costs. Aims To conduct a critical review of the direct and indirect costs related to BPD. Additionally, to critically evaluate the economic evidence on interventions for BPD. Methods A comprehensive search strategy was conducted across PsycINFO, PubMed, and SCOPUS. Studies were screened in two phases: an initial title and abstract review, followed by full-text screening against a predefined eligibility criteria. Results Across 23 studies, high direct and indirect costs were consistently reported. Economic evaluations primarily examined established psychological interventions for BPD, such as Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), Cognitive Behavior Therapy (CBT), and Schema-Focused Therapy (SFT), comparing costs and quality-of-life outcomes, with most demonstrating cost savings through reduced inpatient and emergency service use. Conclusions This review highlights the substantial economic burden of BPD, driven by high service use, productivity losses, and unmet needs. While interventions such as DBT, CBT and SFT show potential cost-effectiveness, inconsistent cost definitions and the omission of key components, including informal care and long-term impairment, likely lead to an underestimation of true costs. More comprehensive and standardized economic evaluations are needed.

1 Introduction Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD) is a serious mental illness characterized by persistent patterns of affective instability, an unstable self-image, fear of abandonment, impulsivity, and behavioral dysregulation ( 1 ). BPD is estimated to be present in 0.7–2.7% of adults over their lifetime, with higher rates of BPD observed in clinical settings, for example 22% among inpatient populations and 12% in outpatient services ( 2 , 3 ). Onset typically peaks during adolescence and young adulthood ( 4 ), where symptoms can fluctuate over time, with periods of improvement and recurrence even without treatment ( 5 ). Individuals with BPD often experience complex unmet needs that contribute to long-term adverse effects, such as high-level functional impairment and premature mortality ( 6 ). Although research indicates that BPD diagnosis should not be controversial, it continues to be perceived as such by some, partly due to stigma and prejudicial attitudes toward the diagnosis ( 7 ). Additionally, terminology varies internationally; historically, ICD-based systems including those used in the United Kingdom and Europe, traditionally use Emotionally Unstable Personality Disorder ( 8 ), while more recent diagnostic frameworks, ICD-11 and the DSM-5 Alternative Model for Personality Disorders, adopt dimensional models ( 1 , 9 ). Most economic studies rely on the categorical BPD construct, and this review uses the term BPD for consistency with the evidence base. BPD poses a significant economic burden to the healthcare sector and society, in terms of direct and indirect costs ( 10 ). BPD is associated with substantial societal costs of €31,130 per person annually ( 11 ), reflecting both intensive service use and substantial productivity losses ( 12 ). These costs are substantially higher than those observed for other mental disorders (e.g. depression and alcohol dependence are associated with estimated costs of €7,139 and €1,110 annually per person respectively ( 13 , 14 )). People with BPD are disproportionately high service users, in comparison to those without BPD, with elevated rates of emergency presentations, repeated inpatient admissions, crisis-team involvement that concentrate spending in acute services ( 15 ). A ten-year prospective follow-up of the McLean Study of Adult Development cohort for those with BPD found sustained outpatient treatment use with more intermittent inpatient reliance, and although many individuals with BPD discontinued psychotherapy or medication at least once, most later resumed treatment, underscoring the fluctuating pattern of service use ( 16 ). In relation to indirect costs, individuals with BPD experience markedly disrupted work trajectories, spending a smaller proportion of adulthood in employment and facing far higher rates of job loss than their non-BPD peers, thereby driving significant productivity losses ( 17 ). The considerable economic burden of BPD, together with its detrimental effects on quality of life ( 18 , 19 ), emphasizes the urgent need for interventions that are not only effective in terms of leading to clinical benefits, such as improving functioning, reducing symptom severity and lowering self-harm and crisis use ( 20 , 21 ), but also have good value for money, representing efficient use of limited healthcare resources. However, current economic evidence on BPD is limited. Meuldijk et al. ( 22 ) conducted a systematic review of cost-evaluations for BPD interventions and results suggest that effective treatment is likely to be cost-effective, with potential reductions in healthcare and community service use. However, the review highlighted substantial methodological limitations, including heterogeneity in cost definitions and measurement, variation in study design and quality, and limited generalizability across healthcare systems, all of which restrict comparability and warrant caution in interpretation. Meuldijk et al. ( 22 ) noted that the number of economic evaluations remains small relative to the wider clinical literature on personality disorders. As their review included studies published only up to 2015, a critical review is needed as more recent psychological interventions, updated economic analyses, and advances in methodological approaches were not captured. Although previous literature highlights the large societal burden associated with BPD, key gaps remain in understanding the main direct and indirect cost drivers, and important components, such as informal care and criminal justice involvement are consistently excluded in cost calculations. To our knowledge, no comprehensive critical review currently synthesizes these costs and as a result, important questions remain unresolved, including which factors account for the largest proportion of costs and whether key components are systematically captured. Overall, an up-to-date critical review is essential to critically synthesize literature on the main direct and indirect cost drivers for BPD and evaluate newer evidence on the cost-effectiveness of interventions for BPD. A critical review is particularly valuable in the context of BPD because the existing economic literature is fragmented, and methodologically heterogeneous in how costs and outcomes are measured and interpreted. Such an approach enables a deeper examination and critique of how economic evidence has been generated for BPD ( 23 ), it highlights gaps that have limited the field’s development, and clarifies where future research and resource allocation should be directed, functions that extend beyond the scope of a standard systematic review ( 24 ).
