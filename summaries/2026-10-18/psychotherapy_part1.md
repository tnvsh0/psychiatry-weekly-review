### Dismantling Psychological Interventions for Youth with Complex Trauma: A Systematic Review and Component Network Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 10

INTRODUCTION: Complex trauma-related posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and comorbidities pose a significant mental health burden in children. However, the comparative effectiveness of psychological interventions and shared therapeutic components remains unclear.

METHODS: We conducted a systematic review, network, and component network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of psychological interventions for children and adolescents with complex trauma. PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, PsycINFO, and Cochrane Library were searched up to September 2024. PTSD was the primary outcome; retention and comorbid symptoms (depression, anxiety, dissociation, externalizing problems) were secondary outcomes.

RESULTS: Forty-eight studies (6,017 participants) were included in this review. Multimodal group interventions (SMD -1.68, 95% confidence interval [CI] -3.04 to -0.32), phase-based trauma-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (TF-CBT) with parental involvement (-1.12, -1.53 to -0.70), TF-CBT (-1.06, -1.59 to -0.52), non-TF-CBT with parental involvement (-0.93, -1.70 to -0.17), eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (-0.85, -1.60 to -0.10), and phase-based TF-CBT (-0.79, -1.20 to -0.39) significantly reduced PTSD. Exposure emerged as the only significantly effective component for PTSD (-0.85, 95% credible interval [CrI] -1.60 to -0.06). Efficient component combinations included affective modulation with a therapeutic foundation of psychoeducation and nonspecific elements for PTSD (-0.97, 95% CrI -1.92 to -0.01) and exposure plus parental involvement with the same foundation for depression (-2.11, 95% CrI -4.06 to -0.20).

CONCLUSION: Trauma-focused approaches were the most effective treatments for pediatric PTSD and emotional comorbidities related to complex trauma. A more cost-effective intervention approach may prioritize exposure techniques, incorporate affective modulation, and include parental involvement when feasible.

### Effectiveness of psychosocial interventions addressing informal caregivers' mental health: an umbrella systematic review with 26,481 participants
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Clinical Psychology Review (Clin Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Bano B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 7

AIM: We aimed to provide a comprehensive overview of the effectiveness of psychosocial interventions on informal caregivers' mental health, organized by the clinical condition(s) of the care recipient.

METHODS: We conducted an umbrella systematic review and random-effects meta-analyses to pool the results of the included studies for each combination of clinical condition, intervention type, comparison and outcome.

RESULTS: We included 19 systematic reviews and 50 meta-analyses, with 26,481 participants. Several interventions improved mental health outcomes in caregivers, and across various clinical conditions of the care recipients. In dementia caregivers, multicomponent interventions significantly reduced depressive and anxiety symptoms, and caregiver burden (SMD from -0.65 to -0.19). For caregivers of people with lung cancer, psychoeducation and psychological support reduced depressive (SMD = -1.66, 95% CI -3.19 to -0.13) and anxiety symptoms (SMD = -1.37, 95% CI -2.54 to -0.21). Multicomponent interventions reduced depressive and anxiety symptoms in parents of children with cancer (SMD = -0.38, 95% CI -0.64 to -0.12; SMD = -0.89, 95% CI -1.33 to -0.45). Caregiver burden of caregivers of people with psychosis and schizophrenia decreased after psychoeducation and skills-training (SMD = -2.10, 95% CI -3.46 to -0.74; SMD = -0.94, 95% CI -1.72 to -0.17). Certainty of evidence was low to moderate, and the strength of association was overall weak.

CONCLUSIONS: We rigorously assessed the quality, certainty of evidence, and the strength of associations. Despite the appraisals highlighting the need for further high-quality research, our results can inform policy and practice on the use of psychosocial interventions for caregivers of people with different clinical conditions.

### Lack of fear acquisition to film clip unconditional stimuli despite contingency awareness: Implications for electrodermal fear conditioning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Richardson M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Fear conditioning is most commonly assessed by measuring electrodermal activity to a conditioned stimulus (CS) in anticipation of an electric shock or another unconditioned stimulus (US). Replacing the electric shock with aversive film clips is an innovation that potentially has higher ecological validity for posttraumatic stress disorder. The present report examined boundary conditions of this paradigm across two between subjects experiments, with a particular focus on the effectiveness of film clip versus electric shock USs in promoting physiological fear conditioning. In Experiment 1, we tested whether fear acquisition was influenced by the inclusion of a shock US (n = 30), prior exposure to a longer trauma film (n = 29), or the use of face (n = 33) versus shape (n = 33) conditioned stimuli (CSs). Fear acquisition was enhanced by the inclusion of a shock US and to a lesser extent by prior trauma film exposure; however, conditioning in groups using film clip USs alone was characterised by lack of conditioned electrodermal responding. Experiment 2 investigated whether this absence of conditioned responding reflected reduced contingency awareness by measuring online US expectancy across film-based (four US film clip group with n = 55 and one US film clip group with n = 44) and shock-based (n = 30) US conditions, as well as salivary stress responses following acquisition. Despite equivalent US expectancy, CS pleasantness, and subjective US ratings across groups, conditioned electrodermal responses during acquisition were observed only in the shock US condition. Differences between groups may be explained by differences in salivary cortisol reactivity, with higher cortisol responses following acquisition in the shock group. Together, these findings indicate that shock USs increase physiological arousal, which appears to be essential for electrodermal reactivity, irrespective of conditioned learning processes. This finding has potentially important implications for the interpretation of electrodermal measurement in fear conditioning paradigms.

### Mantra Meditation for Pain Conditions: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Pain Res Manag | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Coitinho Biurra Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background Pain is biopsychosocial in nature, and international guidelines now emphasise interdisciplinary treatment. Psychotherapeutic interventions such as mantra meditation (MM) are being increasingly studied for their potential benefits in pain management. Objectives To conduct a systematic review and meta‐analysis of the evidence for MM in improving biopsychosocial outcomes in acute and chronic pain conditions. Methods A systematic search of MEDLINE, PsycINFO, CINAHL, CENTRAL, EMBASE and Scopus identified controlled clinical trials of MM for acute and chronic pain, with no date limits. Data were synthesised using an inverse‐variance random‐effects meta‐analysis of continuous outcomes with precalculated effect sizes (Hedges’ g). Results Seven studies ( n = 374) met inclusion criteria, with low‐to‐moderate methodological quality. Meta‐analyses showed significant effects of MM versus controls for improving mental quality of life (QoL) and reducing negative mood states. No significant effects were found for pain, physical QoL, depression symptoms, anxiety, stress, psychological well‐being or sleep. However, subgroup analysis indicated significant improvement in depression symptoms for participants with chronic pain compared with acute pain. Conclusion Further high‐quality randomised controlled trials are required to determine the efficacy of MM in pain management. Current evidence suggests MM may improve selected psychological outcomes, including mental QoL, negative mood states and depression in chronic pain. These findings indicate that MM has potential as an adjuvant intervention within holistic, interdisciplinary approaches to pain management.

1. Introduction Pain is defined by the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) as ‘an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage, or described in terms of such damage’ [ 1 ]. Although criticised for oversimplification [ 2 ], pain is usefully categorised into nociceptive, inflammatory and pathological subtypes [ 3 ], and as acute or chronic by duration [ 4 ]. Chronic pain lasts at least 3 months (e.g., endometriosis, cancer‐related pain) [ 5 ], while acute pain involves rapid onset and short duration (e.g., burns, childbirth) [ 4 ]. Shared physiological and psychological mechanisms [ 6 ] support examining both forms together. The biopsychosocial model best captures pain’s complexity, integrating biological, psychological and social influences [ 7 , 8 ]. Individuals with pain frequently experience anxiety, depression, stress, sleep and mood disturbances, impaired function and disrupted relationships [ 9 ], driven by inflammation and psychosocial factors such as poor mental health or limited support [ 10 ]. These erode quality of life (QoL). Global prevalence is 28% across 52 countries ( n = 199,862) [ 11 ], generating an annual economic burden of $560–635 billion in medical costs, lost productivity and disability support [ 12 ]. Such personal, societal and financial costs demand effective interventions. Pharmacological treatment predominates, beginning with over‐the‐counter analgesics before diagnosis [ 13 , 14 ] and progressing to tailored regimens of opioids [ 13 , 15 ] and NSAIDs [ 15 , 16 ]. Long‐term NSAID use carries cardiovascular and gastrointestinal risks [ 17 ]. Opioids exacerbate dependence, fuel the current crisis [ 18 ] and nearly double all‐cause mortality in older adults compared with NSAIDs [ 19 – 21 ]. Recognising these limitations and pain’s psychosocial burden, the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) advocates multidisciplinary management [ 22 ]. This emphasises psychotherapeutic interventions delivered by mental health professionals [ 23 – 25 ], sometimes prioritised over pharmacotherapy or surgery [ 26 , 27 ]. Established psychological therapies include cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) [ 28 ], acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) [ 28 ], hypnotherapy [ 29 ] and mindfulness meditation [ 30 ]. CBT has the strongest evidence, significantly reducing pain and disability [ 31 – 34 ], as shown in a review of 75 trials ( N = 9401) [ 34 ]. ACT improves distress and function, particularly in chronic pain [ 35 , 36 ]. Hypnotherapy benefits irritable bowel syndrome and emotional well‐being [ 37 – 40 ]. Mindfulness meditation stands out for its accessibility—no therapist required, fully self‐administered [ 41 ]—and consistent reductions in pain intensity and distress across populations [ 42 – 48 ], aligning with demand for nonpharmacological self‐management. Mantra meditation (MM), a concentrative practice centred on repeated mantras [ 49 , 50 ], is emerging as a promising alternative. While MM improves mental health [ 50 – 52 ] and spiritual well‐being [ 53 ], its role in pain management is still nascent. Rooted in Hinduism, Buddhism [ 54 ] and Zoroastrianism [ 55 ], ‘mantra’ derives from Sanskrit roots denoting mental repetition that ‘saves’ or protects from suffering [ 56 ]. Mantras—sounds, words or phrases—may be breath‐synchronised [ 57 ], chanted aloud or silently [ 58 ] or simply listened to [ 57 ]. Examples include Transcendental Meditation, Kirtan Kriya and Sahaj Samadhi [ 59 – 62 ]. MM uniquely engages linguistic pathways rather than somatic focus [ 63 ], a mechanism paralleled in speech‐language pathology by melodic intonation therapy, where repetitive, rhythmic vocal production recruits dedicated linguistic‐motor networks in speech rehabilitation [ 64 , 65 ], and by neuroscientific evidence that sustained verbal recitation is associated with structural changes in memory‐ and language‐related brain regions [ 66 ]. Traditional science holds that efficacy peaks when mantras are ‘chetan’—transmitted by a perfected teacher [ 57 , 67 – 69 ]. Neuroimaging reveals MM’s mechanisms. One study showed 40%–50% reductions in thalamic, prefrontal and global brain responses to pain, with modest anterior cingulate attenuation, dampening emotional and motivational processing [ 70 ]. Another study ( n = 16) identified a signature of heightened attention (elevated anterior cingulate and dorsolateral prefrontal flow) paired with reduced arousal (decreased pons and cerebellar activity), confirming low effort and high feasibility [ 71 ]. Global interest is rising [ 51 ]; 1.6% of US adults (3.6 million) practised MM over 12 months, only 0.3% below mindfulness [ 72 ]. Despite this, no systematic review has synthesised MM’s effects on pain. Given the field’s early stage, broad inclusion criteria are recommended to capture available data comprehensively [ 50 , 73 , 74 ]. This review therefore evaluates MM interventions for acute and chronic pain conditions, across adult and paediatric populations, and all controlled study designs. It addresses four research questions: Can MM improve self‐reported pain intensity in individuals with pain conditions? Can MM improve QoL in people with pain conditions? Can MM reduce symptoms of depression, anxiety and stress in people with pain conditions? Can MM enhance mood states, psychological well‐being and sleep quality in people with pain conditions? 2. ### Amygdala neurofeedback as an adjunct to residential dialectical behavior therapy for borderline personality disorder: a pilot randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Hofmanová M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 16

BACKGROUND: Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is characterized by affective instability and amygdala hyperreactivity. Although dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) reduces symptoms and amygdala-related abnormalities, many patients show only partial remission. Real-time functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) amygdala neurofeedback (amy-NF) directly targets hyperactive limbic responses and has shown promise in uncontrolled trials. We examined whether amy-NF adds benefit to inpatient DBT.

METHODS: Thirty-six mid-therapy people with BPD and clinically significant residual symptoms (BSL-23 > 1.5) were randomized to DBT plus amy-NF (n = 23) or DBT alone, termed treatment as usual (TAU = 13). Post-treatment assessments were completed by 15/9 (amy-NF/TAU), with 15/8 and 12/7 completing the 3- and 6-month follow-ups. Ecological momentary assessment (EMA) of daily-life affective instability served as the primary outcome. Secondary outcomes included self-reports, amygdala responses during two fMRI tasks, and resting-state fMRI. Assessments occurred at pre-, post-, 3-, and 6-month follow-up.

RESULTS: Participants successfully downregulated amygdala activity during neurofeedback. EMA showed improvements in negative affect, inner tension, and affective instability over time, without consistent superiority of amy-NF. Self-reports likewise showed no additional benefit of amy-NF, and BSL-23 scores increased after discharge in both groups. Amy-NF was associated with transient pre-post reductions in amygdala activity during both viewing and regulation in the emotion regulation task, and with more stable amygdala reactivity over time than TAU in the faces task. Resting-state analyses showed transient pre-post increases in amygdala connectivity with precuneus and dorsomedial frontal regions in amy-NF.

CONCLUSIONS: Amygdala neurofeedback is feasible and reduced amygdala activity in BPD, but did not yield additional clinical benefit when delivered alongside intensive residential DBT, although the small sample may have limited power to detect such effects.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: The study protocol was preregistered 2020/03/26 on ClinicalTrials.gov and received the ID NCT04333888.

### [The Use of Dialectical-Behavior Therapy in the Treatment of Substance Use Disorders]
**כתב עת:** 📄 Fortschr Neurol Psychiatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Marth B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 16

BACKGROUND: The treatment of substance use disorders (SUD) remains a persistent challenge. Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) is a multimodal cognitive-behavioral treatment approach that integrates acceptance- and change-oriented strategies and was originally developed to address severe emotion dysregulation. In the context of SUD, DBT-based adaptations and skills-oriented formats are particularly used to target emotion regulation, distress tolerance, mindfulness and relapse prevention. Aim: The aim of this review was to synthesize the current state of research in order to explore and evaluate the use and effectiveness of DBT-based interventions for SUD.

METHODS: A systematic literature search in databases, Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar identified 13 relevant studies from the period 2010 to 2024, which were narratively synthesized.

RESULTS: Despite methodological limitations, the findings suggested that the DBT-based formats used, in particular DBT skills training (DBT-ST), might improve emotion regulation, distress tolerance and adaptive coping strategies. Additionally, there was evidence for beneficial effects on substance use, abstinence and craving. Furthermore, several findings suggested promising treatment acceptance and adherence, although these results depended on the context and study design.

CONCLUSIONS: Overall, it can be concluded that DBT-ST, in particular, may be a suitable intervention option for individuals with SUD. However, further robust studies with standardized diagnostic procedures, longer follow-up periods and active control groups are needed to more precisely determine its specific efficacy and indications.

Die Behandlung von Substanzgebrauchsstörungen (SUD) stellt eine anhaltende Herausforderung dar. Die Dialektische Verhaltenstherapie (DBT) ist ein kognitiv-verhaltenstherapeutisches, multimodales Verfahren, das Akzeptanz- und Veränderungsstrategien verbindet und ursprünglich zur Behandlung schwerer Emotionsdysregulation entwickelt wurde. Im Suchtkontext werden insbesondere DBT-basierte Adaptationen und Skills-orientierte Formate eingesetzt, die auf Emotionsregulation, Belastungstoleranz, Achtsamkeit und Rückfallprävention abzielen. Die vorliegende Übersichtsarbeit bündelt den gegenwärtigen Forschungsstand, um den Einsatz und die Effektivität von DBT-basierten Interventionen bei SUD zu explorieren und zu bewerten. Methode: Anhand einer systematischen Literaturrecherche in Datenbanken, in Google Scholar sowie in Semantic Scholar wurden 13 relevante Studien aus dem Zeitraum 2010 bis 2024 identifiziert und narrativ synthetisiert. Die Resultate deuten trotz methodischer Einschränkungen darauf hin, dass die angewandten DBT-basierten Formate, insbesondere das DBT Skills-Training (DBT-ST), Emotionsregulation, Belastungstoleranz sowie adaptive Bewältigungsstrategien verbessern. Zudem zeigten sich Hinweise auf günstige Effekte hinsichtlich Substanzkonsum, Abstinenz und Craving. Weiters sprechen mehrere Befunde für eine vielversprechende Behandlungsakzeptanz und Therapietreue, wobei diese Ergebnisse kontext- und studiendesignabhängig sind. In der Zusammenschau lässt sich ableiten, dass insbesondere das DBT-ST für suchtkranke Personen eine geeignete Interventionsoption darstellen kann. Jedoch sind weitere robuste Studien mit standardisierter Diagnostik, längeren Nachbeobachtungszeiten und aktiven Kontrollgruppen erforderlich, um die spezifische Wirksamkeit und Indikationsstellung präziser zu bestimmen.
