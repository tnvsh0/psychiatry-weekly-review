### Idea generation during emotion regulation: A proposed framework
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Review (Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bellaiche L, LaBar KS | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

The field of emotion regulation (ER) is increasingly recognizing the influence of context on an individual's regulatory processes, prompting researchers to examine how specific circumstances dynamically shape ER. Well-accepted theoretical models and supporting empirical studies frame such situation-specific ER as a sequence of stages involving decision points: choosing to regulate, selecting a strategy, deciding whether to maintain current regulatory efforts, and so forth. These frameworks, however, omit an underappreciated process that we argue underscores ER at its highest specificity: idea generation. In other words, individuals do not just select properties of their regulatory attempt, but must actually generate an attempt-a distinction that leverages an underexplored level of analysis in ER known as techniques. In this article, we integrate multiple theoretical perspectives across domains to demonstrate the feasibility in (and insight afforded by) measuring idea generation in ER. Next, we review how existing empirical paradigms already utilize generation-related principles in largely unacknowledged manners, with some studies that have specifically targeted these generative operations during ER. We combine these observations into a novel framework called Emotion Regulation Generation of Techniques, which serves to deepen the study of ER by delineating a generation stage that follows and informs selection mechanisms. To help study idea generation in practice, this framework is equipped with methodological protocols that draw from neighboring domains (e.g., divergent thinking), paired with future directions and testable hypotheses that can inform the role of idea generation in healthy regulation and in dysregulated affective disorders. ### Near-random behavior in strategic games
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Boger T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Humans struggle to produce random behavior when asked to do so, leading some to suggest that our ability to randomize is attenuated (or even absent). Here, we demonstrate that the capacity for random behavior is much greater than previously acknowledged: Humans can behave in (near) optimally random ways when the context demands it. We created a custom, online version of the game Battleship in which participants competed against one another. On each turn, one player chose a tile on a 3 × 3 grid to place a ship, while the other player tried to guess the ship's location. Across six preregistered studies, this strategic context led to dramatic increases in random behavior relative to closely matched non-strategic tasks. Such random behavior was also remarkably flexible and optimal depending on context; both players readily incorporated varying information about the game (e.g., that tiles can differ in value) while simultaneously accounting for their opponents' knowledge of that information. A computational model validated these findings and revealed nuanced differences across contexts. Contrary to decades of work finding that humans fail to randomize, we find that strategic games promote highly flexible, near-random behavior.

### Dissociating accuracy and metacognition in nutritional judgements
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hoogervorst K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

The gap between nutritional knowledge and dietary behaviour remains poorly understood, yet may partly reflect systematic biases in how individuals evaluate food and assess their own nutritional judgements. Here, we introduce the MetaBites task, a novel two-alternative forced-choice paradigm assessing nutritional judgements under adaptive difficulty control. Thirty-two healthy volunteers compared foods on either caloric density or nutritional quality, the latter quantified using the Nutrient-Rich Foods (NRF) 9.3 index. Participants provided trial-level, block-level, and task-level confidence ratings, enabling assessment of metacognitive monitoring across multiple levels. The task showed adequate validity, supported by staircase convergence, a clear speed-accuracy trade-off, and higher confidence on correct relative to incorrect trials. Results revealed a dissociation across conditions: participants showed higher objective performance when judging nutritional quality, yet reported significantly higher confidence for calorie judgements, and task-level global confidence decreased significantly in the NRF condition but remained stable for calorie judgements. Metacognitive efficiency did not differ between conditions. Familiarity with food items was associated with choice behaviour in opposing directions across conditions, consistent with a tendency to perceive familiar foods as more nutritious and less calorie-dense. Liking rather than familiarity was associated with higher trial-level confidence. Together, these findings show that objective performance and subjective confidence can dissociate in nutritional judgements, with distinct heuristic cues shaping choice and confidence. The MetaBites task offers a promising tool for investigating metacognitive contributions to nutritional decision-making in both healthy and clinical populations.

### Diversity in how people use racial categories to generalize
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Noyes A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 12

Generic statements, such as "Black people are strong", can imply that there are racial essences. But what about generic statements like "Black people face barriers to career advancement"? Statements like these may instead imply the existence of systemic racism. In three empirical studies, we examined the beliefs and attitudes held by Black (N = 387) and White (N = 400) American participants who endorse generic statements, controlling for endorsement of non-generic statements about the same events. For both Black and White Americans, generic statements about ability and character predicted a belief in racial essences (and endorsing the racial hierarchy), whereas generic statements about experiences and outcomes in social institutions predicted a belief in systemic racism (and rejecting the racial hierarchy). Thus, there is diversity in how and why people use race to generalize, and generic language flexibly accommodates that diversity.

### The effect of social robot authority on epistemic vigilance in early childhood
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jin X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Social robots are increasingly deployed as tutors and learning partners, yet their authority cues may discourage children from questioning or verifying information. We investigated whether robot authority influences Chinese children's epistemic vigilance and which source of authority drives this effect. In Study 1 (N = 128, ages 4 and 6, M age  = 5.39 years, SD = 0.92, 66 girls and 62 boys), robots with high and low overall authority asserted that all aliens in a task had three eyes. Children's verification behaviors were assessed in terms of the number of times they checked the information, the length of time they spent checking, and how much time passed between checks. Results showed that children engaged in significantly less verification in the high-authority condition, and 6-year-olds checked more often than 4-year-olds. Study 2 (N = 200, ages 4 and 6, M age  = 5.19 years, SD = 0.92, 97 girls and 103 boys) built on Study 1 by manipulating two sources of authority: role authority (teacher-like vs. peer-like) and cognitive authority (knowledgeable vs. unknowledgeable). The same task and outcome measures were used as in Study 1. The results showed that cognitive authority significantly decreased children's verification behavior, but there was no significant effect of role authority or child age. Together, these findings suggest that authority cues can shape how much evidence children seek before accepting robot testimony, particularly when the robot is presented as knowledgeable.

### Children successfully use external cues as a cognitive strategy in an unsolvable memory task but demonstrate limitation
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Haseltine EL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 10

Cognitive offloading refers to the process of using aids to reduce the cognitive demands of a task. A simple type of offloading-like behavior involves using cues in the environment to later aid in making a correct choice or response when this would otherwise be difficult or even impossible. This project assessed children's ability for spontaneous creation of this type of cognitive strategy use. Children three to nine years old first participated in a task that produced uncertainty surrounding the future location of a preferred toy. Children assigned different toys to four opaque cups, one of which was uniquely colored, before the cups were mixed and re-presented. To guarantee success, children had to place their preferred toy in the uniquely colored cup. Otherwise, they would have to select among three identical cups with no information about where the preferred item was hidden. No instructions were provided initially about where to place items, allowing children to formulate their own approach. Children demonstrated high levels of proficiency. Although age was not a strong indicator of overall performance, older children reached the criterion for proficiency more quickly than younger children. Children also decreased their likelihood of making errors with increased experience. However, subsequent variations of the task showed that preschool age children struggled to set up the game in such a way that an optimal strategy could be implemented. This suggests the possible lack of awareness of why a strategy works to make a task solvable and ensure success at this age.
