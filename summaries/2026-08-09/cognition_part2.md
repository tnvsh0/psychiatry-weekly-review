### Theta and High-alpha Phase-locking Dynamics Unveil Face Perception in Ambiguous Stimuli
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 4

Previous studies emphasize phase synchronization as a fundamental mechanism for integrating local features into coherent percepts. We employed a novel paradigm and dynamic graph analysis based on EEG to track neural dynamics associated with perceiving a face (or not) while keeping the stimuli identical. Thirty participants underwent a pretest to establish perceptual thresholds for detecting faces within images overlaid with visual noise. These thresholds were then applied in an EEG experiment with the same task, focusing on images with 50% and 75% detection probabilities. In the high-alpha band, we observed increased coupling (100-275 msec after stimulus onset) between the left and right occipitotemporal electrodes when an ambiguous stimulus was perceived as a face, indicating conscious face perception. The failure to perceive a face resulted in enhanced theta band phase synchronization between bilateral occipitotemporal electrodes and increased high-alpha-band coupling between the left frontal and right occipitotemporal electrodes, which showed face-selective responses. These synchronizations are likely to reflect the continuous gathering of individual bits of facial information rather than signaling the presence or absence of a face percept. Additionally, we identified frontal-occipitotemporal theta-band couplings related to the stimulus's informational content, irrespective of whether the inherent face was perceived. Our findings reveal distinct temporal dynamics in phase synchronization at specific frequencies, indicating a specialized system for face perception during integrated information processing across the frontal and visual cortex, resolving ambiguities.

### Intracranial Evidence for the Cerebellum's Role in Semantic Processing
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Floden DP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 4

Language impairments in patients with cerebellar damage or dysfunction strongly implicate the cerebellum in language processing but the nature, timing, and specificity of its contributions are unclear. A clinical trial of cerebellar stimulation for motor recovery after middle cerebral artery stroke provided a rare opportunity to record invasive, intracranial local field potential activity from the cerebellar dentate nucleus of eight patients during language processing. Patients performed semantic priming tasks that either discouraged or encouraged semantic predictions. We found evidence for communication between the dentate nucleus and frontal scalp EEG sites in the alpha band when semantic predictions were violated. This did not occur when semantic predictions were fulfilled or when prediction violations were nonlinguistic in nature. This language-specific activity occurred in the N400window where semantic processing of a visually presented word takes place. These data point to a linguistic feedback function that could help to account for the language deficits observed in diseases and disorders involving the cerebellum.

### Toddlers show heightened attention to selective distributors
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognitive Psychology (Cogn Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Margoni F et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 6

We investigated whether Norwegian 21-month-olds (N = 176) hold expectations about agents' dominance based on their distributive fairness (data collected during 2021-2024). Toddlers were presented with two agents who first distributed resources, one acting evenly/fairly and the other selectively/unfairly. Then the agents engaged in territorial competition (Experiment 1). Toddlers looked longer when the unfair, selective agent prevailed and gained center-stage visibility. Experiment 2 ruled out the influence of low-level non-social factors. In Experiments 3 and 4, toddlers watched distributive events like in Experiment 1, but unlike earlier experiments, the test phase did not include competition. Toddlers again looked longer at the selective distributor when it gained center-stage visibility. Overall, these results suggest that toddlers pay special attention to unfair, selective distributors at large, also in the absence of dominance conflict or a clear violation event during the test phase.

### A speaker-centric motivation for the use of optional adjectives during language production
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bohlman ER, Montag JL | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 6

Speakers often make choices to alleviate planning difficulty during language production. We investigate the use of adjectives as optional words that speakers can use implicitly and strategically to provide extra planning time during periods of planning difficulty. Speakers described target objects in an array that either contained a different-colored competitor, so producing a color adjective was necessary to identify the target item, or did not have a different-colored competitor. Target items crucially differed in name agreement. Objects with high name agreement have a single, dominant label (candle) and are easier to produce than objects with low name agreement, which have multiple possible labels (coat/jacket). Speakers predictably produced a high rate of adjectives when describing high and low name agreement items in arrays containing a different-color competitor. However, when the array did not contain a different-color competitor, so adjectives were not necessary to identify the target, participants produced more adjectives preceding low name agreement objects. Further, when speakers produced adjectives, the duration of those adjectives were longer preceding low name agreement objects. We interpret these results as suggesting that the use of optional adjectives may sometimes have a speaker-centric motivation, to alleviate planning difficulty during language production.

### Interplay of mentalization, emotion regulation and identity integration among adolescents
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Eloranta SJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

Mentalization, emotion regulation (ER) and identity integration are important transdiagnostic concepts contributing to mental health. Yet, their interconnections are relatively poorly understood. Adolescence is a critical period for developing these skills, as well as for risk of developing mental health problems. In this study, we used network analysis among adolescents with and without mental health problems to examine how mentalization, ER, and identity integration overlap and interplay with each other, and which aspects of them are the most central in mental health problems. A non-clinical (N = 360) and a clinical sample (N = 110) of Finnish adolescents filled out the Mentalization Questionnaire (MZQ), the short version of the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS) and the Development and Identity Diffusion in Adolescence (AIDA) questionnaire. Network analyses were conducted on the two data sets. With non-clinical data, measures clustered more expectedly around themselves, whereas with clinical data, different measures overlapped and connected more with each other. Limited access to effective ER strategies (DERS) and Consistent self (AIDA) were more central subconstructs in the clinical sample than in the non-clinical sample, implying that they could have the most central impact on maintaining difficulties in mentalization, ER, and identity development in adolescents with psychiatric disorders. Differences between the networks might imply complexity and individuality of factors contributing to problems with them. This would highlight the importance of individual case formulation about factors contributing to and maintaining problems when considering treatment targets, as well as the need for more research on individual dynamics of psychological problems.

### The low prevalence effect in children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Inscore J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 5

Low prevalence search tasks occur everyday and are characterized by targets occurring infrequently across repeated searches. This phenomenon, termed the low prevalence effect (LPE), refers to observers' reduced likelihood of detecting rare targets when they are present. This pattern holds particular importance in high-stakes visual search contexts. As an example, 20 to 30 percent of cancers are missed in radiological screenings due to the perceptual challenge of finding low prevalence abnormalities. While the LPE is well documented in adults, little is known about whether young children demonstrate similar prevalence-related search difficulties. Prior developmental research suggests that young children may distribute attention more broadly than adults, including task-irrelevant information. This global attentional style raises the possibility that children may show reduced susceptibility to the LPE. The present study is the first to empirically test this question using an eye-tracking visual search task with 4- to 5-year-old children compared to adults. Results indicated that both children and adults demonstrated the LPE, evidenced by reduced accuracy during low-prevalence target-present trials. Target-absent accuracy remained consistently high across prevalence levels. Children's search times were slower than adults, but age group did not account for the prevalence-related accuracy effect. These findings challenge the idea that young children are immune to the LPE and suggest that low target prevalence can influence visual search performance early in development. This study extends LPE research to younger populations, offering an expanded cognitive developmental perspective and highlighting the need for continued research on children's visual attention in real-world search contexts.
