### Disparities in Autism Spectrum Disorder and Perinatal Risk Factors

Key Points Question When financial barriers to care are minimal, do disparities in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) diagnosis by socioeconomic and immigration characteristics persist, and do known perinatal risk factors for ASD show similar gradients that may help explain these disparities? Findings In this population-based cohort study of 2.3 million children born in Ontario, Canada, between 2002 and 2020, ASD incidence was highest among children from the most socioeconomically deprived, lowest-income neighborhoods and born to immigrant mothers, and these disparities widened over time. Children in socioeconomically deprived areas were more frequently exposed to maternal diabetes in utero, severe pregnancy complications, and prematurity, with disparities in maternal diabetes worsening over time. Meaning In a universal health care setting, ASD diagnosis was more common among children from disadvantaged and immigrant families, with disparities worsening over time; similar social gradients in perinatal risk factors suggest a potential contribution that warrants direct quantification.

This cohort study examines temporal trends in autism spectrum disorder diagnosis and in perinatal factors associated with risk of autism spectrum disorder across maternal socioeconomic and immigration factors.

Importance In universal health care settings, trends in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) diagnosis remain poorly characterized, yet are critical for identifying inequities that persist despite more equitable health care access. Objective To describe temporal trends in ASD diagnosis and in perinatal risk factors for ASD across maternal socioeconomic and immigration factors. Diagnosis was 1.4-fold (95% CI, 1.3-1.5–fold) higher among children of immigrant mothers, with the highest rates among mothers from low- and middle-income regions. ### 'Navigating the ride': stakeholder perspectives on public transportation challenges for children with autism in India
**כתב עת:** ⭐ BMJ Open *(IF: 105.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Biju AS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 5

Abstract Objective To explore the perspectives of primary caregivers, healthcare professionals and transport personnel regarding the challenges associated with public transportation for children with autism spectrum disorder in India. Design This study applied a descriptive phenomenological qualitative design. Setting The study was conducted at a tertiary care teaching centre in South India. Participants 21 participants comprising nine primary caregivers of children with autism spectrum disorder, six healthcare professionals and six transport personnel were recruited using purposive maximum variability sampling. Methods In-depth interviews were conducted with the participants and audio-recorded for transcription. The transcripts were manually coded and analysed using Systematic Text Condensation. Results The results demonstrate that public transport is a complex social ecosystem comprising the child, caregivers, co-passengers and transport personnel. Healthcare professionals and policymakers, while not directly involved in travel, also influence the experience. Three major themes were identified: challenges associated with public transport, facilitators for better travel experiences and stakeholder recommendations for improving travel experiences. Conclusions This study identified key barriers to accessing public transport for children with autism spectrum disorder and their families. Facilitators and potential strategies to address travel-related challenges are also discussed. These findings provide insights into stakeholder perspectives that may inform efforts to improve accessibility and travel experiences on public transport for children.

STRENGTHS AND LIMITATIONS OF THIS STUDY This qualitative study included primary caregivers, healthcare professionals and transport personnel, who provided perspectives on different aspects of public transportation for children with autism spectrum disorder. The findings provide insights into barriers to public transportation from individual, familial, societal and healthcare perspectives. To the best of our knowledge, this is among the few qualitative studies from the Global South to explore public transportation for children with autism spectrum disorder and their families. Children with autism spectrum disorder were not interviewed or directly observed; therefore, their experiences of public transportation were represented through adult stakeholder accounts and may not fully reflect their own perspectives. The study did not include government personnel responsible for transport policies, whose perspectives could have provided additional insights into policy-related barriers and potential solutions. Meeting these diverse needs requires a comprehensive understanding of the contextual factors influencing participation and outcomes. Public transportation is a critical component of Indian cities, as it provides mobility for two-thirds of Indian households that do not possess a personal vehicle. 4 Individuals with disabilities who live in rural regions face key challenges related to accessibility, availability and affordability of rehabilitation services. 5 Individuals with disabilities experience various barriers to using public transportation, including physical and social challenges, such as the absence of ramps, long walking distances, extended waiting times, insufficient information at bus stops or stations and negative attitudes from drivers. 6 This might especially be challenging for individuals with ASD who rely on public transport for their mobility needs. 7 Most parents of children with ASD experience difficulties while travelling. 8 Safety and spatial awareness are significant concerns for individuals with ASD and their families when using public transport. 9 Unexpected situations and sensory sensitivities may pose additional challenges during travel, leading to the exhibition of externalising behaviours during travel, which might pose potential danger to self and others. 10 However, the difficulties experienced by children with ASD and their families while using public transportation, such as the public bus systems, have not been extensively examined. 7 India’s Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act (RPWD) (Government of India, 2016) assures accessibility and access to safe transport for persons with disabilities. Specifically, we aimed to understand the perceived barriers and facilitators to accessing and using public transportation, as well as the strategies and recommendations described by participants to address these challenges. For this study, ‘public transport’ was operationalised as either formal, mass transit networks such as buses, trains or aeroplanes or privately operated para-transit networks like shared auto-rickshaws or taxis. Hence, it was decided to include caregivers, transport personnel and healthcare professionals, not as distinct stakeholder groups, but as persons who share different aspects of the experience, based on the concept of intersubjectivity. The present study seeks to investigate cerebral lateralization for language not only in terms of ASD, but also as a potential biomarker for the broad autism phenotype (BAP)—a sum of subclinical traits similar to those of ASD. Indeed, it has been suggested that atypical language lateralization may represent a potential neurophysiological marker of ASD (Herringshaw et al. 2016 ; Jouravlev et al. 2020 ). Yet, these findings have rarely been extended to individuals exhibiting the broad autism phenotype (BAP)—a constellation of subclinical traits that mirror core features of ASD in attenuated form and are often observed in first‐degree relatives of individuals with autism (De Groot and Van Strien 2017 ; Hurley et al. 2007 ; Piven 1999 ). This gap in the literature persists despite substantial evidence that subclinical autistic traits may share underlying neurocognitive mechanisms with clinically diagnosed ASD (Lai et al. 2013 ; Losh et al. 2008 ; Ozonoff et al. 2011 ; for a review see Pisula and Ziętgart‐Sadowska 2015 ). Consequently, systematic investigation of cerebral language lateralization across individuals with ASD, individuals with BAP traits, and the general population is warranted to determine whether meaningful differences exist. Introduction Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is an established intervention for severe, treatment-refractory, or clinically urgent psychiatric disorders and may be life-saving in catatonia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, and other conditions requiring rapid symptom relief. Recent systematic reviews, registry studies, and retrospective cohorts nevertheless support clinical benefit in carefully selected young people while emphasizing the low certainty of much of the evidence and the inconsistency of procedural and safety reporting [ 1 – 6 ]. A systematic review focused on treatment-resistant mood disorders reported generally favorable outcomes with mainly transient adverse effects [ 2 ]. A European review of 79 publications from 18 countries similarly found improvement across several severe diagnostic presentations but identified substantial variation in practice and major reporting gaps [ 3 ]. More recent cohorts have expanded the available sample sizes and diagnostic breadth [ 1 , 5 , 6 ]. Detailed anesthetic and procedural data are another important gap in adolescent ECT research. ECT is a multidisciplinary intervention delivered under general anesthesia with neuromuscular blockade. ### Exploring the Neural Mechanisms of Acupuncture Stimulation in Autism Spectrum Disorder: Protocol for a Randomized, Sham-Controlled, Multimodal Neuroimaging Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Res Protoc | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Sim J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 15

Abstract Background Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by restricted and repetitive behaviors and disturbances in social communication and interaction. Currently, there is no fundamental treatment for ASD, with only a few medications available for use on a limited basis to manage concomitant neuropsychiatric symptoms. The primary therapeutic goal of such treatments is to alleviate symptoms while maximizing the functional independence and quality of life of affected individuals. Many complementary and alternative medicine therapies have been reported to have effects on ASD symptoms; however, evidence is insufficient to confirm their clinical effectiveness. Acupuncture has traditionally been used as a complementary intervention to manage various neurological and neuropsychiatric diseases, including ASD, in East Asia. However, how acupuncture stimulation modulates neural activity at the macroscale level in individuals with ASD remains largely unknown. Objective This study aims to elucidate disease-related brain alterations and explore the acute neural mechanisms underlying acupuncture stimulation in patients with ASD and in typically developing (TD) individuals, using a multimodal neuroimaging approach. Methods This study was designed as a randomized, sham-controlled trial. In this stratified 2×2 design, 70 participants, including individuals with ASD (n=35) and TD controls (n=35), will each be randomly assigned to either the verum or sham acupuncture group, resulting in 4 groups. The verum acupuncture group will receive stimulation at specific acupoints clinically indicated for ASD, eliciting a deqi sensation. The sham acupuncture group will receive superficial needling at nonacupoints unrelated to ASD symptoms. Each group will undergo clinical assessments and multimodal neuroimaging measurements, including functional near-infrared spectroscopy and electroencephalography, before and after acupuncture stimulation. The primary outcome will be differences in resting state network metrics and relative band power in frontal areas by disease status. Secondary outcomes will include differences in these neural markers following acupuncture stimulation between the verum and sham groups, reflecting the group × intervention interaction. Exploratory outcomes will focus on identifying neural markers that correlate with clinical indicators of ASD. Results Recruitment began in April 2025. To date, 23 individuals with ASD and 21 TD individuals have participated in this study. The recruitment process is expected to continue until December 2026. Conclusions ASD presents a significant socioeconomic burden, with no definitive treatment established to date. Although acupuncture is a widely applied intervention for various neurological conditions, including ASD, its large-scale neural mechanisms remain poorly understood. By using multimodal neuroimaging, the present study aims to investigate the immediate neural substrates underlying acupuncture stimulation in patients with ASD. These findings are expected to provide a robust neurobiological foundation for future longitudinal research and the development of evidence-based integrative strategies.

### Effects of High-Phenolic Extra Virgin Olive Oil on Sensory Processing in Children With Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Pilot Study

ABSTRACT No approved treatments currently exist for the core symptoms of autism spectrum disorder (ASD), prompting clinicians and families to seek alternative approaches, including dietary and nutritional interventions. The Mediterranean diet (MeDi), characterized by a high intake of antioxidant and anti‐inflammatory components, is associated with improved health outcomes and reduced risk of chronic diseases. A key element of the MeDi is extra virgin olive oil (EVOO), which is rich in bioactive phenolics, including oleocanthal, oleuropein and hydroxytyrosol. Through its antioxidant and anti‐inflammatory actions, EVOO may contribute to regulating pathological processes in both the central nervous system (CNS) and the gastrointestinal tract, offering a promising avenue for pharmacological and nutritional intervention in children with ASD. This pilot study investigated the effects of a three‐month intervention combining the MeDi with EVOO supplementation on ASD symptoms. Ten children diagnosed with ASD aged 5.00–11.7 years, all boys, were recruited from Northern Greece. The changes in social interaction and communication were evaluated using standardized instruments: the Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS), Word Finding Vocabulary Test (WFVT), Autism Diagnostic Interview‐Revised (ADI‐R) and the Sensory Profile (SP). 1 Introduction Caffeine is the most widely consumed psychoactive substance in the world, and it is consumed overwhelmingly for its presumed cognitive benefits. Its principal mechanism is antagonism of adenosine A1 and A2A receptors, which prevents adenosine from inhibiting the release of dopamine, noradrenaline and other neurotransmitters in regions supporting attention and executive control (McLellan et al. 2016 ). On this basis, caffeine has been expected to improve the same functions targeted by stimulant medication. The empirical picture is less uniform than the mechanism suggests. Reviewing the acute literature, McLellan et al. ( 2016 ) concluded that doses between roughly 32 and 300 mg reliably enhance alertness, vigilance, reaction time and attention, whereas effects on memory and higher‐order executive function are inconsistent and remain unclear. Effects also depend on the participant's baseline arousal, in line with the inverted‐U relationship between arousal and performance, so that the same dose may benefit a fatigued individual and impair a well‐rested one. This baseline dependency has led to the view that caffeine, particularly when consumed regularly at moderate doses, is better characterised as a normaliser of brain function than as a psychostimulant in the conventional sense: its benefits are most consistently observed when function is compromised, for example, by fatigue, sleep loss or cognitive decline, and are small or absent when performance is already near optimal (McLellan et al. 2016 ; Nehlig 2010 ). Effects on memory illustrate this pattern, being more reliably observed when memory function is degraded than in unimpaired individuals. Regular consumption also engages processes that extend beyond the acute receptor blockade. In habitual coffee drinkers, coffee intake reduced resting‐state connectivity within the posterior default mode network, with a similar trend after caffeine alone, a pattern the authors interpreted as increased readiness for action (Picó‐Pérez et al. 2023 ). In the mouse hippocampus, chronic caffeine intake lowered metabolism‐related processes while inducing neuron‐specific epigenetic changes at genes involved in synaptic transmission and plasticity and enhancing experience‐driven transcription, a profile compatible with an increased readiness to process information (Paiva et al. 2022 ). These findings distinguish the neural consequences of regular, moderate consumption from those of a single dose, a distinction that is central to the question addressed here. This inconsistency is most visible for inhibitory control. Tieges et al. ( 2009 ) distinguished active inhibition, the deliberate suppression of a prepared or ongoing action, from reactive inhibition, the resolution of interference arising from a competing response, and tested both after a 3 mg/kg dose of caffeine, approximately 210 mg for a 70‐kg adult. Across three double‐blind, placebo‐controlled within‐participants experiments using an AX‐version of the continuous performance test, a stop task and a flanker task, they concluded that caffeine does not modulate inhibitory control. Effects on overall flanker performance and on selective response suppression were negligible; in the stop task, caffeine produced a global change in processing speed rather than any effect specific to inhibition; and although AX‐CPT performance was consistent with improved inhibition, the authors noted that this could equally reflect changes in attentional processes. Barry et al. ( 2007 ) reported a comparable dissociation using the same 250‐mg dose employed here, in a randomised, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled crossover study with an auditory Go/No‐Go task. Caffeine reduced reaction time but affected neither omission nor commission errors, and the event‐related potential data showed the same asymmetry: amplitudes of P1, P2 and P3b to Go stimuli increased focally, whereas no component elicited by No‐Go stimuli was affected at all. Recent work from our own laboratory found no acute effect of 215 mg of caffeine on temporal attention, spatial attention or working memory maintenance, despite all three manipulations producing their expected effects (Akyürek et al. 2025 ).
