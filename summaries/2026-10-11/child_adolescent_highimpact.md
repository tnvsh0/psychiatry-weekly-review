### Mental health and urinary incontinence in childhood
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Child & Adolescent Health (Lancet Child Adolesc Health) *(IF: 45.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Joinson C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 6

Intermittent and uncontrolled loss of urine is common among children but requires medical attention when it persists beyond age 5 years. Mostly functional in origin, urinary incontinence is caused by a complex inter-relationship of biopsychosocial factors, and evidence shows that mental health problems and adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are common in children and adolescents with urinary incontinence. Mental health problems and ACEs (including abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction) could be both causes and consequences of paediatric urinary incontinence, but research is needed to understand causal pathways. Treatment guidelines increasingly recognise the need to integrate assessment of psychosocial factors and mental health support into clinical care pathways for children affected by urinary incontinence. Parental and societal beliefs, cultural norms, availability of sanitation facilities, economic constraints, and policy-level gaps affect the management of paediatric urinary incontinence. Early assessment and treatment are needed to prevent urinary incontinence from becoming chronic and negatively impacting on children's quality of life, wellbeing, and education.

### Evaluation of a Commercial Pediatric Sepsis Early Detection Alert in the Emergency Department
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Pediatrics (JAMA Pediatr) *(IF: 27.6)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mitchell CM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 5

Key Points Question How well does the pediatric sepsis early detection alert from the largest electronic health record vendor in US hospitals identify children with sepsis? Findings In a multicenter cohort of 166 943 pediatric emergency department (ED) encounters, the alert identified 36% of children with sepsis according to the Phoenix Sepsis Score and alerted prior to diagnosis in 30% of cases. Sensitivity was lower among Black children, those younger than 5 years, and those seen in hospitals that were not pediatric only. Meaning This early detection alert threshold had low sensitivity, which was poorly suited for pediatric sepsis screening; it did not detect most cases of sepsis present in the first 24 hours after ED arrival.

Importance Screening tools have been implemented with the goal of improving early detection of pediatric sepsis, with limited evidence of effectiveness. The Epic Systems Pediatric Sepsis Early Detection Model (PESM) and its associated clinical alert have been widely implemented in emergency departments (EDs) in the US but have not undergone independent evaluation. Objective To evaluate the diagnostic characteristics of the Epic PESM for (1) identifying sepsis and (2) identifying sepsis before patients met Phoenix sepsis criteria. Design, Setting, and Participants This retrospective multicenter cohort study of pediatric ED encounters was conducted across 8 EDs within a New York health system from January 2024 through June 2025. Children younger than 18 years were included; behavioral health encounters and ED deaths within 1 hour of arrival were excluded. Data were analyzed from August 2025 through July 2026. Exposure Triggering of the PESM alert in the first 24 hours after ED arrival. Main Outcomes and Measures Sepsis was defined as a Phoenix score of 2 or higher. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated for the outcome of sepsis within 24 hours of ED arrival. Secondarily, evaluation was restricted to only instances in which the alert triggered before sepsis onset. Sensitivity was evaluated in relevant subgroups, and a subgroup with a sensitivity 10% or more below the overall cohort sensitivity was considered clinically meaningful. Results Among 166 943 pediatric ED encounters, 189 cases of sepsis occurred in the first 24 hours. A total of 76 685 pediatric patients (45.9%) were female, and 79 032 patients (47.3%) were under the age of 5 years. Sensitivity of the PESM for identifying patients with sepsis was 36% (95% CI, 29%-43%); PPV was 3% (95% CI, 2%-4%), and NPV was greater than 99% (95% CI, 99%-99%). The alert triggered before sepsis with a sensitivity of 30% (95% CI, 24%-37%). Subgroup sensitivity was 10% or more below overall cohort sensitivity in Black children (22%; 95% CI, 9%-40%) and among children less than 5 years old (20%; 95% CI, 12%-30%), as well as in EDs outside of tertiary children’s hospitals (25%; 95% CI, 14%-40%). Conclusions and Relevance Per the results of this multicenter cohort study, the PESM alert had low sensitivity for identifying children with sepsis and does not support timely or equitable pediatric sepsis screening. Sensitivity was low in Black children, in children younger than 5 years, and outside of tertiary children’s hospitals.

This multicenter cohort study evaluates the diagnostic characteristics of the Epic Systems Pediatric Sepsis Early Detection Model for identification and early recognition of sepsis using Phoenix sepsis criteria.
