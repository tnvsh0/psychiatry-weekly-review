### Developmental trajectories of internalizing and externalizing behaviors in LGBTQ+ youth in the ABCD Study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Santos J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 6

LGBTQ+ youth experience elevated internalizing distress, yet research on externalizing behaviors and their early development remains limited. This study examined trajectories of internalizing and externalizing behaviors from childhood into adolescence using data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development Study (N = 11,878 at baseline, M age  = 9.80 years, SD = 0.62, 63.3% White, 52.2% male), assessed four times at ages 9/10, 10/11, 11/12, and 12/13. Linear mixed-effects models tested differences between LGBTQ+ and non-LGBTQ+ youth and among disaggregated sexual and gender identity subgroups. In the omnibus comparison, LGBTQ+ youth showed higher internalizing behaviors at age 9/10 (β = 0.18, 95% CI [0.13, 0.22]) and a more rapid increase into adolescence (β = 0.10 [0.08, 0.11]) than non-LGBTQ+ peers, along with higher externalizing behaviors at age 9/10 (β = 0.18 [0.13, 0.23]) and a smaller increase over time (β = 0.03 [0.01, 0.04]; all ps < .001). Trajectories were substantially heterogeneous across subgroups, with the omnibus pattern disproportionately driven by youth holding multiple minoritized identities, who showed the most rapid increases in both domains. Mental health disparities among LGBTQ+ youth thus emerge by late childhood, persist into early adolescence, and are most acute among multiply-minoritized youth.

### The longitudinal co-development of adverse childhood experiences and child internalizing problems: Examining sociodemographic disparities
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Navarro Flores CM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 6

Internalizing problems are prevalent in childhood and are associated with exposure to adverse childhood experiences (ACEs); however, their co-development across childhood remains understudied. Prior research documents sociodemographic disparities (i.e., race/ethnicity, sex, income) in ACE exposure and internalizing problems. The current study examined individual and co-developing trajectories of ACEs and internalizing problems and sociodemographic disparities among 4,655 diverse at-risk youth at ages 3, 5, and 9. Participants were 52.2% boys and racially and ethnically diverse, with 44.7% Black, 22.5% Hispanic, 16.7% Other, and 16.2% White youth. Results showed various trajectories with stable levels of cumulative ACE exposure over time and internalizing problems trajectories that decreased or increased over time. Findings provide some evidence for a dose-response relationship between ACEs and internalizing problems. Youth of Color (φ = 0.26, 0.11, 0.19) and from low-income backgrounds ( ϵ 2  = 0.07-0.09, 0.02, 0.06-0.08) were more likely to be in worse trajectories of ACEs, internalizing problems, and joint trajectories across ages 3, 5, and 9, respectively. Findings underscore the need for culturally responsive trauma-informed interventions and policy-level efforts that target the root cause of inequities.

### Strength in families: Parental resilience, marital dynamics, and adolescent anxiety and substance use
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sim L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 7

Drawing on Luthar's relational framework of resilience, this study examines how parental resilience operates within marital dynamics to shape adolescent adjustment in Mexican-origin families. Using four waves of data from 604 families (M adolage  = 12.41, SD = 0.97; 54.3% girls), we tested a longitudinal cascade linking W1 parental resilience to W4 adolescent anxiety and substance use via W2 marital warmth, W3 marital satisfaction, and W3 adolescents' perceptions of interparental conflict. Actor-partner interdependence models revealed both intrapersonal and interpersonal pathways: wives' resilience predicted own marital warmth, whereas husbands' resilience predicted wives' warmth. Indirect effects indicated that wives' resilience was associated with lower adolescent anxiety and substance use through warmer marital interactions, higher marital satisfaction, and reduced perceived conflict. These findings position resilience as a promotive relational resource enacted within the marital dyad, advancing interparental positivity spillover theory by identifying parental resilience as upstream drivers of family processes that shape adolescent psychopathology.

### Maternal postnatal depression and sensitive parenting: Considering developmental timing with a causally informative approach
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Padrutt ER et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 5

Models of intergenerational transmission of depression implicate lower sensitive parenting as a key mechanism linking maternal depressive symptoms and offspring outcomes. A well-developed literature demonstrates consistent, modest, negative associations between maternal depressive symptoms and sensitive parenting. However, this literature relies primarily on methods ill-suited to examine developmental timing or account for confounding with time-varying exposures. To address these limitations, we implemented marginal structural models, an approach developed to test timing effects and address confounding for time-varying exposures. Specifically, we used prospective longitudinal data from a sample of families from low-income, rural U.S. contexts (Family Life Project; N = 1,275; 59% White, 41% Black; 31% living below federal poverty level) to test chronicity and timing effects of maternal depressive symptoms during infancy on sensitive parenting at 35 postnatal months. Unadjusted models suggested that, compared with stable lower postnatal symptoms, chronically higher ( d = −.29, p = .001), early only ( d = −.21, p = .009), and later only ( d = −.26, p = .004) symptoms were all associated with less sensitive parenting, consistent with the existing literature. However, these differences were attenuated ( d s ≤ −.14) and non-significant ( p s ≥ .396) after adjusting for confounding. Results suggest the statistical association between maternal depression and sensitive parenting may be explained by confounding and highlight the importance of considering additional intervention targets that co-occur with maternal depression and may drive reductions in sensitive parenting.

Up to one in five mothers experience elevated depressive symptoms during the postnatal period ( Bauman et al., 2020 ; Field, 2010 ; Hahn-Holbrook et al., 2019; O'Hara, 2009 ), with evidence of even higher rates since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic ( Dagher et al., 2021 ; Iyengar et al., 2021 ). These highly prevalent symptoms have substantial negative impacts at both the individual and societal levels, including maternal emotional suffering, worse physical health, and lost productivity ( Luca et al., 2020 ). Children of mothers with elevated depressive symptoms also exhibit higher likelihood of negative outcomes across various domains ( Gotlib et al., 2023 ). Indeed, more than half of the estimated societal costs of maternal psychopathology operate through negative child outcomes, including more physical and mental health problems and lower levels of academic achievement ( Meaney, 2018 ). Thus, interventions aimed at supporting mothers experiencing depression and their children, particularly interventions targeted toward interrupting intergenerational transmission of risk, have a critical public health relevance. However, current understanding of the pathways through which maternal postnatal depression may impact child outcomes is limited. Maternal parenting behaviors are one potential pathway that has been the focus of considerable research ( Goodman & Gotlib, 1999 ). A well-established body of research has demonstrated a negative association between maternal postnatal depressive symptoms and sensitive parenting (e.g., Bernard et al., 2018 ). In the present study, we extend this existing research by applying a rigorous confounding adjustment approach to examine the causal impact of specific timing patterns of maternal postnatal 1 depressive symptoms on maternal sensitive parenting. Further, we specifically focus here on mothers living in rural poverty, as they are at increased risk of experiencing elevated depressive symptoms due to barriers in care and the additional stressors that come from parenting with limited resources (e.g., Abrams et al., 2009 ; Beeghly et al., 2003 , Dolbier et al., 2013 ; Szurek-Cabanas et al., 2024 ). Maternal Postnatal Depression and Parenting Postnatal depression may compromise the cognitive and emotional resources needed to perceive and respond to often subtle and nuanced infant and toddler cues. Potential reductions in sensitive parenting (i.e., “the ability to perceive and to interpret accurately the signals and communications implicit in [the child’s] behavior, and given this understanding, to respond to them appropriately and promptly” [ Ainsworth, 1969 , p. 2]) are of interest because of the well-documented associations between caregiver sensitivity and child development across important domains, such as self-regulation (Samdan et al., 2020) and mental health ( Goodman et al., 2020 ). A substantial body of research, including multiple meta-analyses, has demonstrated an association between elevated depression and less sensitive parenting ( r s = −.08 to −.16; Bernard et al., 2018 ; Goodman et al., 2020 ; Lovejoy et al., 2000 ). Maternal depression and its potential implications for sensitive parenting and, by extension, offspring outcomes may warrant particular attention during infancy and toddlerhood because of its prevalence in this developmental period (i.e., affecting up to 20% of mother-infant dyads; e.g., Bauman et al., 2020 ). As described in Belsky’s classic model of the determinants of parenting ( Belsky, 1984 ), parenting behaviors are multiply determined, with internal parent characteristics (e.g., depressive symptoms), child characteristics (e.g., temperament), and contextual factors (e.g., family income) all likely interacting to shape parenting behaviors. In line with this model, van Doesum and colleagues (2007) found evidence that the accumulation of multiple risk factors (e.g., maternal depression, lower family income, chronic stress) was related to lower parental sensitivity, and that family income explained additional variance in sensitivity beyond depressive symptoms. Relatedly, in their meta-analysis, Lovejoy and colleagues (2000) found that negative associations between depressive symptoms and positive parenting (which is conceptually similar to sensitive parenting) were stronger among samples of low-income mothers. Thus, clarifying the causal processes linking maternal depression and sensitive parenting—particularly among mothers living in poverty—has important implications for supporting mothers and children. Causality and Developmental Timing Maternal depressive symptoms unfold alongside other contextual (e.g., neighborhood safety), maternal (e.g., physical health problems), and child-level (e.g., temperament) factors that vary over time and jointly shape sensitive parenting. In observational studies, these co-occurring factors may act as confounders (i.e., common causes of both depressive symptoms and sensitive parenting) that bias estimation (e.g., by inducing an association in the absence of a causal effect) of the causal effect of depressive symptoms on sensitive parenting ( Foster, 2010 ). In addition to informing theories of the intergenerational transmission of depression, understanding whether the association between postnatal depression and sensitive parenting is indeed causal has important translational implications for supporting children of mothers experiencing postnatal depression. For example, if postnatal depression does negatively impact sensitive parenting, then, on average, reducing postnatal depression through treatment will increase sensitive parenting. However, in the absence of this causal pathway, additional intervention efforts targeting other factors would be necessary to impact sensitive parenting ( Foster, 2010 ). Randomized controlled trials are considered the gold standard for estimating causal effects because, when randomization is successful, they ensure that the randomized groups differ only on the exposure of interest ( Frach et al., 2023 ). ### Infant and early childhood mental health professionals' attitudes toward a system of professional endorsement: A quantitative examination of personal factors, professional support, and perceived benefits
**כתב עת:** 📄 Infant Mental Health Journal (Infant Ment Health J) *(IF: 2.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kubicek LF et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

Demand for recognition of the specialized knowledge and skills required of Infant and Early Childhood Mental Health (IECMH) practitioners, researchers, and policymakers resulted in the establishment of standardized professional credentials that document this expertise. However, to date, research on the influences and impacts of an Infant or Early Childhood Mental Health Endorsement on IECMH professionals is scarce. This descriptive, cross-sectional study aims to fill this gap by reporting quantitative findings from a voluntary online survey, the Perceptions of Endorsement Survey (PES), completed by a diverse group of 911 Endorsed professionals across the United States and Australia. The study by a U.S. research team explores how personal factors, professional supports, and perceived benefits influence the likelihood of continuing to participate in the Endorsement system and of recommending it to others (Attitudes towards Endorsement). Regression analyses revealed three factors that were significant predictors of positive Attitudes towards Endorsement: Confidence and Motivation, Endorsement-related Social Influences, and perceived Opportunities. Moreover, the combination of Endorsement-related Social Influences and Opportunities significantly and uniquely predicted positive Attitudes towards Endorsement above what these factors predicted individually. Discussion focuses on how individual-level demographic characteristics, occupational-level factors, social factors, and perceived benefits relate to Attitudes towards Endorsement.
