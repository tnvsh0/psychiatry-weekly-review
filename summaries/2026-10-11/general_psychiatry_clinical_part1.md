### From discovery to proof: a Translation Evidence Map for precision psychiatry
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Baune BT | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 5

Precision psychiatry aims to improve individual-patient decisions by measuring clinically meaningful heterogeneity, not by privileging any one data type, biomarker, or analytical method. This Personal View proposes a Translation Evidence Map that stages claims, from Level 0 (defined use case or discovery-positioning statement) to Level 6 (post-deployment monitoring), linked to measurement reliability, generalisable inference, decision value, safe delivery, and proportionality to risk, clinical need, and inaction. The Map does not replace Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE), biomarker qualification, prediction-model, regulatory, or roadmap frameworks; it identifies the highest defensible claim across components of unequal maturity and the next evidence step for compound precision claims. The Personal View discusses examples spanning inflammatory and circuit-based depression stratification, psychosis relapse monitoring, and suicidality monitoring, illustrating how the Map makes claim maturity, stopping points, stakeholder roles, and trade-offs important to patients explicit.

### SOS for School Staff Suicide Prevention Program: Perspectives of School Staff
**כתב עת:** 📄 Evid Based Pract Child Adolesc Ment Health *(IF: 3.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mirick RG | **תאריך:** 2026

BACKGROUND: Schools play an important role in child and adolescent suicide prevention efforts and many states have begun to mandate suicide prevention education for school staff.

OBJECTIVE: This study explored school staff's ( N  = 1509) perceptions of the impact of a brief virtual suicide prevention course titled: SOS for School Staff.

METHOD: This study used an online posttest and three-month follow-up to explore school staff's ( N  = 1509) perceptions via a posttest and follow-up measuring preparation, confidence, and knowledge.

RESULTS: In this study, most school staff agreed that the training increased their preparation (85.4%), confidence (86.2%), and knowledge (89.7%) about responding to a suicide disclosure. Approximately one-third of the respondents used the training content three months after the training, but there was a low response rate for the follow-up.

CONCLUSION: This is a promising suicide prevention training for school staff, who report it improves preparation to identify youth in distress, confidence at engaging youth in caring conversations, and knowledge of how to respond to a student's disclosure of suicidal thoughts and behaviors; however, larger follow-up studies are required.

### Risk-Stratified Cardiac Monitoring During Antipsychotic Treatment in Adolescents and Young Adults
**כתב עת:** 📄 Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica (Acta Psychiatr Scand) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Cuomo A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 7

INTRODUCTION: Antipsychotic-associated heart rate-corrected QT (QTc) prolongation and sudden cardiac death are uncommon in young people, but risk is concentrated in patients with identifiable clinical vulnerability and during selected drug- or exposure-related situations. We mapped the evidence and developed a risk-stratified framework for clinical monitoring.

METHODS: This structured narrative review searched PubMed in August 2026 using one comprehensive query and 41 targeted subqueries. After PMID-based deduplication, 934 unique records underwent title screening, 382 abstract review, and 158 full-text relevance assessment; 104 database-derived sources met broad relevance criteria. Backward citation checking and author-identified sources added 58 articles. Overall, 162 sources were considered and 100 providing direct, non-redundant support were cited from the original search; three further sources were incorporated during peer review to address reviewer comments, for a final total of 103 cited references. Two authors independently assessed full-text relevance. Youth evidence was prioritized; adult evidence was used for adult-only agents or when youth-specific data were unavailable. Findings were synthesized narratively without formal risk-of-bias or certainty assessment.

RESULTS: Patient-level factors, particularly electrolyte disturbance, bradycardia, restrictive eating disorder or medical instability, thyroid dysfunction, inherited channelopathy, structural disease, and interacting drugs, were more consistently associated with QTc risk than drug identity for many commonly used agents. Ziprasidone and pimozide had the strongest youth-specific drug-related signals. Adult evidence supported heightened caution with sertindole and iloperidone; high-dose phenothiazines and amisulpride in overdose or renal impairment represented exposure- or context-dependent risks. Clozapine required separate myocarditis-focused surveillance. The evidence supports structured baseline risk assessment and targeted ECG and electrolyte testing, with repeat monitoring driven by risk factors, treatment changes, and agent-specific warnings.

CONCLUSION: Monitoring should be tiered rather than uniformly scheduled for all patients. The proposed framework distinguishes core clinical risk assessment from targeted cardiac testing and identifies precautionary intervals that require prospective validation.

### Associations between perceived stress, sleep quality, and suicidal ideation among Chinese graduating-grade secondary school students: a multi-method study of the moderating role of physical exercise
**כתב עת:** 📄 Front Public Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yin T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Objective This study aimed to investigate the associations among perceived stress, sleep quality, physical exercise, and suicidal ideation among Chinese graduating-grade secondary school students. Specifically, this study examined whether sleep quality mediated the association between perceived stress and suicidal ideation and whether physical exercise moderated these relationships. In addition, machine-learning prediction models and network analysis were applied to identify important predictors and interconnected symptoms associated with suicidal ideation. Methods A cross-sectional survey was conducted among ninth- and twelfth-grade students from 13 secondary schools in Yunnan Province, China. A total of 4,372 valid questionnaires were collected. Participants completed measures of perceived stress, sleep quality, suicidal ideation, and physical exercise. Mediation and moderated mediation analyses were conducted using PROCESS models after adjusting for demographic covariates. Furthermore, ridge regression and gradient boosting tree models were used to predict suicidal ideation, with Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) applied to evaluate predictor contributions. Network analysis was conducted to explore central and bridge nodes among study variables. Results Perceived stress was positively associated with suicidal ideation, and sleep quality partially mediated this association. Physical exercise significantly moderated the direct association between perceived stress and suicidal ideation as well as the association between sleep quality problems and suicidal ideation. Specifically, higher levels of physical exercise weakened the positive relationships between perceived stress, sleep problems, and suicidal ideation. Machine-learning analyses showed that sleep quality problems, perceived stress, physical exercise, and their interaction terms contributed substantially to the prediction of suicidal ideation, with both models achieving an R 2 of approximately 0.41 in the hold-out test set. Network analysis further identified subjective sleep quality, sleep latency, sleep disturbances, stress-coping burden, and physical exercise frequency as highly connected nodes within the network. Conclusion Among Chinese graduating-grade secondary school students, perceived stress, poor sleep quality, and suicidal ideation were closely interconnected, whereas physical exercise appeared to attenuate these risk associations. Integrating stress management, sleep improvement strategies, and regular physical exercise into school-based mental health programs may provide potential approaches for suicide risk prevention among adolescents.

1 Introduction Suicide is a major global public health concern. The World Health Organization has reported that more than 720,000 people die by suicide each year, making suicide the third leading cause of death among individuals aged 15–29 worldwide ( 1 ). Suicide is conceptualized as a continuum process, in which suicidal ideation represents the initial stage. Suicidal ideation refers to thoughts, desires, or plans to end one’s own life ( 2 , 3 ). Epidemiological evidence indicates that suicidal ideation is relatively prevalent among children and adolescents, and those who experience suicidal ideation are at a substantially higher risk of subsequent suicide-related outcomes ( 4 ). In secondary school students, suicidal ideation is not merely a transient or isolated psychological phenomenon; rather, it may serve as an important risk signal for subsequent mental health problems, suicidal behaviors, and impaired social functioning ( 5 , 6 ). Longitudinal studies have further demonstrated that adolescent suicidal ideation is closely associated with later psychopathology, suicide-related behaviors, and long-term social dysfunction ( 7 ). Therefore, identifying the factors associated with suicidal ideation in secondary school students, as well as elucidating its underlying mechanisms, is of critical importance for school-based mental health education and crisis intervention. Among secondary school students, those in their final year experience multiple academic and developmental stressors, including entrance examinations, academic competition, parental expectations, and uncertainty about future trajectories. Prolonged stress may increase SI by affecting emotional regulation, recovery processes, and daily behaviors. Sleep quality represents a potential pathway linking stress to mental health outcomes, as sleep problems may impair emotional regulation and stress recovery capacity. SI is a complex outcome arising from interactions among stress, sleep problems, health behaviors, and psychological adaptation. Traditional models can examine overall pathways but provide limited information about the relative importance of specific variables. Network analysis and predictive models may therefore provide complementary evidence by identifying key nodes and predictive contributors associated with SI. 1.1 Perceived stress and suicidal ideation Perceived stress refers to an individual’s appraisal of the extent to which situations in life are unpredictable, uncontrollable, and overloading ( 8 ). Compared with objective stressful events, PS emphasizes individuals’ cognitive appraisal of environmental demands and their perceived capacity to cope with those demands ( 9 ). Among final-year secondary school students, multiple stressors—such as entrance examinations, academic competition, performance evaluation, parental expectations, peer comparison, and uncertainty regarding future development—do not exert uniform effects on mental health. Instead, their impact largely depends on whether students perceive these demands as exceeding their coping capacity and controllability ( 10 ). When students persistently appraise stressors as unpredictable, uncontrollable, or unmanageable, their psychological resources may become chronically depleted, leading to heightened tension, anxiety, depressive symptoms, helplessness, and self-devaluation. From a theoretical perspective, PS may represent a key risk factor for SI ( 11 ). According to the stress-vulnerability framework, individuals exposed to sustained high levels of stress, particularly in the absence of sufficient psychological resources, are more likely to develop maladaptive cognitive appraisals and affective dysregulation ( 12 , 13 ). For final-year secondary school students, elevated PS not only intensifies threatening interpretations of examination failure, academic fluctuations, and future uncertainty, but may also undermine problem-solving abilities, emotional regulation capacity, and hopefulness ( 14 , 15 ). When students continuously perceive their current situation as unchangeable and future outcomes as uncontrollable, feelings of hopelessness, perceived failure, and diminished life meaning may emerge, thereby increasing the likelihood of SI ( 16 ). Accordingly, PS is not only a salient indicator of psychological risk in this population but may also serve as a key psychosocial determinant of variability in SI.
