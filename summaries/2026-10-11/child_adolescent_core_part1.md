### Targeting Parental Risk Factors for Children's Anxiety: A Factorial Randomized Trial with Three Intervention Components
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry (J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 10.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Rienks K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 8

OBJECTIVE: Parent-focused interventions have the potential to reduce parental risk factors for children's anxiety. We tested three theoretically distinct intervention components, targeting distinct parental risk factors, to improve understanding of the role of these factors in children's anxiety, and to guide intervention program development.

METHOD: We used a 12-week factorial experiment with random allocation to any possible combination and order of three single-session online components: (1) reducing family accommodation, (2) increasing parental empathetic responses, and (3) cognitive restructuring for parental maladaptive cognitions. Assessments took place at baseline (T 0 ), after the first component (T 2 ), after all components (T 6 ), and at follow-up (T 12 ). Our primary outcome was parent-reported child anxiety at T 2 . We used intention-to-treat regression-based ANCOVA models.

RESULTS: The components did not reduce parent-reported anxiety at T 2 , but the empathetic responding component improved parental empathic responding at all timepoints and parent-reported anxiety at T 6  and T 12 . The family accommodation component improved family accommodation at T 2 , but this effect disappeared once parents received additional components. The cognitive restructuring component did not produce any effects.

CONCLUSION: Online, single-session components did not reduce parent-reported anxiety at the primary timepoint, and most outcomes did not change. Nevertheless, components targeting parental empathic responding and family accommodation can meaningfully change parental risk factors for children's anxiety. Targeting empathetic responses seems most promising for reducing children's anxiety. These findings may contribute to the development of more precise parent-focused interventions for children's anxiety.

### Does later chronotype cause poorer adolescent mental health? An adolescent brain cognitive development study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Visontay R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 8

BACKGROUND: This study investigated whether chronotype (biobehavioral preference for sleep and wake timing) across early adolescence impacts mental health symptoms at age 13-14. This is a relationship with growing correlational evidence but limited causal exploration.

METHODS: Participants were 7,489 adolescents (aged 9-10 at baseline; 13-14 at the fifth assessment wave) from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development Study (ABCD). Marginal structural models with machine learning-based weight estimation were used to assess the causal impact of chronotype at ages 11-12 and 12-13, as measured by the youth-reported Munich Chronotype Questionnaire, on several dimensions of mental health symptoms at age 13-14 (parent-reported Child Behavioral Checklist internalizing and externalizing values, n = 2,668; child-reported Prodromal Psychosis Scale, n = 2,698).

RESULTS: There was no effect of chronotype at age 11-12 on any outcome. However, later chronotype at age 12-13 was associated with slightly more severe externalizing symptoms (b = .29, p = .04) and more psychotic-like experiences at age 13-14 (b = .05, p = .02), but not with more severe internalizing symptoms. Post hoc analyses indicated the lack of relationship with internalizing held for both anxiety and depression symptoms and in both sexes.

CONCLUSIONS: There are likely causal effects of adolescent chronotype on mental health symptoms, but these are dependent on the dimension of mental health and period of adolescence. The typically reported association between later chronotype and more severe internalizing symptoms may not manifest until later adolescence (such as with the common postpubertal shift in chronotype).

### Promoting healthy families: a three-arm randomized controlled trial of Triple P and Circle of Security-Parenting versus treatment-as-usual for families with young children (2-6 years)
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Gonzalez A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 5

BACKGROUND: Parenting programs grounded in social learning (SL) and attachment theory are widely implemented, yet few trials have directly compared their effects across multiple parenting domains in community contexts. This study examined the relative effectiveness of an SL-based program (Triple P - Level 4 Group) and an attachment-based program (Circle of Security - Parenting, COS-P) against treatment-as-usual (TAU) when delivered online through community agencies.

METHODS: A three-arm randomized controlled trial was conducted across four community organizations in Ontario, Canada. Caregivers of children aged 2-6 years (N = 502) were randomized (1:1:1) to Triple P, COS-P, or TAU. Primary outcomes were child behavioral difficulties (SDQ-Total) and parental overreactivity with post-intervention, 6-, and 12-month follow-up. Secondary outcomes included parental attributions, parenting stress, and caregiver mental health. Analyses followed an intention-to-treat framework using mixed-effects models (ClinicalTrials.gov #NCT04702191).

RESULTS: Relative to TAU, Triple P demonstrated significant short-term reductions in emotional symptoms and conduct problems, and immediate improvements in hostile and helpless parenting and anxiety; however, most between-group effects did not persist beyond post-intervention. Effects on total child behavior problems at 12 months were no longer significant after correction for multiple testing. Improvements in parental attributions were sustained at 12 months. COS-P differed from TAU on only one outcome, at one time point. Within-group improvements in child behavior, dysfunctional parenting, and caregiver mood were observed in TAU, with COS-P and Triple P showing similar trends. Exploratory analyses suggested broader benefits for Triple P relative to COS-P.

CONCLUSIONS: Triple P demonstrated selective short-term benefits relative to TAU across child behavioral and parenting outcomes, with sustained effects limited to parental attributions. COS-P showed no measurable effects relative to TAU. These findings highlight the importance of behavioral management and self-regulation components and point to the need for further research examining contextual moderators, maintenance strategies, and sustainable delivery approaches, including digital formats.

### Developmental trajectories of tonic and phasic irritability from ages 12 to 21: early predictors, co-development, and psychiatric outcomes in young adulthood
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Silver J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 4

BACKGROUND: Irritability is a common and impairing, transdiagnostic feature of youth psychopathology. Theory distinguishes tonic irritability (persistent irritable mood) from phasic irritability (episodic temper outbursts), yet little is known about how these dimensions co-develop across adolescence into young adulthood and relate to later psychiatric outcomes.

METHODS: Participants were drawn from a longitudinal community cohort assessed at ages 12, 15, 18, and 21 (N = 532 with data at one or more waves). Tonic and phasic irritability were derived from youth-report items spanning multiple measures. We tested the factor structure and longitudinal measurement invariance of tonic and phasic irritability, then modeled co-development using parallel-process latent growth curve models. Sociodemographic factors and parental lifetime psychopathology (assessed at age 9) were examined as predictors of growth factors. Growth factors were used to estimate associations with age-21 psychiatric diagnoses and self-injurious thoughts and behaviors.

RESULTS: A two-factor model distinguishing tonic and phasic irritability fit better than a one-factor model at each wave, and configural, metric, and scalar invariance were supported from ages 12 to 21. On average, tonic irritability increased modestly whereas phasic irritability declined. Despite divergent mean trajectories, tonic and phasic slopes were positively correlated, indicating coordinated change. Baseline tonic irritability predicted steeper declines in phasic irritability, whereas baseline phasic irritability did not predict change in tonic irritability. Higher tonic intercept and steeper tonic increases were associated with higher odds of depressive and anxiety disorders and suicidal ideation at age 21; tonic intercept also predicted suicidal acts, and tonic change additionally predicted non-suicidal self-injury. In contrast, higher phasic intercept predicted greater odds of disruptive behavior disorders but lower odds of depression, and steeper phasic decline was associated with greater odds of depression.

CONCLUSIONS: Tonic and phasic irritability are separable yet developmentally linked across adolescence into young adulthood and show distinct clinical correlates. Distinguishing persistent mood-based irritability from outburst-based irritability may refine developmental models of risk and intervention targets.

### Estimating direct and indirect genetic effects on emerging variation in depressive symptoms in early adolescence: a trio polygenic score analysis in the MoBa cohort : Author list
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bazezew MM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 6

Early adolescence is a common period of onset for depressive symptoms. In part, this may reflect a developmental expression of individuals' genetic propensities as they undergo physiological and hormonal changes and interact with new environments. Many commonly proposed mechanisms assume direct effects of an individual's own genes on emerging variation in their depressive symptomatology. However, estimates of genetic influence based on analyses in unrelated individuals capture not only direct genetic effects but also genetic effects from parents and other biologically related family members. In data from the Norwegian Mother, Father and Child Cohort (MoBa), we used linear mixed models to distinguish developmentally-stable and adolescence-specific direct and parental indirect genetic effects. In within-family models, we examined effects of polygenic scores for major depressive disorder (MDD), ADHD, anxiety disorders, and educational attainment (EA) on depressive symptoms measured at ages 8 and 14. Children's own MDD polygenic scores showed adolescence-specific effects on depressive symptoms (β PGS*wave =0.041, [95% CI: 0.017, 0.065]). Developmentally-stable direct effects from children's polygenic scores for MDD (β = 0.016, [0.006, 0.039]), ADHD (β = 0.024, [0.008, 0.041]) and EA (β=-0.02, [ -0.038, -0.002]) were also evident. The only evidence of indirect genetic effects was a stable effect of maternal EA polygenic scores (β = 0.04, [0.024, 0.054]). Direct genetic effects linked to genetic liability to MDD accounted for emerging variation in depressive symptoms in adolescence. These results imply that specific etiological mechanisms related to MDD may become particularly relevant for depressive symptoms during early adolescence compared to at earlier ages.

### Co-developmental trajectories of emotional symptoms and peer problems from childhood to adolescence: associations with positive and negative mental health outcomes
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Haidabieke A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 9

This study used data from the Millennium Cohort Study (MCS) to examine the co-developmental trajectories of emotional symptoms and peer problems from ages 3 to 17. A sample of 16,983 children (48.8% female, 82.4% White ethnicity) was analyzed using parallel-process latent class growth models (PP-LCGMs) to explore how emotional symptoms and peer problems evolve over time and their associations with positive and negative mental health outcomes at age 17. Five trajectory classes were identified: "Primary peer problems" class (12.2%), "Co-increasing high risk" class (4.0%), "Co-decreasing medium risk" class (7.1%), "Primary emotional symptoms" class (10.3%), and "Persistent low risk" class (66.6%). The "Persistent low risk" and "Co-decreasing medium risk" classes reported the highest levels of mental wellbeing and self-esteem, relatively high levels of self-control, and the lowest levels of psychological distress, self-harm, and suicide attempt at age 17. The "Co-increasing high risk" and "Primary emotional symptoms" classes reported the poorest outcomes. The "Primary peer problems" class fell between these classes. Findings highlight the importance of identifying heterogeneous co-developmental patterns of emotional symptoms and peer problems from childhood to adolescence and call for tailored intervention programs to reduce risk and promote long-term well-being.
