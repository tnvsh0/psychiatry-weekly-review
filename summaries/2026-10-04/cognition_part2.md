### Carving stories at their natural joints
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Review (Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Dubourg E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Over the past three decades, psychology has documented the recurrence of specific themes in stories across cultures (e.g., love, alliances, monsters, and imaginary worlds) and linked them to identifiable motivational mechanisms (e.g., mate choice, cooperation, threat detection, and exploration). Yet this body of work has remained fragmented, organized around individual themes rather than integrated into a unified account of what makes narrative content psychologically engaging. Here, we propose the Motivational-Ingredient Framework. We identify a set of motivational ingredients: core narrative features, each defined by the specific motivational mechanism it activates. Drawing on evolutionary psychology, affective neuroscience, and the study of human motivation, we compile a comprehensive table of ingredients grounded in the current understanding of human motivational architecture. This framework offers a theory-driven, cross-culturally applicable foundation for the empirical study of narrative content, with implications for fields ranging from media psychology and computational humanities to education and public policy. ### Shared processing in familiar and unfamiliar face matching
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** White D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Large performance benefits in face processing for familiar compared to unfamiliar faces are often interpreted as reflecting qualitative changes in processing. However, directly testing this claim has proven difficult because of variation in tasks, stimuli and performance scales used to measure unfamiliar and familiar face recognition. Here we introduce a new method to compare familiar and unfamiliar face identity processing abilities, using the same task, and the same faces across experimental conditions. Familiarity was manipulated naturally, based on participants' viewing history for one of two popular TV shows, such that characters from one show were familiar, and the other unfamiliar. Prior exposure to characters led to improved identity matching performance for actors on that show, confirming effective manipulation of familiarity, but our method avoided floor and ceiling effects which have made previous research hard to interpret. Across two experiments, we report strong and reliable associations between viewers' accuracy on familiar and unfamiliar face matching. We conclude that despite the considerable accuracy differences typically observed between tests of familiar and unfamiliar faces, they appear to share underlying processes. We discuss these results in terms of the debate about the degree to which quantitative or qualitative differences best characterise the relationship between familiar and unfamiliar face processing.

### AI is not as conscious as humans: Essentialism in mental state attributions to artificial systems
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Longin L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Artificial systems increasingly behave in ways that resemble humans. But is that sufficient to make us see them as conscious, or as having related mental states? We contrast two broad possibilities. People may rely on a single general principle: the more appropriately an agent responds to its environment, the more conscious it is, whether human or artificial. We call this a functionalist attribution. Alternatively, people may reserve consciousness for certain kinds of beings: even an artificial system that behaves just as responsively as a human is judged less conscious. This is an essentialist attribution. A further question is whether this pattern is unique to consciousness or extends to closely related constructs such as awareness. In two pre-registered sets of studies (five experiments, N = 1092), UK participants read scenarios describing human or artificial agents whose responsiveness to spatial, social, and temporal features of the environment varied, and rated the agents' consciousness and awareness, either separately or in conjunction. Attributions of consciousness followed an essentialist logic: at similar levels of responsiveness, participants consistently attributed greater consciousness to humans. Attributions of awareness followed a functionalist pattern: for both humans and artificial agents, awareness increased steadily with responsiveness, although humans were rated more aware at the highest levels. These findings suggest that people use different inferential rules for different mental concepts. Consciousness is treated as depending on what an agent is; awareness largely on what an agent does.

### Young children can infer what speakers know based on their causal influence over listeners
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chuey A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 3

A causal, mentalistic understanding of communication-a process by which a speaker's mind causes a change in a listener's mind-can support powerful inferences that go beyond observable data. If A struggles to activate a toy but succeeds after B whispers to A, you can infer that B knew how to activate it even without hearing what was said. Although humans are precocious communicators, much remains unknown about how we come to understand communication as a process by which speakers causally influence listeners. Extending prior work on children's forward inferences about communication-from speakers to listeners-the current work examined how children reason backwards from listeners' behaviors to recover speakers' knowledge. Children viewed two scenarios where a listener failed to activate a toy twice before finally succeeding. In one scenario the speaker communicated before a listener's success, whereas in the other scenario the speaker communicated before a listener's failure. Even 3-year-olds chose the former speaker as the one who knew how the toy works (Exp.1). However, when communication always preceded success but the scenarios differed in what would have happened without communication (i.e., the listener was poised to fail or succeed), a preference for the former speaker did not emerge until later (Exps.2-3). Importantly, across all experiments, children held no preference when speakers produced non-speech vocalizations. These results suggest that even young children have an abstract understanding of communication as a causal, mentalistic process, but their inferences are constrained by the causal cues available in context and continue to develop throughout early childhood.

### Preschoolers use exploratory approaches to selective social learning
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Palmquist CM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 30

Preschoolers evaluate many cues when making inferences about others (Harris et al., 2018). It is unclear whether children's understanding of inference-making is sophisticated enough that they alsoseek outthe most relevant cues for making specific inferences. Here, we tasked 4- and 5-year-olds and adults with a modified epistemic trust paradigm in which they were told to select one of two informants to help them learn about novel objects. However, before doing so, participants were told that they could learn up to three pieces of information about the informants: what they know (epistemic cue), how they play with their friends (benevolence cue), or what they look like (appearance cue). Of particular interest was which cue participants prioritized (picked first) and what strategies they used for seeking information. In Study 1, adults prioritized learning epistemic information and tended to employ efficient information-seeking strategies (selecting the fewest cues possible to make their inferences). Children, on the other hand, chose each cue first at relatively equal rates and tended to employ exploratory strategies for information-seeking (sampling broadly from the available information). This was even the case in Study 2, in which the informativeness of the appearance and benevolence cues was reduced. These studies raise questions about the way in which children make inferences about others and point to a tendency for children to employ exploratory strategies for learning about others (Gopnik, 2020).
