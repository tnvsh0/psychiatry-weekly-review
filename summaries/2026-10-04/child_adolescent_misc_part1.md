### Orbitofrontal and Medial Frontal Gray Matter Volumes Are Associated With Hyperactivity Traits in 2548 Nonclinical Adults
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Hum Brain Mapp *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Putra HA, Park K | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

ABSTRACT Attention‐deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) traits vary continuously across the general population, yet whether the frontal structural correlates established in clinical ADHD extend to trait variation in nonclinical adults is unknown. Based on meta‐analytic evidence, we hypothesized that orbitofrontal gray matter volumes would be negatively associated with hyperactivity traits in 2548 nonclinical Japanese adults (1488 males, 1060 females; aged 23–76 years) who underwent structural MRI and completed the Adult ADHD Self‐Report Scale. Regional gray matter volumes of 61 bilateral‐averaged regions were tested against inattention, hyperactive motor, and hyperactive verbal trait subscales using partial correlations controlling for age and sex, with false discovery rate (FDR) correction. Five associations survived correction, all linking smaller orbital or medial frontal volumes to higher hyperactivity: medial frontal cortex with hyperactive motor and hyperactive verbal traits, and anterior orbital gyrus, lateral orbital gyrus, and orbital inferior frontal gyrus with hyperactive verbal traits ( r = −0.070 to −0.078). The effect sizes were within the range reported for clinical ADHD by the ENIGMA consortium, and secondary analyses found no evidence that bilateral averaging concealed an additional hemisphere‐specific association. The findings were robust across sensitivity and permutation analyses. Inattention showed no FDR‐significant associations, with Bayesian and equivalence analyses supporting near‐null effects at the benchmarked magnitude for most regions. Brain volumes did not improve individual‐level prediction of trait scores. These findings identify small orbitofrontal and medial frontal volume associations with hyperactivity along the adult ADHD trait continuum and provide a foundational nonclinical baseline for clinical and transdiagnostic studies of ADHD traits.

In 2548 nonclinical adults, smaller orbitofrontal and medial frontal cortex gray matter volumes were associated with higher hyperactivity traits ( r = −0.07 to −0.08). These small, robust effects, consistent with ENIGMA ADHD findings, suggest frontal structure–trait mapping extends to the nonclinical ADHD continuum.

1 Introduction Attention‐deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) symptoms are increasingly recognized as dimensional traits that vary continuously across the general population, rather than defining a discrete diagnostic category (Franke et al. 2018 ; Lubke et al. 2009 ; Marcus and Barry 2011 ). This dimensional perspective is supported by genetic evidence showing shared etiology between clinical ADHD and subclinical traits in community samples (Riglin et al. 2016 ), and by epidemiological data indicating that even subthreshold symptoms are associated with meaningful functional impairment (Asherson et al. 2016 ). Despite this, the vast majority of ADHD neuroimaging research has relied on case–control designs, comparing diagnosed patients to healthy controls. Whether the structural correlates identified in diagnosed samples extend into the nonclinical adult range therefore remains an open question that case–control designs cannot address. Structural neuroimaging studies of ADHD have consistently reported volumetric reductions in subcortical regions (accumbens, amygdala, caudate, hippocampus, and putamen) as well as in frontal cortical areas (Frodl and Skokauskas 2012 ; Hoogman et al. 2017 ; Nakao et al. 2011 ). The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) has emerged as a region of particular relevance, given its role in impulse regulation, response inhibition, and emotional decision‐making (Hesslinger et al. 2002 ). Recent meta‐analyses have also reported reduced OFC gray matter in ADHD (Chen et al. 2025 ; Yu et al. 2022 ). However, the effect sizes reported by the ENIGMA (Enhancing NeuroImaging Genetics through Meta‐Analysis) consortium mega‐analysis are small (Cohen's d = 0.10–0.19; Hoogman et al. 2017 ), and findings from clinical samples may not generalize to the broader trait continuum. At these magnitudes, brain–behavior associations are informative about population‐level structure–trait mapping but are not expected to support individual‐level prediction. Sex is an important consideration in this context. Males and females differ in both ADHD symptom profiles, with males presenting more hyperactive–impulsive symptoms and females more inattentive symptoms (Ramtekkar et al. 2010 ; Willcutt 2012 ), and in brain morphometry, with large sex differences in total gray matter volume ( d > 1.0; Ritchie et al. 2018 , Ruigrok et al. 2014 ). Onnink et al. ( 2014 ) reported a sex‐by‐diagnosis interaction for caudate volume in adult ADHD, suggesting that sex should be accounted for in brain–ADHD analyses. Using a subset of the present sample, we previously reported that ADHD traits mediated associations between regional gray matter volumes and traffic crash risk via path analysis (Putra et al. 2023 ). In related large‐scale regional gray matter volume (rGMV) studies in nonclinical adults, we have also shown that regional gray matter volumes covary with fatigue perception and exhibit sex differences (Putra, Park, and Yamashita 2022 ; Putra, Park, Yamashita, et al. 2022 ). Neither line of work, however, tested whether the frontal structural correlates established in clinical ADHD extend to dimensional trait variation in adults without a diagnosis. Testing this requires a sample large enough to detect associations of the size that clinical meta‐analyses predict (| r |≈0.05–0.10; Hoogman et al. 2017 ) and to separate small true associations from null effects. The present study examined this question in 2548 nonclinical adults who underwent structural MRI and completed the Adult ADHD Self‐Report Scale as part of a brain health check‐up program in Japan. ADHD traits were decomposed into three subscales (inattention, hyperactive motor, and hyperactive verbal), following the factor structure established by Stanton et al. ( 2018 ) and consistent with the three‐community structure reported by Panagiotidi et al. ( 2024 ). Based on meta‐analytic evidence of OFC involvement in ADHD (Chen et al. 2025 ; Nakao et al. 2011 ; Yu et al. 2022 ) and the role of the OFC in impulse regulation and the suppression of inappropriate responses (Hesslinger et al. 2002 ), we hypothesized that orbitofrontal gray matter volumes would be negatively associated with hyperactivity traits in this nonclinical population. Because clinical meta‐analyses indicate that such effects are small, findings were interpreted against an ENIGMA‐informed benchmark of (| r | = 0.05). Whether inattention shows volumetric correlates in nonclinical adults, whether associations extend beyond the OFC, and whether any associations are subscale‐specific were treated as open empirical questions. ### A Community-Driven Toolkit to Improve Rural Emergency Department Accessibility for Autistic Children and Their Families
**כתב עת:** 📄 Prog Community Health Partnersh | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kabakov S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Autism core characteristics and sensory processing differences can create significant barriers to families accessing and children receiving emergency health care in the heightened sensory and fast-paced environment of emergency departments (EDs), particularly in rural communities. This project aimed to use a community-collaborative approach to develop and disseminate educational materials, recommend ED environmental modifications, and implement sensory regulation supports to enhance the capacity of health care providers and staff in supporting autistic children within rural ED settings. An interdisciplinary community-academic partnership team including researchers, graduate students, parents, an autistic focused organization, health care providers, and hospital staff used an iterative process to co-create materials. The collaboration yielded a multifaceted toolkit with high acceptability from health care team members. The toolkit products included educational materials, environmental adaptations, and sensory-friendly tools. Future research should explore toolkit modifications to allow for implementation across unique ED contexts and assess health care related outcomes to measure toolkit effectiveness.

### Affective Computing Approaches in Child-Robot Interaction: A Systematic Review and Taxonomy
**כתב עת:** 📄 Sensors (Basel) | **סוג מחקר:** סקירה שיטתית | **מחברים:** Cano S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 9

Affective computing has become increasingly relevant in child-robot interaction (CRI), particularly in social robotics, emotion recognition, engagement assessment, and autism-related interventions. This systematic review with a critical and integrative synthesis analyzes 105 included studies to examine how affect is sensed, represented, processed, expressed, and evaluated in CRI. The literature search was conducted in IEEE Xplore, Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed, following a systematic screening process guided by the review objectives. A descriptive and structured narrative synthesis was conducted considering publication characteristics, robot platform and morphology, target population, sensing modalities and observed affect-relevant features, affective constructs and representation models, computational and control mechanisms, robot affective expression, evaluation strategies, and remaining research gaps. The findings show a strong emphasis on ASD-related contexts, visually observable and behavioral features, facial emotion recognition, body movement analysis, and engagement assessment. The review also identifies important limitations, including reliance on camera-based affect recognition, comparatively limited use of physiological and other complementary sensing modalities, unclear alignment between robot roles and interaction strategies, insufficient reporting of robot emotional expressiveness and control mechanisms, and limited attention to explainability, data governance, and long-term ethical implications. Based on these findings, an integrative taxonomy of affective computing in CRI is proposed, comprising six interconnected dimensions: interaction context; sensing modalities and observed features; affective constructs and representation models; computational and control mechanisms; robot affective expression; and evaluation and adaptation strategies. Rather than treating these dimensions as entirely novel categories, the taxonomy consolidates and extends previously fragmented classifications into a child-centered representation of the affective interaction process. Overall, this review argues that affective CRI should move beyond automatic emotion recognition toward multimodal, embodied, developmentally appropriate, explainable, and ethically grounded robot interaction.

### Association Between Toe Walking and Repetitive Behaviors in Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Cross-Sectional Phenotypic Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Medicina (Kaunas) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Gnazzo M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 13

Background and Objectives : Toe walking (TW) is frequently observed in children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), yet its clinical significance remains incompletely understood. While previous studies have linked TW to ASD severity and multisystem involvement, its relationship with the behavioral phenotype of repetitive behaviors has not been systematically explored. Materials and Methods : This study analyzed a cohort of 289 children with ASD who underwent comprehensive clinical assessment, including motor evaluation, sleep assessment using the Sleep Disturbance Scale for Children (SDSC), feeding behavior using the Brief Autism Mealtime Behavior Inventory (BAMBI), and detailed behavioral characterization through the Repetitive Behavior Scale-Revised (RBS-R) at the item level. Statistical analyses included non-parametric group comparisons, Spearman correlation analyses, and multivariable regression models. Results : TW was observed in 27.3% of participants. Children with TW showed significantly higher scores in stereotyped behaviors (7.71 ± 2.09 vs. 4.26 ± 2.06, r = 0.756, p FDR < 0.001), ritualistic/sameness behaviors (11.78 ± 3.21 vs. 7.05 ± 3.03, r = 0.709, p FDR < 0.001), and total RBS-R scores (25.52 ± 4.51 vs. 15.88 ± 4.64, r = 0.867, p FDR < 0.001). Correlation analyses revealed a strong association between TW and stereotyped behaviors (ρ = 0.588, p < 0.001) and a moderate association with ritualistic/sameness behaviors (ρ = 0.549, p < 0.001). RBS-R total scores were also correlated with sleep disturbances (ρ = 0.532, p < 0.001). In the multivariate linear model, TW was associated with an adjusted mean increase of 7.77 points in the RBS-R Total Score (B = 7.771, 95% CI 6.603–8.939, p < 0.001, β = 0.551). In the multivariate logistic model, the combined Ritualistic/Sameness behavior domain independently predicted the presence of TW (OR = 1.549, 95% CI 1.366–1.757, p < 0.001). Conclusions : In children with ASD, TW was associated with specific domains of repetitive behavior, particularly stereotyped and ritualistic/sameness patterns. These cross-sectional findings suggest a phenotypic association between motor and behavioral features, without establishing specificity, causality, or a shared underlying mechanism. This multidimensional perspective may support future studies investigating the clinical and biological correlates of TW in ASD.

1. Lay Summary Children with autism who show toe walking also tend to present more severe repetitive and ritualistic behaviors. Rather than being considered only a gait abnormality, toe walking may be a clinically relevant motor feature associated with specific patterns of repetitive behavior. These cross-sectional findings indicate a phenotypic association between these domains, but they cannot establish causality or a shared biological mechanism. Observing persistent toe walking may nevertheless provide a reason to consider a broader developmental assessment, including behavioral, sleep, feeding, and motor domains. Future studies are needed to determine whether this association is specific to autism and whether it has clinical value. 2. Introduction Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent difficulties in social communication and interaction together with restricted and repetitive patterns of behavior [ 1 ]. Beyond these core diagnostic features, motor abnormalities, sleep disturbances, feeding difficulties, and gastrointestinal symptoms are frequently reported in children with ASD and may contribute substantially to clinical heterogeneity and functional burden [ 2 , 3 ]. The prevalence of toe walking (TW) in the general population has been reported as high as 5% during early childhood, with spontaneous resolution in up to 79% of cases [ 4 ]. On the contrary, the prevalence of persistent TW in patients with neuropsychiatric conditions has been reported to be as high as 41% [ 4 , 5 ]. Among motor manifestations, TW is a frequent but still incompletely understood feature of ASD [ 6 , 7 , 8 ]. Previous studies have documented its prevalence and clinical associations within the autistic phenotype, ranging from 6.3% to 62.9% [ 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 ]. TW has also been discussed in relation to atypical sensorimotor processing, motor control, and sensory modulation, although the mechanisms underlying its occurrence in ASD remain uncertain [ 8 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 ]. Restricted and repetitive behaviors constitute one of the core diagnostic domains of ASD and encompass a heterogeneous range of manifestations, including motor stereotypies, ritualistic behaviors, insistence on sameness, and restricted interests [ 17 ]. These behaviors have been discussed within several neurodevelopmental frameworks as potentially related to atypical behavioral regulation and adaptation to environmental demands [ 17 , 18 ]. At a neurophysiological level, this behavioral inflexibility has been linked to a reduced capacity to dynamically modulate and shift brain states across large-scale neurocognitive networks [ 19 ]. Repetitive motor patterns have also been hypothesized to have potential attentional or regulatory functions, although their specific significance has yet to be established [ 20 ]. Neuroimaging studies have further reported associations between specific patterns of cortical thickness and the severity of restricted and repetitive behaviors in ASD [ 21 ]. Sleep disturbances are among the most prevalent comorbidities in ASD and have important implications for daytime functioning and clinical management [ 22 , 23 ]. Several studies have also reported associations between sleep disturbances and greater sensory-processing difficulties, repetitive behaviors, compulsive behaviors, and self-injurious behaviors [ 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 ]. Although these associations suggest potential interactions among these clinical domains, the mechanisms underlying these relationships remain incompletely understood. Within this perspective, a dimensional and integrative approach becomes essential. Rather than examining individual domains in isolation, the simultaneous assessment of motor patterns, repetitive behaviors, and sleep disturbances may provide a more accurate representation of the clinical phenotype. The present study adopts this approach by examining TW not merely as an isolated gait characteristic, but as a motor feature potentially associated with a broader pattern of repetitive-behavior characteristics within ASD. We hypothesized that TW would be associated with selected behavioral domains rather than uniformly with the overall severity of repetitive behaviors. Although this study uses the same multicenter cohort described by Costanza et al. [ 9 ], its scientific objectives differ substantially. The previous publication focused on the prevalence of TW and its general clinical correlates in children with ASD. In contrast, the present study represents a hypothesis-driven secondary phenotypic analysis designed to investigate whether TW is selectively associated with specific domains of restricted and repetitive behaviors using multivariable statistical models adjusted for relevant clinical covariates. ### Association between attention-deficit/hyperactivity symptoms and body composition in children: a health examination-based study
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Pediatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 23

Background Although attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) has been associated with obesity, research examining specific body composition components and dimensional symptom domains remains limited. This study aimed to investigate the associations between dimensional ADHD symptom domains and specific body composition indicators in children, and to explore potential moderating and mediating pathways. Methods A retrospective cross-sectional analysis was conducted in 636 children aged 6–18 years. ADHD symptoms were assessed using the parent-reported SNAP-IV scale, and body composition (visceral fat area [VFA]; skeletal muscle mass to weight ratio [SMM/WT]) was assessed via bioelectrical impedance analysis. Multiple linear regression examined independent associations, adjusting for age, sex, and BMI z-score. Moderation and mediation analyses tested whether BMI z-score moderated the SMM/WT-symptom link, and whether metabolic markers mediated the SMM/WT-inattention association. Results Higher SMM/WT was independently associated with lower inattention (β = -0.017, p = .041) and hyperactivity-impulsivity scores (β = -0.018, p = .031). Greater VFA was linked to lower hyperactivity-impulsivity (β = -0.002, p = .048) and oppositional defiant symptoms (β = -0.003, p = .011). BMI z-score showed no independent association with any symptom domain and did not moderate the SMM/WT-symptom relationship (all interaction p > .05). None of the metabolic markers (HDL-C, ALT, AST, fasting glucose) significantly mediated the SMM/WT-inattention association (all indirect effect 95% CIs included zero). Conclusions Specific body composition components show distinct, symptom-domain-specific associations in children with ADHD, independent of overall weight status. The inverse association of skeletal muscle mass with inattention was not explained by the metabolic markers examined.

Background Attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a prevalent neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by inattention, hyperactivity, restlessness and impaired impulse control. It represents a major global challenge in pediatric mental health, ranking as the 6th leading cause of years lived with disability (YLDs) among children aged 5–14 years [ 1 , 2 ]. ADHD exhibits a high prevalence among children and adolescents, affecting approximately 8.0% globally, with boys nearly twice as likely to be affected as girls [ 3 , 4 ], leading to significant implications for long-term academic, social and psychological outcomes [ 5 ]. Beyond its core diagnostic symptoms, ADHD is increasingly recognized as a systemic condition that frequently co-occurs with a broad spectrum of physical and mental health phenotypes, as demonstrated in an umbrella review encompassing over 234 million participants [ 6 ]. Among these co-occurring conditions, the link between ADHD and obesity has garnered substantial research interest in recent decades [ 7 – 9 ]. Specifically, children and adolescents with ADHD exhibit a 56% increased likelihood of overweight or obesity compared to neurotypical peers [ 10 ], and evidence suggests an elevated risk for early metabolic syndrome and related complications in this population [ 7 , 8 ]. Notably, subclinical ADHD symptoms, which do not meet full diagnostic criteria but remain functionally impairing, are common, affecting at least a further 5% of children and adolescents [ 4 ], and are increasingly linked to adverse lifestyle behaviors and early metabolic risks [ 10 , 11 ], thereby underscoring the public health importance of investigating this association across the full spectrum of symptom expression. Building on this foundation, research has begun to shift from merely establishing an ADHD-obesity correlation to elucidating the associations with specific aspects of body composition. For instance, emerging evidence, including our prior work, suggests that visceral adiposity, a metabolically active fat depot, may be particularly relevant in children with ADHD and co-occurring obesity [ 12 ]. Concurrently, a growing body of literature highlights the potential protective role of skeletal muscle mass against cognitive deficits and neurodevelopmental challenges through mechanisms involving myokine secretion and improved metabolic regulation [ 13 – 15 ]. However, a critical limitation persists in much of the existing literature: ADHD is predominantly treated as a categorical entity. This categorical approach may obscure important, symptom-specific relationships, given that core ADHD dimensions possess distinct neurobiological and behavioral underpinnings [ 16 – 19 ]. In contrast, a dimensional approach conceptualizes ADHD symptoms as continuously distributed traits across the population, rather than a binary categorical diagnosis [ 20 , 21 ]. Accordingly, the present study investigates symptom-specific correlates, acknowledging that core ADHD dimensions may exhibit heterogeneous associations with metrics such as visceral adiposity and skeletal muscle mass. To address this gap, we conducted a retrospective analysis within a cohort of children undergoing routine health examinations. This study aims to dissect the associations between dimensional ADHD symptom scores and body composition, with a specific focus on visceral fat area and skeletal muscle mass relative to body weight. By adopting this nuanced, symptom-domain-specific approach, our study seeks to provide a more refined understanding of the associations between body composition and ADHD symptomatology. All participants were drawn from a community-based preventive care cohort; none were referred for ADHD evaluation or psychiatric consultation. The initial inclusion criterion was the availability of complete data for body composition analysis, the Swanson, Nolan, and Pelham Rating Scale-IV (SNAP-IV) parent-rated scale [ 22 , 23 ], and basic demographic information. We applied the following exclusion criteria to define our final analytical sample: (1) underweight (body mass index [BMI] z-score ≤ -2 standard deviations [SD] based on the World Health Organization growth standards) [ 24 ], as underweight in children is frequently associated with underlying chronic diseases, malnutrition, or eating disorders that independently alter body composition and may compromise neurobehavioral functioning [ 25 , 26 ]; (2) a known diagnosis of any neurodevelopmental or psychiatric condition; (3) presence of chronic medical conditions affecting growth, metabolism, or neurodevelopment; (4) current or historical use of psychotropic medications; and (5) missing blood biochemical parameters. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Children’s Hospital (2025-027-D), and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.
