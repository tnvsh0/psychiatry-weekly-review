### Sex differences in the efficacy, acceptability, and tolerability of antipsychotics in people with acute schizophrenia: individual participant data pairwise, dose-response, and network meta-analyses
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The Lancet Psychiatry (Lancet Psychiatry) *(IF: 64.3)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Furukawa Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 30

BACKGROUND: Antipsychotics are the mainstay of schizophrenia treatment, but their efficacy and tolerability might differ between females and males, and previous evidence on sex differences has been inconsistent and methodologically limited. We aimed to evaluate sex differences in the efficacy, acceptability, and tolerability of antipsychotics in adults with acute schizophrenia.

METHODS: We performed individual participant data pairwise, dose-response, and network meta-analyses of antipsychotics versus placebo or another antipsychotic, using data from the Vivli and YODA trial data-sharing platforms (inception to April 9, 2025); the main analyses were restricted to fixed-dose trials. The primary outcome was overall symptoms on the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale at week 6 (or closest). Secondary outcomes included all-cause dropout and other efficacy and tolerability outcomes. We performed one-stage pairwise meta-analyses to quantitatively test sex-treatment interactions, and two-stage sex-stratified dose-response and network meta-analyses to visually examine potential sex differences. Credibility of effect modification was assessed with the Instrument to assess the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses. People with lived experience were involved in planning and interpretation of the study. This study was registered with PROSPERO, CRD420251022957.

FINDINGS: We identified 33 eligible trials; 18 fixed-dose trials with 7062 participants (2168 females and 4894 males; of 5161 with race reported, 2954 White, 1187 Black, 752 Asian, and 268 of other or unspecified race; mean age 40·9 years; receiving haloperidol, olanzapine, paliperidone, risperidone, or placebo) were included in the main analyses. There was no evidence of sex differences in drug effects on overall symptoms (difference of standardised mean difference [DoSMD] 0·04, 95% CI -0·09 to 0·18). Dose-response curves were similar in females and males, plateauing at 3-5 mg risperidone equivalents, and the network meta-analysis showed no clear sex-specific treatment effects. Antipsychotics reduced all-cause dropout more in females than in males (ratio of odds ratios 0·74, 95% CI 0·56-0·98; moderate credibility). Prolactin increase was greater in females (DoSMD 0·83, 0·67-0·99; high credibility); other tolerability outcomes showed no clear sex difference.

INTERPRETATION: We found no evidence of sex differences in the efficacy or overall adverse event profiles, and no different dose-response relationships, for the drugs studied. However, we found some evidence that, among females, antipsychotics reduce all-cause dropout more, and increase antipsychotic-induced prolactin elevation versus placebo, as compared with males. Overall treatment efficacy estimates from previous meta-analyses are relevant to both sexes. To promote sex and gender equity, female participation in clinical trials and sex-stratified reporting should be encouraged.

### Brain Stimulation Therapeutic Interventions in Neuropsychiatry: A Cambrian Explosion
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Sackeim HA, George MS | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Brain stimulation interventions alter neural activity by applying electrical, magnetic, sonic, or other forms of energy to the nervous system, using invasive or noninvasive methods. In recent years, the basic science and clinical science of neuromodulation have markedly advanced, and the evidence for clinical utility has resulted in new U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance for multiple forms of brain stimulation and multiple indications in neuropsychiatry. This review summarizes the status of the major brain stimulation interventions for neuropsychiatric disorders, emphasizing key strengths and challenges in their therapeutic application. It also discusses opportunities for additional breakthroughs in treatment in this rapidly advancing field. These topics include noninvasive techniques for focal intracerebral drug delivery that bypass the adverse effects linked to systemic pharmacologic administration; interventions that noninvasively stimulate deep or superficial brain targets without impacting surrounding tissue; interventions that, in addition to or instead of altering symptomatic expression, reduce stress sensitivity, enhance resilience, and prevent manifestation of neuropsychiatric disorders; and the development of responsive or closed-loop stimulation techniques whereby the timing and nature of the stimulation are adjusted based on personalized biomarkers of future pathological brain states. Finally, noninvasive brain stimulation interventions intended for home use have recently shown clinical utility and have received FDA clearance. The field of brain stimulation represents a paradigm shift that is revolutionizing therapeutics and mechanistic understanding in neuropsychiatry.

### Artificial Intelligence as the Computational Framework for Adaptive Closed-Loop Neuromodulation in Psychiatry
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Mahmoudi B | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Adaptive neuromodulation offers a path toward personalized neuropsychiatric treatment by allowing therapy to respond to clinically meaningful neurophysiological and behavioral changes. Its clearest clinical precedents are responsive neurostimulation for epilepsy and adaptive deep brain stimulation for movement disorders. Both rely on signals that can be measured reliably and linked directly to stimulation. Psychiatric disorders pose a different problem: symptoms are heterogeneous, evolve over multiple timescales, and are observed only indirectly through noisy and incomplete measurements. Artificial intelligence can provide data-driven components of an adaptive neuromodulation system, connecting multimodal sensing, inference of patient state, prediction of treatment response, and constrained therapeutic adaptation. Interpretability is required throughout to support clinician and patient trust. Clinical evidence, however, remains limited to small proof-of-concept studies. Translation to practice will require replicated biomarkers and prospective multisite validation. It will also require explicit management of uncertainty and system designs that match adaptation to symptom and treatment timescales. Clinician and patient oversight must be preserved throughout, alongside safety constraints and governance across the treatment life cycle. This review presents a system-design view of how these components combine into a single adaptive architecture for psychiatric neuromodulation.

### Endocannabinoid plasma concentrations and their relationship with clinical outcome and cannabis use in antipsychotic-naïve first episode psychosis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Psychiatry Res *(IF: 3.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Martinez-Sadurni L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 28

Disturbances in the endocannabinoid system (ECS) have been reported in psychosis, with evidence suggesting consistent alterations in N-arachidonoylethanolamine (anandamide; AEA) and inconclusive findings for 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG). Lower AEA concentrations have been associated with greater symptom severity, while the relationship between ECS and cannabis use remains unclear. However, the prognostic value of peripheral endocannabinoid concentrations for clinical outcomes and cannabis use in first-episode psychosis (FEP) remains largely unknown. This study examined whether baseline plasma endocannabinoid concentrations are associated with clinical outcomes and cannabis use at one-year follow-up in antipsychotic-naïve patients with FEP. Plasma AEA and 2-AG concentrations were measured in 58 antipsychotic-naïve patients. Psychopathology was assessed using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), and cannabis use was evaluated using Standard Joint Units to estimate THC consumption. Associations were examined using correlation and regression analyses adjusted for relevant covariates. Higher baseline AEA concentrations were associated with lower general psychopathology scores at follow-up. No significant associations were observed between endocannabinoid concentrations and total PANSS scores or positive and negative symptom subscales. Age emerged as a significant predictor of cannabis use at follow-up, while 2-AG showed a non-significant trend toward a higher likelihood of use. These findings suggest that higher baseline plasma AEA concentrations may be associated with a more favourable course of general psychopathology in early psychosis, potentially reflecting ECS involvement in stress- and anxiety-related symptom domains. Further studies are warranted to clarify the role of endocannabinoids in clinical outcomes and substance use trajectories in early phases of psychosis.

### The burden of suicide by paraquat poisoning in Taiwan (1987-2017): trends, geographic variations, and demographic characteristics
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Public Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Chang HY et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 2

Background Pesticide self-poisoning accounts for 14%–20% of global suicides. Paraquat, a highly lethal pesticide, is involved in a considerable number of suicides in some countries. This study investigated the burden, trends, geographic variations, and demographic characteristics of pesticide and paraquat suicides in Taiwan. Methods Suicide data (1987, 1992, 1997, and 2002–2017) were obtained from Taiwan’s cause-of-death mortality data files. Trends and geographic variations in pesticide and paraquat suicides were examined using graphs and maps. Logistic regression was used to investigate the differences in the characteristics of pesticide versus non-pesticide suicides and paraquat versus non-paraquat pesticide suicides. Results Age-standardised pesticide suicide rates decreased by 74%, from 7.7 to 2.0 per 100,000, in 1987–2017. Paraquat was the leading agent involved in pesticide suicides, accounting for 59.9% (2,268/3,786) of pesticide suicides where the pesticide agents involved were recorded on the death certificate and at least 4.0% (2,268/56,361) of overall suicides in Taiwan. The percentage of paraquat suicides in overall suicides varied markedly across cities/counties (0.4%–15.6%). Pesticide suicides were more likely to be male, older people aged 65 + years, and from rural areas than non-pesticide suicides. By contrast, paraquat suicides tended to be younger than non-paraquat pesticide suicides, with no difference in sex distributions in the two groups. Conclusions The ban on paraquat implemented in 2018–2020 would have a significant impact on pesticide suicides and overall suicides, particularly in high-risk areas and demographic groups. Past research suggested marked variations in pesticide suicide rates over time [ 2 , 3 ], across different regions within a country [ 4 – 6 ], and across individuals of various demographic and socioeconomic characteristics [ 7 ]. Globally, the majority of pesticide suicides occur in the rural areas of countries in South East Asia and the Western Pacific region [ 1 , 8 ]. Pesticide self-poisoning is also among the most commonly used methods for suicide in Taiwan, with pesticides being the leading agents involved in poisoning-related suicides [ 9 ]. Pesticides commonly used in suicide vary markedly in case fatality [ 10 ]. Paraquat, a commonly used herbicide, is among the most lethal pesticides involved in self-poisonings [ 11 ], with a case-fatality ratio exceeding 70% in some studies [ 12 , 13 ]. Paraquat was found to be the most frequently ingested pesticide in South Korea and Taiwan, accounting for 35.5% and 34.7% of pesticide poisonings in previous hospital-based studies in South Korea (2008) and Taiwan (2012–2019), respectively [ 4 , 14 ]. However, few previous studies investigated the epidemiology of paraquat suicides based on population-based nationwide samples. Product-specific analyses of the characteristics of people who died by pesticide self-poisoning could inform prevention strategies targeted at regulating certain highly hazardous pesticides (HHPs), such as paraquat, and regulating or banning HHPs was recommended by the World Health Organization and Food and Agriculture Organization as among the most effective suicide prevention strategies [ 15 , 16 ]. In South Korea, for instance, the age-standardised rate of pesticide suicide fell annually by 28.2% between 2011 and 2014 following the ban on paraquat in 2011–2012 [ 2 ]. A systematic review of 27 studies from 16 countries showed that national bans on HHPs commonly used in self-poisonings appeared to be effective in reducing pesticide-specific and overall suicide rates, whilst the evidence for an effect of restricting HHPs sales to authorised users was less consistent [ 17 ]. ### The severity of dissociation and dissociative phenomena in non-affective psychosis: a systematic review and meta-analyses
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** O'Neill A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Aug 19

Background Non-affective psychosis is associated with elevated risk of suicide, self-harm, and reduced life expectancy. Symptoms of dissociation are common in non-affective psychosis, and dissociative experiences are known to be linked to multiple markers of elevated clinical risk, including sleep disturbance and negative affect. The present review represents the first comprehensive quantification of dissociative phenomena severity across multiple instruments in non-affective psychosis. Methods Six databases (Embase, MEDLINE, PsycINFO, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) were searched from inception and systematically screened. Eligibility was established against ICD/DSM criteria or service-defined categorisation, including first-episode psychosis, brief limited intermittent psychotic symptoms, and at-risk of psychosis mental states. In total, data were extracted for 22 measures. Four random effects meta-analyses were conducted for measures with three or more independent datasets: the Dissociative Experiences Scale (DES) ( k = 51; n = 3,878); the Cambridge Depersonalization Scale (CDS) ( k = 6; n = 351); the Examination of Anomalous Self Experience scale, using the dichotomous scoring method (EASE-D) ( k = 11; n = 340); and the Examination of Anomalous Self Experience scale, using the continuous scoring method (EASE-C) ( k = 6; n = 235). Results DES data yielded a pooled mean of 21.28 (95% CI 19.19–23.38), consistent with moderately elevated dissociation. CDS data yielded a pooled mean of 36.38 (95% CI 26.89–45.87), also raised relative to community norms. EASE-D pooled means (M = 17.76 95% CI 15.29–20.24) and EASE-C pooled means ( M = 74.86 95% CI 52.36–97.35) both indicated high and clinically relevant loads of basic self-disturbance. High heterogeneity (I 2 > 85%) across measures highlights considerable variability in dissociative severity across samples, suggesting that levels of dissociation and dissociative experiences are not uniform in populations who experience non-affective psychosis. Conclusions The present findings emphasise the importance of assessing dissociation in non-affective psychosis, and where necessary, intervening clinically. Nonetheless, the development of trauma-informed, dissociation-targeted clinical interventions is required to optimise approaches to support. Throughout the literature, there is a pressing need for more harmonised measurement efforts and further mechanistic research. Introduction Non-affective psychosis is an umbrella term which refers to a cluster of mental health conditions characterised by atypical perception, thought, and affect [ 1 ]. Contemporary diagnostic systems [ 2 , 3 ] describe non-affective psychosis in terms of positive symptoms , such as hallucinations, delusions, and disorganised thinking; negative symptoms , including apathy, anhedonia, and social withdrawal; and cognitive symptoms , such as inattention and memory difficulties. Unlike affective psychoses, non-affective psychoses are defined by the absence of a primary mood component [ 4 ]. Schizophrenia and related non-affective diagnoses, such as schizoaffective disorder and delusional disorder, are considered prototypical non-affective psychotic disorders, and the term schizophrenia spectrum disorder (SSD), covering all those conditions, is often employed interchangeably with non-affective psychosis [ 5 ]. In clinical settings, early intervention for psychosis services and community mental health teams support individuals diagnosed with SSD, and also those experiencing first episode psychosis (FEP), brief limited intermittent psychotic symptoms (BLIPS), or who present with ultra-high-risk (UHR) indicators for developing psychosis [ 6 ]. Notably, non-affective psychosis is associated with profound psychosocial and functional impairment [ 7 , 8 ], as well as markedly elevated risk of suicide, self-harm, reduced life expectancy, and excess premature mortality [ 9 – 11 ]. Against this background, there has been growing interest in the role of dissociative phenomena in the aetiology and phenomenology of non-affective psychosis, as well as their influence on outcomes [ 12 , 13 ]. As a concept, dissociation is difficult to define [ 14 ], however, it has been found to occur across multiple psychiatric disorders [ 15 ] and appears to be characterised by certain core components, including: derealisation , where a person feels the world is unreal; depersonalisation , where a person has feelings of being outside of themselves or somehow separated from their experiences; dissociative amnesia , characterised by gaps in memory; identity confusion , where a person finds it difficult to define themselves; and identity alteration , where a person switches noticeably between different aspects of their personality [ 16 – 19 ]. In non-affective psychosis specifically, recent work has conceptualised the core subjective experience of dissociation as a “felt sense of anomaly” (FSA) [ 20 , 21 ]. The investigating authors define this as “a subjective sense of strangeness” [ 21 ] which may take the form of unreality, disconnection, automaticity, or unfamiliarity, and occurs within one or more domains such as perception, identity, the body, and cognitions. Historically, SSDs and dissociative disorders have been framed as distinct diagnostic categories [ 13 ]. Nonetheless, systematic review evidence demonstrates substantial overlap, with symptoms of dissociation frequently present in SSDs, and the positive and negative symptoms typically associated with SSDs also commonly observed in dissociative disorders [ 13 ]. In parallel, dissociative experiences appear to be elevated in schizophrenia relative to healthy controls [ 17 ], and quantitative syntheses have provided evidence that dissociation is closely linked to the occurrence of psychotic symptoms. In one meta-analysis [ 22 ], a medium sized association between dissociative experiences and positive psychotic symptoms was reported ( r = 0.437), with significant symptom-specific relationships found for hallucinations ( r = 0.461), delusions ( r = 0.418), and paranoia ( r = 0.447). These findings extend earlier work focusing on auditory verbal hallucinations, which found a large, pooled correlation between voice hearing and dissociation across clinical and non-clinical samples ( r = 0.52) [ 18 ]. Together, this evidence supports that dissociative processes are not only common in people who experience psychosis but appear to be particularly and selectively related to positive symptoms. Converging evidence also links dissociation in psychosis to adverse childhood experiences. A meta-analysis of this relationship in severe mental illness [ 19 ] found a medium strength association between exposure to traumatic experiences in childhood and dissociation ( r = 0.33), with the association being strongest in SSD ( r = 0.39). All examined forms of adversity, including physical, sexual, and emotional abuse and neglect, were positively associated with dissociation, with emotional abuse emerging as a particularly robust correlate ( r = 0.41 in SSD). Recent psychosis-specific research replicated this finding [ 23 ], showing exactly the same level of medium association between developmental trauma and dissociation ( r = 0.33). This pattern is consistent with broader research exploring the link between trauma and psychosis [ 24 ], with recent evidence demonstrating a dose-response relationship between cumulative childhood adversity and psychosis risk [ 25 ]. These empirical findings have stimulated theoretical models in which dissociation is positioned as a key psychological process mediating developmental trauma and psychosis.
