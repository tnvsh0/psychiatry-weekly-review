Stibbard-Hawkes and von Rueden highlight consensus-based decision-making as a key process underlying egalitarianism. I argue that this terminology remains unnecessarily imprecise and can be potentially misleading. In line with the authors' own argument, I underline the need to focus on  deliberation  instead and provide several reasons why this could further both the study of egalitarianism and that of deliberation.

### Understanding "societies of near-equals"
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Behavioral and Brain Sciences (Behav Brain Sci) *(IF: 20.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Codding BF | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 2

Egalitarian societies are defined as those of near-equals, yet some have confused the concept to be analogous with equality and used the absence of evidence of truly equal societies to reject the concept altogether. Stibbard-Hawkes and von Rueden seek to correct course, highlighting that egalitarian societies, properly defined, do exist and that by focusing on process, we can come to learn about the factors that influence variation in social organization. Their synthesis provides a nuanced image of egalitarian societies and offers direction for future research focused on examining the emergence of egalitarian relations out of the interactions between individual decisions and local environmental and social contexts. Such a program has and will continue to illuminate the diversity of ways societies organize, identify general processes, and provide insights into factors that promote opportunities for individuals to live among near-equals.

### Metabolism: A constraint and driver of neurodevelopment
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Behavioral and Brain Sciences (Behav Brain Sci) *(IF: 20.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Siddiqui M, Jones EJH | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 2

This commentary extends recent accounts of metabolic constraints in cognitive modelling by considering their role in brain development. We argue that shifting energy availability both constrains and drives the emergence of functional specialisation and network organisation. Integrating interactive specialisation, we show how metabolic factors shape competitive and cooperative interactions, and how atypical energy metabolism may contribute to neurodivergent developmental trajectories.

### What predicts expertise in Baduk? Intelligence, practice, and a natural test of the range restriction hypothesis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Experimental Psychology: General (J Exp Psychol Gen) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Southwick DA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 28

The range restriction hypothesis suggests that intelligence-performance correlations may be underestimated in domains like chess, where most players have above-average cognitive ability. We test this hypothesis using Baduk (Go), a game of even greater complexity but, apparently, without the range restrictions in intelligence typically observed in chess. In Study 1A, experts reported significantly more serious practice alone than amateurs ( d  = 1.42), but scored  lower  in fluid intelligence ( d  = -1.78). In Study 1B, we invited participants back to complete a revised matrix reasoning task. We preregistered that we would observe the same finding with the new measure and, again, experts scored worse in fluid intelligence than amateurs ( d  = -1.79). However, this effect was no longer significant when controlling for education-suggesting that the observed intelligence gap may reflect developmental trade-offs rather than a true cognitive disadvantage among experts. Rather than fluid intelligence driving expertise, time investments in schooling versus practice appear to shape both cognitive scores and Baduk skill. ### Pigs as large-animal models for translational behavioral neuroscience
**כתב עת:** 📄 Behavioral Neuroscience (Behav Neurosci) *(IF: 3.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lorenzo Gonzalez AP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Model choice in behavioral neuroscience is often driven by convenience and established research pipelines, contributing to a persistent reliance on murine species. Although rodents are essential for discovery, their limited alignment with human brain architecture and clinical technologies can constrain translational inference. Here, we argue that the pig ( Sus scrofa ) is an underutilized large-animal model with substantial translational value due to its gyrencephalic brain, human-relevant physiology, and compatibility with clinical imaging and neurotechnology. We review evidence that pigs can support rigorous cognitive and behavioral testing, including spatial learning, recognition memory, and executive function, and we frame pigs as a cross-species replication and scaling platform for testing the generality of canonical brain-behavior relationships. We also outline ethical challenges, practical barriers, and infrastructure considerations relevant to laboratories adopting porcine behavioral neuroscience, including housing, handling, monitoring, regulatory oversight, surgical support, and growth-related design constraints. ### Age-related reversals in the neural support of emotional memory vividness: New evidence for preparatory prefrontal activity during memory search
**כתב עת:** 📄 Behavioral Neuroscience (Behav Neurosci) *(IF: 3.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Faul L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 28

Previous research identified age-related changes in the parametric modulation of neural activity by emotional memory vividness. Within the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex (PFC), older adults showed a stronger relationship between neural recruitment and positive memory vividness, whereas younger adults showed a stronger relationship for negative memories. More work is needed to corroborate these Age × Valence interactions while clarifying if they are uniquely sensitive to vividness ratings made after an instructed elaboration period or predictive of how vividness ratings might change from initial recognition to postelaboration. The present study assessed a new sample of participants ( n  = 60, ages 18-87) who completed a nearly identical paradigm as in previous work. Participants encoded emotional images paired with neutral titles and, during scanned retrieval, were presented with only the neutral titles in an old/new recognition task. Remembered titles were rated on the initial vividness of recollection for the paired image and a second rating of vividness after instructed elaboration. Results confirmed prefrontally mediated alterations to emotional memory phenomenology across the adult lifespan, evidenced by age-related increases in the relationship between PFC activity and positive memory vividness. During initial memory recognition, greater dorsomedial PFC activity among older adults predicted a subsequent increase in positive memory vividness after elaboration. Opposite patterns emerged in posterior sensorimotor and occipital regions that deactivated more strongly for vivid positive memories among older adults. These findings corroborate prior evidence of Age × Valence interactions in parametric modulation by vividness while also providing new evidence for a preparatory role of the PFC in shaping elaboration outcomes.
