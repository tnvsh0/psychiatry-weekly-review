### Multimodal Machine Learning-Based Prediction of Relapse in Major Depressive Disorder
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Teutenberg L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

Key Points Question Does machine learning–based integration of multiple data modalities, relative to single data modalities, improve relapse prediction in major depressive disorder? Findings In this prognostic study, depressive relapse within 2 years was predicted among 564 patients with major depressive disorder, with a highest classification accuracy of 64.43%. The best-performing classification model relied solely on 1 modality including few clinical features; integrating additional modalities of structural and functional neuroimaging, genetics, neurocognition, blood markers, and gut microbiome did not improve relapse prediction. Meaning Multimodal models did not improve prediction beyond a unimodal clinical model, while overall prognostic value for individual-level relapse remained limited.

Importance Early identification of patients at high risk of relapse is key to personalized preventive strategies in major depressive disorder (MDD). However, current predictive models do not adequately account for the multimodal factors contributing to MDD relapse and fail to provide clinically meaningful predictions. Objective To predict relapse within 2 years in patients with MDD by using a comprehensive machine learning pipeline that integrates an unprecedented set of multimodal data. Design, Setting, and Participants This naturalistic longitudinal prognostic study used data from the Marburg-Münster Affective Disorders Cohort Study, with baseline assessments collected from October 2014 to May 2019 and follow-up approximately 2 years later, between October 2016 and June 2021. Data analysis was conducted between July 2024 and June 2026. The study was performed at 2 sites in Germany (Marburg and Münster), with participants recruited from psychiatric inpatient and outpatient departments and the general population. Patients meeting diagnostic criteria for current or lifetime MDD without history of neurological or severe somatic conditions were included. Exposure Natural course of illness in patients with MDD. Main Outcomes and Measures Balanced accuracy (BACC) for binary prediction of relapse vs no relapse during 2-year follow-up served as the primary outcome. Baseline features comprised 9 modalities including clinical and psychometric data, 4 structural and functional magnetic resonance imaging modalities, genetics, neurocognition, blood markers, and gut microbiome. Results A total of 564 patients with a current or lifetime MDD diagnosis were included (367 female [65.1%]; mean [SD] age, 37.34 [13.38] years). A total of 270 patients relapsed during the 2-year follow-up, while 294 did not. Mean (SD) BACC for the prediction of relapse ranged from 47.21% (5.85%) to 64.43% (6.63%). The best-performing model was solely based on few clinical relapse risk factors, while multimodal data integration did not improve performance. Conclusions and Relevance In this prognostic study, the models achieved moderate accuracy, yet the results suggest limited scope for guiding individual-level clinical decision-making. Predictive performance was largely driven by established clinical risk factors, indicating limited incremental value of multimodal measures when assessed at a single time point. Future advances may instead benefit from dynamic modeling approaches that more closely align temporally evolving multimodal markers with longitudinal clinical outcomes.

This prognostic study evaluates whether relapse within 2 years can be predicted among patients with major depressive disorder by using a comprehensive machine learning pipeline that integrates an unprecedented set of multimodal data.

### Media-based surveillance of suicide in Türkiye: a mixed-methods analysis of epidemiological patterns, homicide-suicide events, and contextual factors
**כתב עת:** 📄 Front Public Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Uçar MT et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background Suicide remains a major public health concern worldwide, yet official mortality statistics often provide limited information regarding the contextual circumstances surrounding suicide events. Media reports provide insights into demographic characteristics, suicide methods, homicide–suicide incidents, and the social contexts associated with these events. This study aimed to examine suicide cases reported in Turkish media using a mixed-methods approach with epidemiological surveillance and qualitative document analysis. Methods A convergent mixed-methods design was employed. Suicide-related news reports published in Turkish media between January and December 2025 were prospectively monitored and screened. After removing duplicate reports, 688 suicide cases were included in the quantitative analysis. Descriptive and inferential statistical analyses were conducted to examine demographic characteristics, suicide methods, geographic distribution, and homicide–suicide patterns. Qualitative document analysis was performed on contextual narratives from 179 suicide cases. Quantitative and qualitative findings were integrated during interpretation. Results Among the 688 media-reported suicide cases, 76.7% were male, 15.7% were female, and sex was not reported for the remaining 7.6%. Firearms (43.2%) and hanging (30.4%) were the most frequently reported suicide methods. Homicide–suicide was identified in 16.1% of cases and was significantly more common among males than females (20.3% vs. 2.8%, unadjusted OR = 8.90, 95% CI: 2.77–28.58, p < 0.001). Suicide method was significantly associated with homicide–suicide status ( p < 0.001). Among suicides involving firearms, 30.6% were classified as homicide–suicide. Within the media-reported sample, the proportion of cases classified as homicide–suicide differed significantly across regions. Qualitative analysis identified one descriptive event/legal category—violence, homicide–suicide event characteristics, and legal circumstances (31.8%)—and four contextual themes: physical and psychological health problems and addictions (26.3%); family and intimate relationship conflicts (24.6%); economic and occupational difficulties (15.6%); and social exclusion and social vulnerability (1.7%). Integration of findings indicated that homicide–suicide events were frequently embedded within narratives of domestic violence, relationship conflict, and legal problems, whereas psychological distress and economic hardship emerged as common contextual factors across suicide cases. Conclusion Media-reported suicide cases in Türkiye demonstrated distinct epidemiological and contextual patterns that should be interpreted within the context of media reporting rather than as representative of all suicide deaths in the country. Combining epidemiological surveillance with qualitative analysis provides complementary contextual information that may inform suicide prevention policies, responsible media reporting practices, and future research integrating official mortality data.

1 Introduction Suicide remains one of the most significant public health challenges worldwide, accounting for substantial mortality, disability, and social burden across populations. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 727,000 people died by suicide in 2021, representing more than one in every 100 deaths globally. Suicide is also among the leading causes of death among young people aged 15–29 years, highlighting its profound impact on individuals, families, communities, and healthcare systems ( 1 , 2 ). Despite advances in mental health care and suicide prevention initiatives, suicide continues to pose a major global health concern, reflecting the complex interaction of individual, social, economic, and environmental factors. Suicide is increasingly conceptualized as a public health outcome influenced not only by psychiatric disorders but also by broader social determinants of health, including unemployment, income insecurity, housing conditions, childhood adversity, social exclusion, and access to healthcare services ( 3 , 4 ). Although mental disorders remain among the strongest risk factors for suicidal behavior, growing evidence suggests that broader structural factors—including socioeconomic disadvantage, unemployment, debt burden, social isolation, relationship breakdown, exposure to violence, and limited access to support services—also play critical roles in suicide risk ( 3 , 5 ). Recent studies have highlighted the role of socioeconomic disadvantages, occupational vulnerability, and financial hardship in shaping suicide risk. Men experiencing socioeconomic adversity appear to be at particularly elevated risk, while debt burden has been consistently associated with suicidality and poor mental health outcomes ( 6 , 7 ). Consequently, understanding suicide requires approaches that extend beyond individual-level explanations and incorporate the broader social and contextual environments in which suicidal behaviors occur. Although global age-standardized suicide rates have declined in many regions over recent decades, important demographic and geographic disparities persist. Men consistently die by suicide at higher rates than women, and considerable variations exist across countries with respect to suicide mortality, age distributions, and associated risk factors ( 8 ). Access to lethal means remains an important determinant of suicide mortality, with firearms, hanging, and poisoning among the most commonly reported methods worldwide ( 2 ). These variations underscore the importance of country-specific surveillance systems and context-sensitive suicide prevention strategies that are informed by local epidemiological patterns and social contexts ( 2 ). In Türkiye, suicide has emerged as an increasingly important public health issue. According to the Turkish Statistical Institute (TURKSTAT), 4,460 individuals died by suicide in 2024, corresponding to a crude suicide rate of 5.22 per 100,000 population. Like global trends, males account for the majority of suicide deaths in Türkiye. Previous national studies have identified significant associations between suicide mortality and factors such as gender inequality, socioeconomic conditions, family structure, and regional disparities. Furthermore, recent research has emphasized the growing importance of suicide prevention among children and adolescents, drawing attention to the need for evidence-based interventions tailored to different population groups ( 9–11 ). Recent analysis of official Turkish Statistical Institute (TURKSTAT) mortality data (2007–2022) demonstrated sex-specific trends in suicide mortality, with increasing rates among males and a recent increase among females after 2019. The same study also reported rising suicide mortality among young adults aged 15–29 years ( 12 ). Reliable surveillance systems constitute a cornerstone of effective suicide prevention. Official mortality statistics provide essential information regarding suicide rates, demographic characteristics, and temporal trends ( 2 ). However, these databases often provide limited information regarding the social and contextual circumstances surrounding suicide events. Factors such as interpersonal conflicts, intimate partner violence, economic difficulties, legal problems, family disputes, and other precipitating life stressors are often not systematically documented in routine mortality statistics, highlighting the value of complementary data sources such as media reports ( 13 , 14 ). In recent years, media reports have increasingly been recognized as a valuable supplementary source of information for suicide surveillance. News reports frequently contain detailed descriptions of the circumstances surrounding suicide events, including information regarding family relationships, economic stressors, legal issues, mental health concerns, and social contexts that are often unavailable in official datasets. ### The effectiveness of meaning-based interventions in reducing suicide-related vulnerability: A systematic review
**כתב עת:** 📄 Palliat Support Care | **סוג מחקר:** סקירה שיטתית | **מחברים:** Gwon TK, Jung HO | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

OBJECTIVES: Existential factors such as loss of meaning play a central role in suicide-related vulnerability, yet most prevention interventions target symptom reduction. We aimed to systematically review the effects of meaning-based interventions on suicide-related outcomes and characterize the mechanisms through which these effects operate.

METHODS: A systematic review was conducted following PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Six databases (PubMed, Web of Science, CINAHL, PsycINFO, Research Information Sharing Service, and Korean Studies Information Service System) were searched from inception to April 2026. Studies were included if they evaluated meaning-based interventions and reported suicide-related outcomes. Risk of bias was assessed using RoB 2 for randomized trials and RoBANS for nonrandomized studies. Given heterogeneity across populations, interventions, and outcomes, findings were synthesized narratively by domain.

RESULTS: Ten studies were included (7 randomized controlled trials, 2 quasi-experimental studies, and 1 secondary analysis). Meaning-based interventions most consistently improved existential outcomes, including meaning in life, spiritual well-being, and quality of life. Emotional outcomes showed partial and heterogeneous effects. Suicide-related outcomes, including hopelessness, demoralization, and suicidal ideation, generally showed reductions, although effects varied across studies. Mechanistically, most studies showed patterns of indirect pathways, whereby improvements in existential variables co-occurred with reductions in emotional and suicide-related outcomes.

SIGNIFICANCE OF RESULTS: Meaning-based interventions may reduce suicide-related vulnerability primarily by enhancing existential resources rather than directly targeting symptoms. These findings support integrating meaning-centered approaches into suicide prevention strategies and highlight the need for longitudinal and mediation-based studies to examine causal mechanisms.
