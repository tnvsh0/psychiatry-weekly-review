### Identification of candidate markers for the isolation of neuron-specific extracellular vesicles
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ter-Ovanesyan D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are present in biofluids and may be useful biomarkers to non-invasively monitor brain cells, but isolating cell type-specific EVs remains challenging. Here we introduce an approach to immuno-isolate EVs from neurons and glia based on protein surface markers. We first selected transmembrane proteins suitable as cell type-specific EV markers based on gene expression and EV proteomic data. We developed a high-purity EV isolation method and cataloged the proteins present on EVs in human cerebrospinal fluid and plasma. We then computationally identified cell type-specific candidate markers for isolating EVs from neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes and microglia. Using ultrasensitive immunoassays against several predicted neuron-specific proteins, we confirmed one marker, NRXN3, as present on EVs in cerebrospinal fluid and plasma. Finally, we developed efficient EV immuno-isolation methods and applied them to isolate NRXN3 +  EVs. Our work provides a general methodology for the isolation of cell type-specific EVs, advancing the goal of using EVs to study brain diseases.

### Spatiotemporal remodeling in habenula marks the onset of depression-related phenotypes
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Molina P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Chronic stress has lasting adverse consequences that can lead, over time, to depression, yet the optimal timeframe for effective therapies remains unclear. The lateral habenula (LHb) has been implicated in stress-related behavioral repercussions, but the precise timing of its contribution is not understood. Here, using spatial transcriptomics, we generated a spatially resolved molecular atlas of the LHb of male mice, revealing a topographically organized and temporally defined remodeling of gene expression after chronic stress. Longitudinal in vivo two-photon calcium imaging of individual LHb neurons revealed progressively heightened excitatory responses to aversive stimuli. These neuronal adaptations mirrored both the progression of transcriptional changes and the emergence of depression-related behavioral phenotypes and were reversed by the antidepressant ketamine. Moreover, chemogenetically limiting LHb neuronal activity or administering ketamine at the onset of these changes exerted antidepressant effects. These findings link spatiotemporal signatures of LHb neuronal ensembles and genetic landscape with the onset of stress-induced depression-related states.

### Distinct patterns of whole-body coding in human motor cortex and posterior parietal cortex
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kadlec K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Traditional views of cortical motor areas hold that movements of different parts of the body, or effectors, are represented in distinct anatomical locations. The extent of overlap in effector representations has been a topic of debate for decades. We recorded from single neurons in hand areas of the motor cortex (MC) and posterior parietal cortex (PPC) of tetraplegic humans while they attempted movements across the body. We found a population response to every movement tested in both areas; however, the hand knob of MC more selectively responded to hand movements, whereas PPC represented all effectors strongly. Single neurons in MC responded selectively to a single effector or to two effectors that were part of the same limb. In PPC, neurons exhibited mixed selectivity, responding to combinations of effectors. These findings suggest a transition from a highly effector-general representation in PPC to regional effector specificity in MC.

### Serum β-synuclein predicts dementia conversion and mortality beyond amyloid-β and phospho-tau
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Oberstein TJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

Synaptic dysfunction is among the earliest pathological events in Alzheimer's disease and among the strongest neuropathological correlates of cognitive impairment, yet blood-based synaptic biomarkers are lacking. Whether serum β-synuclein provides prognostic information independent of established amyloid-β (A; CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio) and tau (T1; CSF pTau181) biomarkers has not been determined. We studied 259 non-demented individuals with subjective or mild cognitive impairment from a single-centre memory clinic, classified by CSF biomarkers using externally validated cut-offs into A-T1- controls (n = 97), A-T1+ PASSED ('pTau and Aβ surge with subtle deterioration', n = 65), and A + T1+ Alzheimer's disease (n = 97), with parallel analyses using Aβ40-normalised pTau181. Endpoints comprised dementia conversion (n = 185, median follow-up 4.1 years), all-cause mortality (n = 258, median 7.0 years), longitudinal MMSE trajectories (n = 185), and regional brain volumes (n = 137). Independence from CSF biomarkers was quantified through systematic effect attenuation analysis. Serum β-synuclein was selectively elevated in the Alzheimer's disease group (Cohen's d = 0.69 vs controls, P < 0.001) but not in PASSED individuals (P = 0.40), and was more strongly associated with CSF pTau181 (r = 0.36) than with the Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio (r = -0.20) across the cohort. Each standard deviation increase was associated with higher risk of dementia conversion (hazard ratio (HR) = 1.40; 95% CI: 1.11, 1.77; P = 0.005), faster cognitive decline (-0.11 SD/year, 95% CI: -0.18, -0.04, P = 0.002, corresponding to -0.20 MMSE points/year), and higher mortality (HR = 1.33; 95% CI: 1.07, 1.65; P = 0.010). For dementia conversion and mortality, adjustment for continuous CSF pTau181 and Aβ42/Aβ40 produced minimal attenuation (≤13%). Both associations were preserved under Aβ40-normalised T1 staging but not against continuous pTau181 normalised to CSF Aβ40. For cognitive decline, β-synuclein's effect remained significant after Aβ42/Aβ40 adjustment (25% attenuation; P = 0.007) but was substantially attenuated by pTau181 (58.5%; P = 0.186). Serum β-synuclein was associated with lower bilateral temporal lobe volume-the only lobar regions to survive hierarchical testing-with sub-regional involvement spanning the hippocampi, entorhinal cortex and parahippocampal gyrus, and explained 5.0-8.9% of unique variance beyond CSF biomarkers. Plasma glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) showed a strong association with β-synuclein, estimated in the PASSED subgroup (β=0.34 per SD, P < 0.001). Serum β-synuclein provided prognostic information largely independent of CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 across all endpoints and of pTau181 for survival outcomes, while sharing substantial prognostic variance with pTau181 for cognitive trajectory, supporting its candidacy as a blood-based synaptic complement to core Alzheimer's disease biomarkers.

### TMEM106B risk converges on C-terminal fragment homeostasis: genetics, mechanisms and therapy
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lastra Osua M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

TMEM106B is a genetic modifier influencing susceptibility to multiple neurodegenerative disorders, including frontotemporal lobar degeneration with TDP-43 pathology (FTLD-TDP), Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and limbic-predominant age-related TDP-43 encephalopathy, as well as contributing to healthy brain aging and neuronal reserve. The gene harbours single nucleotide polymorphisms and structural variants that define risk and protective haplotypes, and despite extensive efforts to identify the functional variant within these haplotypes, consensus remains elusive. In this review, we synthesize current knowledge of TMEM106B genetics from human cohorts and mechanistic studies, highlighting how variants may influence its regulatory network and protein levels, with an emphasis on its aggregation-prone C-terminal domain. We further integrate evidence on the roles of TMEM106B within the endo-lysosomal system and beyond, including insights into protein structure, posttranslational modifications, interactors, proteolytic processing, and fibril formation. These findings are contextualized using data from mouse and induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) models, highlighting current gaps in the available models and the advances required to study its functions and cleavage. Finally, we explore therapeutic strategies targeting TMEM106B, focusing on modulation of expression, specific functional pathways, and fibril formation.

### Prefrontal ensembles represent freezing of conspecifics
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Miranda BA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Social buffering is a process in which the presence of other members of the same species (conspecifics) decreases stress reactions during threat exposure. In parallel, prior reports showed that groups of individuals display synchronous actions in non-threatening situations. However, it is unknown if individuals also synchronize actions when facing threats. Prior studies showed social buffering of freezing in pairs of subjects. We now expand these findings and show social buffering of freezing during exposure to a predator and a shock grid in mice of both sexes in a larger group of four conspecifics. Furthermore, mice also displayed synchronized freezing. The presence of freezing synchrony suggests the existence of a neural representation of conspecific freezing. We reasoned that this encoding might be in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), as this region has been implicated in social behaviors and freezing. Strikingly, mPFC neural activity represented the average level of freezing across conspecifics. MPFC ensembles displayed differential encoding of self and conspecific freezing, and during predator exposure the mPFC encoded the number of conspecifics freezing and conspecific freezing bout duration. These data show, for the first time, neural representation of conspecific freezing, and suggest that the mPFC may coordinate behavioral responses to threat across conspecifics. Significance Statement  Social animals display attenuated threat-elicited responses when encountering threats as a group. However, it is unknown how freezing signals from conspecifics are encoded by the brain when facing threats, as prior work has been limited to studies of pairs, or assays without social interactions. We identify a neural population in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) that represents conspecific freezing during grouped threat exposure, and these cells dynamically represent the duration of other animals' freezing bouts. Strikingly, the activity of these ensembles also represents the number of conspecifics freezing. These findings uncover a previously unknown function of the mPFC to encode conspecific freezing.
