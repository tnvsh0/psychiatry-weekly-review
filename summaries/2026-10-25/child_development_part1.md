### Impacts and costs of a mass media program on children's emotion knowledge, recognition, and regulation in Jordan: A cluster-randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Child Development (Child Dev) *(IF: 5.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Moran C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

We evaluate Ahlan Simsim, an emotional development-focused television show produced by Sesame Workshop and Jordan Pioneers, through a cluster-randomized controlled trial in Jordan preprimary classrooms (n = 216 schools, 4,369 children; endline Mage = 66.9 months; 51% female; 97% Jordanian, 3% Syrian; data collected 2021-2022). Treatment classrooms received daily Ahlan Simsim viewing added to the KG2 curriculum for 12 weeks; control classrooms received the curriculum alone. Significant positive impacts were found on expressive emotion recognition, emotion situation knowledge, and one emotion regulation strategy, breathing, but not receptive emotion recognition or other emotion regulation strategies. Effect sizes (d) range from 0.11 to 0.36. Costs of intervention delivery are estimated at $3-7 per child. Implications for children's media and social-emotional learning interventions are discussed.

We evaluate Ahlan Simsim, a television show for young children produced by Sesame Workshop and Jordan Pioneers and designed to foster children's emotional development. We conducted an impact evaluation in Jordanian government preschool classrooms across three regions of the country. The sample included 4,369 kindergarten-age children from 216 schools (51% female; 97% Jordanian, and 3% Syrian). We evaluated daily Ahlan Simsim viewing added to the country's usual government preschool curriculum for 12 weeks; control classrooms received the government preschool curriculum alone. We found that Ahlan Simsim improved children's expressive emotion recognition, emotion situation knowledge, and one emotion regulation strategy, breathing, but not receptive emotion recognition or other emotion regulation strategies. Costs of intervention delivery are estimated at $3–7 per child. We discuss implications for children's media and social-emotional learning interventions.

### Association of adverse childhood experiences patterns with successful aging trajectories: a prospective cohort study
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Geriatr | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yang K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 24

Background The long-term influence of distinct adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) patterns on heterogeneous trajectories of successful aging (SA) remains unclear. Methods Using 2011–2018 China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study data from 3,092 community-dwelling older adults (aged ≥ 60), latent class analysis identified four distinct ACE patterns from 13 binary indicators based on exposure probabilities, clinical significance, and prior literature. SA was evaluated across five domains: absence of chronic disease, no disability, high cognition, no depressive symptoms, and active social engagement. As SA represents a heterogeneous outcome dynamically evolving over time during aging, group-based trajectory modeling mapped its eight-year trajectories, which were linked to baseline latent ACE classes via logistic regression. Finally, network analysis using the graphical lasso algorithm compared network structure, global strength, edge weights, and node centrality of SA domains between trajectory groups to investigate interaction patterns. Results We identified four ACEs patterns (Low Adversity, 54.2%; Maltreatment, 21.7%; Household Dysfunction, 20.0%; Pervasive Adversity, 4.1%) and two successful aging trajectories (Sustained High-Decline, 22%; Chronically Low-Stable, 78%). Compared to the Low Adversity pattern, the Pervasive Adversity [OR = 1.97, 95% CI (1.10, 3.83)] and Household Dysfunction [OR = 1.36, 95% CI (1.07, 1.74)] patterns were associated with the adverse Chronically Low-Stable trajectory, whereas the Maltreatment pattern showed no significant longitudinal association. Low educational attainment was the strongest predictor of this adverse trajectory [OR = 5.23, 95% CI (3.84, 7.13)]. Network analysis indicated differences in overall network structure between trajectory groups ( p = 0.037) but not in global strength. Specifically, compared to Sustained High-Decline, the Chronically Low-Stable group exhibited a more rigid network with a stronger Cognition-Chronic Disease edge weight ( p < 0.001) but lower Cognition centrality ( p < 0.001). Conclusions Culturally specific early-life adversity patterns, particularly household dysfunction and pervasive adversity, predict adverse successful aging trajectories. These childhood patterns are associated with a more rigid late-life mind-body network characterized by tight pathological coupling between cognition and physical illness. Importantly, broader structural inequalities across the life course, particularly low educational attainment and rural residence, operate alongside early-life trauma as dominant predictors of adverse aging trajectories, highlighting the need for systemic, policy-level interventions. Introduction China is navigating one of the most rapid demographic transitions in human history. According to the “2024 National Report on the Development of Aging Undertakings,” China’s population aged 60 and over reached 310.31 million by the end of 2024, representing 22.0% of the total population [ 1 ]. However, extended lifespan does not inherently equate to extended healthspan. As the sheer volume of the aging population amplifies the societal burden of age-related decline, successful aging (SA) has emerged as a key benchmark for assessing the health of older populations [ 2 ]. Alarmingly, the prevalence of SA among Chinese older adults is only 13.7% [ 3 ], which is substantially lower than the international range of 22% to 24% [ 4 , 5 ]. This disparity suggests that improving the rate of SA and enhancing late-life quality of life are urgent public health priorities in China. Since the introduction of theoretical models of healthy aging, the classic Rowe and Kahn three-component model has defined the core elements of SA as low probability of disease and disability, high cognitive and physical functional capacity, and active engagement with life. However, it has been criticized for its limitations and its narrow biomedical perspective focused solely on the absence of disease and disability, and thus is not a universally accepted definition of SA [ 6 – 8 ]. Nonetheless, utilizing the multidimensional biopsychosocial framework refined from this model across different cultural contexts, which incorporates psychological well-being alongside objective health domains, allows us to more comprehensively explore successful aging within a broader sociological scope. While this foundational model rightly recognized the importance of psychosocial engagement, subsequent clinical realities highlighted the need for an explicit focus on psychological well-being [ 9 ]. Many older adults who fail to achieve SA face a dual disease burden, where physical chronic non-communicable diseases frequently coexist with severe mental health issues like depression [ 10 – 12 ]. Furthermore, depression and social isolation can biologically accelerate physical and cognitive decline through systemic inflammatory and neuroendocrine pathways [ 13 – 15 ]. This evidence demonstrates that late-life health does not operate in isolated components, but rather as a highly interconnected mind-body system. Consequently, SA in this study is comprehensively evaluated across five integrated dimensions: the absence of major chronic diseases, freedom from physical disability, robust cognitive functioning, the absence of depressive symptoms, and active social engagement. Despite this consensus, current SA research exhibits critical methodological limitations. First, studies continue to focus predominantly on Western cohorts [ 16 , 17 ], frequently overlooking China’s unique socio-cultural context and macro-level modulators like urban-rural disparities [ 18 , 19 ]. Second, the majority of existing research operationalizes SA as a static, binary outcome (successful vs. unsuccessful) at a single time point [ 18 ]. This simplistic classification ignores the highly heterogeneous and dynamic nature of SA, failing to capture how mind-body resilience unfolds as diverse longitudinal trajectories in later life. According to the life course perspective, aging is a dynamic lifelong process embedded in socio-historical contexts, shaped by interpersonal relationships and macro-structural forces, rather than a static outcome of personal control [ 20 ]. Therefore, to fundamentally improve these late-life trajectories, it is imperative to trace back to their early-life structural determinants. Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are potentially traumatic events occurring before age 18. They are increasingly recognized as structural determinants of lifelong health [ 21 ]. According to life-course epidemiology, early-life trauma does not merely fade over time. Instead, it leaves enduring physiological changes. This process is conceptualized as the “biological embedding of stress” [ 22 , 23 ]. Repeated exposure to childhood adversity permanently alters the developing nervous and endocrine systems, which leads to a chronic dysregulation of stress hormones like cortisol [ 24 ]. This lifelong wear-and-tear on the body creates a cumulative allostatic load, silently exhausting an individual’s physiological and cognitive reserves long before old age. By directly degrading these vital reserves, early-life trauma may gradually compromise the core dimensions required for SA [ 24 ]. Empirical evidence confirms this trajectory. Higher ACE exposure is robustly associated with increased risks of late-life chronic multimorbidity [ 25 , 26 ], accelerated cognitive decline [ 27 , 28 ], and severe depressive symptoms [ 28 , 29 ]. Furthermore, early trauma impairs secure attachment and interpersonal trust, severely compromising the protective effects of active social engagement [ 30 , 31 ]. ### Adverse Childhood Experiences, Emotional-Cognitive Profiles, and Response to Cognitive Remediation and Emotion Skills Training in Anorexia Nervosa
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eur Eat Disord Rev | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Meneguzzo P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 17

BACKGROUND: Anorexia nervosa (AN) involves restrictive eating and marked difficulties in emotion regulation and cognitive flexibility. Cognitive Remediation and Emotion Skills Training (CREST) targets these domains, but outcomes may vary by psychological profile and adverse childhood experiences (ACEs). This study examined the associations of emotional-cognitive profiles and ACEs with treatment response following CREST in inpatients with AN.

METHODS: We studied 198 female inpatients with AN who completed baseline and post-treatment assessments after an 8-week group CREST programme. Cluster analysis of the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS), Toronto Alexithymia Scale-20 (TAS-20), Detail and Flexibility Questionnaire (DFLEX), and Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory-Revised (OCI-R) identified subgroups. Outcomes (ΔEDE-Q, ΔDFLEX, ΔTAS, ΔBMI) were compared across clusters. Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) subscales were tested in multinomial regression and moderation models.

RESULTS: Three clusters emerged: Cluster 1 (low impairment), Cluster 0 (moderate impairment), and Cluster 2 (globally impaired). Groups differed in age, illness duration, BMI, and adversities exposure (all p < 0.05). Psychological outcomes differed: ΔDFLEX (H = 63.7, p < 0.001) and ΔTAS (p < 0.001), with Cluster 2 showing the greatest gains. Emotional abuse was positively associated with both the moderate and globally impaired profiles, whereas physical abuse specifically predicted the globally impaired profile. Sexual abuse was inversely associated with both impaired profiles. Moderation analyses indicated that associations between ACE severity and psychological change varied across emotional-cognitive profiles, with direction and magnitude differing by outcome and ACE subtype.

CONCLUSIONS: Greater short-term psychological changes were observed in the globally impaired profile, although baseline severity warrants cautious interpretation. Adverse childhood experiences were associated with profile membership and heterogeneous patterns of psychological change across profiles.

### Association Between Adverse Childhood Experiences and Later-Life Sarcopenia Among Community-Dwelling Older Adults: Mediating and Moderating Roles of Depressive Symptoms and Cognitive Impairment
**כתב עת:** 📄 Geriatr Gerontol Int | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu Q et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

BACKGROUND: Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are associated with sarcopenia, but underlying pathways remain unclear. This study aimed to examine whether depressive symptoms and cognitive impairment mediate or moderate the associations of threat-related and deprivation-related ACEs with sarcopenia.

METHODS: Three waves (2011-2015) of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (n = 5003, aged ≥ 60 years) were used. Logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards regression tested associations of ACE dimensions, depressive symptoms, and cognitive impairment with sarcopenia. Four-way decomposition and joint multiple mediator analysis quantified mediation and interaction.

RESULTS: Deprivation-related ACEs were associated with sarcopenia (HR = 1.143, 95% CI = 1.013-1.289), whereas threat-related ACEs were not (HR = 1.002, 95% CI = 0.896-1.120). In four-way decomposition, threat-related ACEs showed no significant total or direct effect. The small pure indirect effect (PIE) via depressive symptoms (HR = 1.025, 95% CI = 1.008-1.044) is hypothesis-generating. For deprivation-related ACEs, significant components comprised a direct effect independent of depressive symptoms (HR = 1.222, 95% CI = 1.035-1.436), a small PIE via depressive symptoms (HR = 1.014, 95% CI = 1.004-1.027), and a reference interaction with cognitive impairment (HR = 1.046, 95% CI = 1.006-1.089), reflecting a significant multiplicative interaction (p = 0.006). Joint mediator analysis corroborated the non-significant total effect of threat-related ACEs, although a small joint indirect effect emerged (HR = 1.020, 95% CI = 1.005-1.036). For deprivation-related ACEs, total effect (HR = 1.140, 95% CI = 1.006-1.284) was predominantly direct, and the joint indirect effect explained 11.4%.

CONCLUSION: Deprivation-related ACEs increased sarcopenia risk through direct pathways and amplified vulnerability among individuals with cognitive impairment, with a minor indirect contribution from depressive symptoms. Threat-related ACEs showed no significant total effect. The weak indirect effect via depressive symptoms requires replication. Dimension-specific ACE assessment and targeted psychological intervention are warranted.

### Parent-Adolescent Discrepancies in Perceived Overparenting and Adolescent Mental Health: A Multi-Informant Perspective
**כתב עת:** 📄 Int J Psychol | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yu M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

Parent-adolescent discrepancies in perceptions of parenting may provide important insight into adolescent adjustment. This study examined how consistency and discrepancy in perceived parental overparenting across parent-adolescent and father-mother dyads were associated with adolescent mental health problems in Chinese families. Participants were 860 adolescents (417 boys; M age  = 10.17, SD = 1.54) and their mothers and fathers. Results showed that adolescents reported higher levels of anxiety/depressive symptoms, withdrawal, and somatic complaints when both parents and adolescents perceived high levels of overparenting. Discrepancies between adolescents and parents were also associated with poorer adjustment: adolescents reported more anxiety/depressive symptoms and withdrawal when they perceived higher paternal overparenting than their fathers did, and more anxiety/depressive symptoms when they perceived higher maternal overparenting than their mothers did. In father-mother dyads, adolescents showed more anxiety/depressive symptoms, withdrawal, and somatic complaints when both parents reported high overparenting, whereas interparental discrepancies were not significant. These patterns were consistent across boys and girls. Findings highlight the value of considering adolescents', mothers', and fathers' perspectives simultaneously when examining overparenting and adolescent mental health.
