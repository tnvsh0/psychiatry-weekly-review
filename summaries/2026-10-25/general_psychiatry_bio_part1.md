### A microglial basis for fragile X syndrome via impairment of FMRP-regulated lysosomal function
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 17

Silencing of the FMRP-encoding FMR1 gene results in fragile X syndrome (FXS), the most common inheritable intellectual disability and autism. FMRP regulates various neuronal functions but clinical trials targeting neurons have failed, suggesting unrecognized disease mechanisms. We find that mice with selective Fmr1 deletion in microglia exhibit audiogenic seizures (AGS) and parvalbumin (PV) neuron loss, recapitulating two key phenotypes observed in FXS (Fmr1 -/y ) mice. By combining snRNA-Seq and RNA immunoprecipitation, we identify several lysosomal mRNAs as FMRP binding targets. FMRP promotes lysosomal protein expression and its deficiency disrupts lysosomal function in microglia. Notably, FMRP depletion reduces Grn-encoded secretory progranulin and increases PV neuron apoptosis in immature brains. PV neuron density is decreased similarly in Grn +/- , Fmr1 -/y , and Grn +/- ;Fmr1 -/y  mice, and progranulin overexpression rescues AGS and PV neuron loss in Fmr1 -/y  mice. Thus, our study identifies microglia and progranulin as critical regulators of FXS pathogenesis and potential targets for FXS therapy.

### Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) combined with antidepressant pharmacotherapy for depression: state-dependent mechanisms, clinical evidence, and translational challenges
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Deng J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 18

Depression is a highly prevalent and debilitating mental disorder. Pharmacotherapy remains the primary treatment modality; however, its efficacy is often limited by a delayed onset of action, suboptimal response in some patients, and a high risk of recurrence. Treatment-resistant depression, characterized by poor response to conventional therapies, poses a major clinical challenge and highlights the need for improved treatment strategies. Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), a noninvasive neuromodulation technique, has shown promise for depression, particularly when combined with antidepressant pharmacotherapy. Advances in neuroimaging, neurochemistry, and network neuroscience have clarified how high-frequency rTMS may modulate cortical excitability, monoaminergic transmission, synaptic plasticity, and distributed brain networks. Clinical evidence suggests that combined treatment may accelerate early improvement, increase response and remission, and reduce relapse risk; however, these benefits are heterogeneous across patients and symptom domains and should be interpreted in light of expectancy effects and limitations of sham blinding. Neuroimaging studies have identified treatment-associated functional and structural changes, including hippocampal volume changes, but the causal links among molecular signaling, circuit reorganization, structural findings, and symptom improvement remain unresolved. The interaction between rTMS and antidepressants should therefore not be viewed as uniformly additive or synergistic, but as a state-dependent process shaped by medication class, stimulation protocol and timing, cortical excitability, metaplasticity, and individual neurobiological context. This review integrates mechanistic, neuroimaging, and clinical evidence and discusses how biomarker-, state-, and symptom-informed targeting, optimized treatment sequencing, and pragmatic implementation strategies could improve the precision and accessibility of combined treatment for depression.

### Multimodal associations between posterior hippocampus glutamate metabolism, visual cortex connectivity, and intrusive trauma reexperiencing symptoms
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Clancy KJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

Hippocampal dysfunction is implicated in posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), particularly intrusive reexperiencing symptoms, and may be mediated by glutamatergic excitotoxicity. Markers of glutamate dysfunction (higher glutamate to N-acetyl aspartate levels; Glu/NAA) in the hippocampus (HPC) have been linked to reexperiencing symptoms. However, the HPC demonstrates heterogeneity along its anterior-posterior axis, with different functional connectivity patterns and PTSD symptom associations, motivating investigations into glutamate metabolism in anterior and posterior HPC subregions (a/pHPC). To this end, 121 symptomatic trauma-exposed adults with current trauma reexperiencing symptoms completed magnetic resonance spectroscopy and resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging to examine the regional specificity of HPC Glu/NAA associations with reexperiencing symptoms and the link to a/pHPC functional connectivity. PTSD symptoms were assessed with the Clinician-Administered PTSD Scale for DSM-5. Reexperiencing symptom severity was associated with greater Glu/NAA in the pHPC, but not aHPC. pHPC Glu/NAA was further linked to stronger functional connectivity between the pHPC and visual cortex (VC), which in turn correlated with more severe reexperiencing symptoms. This strengthened pHPC-VC connectivity explained the shared variance between pHPC Glu/NAA and reexperiencing severity, suggesting dysregulated glutamate metabolism in the pHPC may contribute to reexperiencing symptoms through functional connectivity with the VC. These findings replicate prior work linking HPC Glu/NAA to trauma reexperiencing symptoms and provide novel evidence of this association in the pHPC, but not aHPC, which is mediated by its functional connectivity with the VC. This multimodal investigation supports translational models of glutamatergic dysfunction in trauma-related disorders and highlights new targets for pharmacological and neuromodulatory interventions.

### Positive-affective and negative-cognitive brain-behavior axes in clinical high risk for psychosis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vieira S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

BACKGROUND: Individuals at clinical high risk for psychosis (CHR) present with heterogeneous combinations of positive, negative, and affective symptoms, cognitive difficulties, and functional impairment. However, reproducible multivariate links between this multidomain phenotype and brain morphology remain limited. This study explored the multivariate brain-behavior associations in CHR using a data-driven approach.

METHODS: Baseline data from 720 CHR participants from the Accelerating Medicines Partnership® Schizophrenia program (AMP® SCZ) were analyzed using sparse canonical correlation analysis to identify associations between 95 regional brain volumes and 28 behavioral variables indexing symptoms, cognition, and functioning. Generalizability was tested using leave-one-network-out cross-validation (PRoNET and PRESCIENT), and stability was assessed using bootstrapping and leave-one-site-out analyses. Associations with demographic, clinical and site-related variables were examined.

RESULTS: Two brain-behavior dimensions generalized across networks. A positive-affective axis (r=0.21-0.23), linking higher depression, anxiety/stress, positive symptoms and anhedonia with predominantly frontal-cingulate-insular volume reductions and relatively larger corpus callosum and putamen volumes. This axis was associated with comorbid depression, cannabis use and family history of depression or affective psychosis. A negative-cognitive axis (r=0.17-0.18) linked greater asociality, alogia, emotional withdrawal, blunted affect, and motor retardation, poorer cognition and functioning with reduced cerebellar and parietal volumes, and relatively larger lateral ventricles and corpus callosum. This axis was associated with lower IQ and poorer premorbid adjustment.

CONCLUSIONS: Two partially dissociable brain-behavior dimensions were identified: a positive-affective axis capturing the co-expression of affective and attenuated positive symptoms, and a negative-cognitive axis suggestive of neurodevelopmental vulnerability.

### Brain NaV1.5 fine-tunes neuronal excitability to facilitate female-directed social behavior in male mice
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ke W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

BACKGROUND: The voltage-gated sodium channel Na V 1.5, an essential subtype for cardiac action potential generation, is also enriched in limbic regions of the brain, but its function in the central nervous system remains poorly understood.

METHODS: We performed RNAscope, immunostaining, electrophysiology, fiber photometry, Scn5a knockdown, and behavioral testing to dissect the functional role of Na V 1.5 in the brain.

RESULTS: Na V 1.5 channels are enriched in neurons of posterior bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNSTp) and accumulated at the axon initial segment. Activation of these tetrodotoxin-resistant channels lowers action potential threshold and accelerates spike initiation at relatively hyperpolarized levels of membrane potential (V m ). In vivo fiber photometry showed that BNSTp Na V 1.5-expressing neurons in male mice were recruited by salient social stimuli and exhibited hyperpolarizing V m  dynamics during social contacts. Scn5a knockdown selectively attenuated neuronal responses to female chemosensory cues, and disrupted sex preference and female-directed behaviors.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings identify Na V 1.5 as a V m  state-dependent regulator of BNSTp neuronal excitability, and reveal an unexpected role of this channel subtype in female-cue processing and female-directed social behavior in males.

### Efficacy and safety of GLP-1 receptor agonists for antipsychotic-related overweight and metabolic dysregulation: a systematic review and meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eur J Clin Pharmacol | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Zhou L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 24

BACKGROUND: Antipsychotic-related overweight and metabolic dysregulation are highly prevalent among patients with severe mental illness. Although glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) have demonstrated substantial metabolic benefits in the general population, their efficacy and safety in antipsychotic-treated patients remain unclear. This review aimed to systematically evaluate the efficacy and safety of GLP-1RAs in patients with antipsychotic-related overweight and metabolic dysregulation.

METHODS: We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating GLP-1RAs in adults receiving antipsychotic treatment. PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, MEDLINE, the Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov were searched from inception to September 13, 2026. Primary outcomes included changes in body weight, body mass index (BMI), HbA1c, and total cholesterol. Random-effects models were used to pool mean differences (MDs) and risk ratios (RRs). This study was registered with PROSPERO (CRD420261290294).

RESULTS: Eight RCTs involving 535 randomized participants were included. GLP-1RAs treatment was associated with significant reductions in body weight (MD = -6.54 kg, 95% CI: -9.80 to -3.29, P < 0.001, I 2  = 92%), BMI (MD = -2.34 kg/m 2 , 95% CI: -3.34 to -1.34, P < 0.001, I² = 85%), HbA1c (MD = -0.32%, 95% CI: -0.39 to -0.26, P < 0.001, I² = 52%), waist circumference (MD = -5.22 cm, 95% CI: -6.55 to -3.88, P < 0.001, I 2  = 27%), visceral fat mass (MD = -0.31 kg, 95% CI: -0.46 to -0.16, P < 0.001, I 2  = 11%), and LDL-C (MD = -0.16 mmol/L, 95% CI: -0.31 to -0.01, P = 0.03, I 2  = 54%), whereas no significant effects were observed for total cholesterol, triglycerides, or HDL-C. Weight reduction was greatest with semaglutide, followed by liraglutide, and increased with longer treatment duration (both P for subgroup differences < 0.05). GLP-1RAs were associated with higher risks of nausea (RR = 1.61, 95% CI: 1.20 to 2.16), vomiting (RR = 2.10, 95% CI: 1.32 to 3.35), and constipation (RR = 2.34, 95% CI: 1.23 to 4.47), but not of diarrhea, serious adverse events (RR = 0.72, 95% CI: 0.49 to 1.06), all-cause discontinuation (RR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.60 to 1.85), or discontinuation due to adverse events (RR = 2.14, 95% CI: 0.72 to 6.33). Psychiatric adverse events were infrequent and could not be pooled.

CONCLUSION: GLP-1RAs significantly improve weight-related and glycemic outcomes in antipsychotic-treated patients, at the cost of an increased risk of gastrointestinal adverse events. Their psychiatric safety could not be established owing to sparse data. Longer-term trials with standardized safety reporting are warranted.
