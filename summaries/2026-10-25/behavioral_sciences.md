### Serial or Parallel? Sublexical Scanpath Analysis of Eye-movement Sequences in Single Word Reading
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Psychological Science (Psychol Sci) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lahoud H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Are the letters of a written word processed sequentially or in parallel? Despite an abundance of research on visual word recognition, the evidence in this longstanding debate rests almost entirely on indirect inferences from word-length effects and conventional whole-word eye-movement measures. We sought direct evidence on the serial-parallel question by investigating sublexical gaze-fixation sequences-so-called  scanpaths . This study is the first to apply scanpath analysis to fixation sequences within single words. Thirty-four Hebrew-speaking adult university students read aloud three-letter and five-letter real words and pseudowords while their eye movements were tracked. As expected, real words showed more parallel processing, whereas pseudowords displayed more sequential, letter-by-letter scanpaths. Surprisingly, longer words tended to elicit serial processing regardless of word familiarity. Our findings support the applicability of scanpath analysis in visual word recognition and its potential to open up new perspectives on word-recognition processes.

### On bias in detecting bias: A signal detection analysis of attributions to gender discrimination
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Experimental Psychology: General (J Exp Psychol Gen) *(IF: 5.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jakob M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Discrimination is the unjust treatment of people based on their group membership, and detecting discrimination is a necessary first step in countering it. Attributions to discrimination have often been conceptualized in terms of signal detection theory, with researchers speculating about the role of sensitivity (i.e., observers' ability to recognize discrimination) and response bias (i.e., their tendency to judge that discrimination is present) in observers' judgments. Here, we take a first step toward moving beyond conceptual applications to empirical signal detection theory analyses of attributions to discrimination across five studies examining judgments about gender discrimination in corporate pay raise decisions. We find that prototypes (Studies 1-3) and their alignment with the actual pattern of decisions (Studies 2-4) inform observers' response bias, reflecting differences in their attribution of unfair outcomes to discrimination (Study 5). In contrast, the extent of harm caused by discriminatory outcomes exerts a small effect on sensitivity and a large effect on response bias (Studies 1-3), which reflects observers' determination of a fair standard for decisions (Study 5). Theoretically, the present work contributes to the causal analysis of central factors shaping attributions to discrimination. Specifically, we shed light on whether effects of these factors are mediated by response bias or sensitivity, and assess their causal origin in either the application of a merit standard or attributional processing.
