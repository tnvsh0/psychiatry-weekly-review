### Twelve-month follow-up of parent-based child anxiety treatment
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology (J Consult Clin Psychol) *(IF: 5.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Etkin RG et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep

OBJECTIVE: Supportive Parenting for Anxious Childhood Emotions (SPACE) is an efficacious parent-based treatment for child anxiety disorders. Little is known about the maintenance of treatment gains following SPACE, and no comparative follow-up studies have been conducted. We address both gaps by examining child anxiety severity up to 12 months following SPACE and cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT). We also examine whether reductions in family accommodation of child anxiety are maintained through follow-up in both treatments.

METHOD: Participants were 125 mother-child dyads enrolled in a randomized controlled noninferiority trial comparing posttreatment outcomes of SPACE and CBT. Parents and children in both treatments completed measures of child anxiety severity and family accommodation at pre-, mid-, and posttreatment and at 3-, 6-, and 12-month follow-ups. Linear mixed models were used to examine effects of time, treatment, and their interaction across all assessment intervals, with particular interest in maintenance at 12-month follow-up.

RESULTS: SPACE and CBT both produced significant reductions in parent- and child-rated anxiety severity and family accommodation over time, indicating gains were maintained similarly for both treatments through 12-month follow-up. Parent (but not child) ratings of child anxiety severity were lower for SPACE than CBT at posttreatment and 12-month follow-up.

CONCLUSIONS: Results are the first to show maintenance of treatment gains up to 1 year following SPACE, comparable to CBT. These findings may inform clinical decision making and guide the selection of interventions that optimize both short- and longer term outcomes for anxious children and their families. ### What happens after being not-on-track? Patterns of change, profiles and prediction of at-risk patients
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology (J Consult Clin Psychol) *(IF: 5.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Moggia D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep

OBJECTIVES: Clinicians often struggle to accurately identify patients at risk of treatment failure. Feedback-informed treatment offers real-time outcome monitoring to help detect and address potential treatment failures early. This study examined patterns of change among not-on-track (NOT) patients during feedback-informed treatment, explored how patients' characteristics related to these patterns, and assessed the feasibility of predicting them.

METHOD: Data from 573 NOT patients in a university outpatient psychotherapy clinic were analyzed. Growth mixture modeling identified distinct change trajectories, omnibus tests compared patients' characteristics across patterns, and multinomial logistic regression evaluated the predictive value of these characteristics.

RESULTS: Three distinct patterns of change emerged, differing in symptom severity at the point of being flagged as NOT and in subsequent treatment response: C1: severe-modest responders, C2: severe-fast improvers, and C3: moderate-improvers. The patterns were differentiated by key intake variables, including symptom severity, personality disorder traits, interpersonal problems, and emotion regulation difficulties. Two of the patterns were predicted with acceptable accuracy.

CONCLUSION: All patient subgroups demonstrated improvement. Nevertheless, they became NOT for different reasons, and their responses were associated with different clinical characteristics. These findings suggest that a NOT alert should not be interpreted as a homogeneous signal, and patients may require different adaptations. (PsycInfo Database Record (c) 2026 APA, all rights reserved).

### The impact of threat- and safety-enhancing information on the acquisition of fear of interoceptive threat
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Albert CP et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 14

Social learning, such as learning through verbal instruction, has been proposed to play a crucial role in the development pathological fear of bodily sensations. Despite the ubiquity of verbal information in daily life, the effects of verbal information on subsequent interoceptive fear learning remain empirically untested. Therefore, we aimed to examine how verbal information about the threatening vs. benign nature of bodily sensations influences subsequent interoceptive fear conditioning. 77 healthy participants were randomly assigned to receive either threat- or safety-enhancing information about bodily sensations before completing an interoceptive fear acquisition and extinction task. Two geometric symbols served as CSs and temporary dyspnea (inspiratory load) as the US. Verbal reports indicated successful differential learning in both groups, with no modulation of information type. However, participants of the threat information group showed no differential response in startle response magnitudes towards CS + relative to CS-, indexing that the activation of brain defensive networks, that did not differ between CS+ and CS-. This pattern might suggest a generalization of, or failure to inhibit defensive activation toward the safety stimulus. In contrast, the safety information group exhibited significantly smaller startle response magnitudes towards CS- vs. CS+, indicating preserved differentiation between threat and safety. No group differences emerged during the extinction phase. Despite the preliminary nature of our findings, the pattern of results demonstrates interaction of different fear learning pathways in shaping the risk for the development of pathological fear and anxiety.

### Shared and Differential Psychological Processes Associated With Treatment Response in Mindful Self-Compassion and Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy for Chronic Pain: An Exploratory Secondary Analysis of a Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eur J Pain | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Torrijos-Zarcero M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

BACKGROUND: Psychological treatments for chronic pain are effective, but the processes associated with therapeutic change, and whether these are shared across treatments or treatment-specific, remain unclear. This exploratory secondary analysis examined whether changes in pain acceptance, pain catastrophizing facets and self-compassion were associated with clinical outcomes in Mindful Self-Compassion (MSC) versus Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy (CBT).

METHODS: Data were drawn from a randomized trial comparing MSC (n = 61) and CBT (n = 59) in adults with chronic pain and elevated emotional distress. Processes and outcomes were assessed at baseline and post-treatment. Single and theoretically ordered multiple-mediator models examined whether changes in pain acceptance, rumination, magnification, helplessness and self-compassion were associated with changes in anxiety, depression, quality of life, pain interference and pain intensity.

RESULTS: Compared with CBT, MSC produced larger increases in pain acceptance and reductions in helplessness. These processes statistically accounted for improvements in self-compassion, depression, mental quality of life and pain interference; acceptance additionally accounted for reduced pain intensity, and helplessness for reduced anxiety. Rumination and magnification predicted improvements across treatments but did not explain between-group differences. Multiple-mediator models indicated that acceptance/self-compassion and helplessness/self-compassion pathways favoured MSC for depression, anxiety and mental quality of life, explaining up to 51% of change. No mediator accounted for physical quality of life.

CONCLUSIONS: MSC and CBT appeared to engage overlapping processes, with differential associations mainly involving pain acceptance and helplessness. Acceptance may be more relevant to functional and pain-related outcomes, whereas helplessness/self-compassion pathways may be more relevant to emotional recovery.

SIGNIFICANCE STATEMENT: This study identifies pain acceptance and reduced helplessness as candidate processes associated with treatment response in MSC and CBT, with acceptance more closely linked to pain-related and functional change and helplessness to emotional outcomes. These findings support more precise, process-based psychological treatment for chronic pain.

### Auditory Biofeedback Gait Training in Individuals With Chronic Ankle Instability: Protocol for a Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Res Protoc | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Torp DM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

BACKGROUND: Chronic ankle instability (CAI) develops in a large majority of patients with lateral ankle sprain (LAS), who often experience recurring injuries, residual feelings of instability, and abnormal neuromuscular patterns. Specifically, gait alterations are common in those with CAI and considered a contributing factor to poor long-term ankle joint health. Yet, there are no evidence-based gait-training protocols targeted to improve biomechanics, which may improve long-term outcomes related to ankle joint health and patient satisfaction.

OBJECTIVE: This study protocol introduces a progressive auditory biofeedback (ABF) gait-training program aimed to improve short- and long-term lower extremity biomechanics and talar cartilage outcomes and reduce the frequency of ankle-giving-way episodes and recurring injuries.

METHODS: This study is a multisite randomized controlled trial (RCT) with a target enrollment of 100 patients with CAI into a control group (n=50, 50%) or an ABF gait-training program (n=50, 50%). Both groups will complete a progressive 6-week (12-session) protocol comprising walking, ruck marching, and running on a treadmill. Lower extremity kinematics and kinetics, talar cartilage characteristics (via ultrasonography), self-perceived dysfunction and instability, and number of recurring sprains will be assessed prior to intervention, immediately after intervention, and 6 and 12 months postintervention. Finally, the frequency of ankle-giving-way episodes in the 6 months prior to intervention will be compared to the frequency of episodes at the 6- and 12-month time points to determine a longitudinal difference in ankle-giving-way episodes.

RESULTS: The study was funded in September 2024 and prospectively registered through ClinicalTrials.gov on March 10, 2025. The first participant was enrolled in May 2025. As of August 2026, 118 individuals had been recruited and 19 participants enrolled. Recruitment will remain open until September 2027, and study completion is expected in September 2028. Data processing is projected to continue through September 2028, with the final report published in June 2029.

CONCLUSIONS: This study will be the first RCT of ABF gait training to assess the immediate and long-term effects on lower extremity biomechanics, ankle joint health, and patient-reported outcomes in patients with CAI. Results from this study will advance the knowledge of long-term joint health that may be associated with poor outcomes post-LAS.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06868316; https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06868316.

INTERNATIONAL REGISTERED REPORT IDENTIFIER (IRRID): DERR1-10.2196/107775.

### Virtual Reality for Enhancing Self-Compassion and Reducing Burnout Among Mental Health Care Professionals: Protocol for a Pilot Feasibility Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Res Protoc | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Iriqat R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

BACKGROUND: Mental health care professionals working in high-demand clinical environments are frequently exposed to patient suffering and trauma, placing them at increased risk for burnout and secondary traumatic stress. Compassion-focused cognitive behavioral therapy (CF-CBT) has shown promise in reducing psychological distress and enhancing self-compassion; however, traditional delivery methods may be limited by time, accessibility, and resource constraints. Virtual reality (VR) may provide an immersive and accessible approach for delivering compassion-focused interventions.

OBJECTIVE: The primary objective is to evaluate the feasibility and acceptability of a self-administered VR-enhanced CF-CBT (VR-CF-CBT) intervention for mental health care professionals. Secondary exploratory objectives are to estimate changes in self-compassion, burnout, and secondary traumatic stress (STS), as well as between-group differences that can inform the design of a future definitive trial.

METHODS: A 2-arm randomized controlled pilot trial will be conducted among mental health care professionals. Participants will be randomized to either a VR-CF-CBT intervention or an active comparison condition consisting of self-guided CF-CBT delivered via digital booklets. Participants will complete 8 brief sessions (approximately 10 minutes each) over a 4- to 6-week period. Exploratory clinical outcomes will include changes in self-compassion, burnout, and STS measured using validated self-report instruments. Primary feasibility and acceptability outcomes will include recruitment, retention, intervention adherence, session completion, participant satisfaction, and scores on the Acceptability of Intervention Measure, Intervention Appropriateness Measure, and Feasibility of Intervention Measure.

RESULTS: The study was funded in April 2026. As of October 2026, 38 participants had provided consent and enrolled. Recruitment for a second wave of participants is ongoing, and data collection is in progress. Enrollment and data collection are expected to continue through 2026, with data analysis and dissemination of findings anticipated by spring 2027.

CONCLUSIONS: This pilot study will provide information on the feasibility and acceptability of delivering CF-CBT through immersive VR to mental health care professionals. Findings will help identify whether modifications are needed before a larger trial and provide preliminary estimates to inform the design of future efficacy testing.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07666867; https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07666867.

INTERNATIONAL REGISTERED REPORT IDENTIFIER (IRRID): DERR1-10.2196/105754.
