### Shared cortical morphometry and molecular signatures across depression and physical diseases
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jiang C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is highly comorbid with physical diseases, suggesting shared neurobiological mechanisms. However, the common structural and molecular substrates underlying this comorbidity remain poorly understood. We analyzed multimodal MRI data from 577 patients (MDD, asthma, fibromyalgia, gastroesophageal reflux disease, type 2 diabetes) and 275 healthy controls, constructed individual morphometric similarity networks (MSN) to identify shared cortical alterations, and applied principal component analysis with multiscale molecular annotation (transcriptomic, cell-type, neurotransmitter, and cognitive mapping) to characterize underlying mechanisms. All patient groups exhibited regional MSN abnormalities. A shared MSN pattern emerged, featuring increased similarity in visual and somatomotor regions and decreased similarity in ventral attention, limbic, and default mode networks, and was replicated in independent cohorts. This pattern was significantly associated with spatial gene expression enriched for synaptic signaling, membrane potential regulation, and neuronal architecture, with predominant expression in excitatory/inhibitory neurons and astrocytes. It further mapped onto specific neurotransmitter systems, with exploratory cognitive decoding suggesting possible relevance to emotion and social cognition. Related genes overlapped with known risk loci for Alzheimer's disease and bipolar disorder. These findings identify a robust across diseases cortical MSN phenotype linked to convergent molecular and neurochemical correlates, offering a framework for studying shared vulnerability and transdiagnostic mechanisms across depression and physical diseases.

### The non-inflammatory effect of neuronal NLRP3 on spatial memory after traumatic brain injury
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tan SW et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

The NLRP3 inflammasome has emerged as a critical regulator in neuroinflammatory responses to various brain insults from traumatic, ischemic and neurodegenerative conditions, an effect almost exclusively attributed to microglial NLRP3. Here, we demonstrated that in the absence of changes in inflammatory factors IL-18, IL-1β and TNF-α, neuronal Nlrp3 knockout in hippocampus (nNlrp3 -/- ) improved long-term spatial memory performance, with increased synaptic proteins levels and enhanced amplitude of long-term potentiation at 4 weeks after traumatic brain injury (TBI), compared to wildtype littermates. Notably, the nNlrp3 -/-  mice out-performed microglia-specific knockout and global Nlrp3 knockout mice in cognition and synaptic function. We further identified that NLRP3 structurally and functionally directly interacted with the R1 subunit of N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NR1) in hippocampus after TBI. Accordingly, pharmacological inhibition of NLRP3 by MCC950 rescued the decrease in the NMDAR-gated calcium influx by LPS-stimulated hippocampal neurons while selective Nlrp3 knockout in hippocampal neurons partially reversed the decreased phosphorylation level of NR1 after TBI. Collectively, this study provides the first evidence that neuronal NLRP3 constitutes a non-inflammatory response to control synaptic plasticity and cognitive function via NR1-dependent non-inflammatory mechanism in TBI and thus broadens the central role of NLRP3 in neuronal adaptive mechanisms to include both microglial NLRP3-inflammation arm and the neuronal NLRP3-synaptic plasticity arm.

### Thalamocortical dysconnectivity shapes cortical hierarchy disruption across illness durations in schizophrenia
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Huang H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

BACKGROUND: Schizophrenia is increasingly conceptualized as a disorder of circuit-level dysfunction. However, how thalamocortical abnormalities differ across cohorts with varying illness durations and relate to large-scale cortical hierarchical organization remains unclear.

METHODS: We analyzed resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging data from 1,223 patients with schizophrenia and 1,224 matched healthy controls across 12 datasets. The datasets were grouped as early-, middle-, and late-stage cohorts. Connectivity gradient mapping was applied to characterize thalamocortical organization, and computational modeling evaluated simulated hierarchical changes after modifying thalamic-salience connectivity.

RESULTS: We identified a schizophrenia-relevant sensorimotor-salience-association axis that captures systematic differences in thalamocortical abnormality profiles across the stage cohorts. Along this axis, case-control effects in association regions were positive in the early-stage cohort but negative in the middle- and late-stage cohorts, whereas sensorimotor regions showed the converse pattern. This cross-cohort pattern was accompanied by reduced cortical hierarchical separation in cohorts with longer illness duration. In contrast, salience-related thalamic regions showed negative case-control effects in all three stage cohorts, with larger effect sizes in the middle- and late-stage cohorts, and these alterations were associated with clinical symptoms. Computational modification of thalamic-salience connectivity increased simulated cortical hierarchical differentiation, particularly in the middle-stage cohort.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings indicate that thalamocortical dysconnectivity, centered on disrupted thalamic-salience interactions, is associated with systematic differences in cortical hierarchical organization across cohorts. The cross-cohort pattern of salience-related abnormalities highlights thalamic-salience coupling as a circuit-level link to cortical hierarchical differentiation and a candidate for future mechanistic and interventional investigation.

### Simultaneous measurement of Midbrain and Striatal Dopamine in Schizophrenia using an agonist radiotracer
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Slifstein M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

BACKGROUND: Imaging studies showing greater amphetamine-induced dopamine release in patients with schizophrenia (SCZ) compared to matched healthy controls (HC) have been repeatedly replicated, but these studies all used radiotracers that were dopamine D2 receptor antagonists. A more recent study using an agonist radiotracer did not detect this increase, raising a question whether the difference in radiotracer pharmacology affected the result. Another study examining the extrastriatal response to the amphetamine challenge with an antagonist tracer observed a blunted effect in SCZ in the midbrain, suggesting mechanisms local to the striatum might have contributed to the striatal imaging results independent of midbrain dopamine availability. Here, we sought to address both of these questions by performing neuroimaging in SCZ and HC with the agonist tracer [11C]-(+)-PHNO that is quantifiable in striatum and extrastriatal brain regions including midbrain.

METHODS: 28 SCZ and 29 HC were scanned at baseline and 3 hours after oral administration of 0.5 mg/kg d-amphetamine. Outcome measures were baseline binding (BP ND ) and the percent change following amphetamine (ΔBP ND ).

RESULTS: There were no significant group differences in any of the outcome measures, but we observed trend level lower BP ND  and ΔBP ND  in SCZ compared to HC. Post-hoc analysis showed this was mainly driven by dorsal striatum subregions. Associations between ΔBP ND  in midbrain and striatal regions were modest in both groups.

CONCLUSIONS: Our results are consistent with the prior study using an agonist tracer that did not observe greater ΔBP ND  in SCZ.

### Antidopaminergic Medications and Clinical Outcomes in Huntington Disease: Evidence From the PROOF-HD Placebo-Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Neurology *(IF: 4.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Anderson KE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov 24

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Antidopaminergic medications (ADMs), including vesicular monoamine transporter-2 (VMAT2) inhibitors and antipsychotics, are widely used to manage motor and psychiatric symptoms in Huntington disease (HD). Observational evidence suggests that ADM use may be associated with accelerated worsening of clinical outcome measures used in HD trials, but this relationship has not been examined in a controlled trial setting. We assessed the association between ADM exposure and longitudinal clinical outcomes in the Pridopidine's Outcome On Function in Huntington Disease (PROOF-HD) placebo arm.

METHODS: This post hoc analysis included placebo-arm participants from PROOF-HD (NCT04556656), a randomized controlled trial in adults with Total Functional Capacity (TFC) 7-13 and cytosine-adenine-guanine (CAG) ≥36. Participants were categorized as on-ADMs or off-ADMs based on concurrent medication use across the study period. Outcomes included TFC, composite Unified Huntington's Disease Rating Scale (cUHDRS), Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT), Stroop Word Reading (SWR), and Total Motor Score (TMS). Primary analyses used mixed-effects models for repeated measures, corroborated by propensity score weighting (PSW) and time-varying sensitivity analyses.

RESULTS: Of 247 placebo-arm participants (mean age 52.6 years [SD 11.4]; 51% female), 135 (54.7%) were on-ADMs and 112 (45.3%) were off-ADMs. On-ADM participants demonstrated faster clinical decline across multiple outcome measures. Between-group differences in cUHDRS and TFC favored off-ADMs from weeks 39-78; at week 52, the difference in mean change was 0.66 (95% CI 0.31-1.01;  p  = 0.0002) for cUHDRS and 0.85 (95% CI 0.47-1.22;  p  < 0.0001) for TFC as compared with on-ADMs. Significant or directionally consistent findings for SDMT, SWR, and TMS emerged from week 39 onward. All TMS subdomain scores, except chorea, directionally favored off-ADMs at all visits. Antipsychotic-only, VMAT2 inhibitor-only, and higher-dose ADMs were each associated with greater clinical decline in cUHDRS and TFC as compared with off-ADMs. PSW-adjusted and time-varying analyses were directionally consistent with primary findings.

DISCUSSION: In this study of the PROOF-HD placebo arm, ADM use was associated with greater worsening across global, functional, cognitive, and motor outcome measures as compared with off-ADM participants. Residual confounding by disease severity cannot be fully excluded, although sensitivity analyses were directionally consistent. Taken together, ADM exposure, class, and dose should be systematically considered when designing and interpreting HD clinical trials.

TRIAL REGISTRATION INFORMATION: ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT04556656.

CLASSIFICATION OF EVIDENCE: This study provides Class II evidence that in patients with HD, the use of ADMs is associated with greater worsening across global, functional, cognitive, and motor outcome measures as compared with patients not on ADMs.

### Risperidone for children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and severe behavioral dysregulation: a randomized placebo-controlled assessor-blinded trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Abbasi E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 23

Background Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a common neurodevelopmental disorder in childhood, and treatment of children with severe behavioral dysregulation remains challenging. This study evaluated the efficacy and safety of adjunctive risperidone specifically in children with ADHD and clinically significant behavioral dysregulation — including irritability, impulsive aggression, and oppositional symptoms — refractory to behavioral intervention alone. Methods In this 48-week randomized, placebo-controlled, assessor-blinded trial, children aged 3–12 years with moderate-to-severe ADHD and significant behavioral dysregulation refractory to behavioral intervention alone were allocated to risperidone ( n = 48) or matched placebo ( n = 46), both in addition to ongoing structured behavioral intervention and parental psychoeducation. The primary outcome was change in clinician-rated ADHD-RS-IV score from baseline to week 48. Secondary outcomes included parent-rated Conners’ scores, Children’s Global Assessment Scale (CGAS), treatment response, adverse events, and metabolic parameters. Results Risperidone produced a significantly greater reduction in ADHD-RS-IV scores than placebo over time (baseline: 28.85 ± 2.62 vs. 28.70 ± 2.50; week 48: 15.63 ± 1.82 vs. 26.47 ± 2.92; p < 0.001). Parent-rated Conners’ scores also improved more in the risperidone group (week 48: 16.94 ± 1.81 vs. 28.42 ± 3.04; p < 0.001). Functional improvement was greater with risperidone, with higher CGAS scores and a higher clinical response rate (70.8% vs. 21.7%; p < 0.001). The most common adverse effects were somnolence and increased appetite. Risperidone was associated with greater weight gain and prolactin elevation than placebo, but no serious adverse events occurred. Conclusion In this study, risperidone was associated with improvement in ADHD symptom severity and global functioning in carefully selected children with ADHD and prominent behavioral dysregulation. However, treatment was also associated with clinically relevant metabolic and endocrine adverse effects. These findings support selective use as a pharmacological adjunct to behavioral intervention in closely monitored patients with significant behavioral comorbidity, and do not justify routine use for uncomplicated ADHD. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-026-08433-y .

Introduction Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is one of the most prevalent neurodevelopmental disorders in childhood and adolescence, characterized by persistent symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that interfere with academic performance, social relationships, and emotional functioning [ 1 ]. According to recent epidemiological studies, the worldwide prevalence of ADHD is estimated to range between 5% and 7% among school-aged children, making it a major psychiatric and public health concern [ 2 ]. Although ADHD symptoms often begin in early childhood, the disorder may persist into adolescence and adulthood, contributing to long-term impairments in educational achievement, occupational performance, interpersonal relationships, and quality of life [ 3 ]. The pathophysiology of ADHD is multifactorial and involves genetic susceptibility, environmental influences, and neurobiological dysfunction in dopaminergic and noradrenergic pathways, particularly within frontostriatal neural circuits [ 4 ]. In addition to the core symptoms, many children with ADHD experience emotional dysregulation, aggression, oppositional behaviors, and psychiatric comorbidities such as oppositional defiant disorder (ODD), conduct disorder, anxiety disorders, and autism spectrum disorder (ASD) [ 5 ]. The presence of these comorbidities significantly complicates clinical management and is associated with poorer functional outcomes and increased caregiver burden [ 6 ]. Pharmacological treatment remains the cornerstone of ADHD management, especially in moderate-to-severe cases. Psychostimulants such as methylphenidate and amphetamine derivatives are currently considered first-line therapies because of their well-established efficacy in reducing inattentiveness and hyperactivity symptoms [ 7 ]. However, despite their effectiveness, a substantial proportion of children either show inadequate response or experience adverse effects including appetite suppression, sleep disturbances, irritability, emotional lability, and cardiovascular concerns [ 8 ]. Furthermore, stimulant medications may not sufficiently control severe aggression, disruptive behaviors, or emotional dysregulation in children with complex psychiatric comorbidities [ 9 ]. These limitations have led clinicians and researchers to investigate alternative or adjunctive pharmacological strategies. Among atypical antipsychotics, risperidone has become one of the most frequently prescribed agents in pediatric psychiatry because of its efficacy in controlling aggression, irritability, impulsivity, and behavioral dysregulation [ 10 ]. Risperidone acts primarily through antagonism of dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors and holds FDA approval for irritability associated with autism spectrum disorder in children aged five years and older — the largest regulatory approval of any atypical antipsychotic in a pediatric psychiatric indication. It also has the most extensive evidence base of any atypical antipsychotic for pediatric disruptive behavior disorders, including multiple randomized controlled trials and long-term safety data. Although newer agents such as aripiprazole and alpha-2 agonists such as guanfacine extended-release represent important therapeutic alternatives with potentially more favorable risk-benefit profiles in some clinical contexts, risperidone remains the most widely available, most commonly used, and most extensively studied atypical antipsychotic for pediatric behavioral dysregulation in many regions, including Iran, where regulatory approval and clinical availability of these newer agents remain limited. In recent years, off-label use of risperidone in children with ADHD has increased, particularly in patients presenting with severe impulsivity, oppositional symptoms, or inadequate response to stimulant therapy, making a rigorous evaluation of its long-term efficacy and safety in this population both timely and clinically necessary [ 11 , 12 ]. Although stimulant medications remain the first-line pharmacological treatment for ADHD, a subset of children exhibit severe behavioral dysregulation, irritability, aggression, or oppositional symptoms that may not adequately respond to behavioral interventions or conventional ADHD medications alone. In such complex clinical presentations, atypical antipsychotics, particularly risperidone, have occasionally been used as adjunctive or alternative therapeutic options to reduce disruptive behaviors and improve emotional regulation. Nevertheless, evidence regarding the long-term efficacy and safety of risperidone in pediatric ADHD populations remains limited, particularly in randomized controlled settings with extended follow-up durations. Several clinical studies have evaluated the efficacy of risperidone in pediatric ADHD populations. Previous investigations have demonstrated that risperidone may improve behavioral symptoms and reduce ADHD severity scores, either as monotherapy or as adjunctive treatment combined with stimulants [ 13 – 15 ]. However, findings across studies remain inconsistent because of methodological limitations such as small sample sizes, short follow-up periods, heterogeneous patient populations, and lack of placebo-controlled designs [ 5 ]. In addition, concerns regarding metabolic adverse effects, sedation, weight gain, and hyperprolactinemia continue to limit widespread use of atypical antipsychotics in children [ 16 ].
