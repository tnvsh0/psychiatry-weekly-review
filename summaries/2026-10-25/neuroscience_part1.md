### Sustained antidepressant-like effects of psychedelics through 5-HT(2A)R-β-arrestin signaling
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** He L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

Psychedelics produce long-lasting antidepressant effects, but their hallucinogenic properties limit clinical use. Using structure-guided design of 5-HT 2A R-β-arrestin-biased agonists, we identified β-arrestin signaling as a critical driver of prolonged antidepressant-like effects in mice. Here we show that the slow-dissociation kinetics of lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) enable progressive accumulation of 5-HT 2A R engagement during repeated low-dose administration, approaching the receptor occupancy achieved by an acute high dose while avoiding its high peak concentration. This kinetic separation sustains β-arrestin signaling while limiting robust Gα q -mediated Ca 2+  responses. Consistent with this framework, repeated low-dose LSD and repeated low-dose psilocybin pretreatment followed by a high-dose psychedelic challenge produced sustained antidepressant-like behavioral effects in mice. In parallel, these regimens produced no detectable psychedelic-associated behavioral disruption in mice and cynomolgus macaques. Together, these findings uncover the signaling basis of sustained psychedelic efficacy and provide a rational framework for developing nonhallucinogenic psychedelic therapies for psychiatric disorders.

### Behavioral genomics identifies Creld1 as a cross-modal regulator of somatosensation
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jiang Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Somatosensation is mediated by dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, which convert physical and chemical stimuli into electrical signals. However, the full repertoire of molecular components underlying somatosensation remains incompletely identified. Here we developed an efficient postnatal CRISPR-Cas9 knockout platform for interrogating DRG-expressed genes (such as Piezo2, Trpv1 and Scn9a) in mice using AAV9-mediated single-guide RNA delivery. Combining this approach with somatosensory behavioral assays, we performed a targeted screen of 20 genes and identified Creld1 as a cross-model regulator of somatosensation. Either single-guide RNA-mediated postnatal knockout or tamoxifen-induced Cre-mediated deletion of Creld1 in DRG neurons resulted in profound behavioral deficits in touch, temperature, pain and itch, whereas its overexpression enhanced touch and thermal responses. Mechanistically, CRELD1 functions as an auxiliary regulator of voltage-gated sodium channels (Na v ) for controlling DRG neuron excitability. Together, this work establishes a robust postnatal screening platform for behavioral genomics and identifies Creld1 as a master regulator of somatosensory function.

### A vagal gut-brain axis for feeding-induced gastric secretion in mice
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Feeding begins with food-seeking and ends with nutrient absorption and waste elimination. Although meal-related sensory cues shape feeding behavior, how they engage neural circuits to control digestion remains unclear. We dissected vagal gut-brain-gut pathways mediating meal-induced gastric secretion. Stomach-innervating vagal afferents were necessary and sufficient for secretion. Among them, Glp1r +  and Sst +  neurons drove acid, pepsin, and gastrin secretion, whereas Oxtr +  and Vip +  neurons had little effect. Piezo2 was expressed in Glp1r +  mechanosensory afferents, and conditional deletion of Piezo2 attenuated distension-evoked secretion. On the efferent limb, genetically defined vagal motor-neuron subsets showed distinct secretory effects: Cck +  neurons broadly increased acid, pepsin, and gastrin, whereas Grp +  neurons preferentially affected acid secretion. Finally, enteric circuit dissection revealed modular output control, with Calb2 +  neurons driving secretion with minimal motility effects. Together, these results define a cell-type-resolved vagal-enteric architecture for gastric control.

### A GABAergic preoptic-raphe circuit mediates paradoxical coupling of anxiety and social preference after comparative social deficit
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhang YY et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

Social disconnection is a major risk factor for anxiety disorders, the most prevalent mental illnesses worldwide, yet its neural substrates remain unclear. Here, we establish a comparative social deficit (CSD) model in adult male mice that induces a paradoxical phenotype of increased anxiety-like behavior together with heightened social preference, a pattern conceptually similar to human loneliness. Mechanistically, CSD increases the excitability of medial preoptic GABAergic (mPOA Gad2 ) neurons, which bidirectionally regulate CSD-induced behavioral changes. Transcriptomic and electrophysiological analyses identify downregulation of the Kv4.3 potassium channel (Kcnd3) as a molecular driver of mPOA Gad2  hyperexcitability. Furthermore, mPOA Gad2  neurons exert monosynaptic inhibition onto dorsal raphe serotonergic (DRN 5-HT ) neurons. This inhibitory projection, strengthened by CSD, mediates CSD-induced behavioral effects. These findings define a molecular and circuit mechanism by which social disconnection remodels mPOA-DRN interactions to drive the paradoxical coupling of anxiety and elevated social preference, providing a translational framework for understanding loneliness-related psychiatric vulnerability.

### Clinically unresponsive patients with acute brain injury activate a brain-computer interface
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Shen Q et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Brain activation to verbal commands can be detected by EEG in clinically unresponsive patients. These patients with cognitive motor dissociation (CMD) are unable to communicate with the environment despite preserved consciousness. Advanced brain-computer interfaces (BCI) are promising to support motor function in patients with motor neuron disease or spinal cord injury. Our primary objective was to determine if clinically unresponsive patients can control a self-paced, one-way BCI early after acute brain injury. Secondarily, we assessed the association of BCI control with short- and long-term outcomes. We conducted a prospective single-centre cohort study in 41 clinically unresponsive, critically ill and acutely brain injured patients, as well as in a convenience sample of 8 healthy volunteers and 22 conscious critically ill patients. The self-paced BCI analysed EEG in real time and modulated frequency and pitch of a tone that patients controlled by attempting a motor task: patients were provided with instructions 'to make the beep sound faster, keep opening and closing your right hand' alternating with 'to make the beep sound slower, stop opening and closing your right hand'. CMD was detected by applying machine learning to EEG recorded while patients listened to motor commands. Clinical outcomes were recovery of consciousness by hospital discharge, assessed with the Coma Recovery Scale-Revised, and recovery of functional independence at 12 months, defined as a Glasgow Outcome Scale-Extended score of 4 or higher (denoting the ability to function independently for 8 h or more). Additionally, we invited conscious patients as well as family members of conscious and clinically unresponsive patients to complete a survey on perceived utility and challenges associated with the BCI. In this study, 13 clinically unresponsive patients (32%) achieved BCI control and 8 of 41 (20%) were CMD-positive. All healthy volunteers and nine conscious patients (41%) were able to control the BCI. Median signal delay for BCI control was 49.8 [interquartile range (IQR) 39.9-75.6] seconds for clinically unresponsive patients, 17.1 (IQR 7.8-19.9) for conscious patients and 15.5 (IQR 12.9-21.2) for healthy volunteers. In the group of 41 clinically unresponsive patients, 4 of 13 patients with (31%) and 1 of 28 without BCI control (4%) recovered clinical consciousness at hospital discharge [odds ratio (OR) 21.2; 95% confidence interval (CI) 2.3-930.7]. At 12 months, 6 of 13 patients with (46%) and 2 of 28 without BCI control (7%) recovered functional independence (OR 9.9; 95% CI 1.8-81.6). None of the patients that were unable to control the BCI and were CMD-negative recovered. Patients with BCI control had shorter times to recovery of functional independence by 12 months than those who did not (hazards ratio 6.0; 95% CI 1.2-30.6). All respondents of the BCI survey (n = 37) expressed strong support for the potential future use of BCI devices. These results suggest that BCI control is a strong predictor of outcomes following acute brain injury and provide initial evidence that a bedside, EEG-based BCI system might permit bidirectional communication in critically ill unresponsive patients.

### Progenitor transcriptional alterations link fetal and adult pathology in Huntington's disease
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Battistella M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 22

Huntington's disease (HD) is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by a CAG repeat expansion in the HTT gene. Although the mutant huntingtin protein is expressed from conception, clinical symptoms typically emerge in mid-adulthood. Imaging and molecular studies have raised the possibility that HD may involve neurodevelopmental abnormalities, but direct evidence at the single-cell level in the human fetal brain has been lacking. Here we investigated whether early, cell type-specific transcriptional vulnerabilities arise during neurodevelopment, persist into adult pathology, and might be rescued. To address this, we performed single-cell transcriptomic profiling of the lateral ganglionic eminence (LGE) and cortex from a rare, anatomically intact human HD fetal brain, alongside matched controls. These data were complemented by an allelic series of HD mouse embryos and cross-compared with already published human post-mortem brains and brain organoid models' datasets. Neural progenitors displayed the highest number of differentially expressed genes in the human fetal brain, and were also impacted in the HD mouse model, where additional cell types displayed transcriptional alterations. Notably, human HD progenitor transcriptional signatures were moderately enriched in the developing mouse LGE. These progenitors exhibited increased expression of synaptic pathways coupled with reduced expression of cell maintenance genes. Cross-comparison with publicly available post-mortem HD datasets revealed that components of this transcriptional signature persist into adulthood. Furthermore, cellular interactions were found to mitigate these alterations, highlighting a potentially rescuable fetal-to-adult transcriptional signature with important implications for HD pathogenesis and therapeutic strategies. Together, these findings reinforce the view of HD as a disorder with developmental origins, in which early transcriptional changes may shape neuronal function while increasing susceptibility to later neurodegeneration.
