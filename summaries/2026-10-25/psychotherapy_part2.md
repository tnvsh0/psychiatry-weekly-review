### Concentrated Exposure Therapy vs Internet-Based Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Netw Open *(IF: 120.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Jelinek L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 1

Key Points Question Is the Bergen 4-day treatment for obsessive-compulsive disorder superior to internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy in reducing obsessive-compulsive symptoms? Findings In this randomized clinical trial of 119 adults with obsessive-compulsive disorder, the Bergen 4-day treatment group was found to be superior to internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy in reducing obsessive-compulsive symptoms, with large effects at 3-month follow-up. Meaning These findings suggest the Bergen 4-day treatment is effective for patients with obsessive-compulsive disorder.

This randomized clinical trial evaluates the efficacy of the Bergen 4-day treatment compared with internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy in reducing obsessive-compulsive disorder symptom severity.

Importance Given the high prevalence, chronicity, and associated disability of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), optimizing treatment is a global priority. The Bergen 4-day treatment (B4DT), a concentrated exposure-based intervention, may reduce treatment burden and improve access to evidence-based care. Objective To evaluate the efficacy of B4DT compared with internet-based cognitive behavioral therapy (iCBT) in reducing OCD symptom severity. Design, Setting, and Participants Single-center randomized controlled trial (1:1 allocation). Between May 8, 2024, and August 8, 2025, adults (aged 18 to 72 years) with OCD were enrolled and were followed up for 3 months. Key exclusion criteria included schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, acute suicidality, substance dependence, and recent psychopharmacological changes. Outcomes were assessed at baseline, 6 weeks, 8 weeks (posttreatment), and 3 months (follow-up) by masked evaluators. Interventions Patients were randomized to B4DT (including preparatory sessions) or an 8-week unguided iCBT program. Main Outcomes and Measures The primary outcome was the change in OCD symptom severity, measured by the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS). Secondary outcomes included self-reported OCD (OCI-R), dysfunctional cognitions, quality of life, depressive symptoms, experiential avoidance, global functioning, self-esteem, and treatment satisfaction. Results A total of 253 individuals were screened, and 119 were enrolled after 53 were found to be ineligible and 81 declined to participate. Sixty of the 119 randomized participants (mean [SD] age, 33.5 [10.1] years; 79 [66.4%] women) were assigned to B4DT and 59 to iCBT. The primary analysis included all randomized participants (intention-to-treat). B4DT was associated with significantly greater reductions in OCD symptoms (Y-BOCS) than iCBT, with large effects at posttreatment (mean [SE] difference, −7.9 [0.9]; 95% CI, −9.7 to −6.0; Hedges g , −1.6) and sustained effects at 3-month follow-up (mean [SE] difference, −5.6 [1.1]; 95% CI, −7.8 to −3.4; Hedges g , −1.0). Secondary outcomes consistently favored B4DT. Response and remission rates at 3-month follow-up were higher with B4DT (35 of 57 [61.4%] vs 11 of 44 [25.0%] and 24 of 57 [42.1%] vs 6 of 44 [13.6%], respectively). Conclusions and Relevance In this randomized clinical trial of 119 adults with OCD, B4DT was superior to iCBT in reducing OCD symptom severity, with sustained benefits. Its brief, concentrated format may represent a scalable strategy to improve access to effective OCD treatment globally. Trial Registration German Clinical Trials Register: DRKS00034236

Introduction Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) affects 1% to 3% of the population and is associated with substantial functional impairment, reduced quality of life, and societal burden worldwide. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 Exposure and response prevention (ERP) is the recommended first-line psychological treatment 5 , 6 and has demonstrated robust efficacy across meta-analyses. 7 , 8 However, access to and usage of ERP remain limited due to shortages of trained therapists, long waiting times, 9 and treatment refusal and dropout, 10 further limiting its scalability in routine care. These challenges have recently been highlighted in a report by the International OCD Foundation that points out persistent barriers to access to evidence-based OCD care. 11 As mental health systems increasingly emphasize efficiency and accessibility, optimizing delivery formats while preserving clinical benefits is a priority. 12 The Bergen 4-day treatment (B4DT) was developed as a concentrated ERP intervention (cERP) with an intention-focused approach that is delivered over 4 consecutive days following a brief preparatory phase. 13 By compressing treatment traditionally delivered over several months into 4 days, B4DT aims to enhance efficiency and accessibility while maintaining therapeutic intensity. A key innovation is the Leaning into Anxiety (LET) protocol, 13 in which patients are guided to intentionally engage with feared stimuli. This approach emphasizes willingness and approach behavior, potentially altering how patients relate to anxiety during and beyond exposure. Studies conducted primarily in Scandinavia report high response and remission rates. 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 However, the existing evidence is dominated by uncontrolled designs. Only 1 randomized clinical trial (RCT) has evaluated B4DT, comparing it with waiting list and structured self-help conditions. 19 Although that study provided important proof-of-concept evidence, it did not address whether B4DT confers incremental benefit over scalable digital cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) interventions that are increasingly being implemented within stepped-care systems. Moreover, waiting list comparisons may inflate estimated treatment effects relative to more stringent controls, 20 and psychotherapy trials more broadly can be susceptible to researcher allegiance effects associated with observed outcomes. 21 , 22 Determining whether a concentrated, specialist-delivered intervention, such as B4DT, confers meaningful advantages over scalable alternatives is central to contemporary stepped-care models. Internet-based CBT (iCBT) is an evidence-based and cost-effective approach that is increasingly being used, even with minimal guidance. 23 , 24 As digital-first pathways expand, comparative effectiveness data are needed to guide resource allocation, particularly given that digital interventions often yield smaller effects and have higher dropout rates than therapist-guided approaches. 25 Methodological considerations further complicate the interpretation of findings in prior B4DT studies. In several studies, baseline was assessed immediately before the core exposure phase, following a preparatory period. Because B4DT includes structured preparation, defining baseline at that point may obscure treatment-related change and limit comparability with standard ERP trials, which define baseline before initiation of therapeutic procedures. In addition, this approach may introduce sampling bias as individuals who discontinue during the preparatory phase may not be represented. This RCT addressed these gaps by comparing B4DT with an active, evidence-based iCBT intervention. 26 Baseline assessment was conducted prior to the preparatory phase to capture the full intervention trajectory and align with established ERP methodology. This study provided the first evaluation of B4DT against an active, evidence-based treatment and generated clinically actionable evidence to inform treatment selection, implementation strategies, and health system resource allocation in OCD care. ### Cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression as processes of change in transdiagnostic guided self-help cognitive behavioral therapy for adults with internalizing distress: Disaggregating within- versus between-person variability
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology (J Consult Clin Psychol) *(IF: 5.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** De Jesús-Romero R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep

OBJECTIVE: Processes of change in cognitive behavioral therapies (CBTs) remain frequently debated, perhaps due to methodological difficulties in conducting rigorous process research. We investigated two contrasting emotion regulation strategies, cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression, as processes of change in CBT disaggregating within- from between-person changes.

METHOD: We examined whether week-to-week changes in cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression (Emotion Regulation Questionnaire) predicted subsequent changes in distress (Kessler-6 Psychological Distress Scale) and well-being (World Health Organization-5 Well-Being Index) pooling two trials of a guided self-help CBT based on acceptance and commitment therapy. We used a random-intercept cross-lagged panel model to disaggregate between- versus within-person changes. Participants (N = 274) were 82% female, 80% non-Hispanic White, and mean age 37 years (SD = 13).

RESULTS: At the within-person level, week-to-week changes in reappraisal were consistently associated with subsequent changes in distress but less consistently with well-being. Changes in expressive suppression were less consistently associated with subsequent changes in distress or well-being. The associations between reappraisal and distress were robust to different model specifications, including detrending.

CONCLUSIONS: Cognitive reappraisal may be involved in the process of change in CBTs, including the so-called "third-wave" CBTs, which have traditionally de-emphasized its importance. (PsycInfo Database Record (c) 2026 APA, all rights reserved).

### In-session cortisol reactivity as a predictor of treatment response in Korean patients with PTSD
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yun JA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 9

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is associated with hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis dysregulation, and in-session cortisol reactivity during exposure-based therapy may carry prognostic value. This study examined whether within-session salivary cortisol reactivity during Written Exposure Therapy (WET) predicts treatment response in Korean patients with PTSD. In a secondary analysis of a non-randomized waitlist-controlled trial (N = 41), cortisol was collected before and 15 min after the writing task at each of five sessions. Treatment response was defined using the reliable change index and assessed with the CAPS-5 and PCL-5 at the 6-week assessment, 12-week follow-up, and 24-week follow-up. Post-session cortisol suppression was numerically observed across all five sessions but reached significance only at Sessions 2 and 3. No significant group differences in Session 1 cortisol reactivity were found at the 6-week assessment; however, at 12-week follow-up responders showed significantly greater Session 1 cortisol suppression than non-responders on both CAPS-5 and PCL-5 (rs = 0.502 and 0.651, respectively), and this discrimination persisted at 24-week follow-up for PCL-5 (r = 0.563) but not CAPS-5. Subjective distress and cortisol reactivity were largely independent. These findings suggest that efficient HPA axis regulation during the first WET session may serve as an early biological marker of treatment consolidation.

### Identifying predictors of treatment response in Hoarding Disorder using machine learning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zakrzewski JJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 16

The current gold standard treatment for Hoarding Disorder (HD) is cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). However, typical symptom reduction is 25% and only about one third of individuals achieve a clinically meaningful response. We used machine learning (ML) to identify predictors of treatment response using data from a non-inferiority trial of clinician vs peer-led group CBT for HD. Assessments were completed for 323 individuals with HD at post-treatment (N = 323) and at one-year follow-up (N = 183). Assessments included neuropsychological testing, symptom severity and psychiatric diagnoses, beliefs about treatment, treatment participation, and adherence measures. Outcomes included changes in HD severity (Saving Inventory-Revised) and functioning (Activities for Daily Living-Hoarding), which were modeled separately. Linear regression and decision tree models were optimized with cross-validation and used to select predictors of response. LASSO was used to distinguish predictors for symptom trajectories (sustained response, regressed response, late response, non-response) determined at one-year follow-up. Optimized models found that reduction in HD severity immediately post-treatment was predicted only by beliefs that treatment was beneficial. Improvement in HD functioning post-treatment was predicted by beneficial beliefs about treatment, memory related saving, and better sustained attention. At follow-up, higher HD severity reduction was predicted by identifying as non-White, more time maintaining progress, and beneficial treatment beliefs. Higher HD functioning reduction at follow-up was predicted by memory related saving, beneficial treatment beliefs and better performance on a sorting task at pre-treatment. Beliefs about treatment, possessions as memory support, sorting, attention, and visual memory may be key factors in treatment response.

### Neurofeedback for Management of Musculoskeletal Pain: A Randomized Control Trial Using DOMS
**כתב עת:** 📄 Pain Res Manag | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Gregersen KA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Introduction Chronic musculoskeletal (MSK) pain is associated with maladaptive neuroplasticity, often reflected in altered alpha activity (8–13 Hz). Neurofeedback may offer a way to modulate this activity, yet most protocols target alpha activity during rest, despite movement being a common pain trigger. This study investigated whether neurofeedback during movement could modulate alpha activity and reduce pain in healthy individuals experiencing induced pain via delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS). Method This experiment was a randomized, two‐armed, parallel‐group trial. Twenty‐nine participants were included (15 females, mean age = 28 years). Exclusions included neurological, psychiatric, MSK conditions. Participants were randomly assigned to an intervention group (aiming to reduce alpha activity) or a control group (aiming to increase it). Alpha activity was assessed as the alpha power during rest and movement. DOMS was induced using eccentric exercise of muscle extensor carpi radialis in Session 1. Neurofeedback was administered in Session 2, where participants underwent three blocks of neurofeedback training, using individualized EEG‐based visual feedback to modulate alpha activity. Session 3 assessed potential lasting neurofeedback effects. Pain was rated across the three sessions, which took place 24–48 h apart. Results DOMS induction was confirmed by increased pain ( p < 0.001, r = 0.82) and reduced alpha activity during movement ( p = 0.017, r = 0.49). Neurofeedback did not significantly alter alpha activity ( p = 0.114, W = 0.05 for the control group, p = 0.338, W = 0.037 for the intervention group). Conclusion A single neurofeedback session was insufficient to modulate brain activity or pain perception. Methodological factors, including limited training duration and participants’ strategies, may have contributed to the lack of an observable effect. Significance Although neurofeedback did not result in significant changes after one session, the observed alpha modulation following DOMS highlights the relevance of targeting neuroplasticity during MSK pain. Future studies should optimize training protocols and consider multisession designs to enhance clinical applicability.

1. Introduction WHO estimates that 20–33% of the world’s population is affected by chronic musculoskeletal (MSK) pain, making it a major cause of disability and reduced quality of life [ 1 ]. MSK pain often originates from repetitive movements or exertion, initially presenting as acute nociceptive pain. If unresolved, it might lead to maladaptive neuroplastic changes, including persistent activation of spinal neurons independent of peripheral stimuli and structural changes in the brain regions involved in pain processing [ 2 , 3 ]. These changes can contribute to the development of pathological chronic pain [ 1 , 4 – 6 ]. Current treatments include pharmacological and nonpharmacological approaches. Pharmacological treatments have side effects and can lead to dependence or addiction [ 7 ]. Nonpharmacological methods include passive modalities, such as heat or cold exposure, exercise, transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation, together with management of psychological factors [ 1 ]. There is limited and varying evidence on the efficacy of passive interventions [ 1 ]. Exercise therapy is widely recommended as a treatment for chronic MSK pain and is supported by systematic reviews and international clinical guidelines [ 8 , 9 ]. Nevertheless, treatment effects vary considerably between individuals, and long‐term adherence remains a challenge in clinical practice [ 9 , 10 ]. Together, these limitations highlight the need to explore complementary approaches that may further improve treatment outcomes, such as neurofeedback. Neurofeedback may counteract maladaptive plasticity by training individuals to regulate specific neural patterns [ 11 , 12 ]. EEG‐based neurofeedback for management of pain typically targets frequency bands related to pain processing, where alpha activity has received particular attention due to its association with pain perception [ 7 , 11 , 13 – 15 ]. Several studies have reported pain reduction following protocols aimed at increasing alpha power during rest [ 13 , 16 ], although other studies found no significant differences between active and sham groups, despite both showing increased alpha and reduced pain [ 18 , 19 ]. These mixed findings suggest that evidence for a consistent association between alpha activity modulation and pain reduction remains inconclusive. Importantly, MSK pain is often movement‐evoked, yet most neurofeedback protocols are designed for resting conditions. Mrachacz‐Kersting et al. [ 20 ] demonstrated that targeting alpha reduction during movement led to reductions in both alpha activity and pain after three sessions [ 20 ]. Similarly, Gervasio et al. [ 21 ] found that individuals with chronic MSK pain exhibited increased alpha power during movement but not at rest, compared to healthy controls, highlighting the relevance of movement‐specific alpha modulation [ 21 ]. This study aims to provide a proof of concept for the effectiveness on neurofeedback in modulating alpha activity during movement in the context of MSK pain management. Delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS), which induces acute MSK pain 24–48 h after exercise, has been proposed as a suitable experimental model of chronic MSK pain [ 22 ]. Moreover, previous research has shown that a single neurofeedback session can effectively modulate event‐related alpha activity in healthy participants [ 23 ]. CONSORT guidelines were followed for reporting. Participants were recruited via social media and oral outreach. A total of 30 individuals were enrolled. Inclusion criteria were age between 18 and 65 years and general good health, assessed based on participants’ self‐report [ 24 ]. Exclusion criteria included active drug use, pregnancy, or a previous or current diagnosis of neurological, psychiatric, or MSK disorders, recent surgery or fractures, participation in other clinical trials involving pain, or engaging in muscle training more than twice per week. Additional exclusion criteria were consumption of alcohol, caffeine, nicotine, or painkillers on test days, and inability to cooperate. One participant withdrew after the first session due to illness, resulting in 29 participants completing all three sessions. The final sample consisted of 15 females and 14 males (mean age = 28 years, SD = 9.6). The majority were right‐handed ( n = 27, 93.1%). Prior to participation, all individuals received written and verbal information about the study procedures, potential risks, and their rights as research participants. ### TIRELESS study protocol: non-inferiority study of nurse-led web-based cognitive behavioral therapy for cancer-related fatigue in patients with incurable cancer
**כתב עת:** 📄 BMC Cancer | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** van der Pas HB et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 28

BACKGROUND: Fatigue is a prevalent and debilitating symptom for patients with incurable cancer. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) can significantly reduce cancer-related fatigue in this patient group. However, implementation of CBT into clinical care is difficult as the intervention is time-intensive, costly, and requires face-to-face consultations with a specialized CBT therapist. Since nurses already support patient with symptom management, including fatigue, internet supported CBT delivered by nurses could be a promising alternative for patients with incurable cancer. The primary aim of this study is to determine the non-inferiority of nurse-led web-based CBT in its effect on cancer-related fatigue, compared to CBT provided by CBT therapists.

METHODS: The study is a prospective, non-randomized study using historical cohorts as the benchmark for comparison. Severely fatigued patients with incurable cancer of a solid tumor receive a 12-week nurse-led web-based CBT intervention. Primary and secondary outcome measures are assessed at baseline, post-intervention (14 weeks), and follow-up (26 weeks). The primary outcome measure is fatigue severity assessed with the Checklist Individual Strength (CIS-subscale fatigue severity). Secondary outcomes include functional impairment, quality of life and fatigue assessed with EORTC-QLQ-C30, and health care costs. Primary and secondary outcomes of the TIRELESS study and the benchmark are compared to determine non-inferiority. Non-inferiority of fatigue reduction is concluded if the upper bound of the one-sided 95% CI does not exceed + 5 on the CIS, with higher scores indicating more severe fatigue. In addition, interviews are conducted to gain insight in nurses' and patients' experiences with the intervention. These data are analyzed applying a thematic analysis method. Recruitment has started in June 2024.

DISCUSSION: Especially for this burdened patient group, integration of CBT for fatigue in routine medical care is essential. With the TIRELESS study, we aim to reduce patient burden, foster scalability and accessibility of CBT for fatigue, and increase patients' self-management. If this approach is non-inferior to the original evidence-based (benchmark) intervention, this will enable implementation of cancer-related fatigue interventions for the growing group of patients with incurable cancer.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: NCT06511518; date of registration: 23/02/2024.
