### Confounds in Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging-Part I: Mechanisms
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Inglis B, D'Esposito M | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

In this review, the first in a three-part series, we consider the origins of confounding in fMRI. We review the signal properties of an fMRI experiment and the intended sensitivity to neurovascular coupling, the hemodynamic response function, and the spatial-temporal biases imposed by the heterogeneous anatomy of the vascular system. We then explore the various mechanisms that can leave nuisance signals, often termed physiologic noise, in fMRI data. All mechanisms having a biological dependence are considered, including mechanical effects from head motion and respiration, blood gases, brain motion from the cardiac pressure wave, blood pressure, and scanner instabilities that couple with the participant's body.

### Confounds in Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging-Part II: Modifiers
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Inglis B, D'Esposito M | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

In the first part of this three-part series, we reviewed the mechanisms that cause nuisance signals in fMRI data. Here, in the second part of the series, we review "human factors" that modulate these confounding mechanisms. The modulators may be trait characteristics such as age or bodyweight, or they may be state dependencies that alter the relative contributions of nuisance signals in the fMRI data. A wide array of psychological (e.g., motivation/arousal, cognitive strategy, task engagement), lifestyle (e.g., exercise, sleep, and dietary habits), experimental (e.g., scanner anxiety, task-related movement), and situational (e.g., prandial state, time of day, menses) factors can affect the brain state directly as well as via changes in systemic physiology mediated by autonomic nervous responses.

### Spatial Memory Guides Attention before Object Recognition: Evidence from Lateralized N1pc Responses
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience (J Cogn Neurosci) *(IF: 4.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fan C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 21

Visual search is facilitated when targets reappear within invariant distractor layouts (contextual cueing). Although contextual cueing can bias attention within 150 msec, it remains unclear whether this guidance relies on global spatial configuration or requires object identity. We recorded ERPs while participants searched for targets in repeated versus novel arrays. Critically, each trial began with a placeholder display that conveyed only spatial configuration, preceding the search display by 500 msec. Following learning, targets were relocated and swapped their locations with a distractor in the opposite visual field, such that the overall search layouts remained unchanged. ERPs time-locked to placeholder onset revealed an early poster contralateral negativity (N1pc, 140-160 msec) for repeated contexts, despite the absence of target-specific object information. After relocation, this effect reversed polarity, indicating persistent orienting to the learned target location. A second N1pc, followed by N2pc and CDA, emerged only after search onset. These findings show that learned spatial structure rapidly guides attention, whereas effective selection requires additional processing of object information.

### Cognitive forecasting: How intuitive theories of attention shape anticipated risk preferences
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Demircioglu D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

How do people forecast their own and others' risk preferences? Although it is well established that affective forecasts shape decision-making, less is known about where these forecasts come from. Five studies demonstrate that people rely on intuitive theories of attention when forecasting future affect-such as dread and savoring while waiting for a risk to be resolved-with these affective forecasts in turn shaping willingness to take such risks. In Study 1, forecasts of the participant's own expectations and attention were systematically related to forecasts of positive and negative affect during a waiting period, which in turn predicted risk preferences. Studies 2a and 2b demonstrate substantial heterogeneity in intuitive attention-affect theories: some participants believed attention amplifies the emotional consequences of expectations, whereas others believed attention is uniformly affectively negative. These differences led to sharply divergent predictions about when attention should increase or decrease risk-taking. Study 3 shows that attention is also informative about expectations: participants differed in whether they believed attention signals optimism or pessimism, producing downstream consequences for affective forecasts and risk-taking. Finally, Study 4 reveals that many people endorse asymmetric causal models, viewing optimism as a cause of risk-taking, but risk-taking as a cause of attention. Together, these findings show that forecasts of risk preferences depend critically on people's intuitive theories about how cognition shapes affect and how affective utility is generated over time. By integrating affective forecasting, attention, and decision-making under uncertainty, this work highlights the role of metacognitive beliefs in shaping economic preferences.

🔗 [קישור למאמר ב-PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42896339/)

### Successful modal reasoning depends on WHAT went WHERE
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mazalik P, Halberda J | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Modal reasoning requires quantifying over possibilities (e.g., categorizing events as necessary, possible, and impossible). This suite of abilities matures during the preschool years, but toddlers perform better in some modal reasoning tasks than others. We propose that these mixed findings reflect a cognitive distinction: toddlers perform worse when reasoning about ambiguous locations of objects (WHERE) than when reasoning about ambiguous kinds of objects (WHAT). In Experiments 1 and 2, we directly compared these forms of ambiguity using matched tasks. Consistent with the hypothesis, toddlers performed substantially better when reasoning about ambiguous kinds than ambiguous locations. Experiment 3 revealed a similar asymmetry in language comprehension, with preschool children showing better comprehension of modal terms such as "can" and "have to" in a What-based task than in a Where-based task. Thus, modal reasoning emerges earlier than previously suggested, and diversity in results reflects differences in how kind and location information are represented and processed.

### Metacognitive monitoring in Kenyan, Chinese, and U.S. children
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Buehler FJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 19

Theories of cognitive development propose that cultural values shape metacognitive skills. However, cross-cultural research on metacognition remains limited and often relies on verbal assessments that may lack validity across cultural contexts. The present study examined metacognitive monitoring in Kenyan, Chinese, and U.S. children (N = 95; 6-10-year-olds; 46% female) using a non-verbal problem-solving task in which children assembled objects according to a model. Across all three cultural contexts, children exhibited various monitoring behaviors (e.g., checking the model), and the frequency of these behaviors increased with age. Cross-cultural differences emerged in the specific monitoring strategies children used: Chinese children engaged in more thorough monitoring than Kenyan and U.S. children. These findings highlight both culturally-recurrent and culturally-variable patterns in children's metacognitive monitoring.

### Noncausal joint-action scenes facilitate Mandarin-speaking preschoolers' interpretation of intransitive verbs with conjoined subjects
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 18

Children learn novel verb meanings by integrating sentence-level cues with information from the surrounding event. Although sentence frames provide information about verb transitivity, success with intransitive frames varies across languages and tasks. In Mandarin, preschoolers have been reported to struggle with conjoined-subject intransitive sentences, raising the question of whether this difficulty reflects a limitation in syntactic bootstrapping or partly stems from how children construe the comparison scenes. In a forced-choice pointing task, Mandarin-speaking 3-, 4-, and 5-year-olds heard transitive or conjoined-subject intransitive sentences containing novel verbs and chose between a causal scene and a noncausal scene. Critically, the noncausal scene was either a parallel-action scene or a joint-action scene. In the joint-action scene, the two participants acted together in physical contact but without a causal asymmetry. Children at all ages reliably matched transitive sentences to causal scenes. For intransitives, 3-year-olds performed at chance in both scene conditions, 4-year-olds succeeded only with joint-action scenes, and 5-year-olds succeeded in both. Thus, performance with intransitives improved with age and was facilitated when the noncausal alternative was easier to construe as a single noncausal event. These findings suggest that previously reported failures with Mandarin conjoined-subject intransitives cannot be attributed solely to insensitivity to argument structure, but also depend on how candidate events are visually and conceptually represented. More broadly, the results are consistent with a multiple-factor view of verb learning, in which syntactic cues interact with semantic, and event-based information.

### Self-report measures of mindfulness for children: convergent validity, predictive validity, and impacts of social desirability bias
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhuang W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 17

Mindfulness-based interventions have shown promise in supporting psychological well-being in children. However, mindfulness is commonly measured using self-report questionnaires, a method that might be problematic due to its reliance on children's accurate self-perceptions of their own mindfulness and the potential influence of social desirability bias. In the current study, we examined the convergent validity, the predictive validity, and the influence of social desirability bias on three mindfulness self-report measures for children ages 9-13  years (N = 109). Results showed that children's self-reported mindfulness demonstrated good convergent validity and predictive validity, as higher levels of self-reported mindfulness were positively associated with higher levels of parent-reported executive functioning. Self-reported mindfulness also covaried with social desirability bias. However, controlling for this bias did not substantively affect the convergent validity nor the predictive validity of self-reported mindfulness. These findings suggest that children's self-reported mindfulness also captures social desirability bias, but this bias may not affect conclusions about mindfulness when assessed as a latent factor across multiple measures. Future work measuring children's mindfulness with self-reports should account for social desirability bias when possible, as well as explore objective methods of assessing mindfulness in children.

🔗 [קישור למאמר ב-PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42892216/)
