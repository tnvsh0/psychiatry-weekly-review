BACKGROUND: To determine whether in utero exposure to antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) is associated with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and intellectual disability (ID) in offspring and to test robustness to familial confounding.

METHODS: We conducted a nationwide population-based cohort of all live births between January 1, 2004, and December 31, 2016, with follow-up through 2021, linking birth, prescription, and health registries. Exposure was maternal dispensing of AEDs during pregnancy. We estimated hazard ratios with Cox models adjusted for parental demographics and psychiatric/medical comorbidities. To address shared familial factors, we performed sibling comparisons using stratified Cox models with mother as the stratum. We further conducted trimester-specific analyses, restricted to any neurodevelopmental disorder because of limited numbers for individual outcomes, by separately defining maternal AED dispensing during the first, second, and third trimesters to evaluate whether associations differed according to the timing of prenatal exposure.

RESULTS: Among 2,196,156 births, 9,809 (0.45%) were exposed to AEDs during pregnancy. ### Bully-victim prevalence, over-time stability, and risk and protective factors during elementary school
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Gao X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 17

BACKGROUND: Children who both bully other children and are victims of bullies (BVs) are at greater risk for externalizing and internalizing problems, poor academic outcomes, and increased involvement in criminal behavior later in life. Yet the early emergence, over-time stability, and risk and protective factors for being BVs are largely unknown.

METHODS: We analyzed data from 7,326 children in third through fifth grade participating in the Early Childhood Longitudinal Study, Kindergarten Class of 2010-2011 (ECLS-K: 2011), a nationally representative and multiyear longitudinal cohort. We first descriptively identified children exhibiting high levels of both bullying perpetration and victimization. We examined BV prevalence and over-time stability. We then used survey-weighted logistic regression models to identify risk and protective factors for BV, including repeatedly across time in analyses adjusting for prior perpetration and victimization.

RESULTS: Between 9% and 20% of children were BVs across 3rd-5th grade. About 40% of BVs in one grade were BVs in the next grade. BVs concurrently displayed elevated socioemotional problems, executive dysfunction, strained student-teacher relationships, and lower academic achievement. ### The effect of a Dutch early language intervention on children's oral language skills: a cluster randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** van Driel S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 17

BACKGROUND: Early oral language skills are crucial for children's later academic and social development, yet systematic support for oral language development remains limited in Dutch primary education. The Nuffield Early Language Intervention (NELI) has proven effective in improving oral language skills among preschool children in the United Kingdom. The present study investigated whether the Dutch adaptation of NELI is similarly effective for Dutch preschoolers with relatively weak language skills in the language of instruction.

METHODS: A total of 44 primary schools, 116 classrooms (58 intervention; 58 control), and 692 children aged 4 to 7 years participated in a cluster randomized controlled trial. Classrooms within schools were randomly assigned to intervention or control conditions. Within each classroom, five or six children with the lowest scores on a language screening measure were selected. Children in the intervention condition participated in the 20-week NELI program, receiving daily individual and small-group tutoring, while children in the control condition received education as usual. Before and after the intervention, children's oral language skills were individually assessed using standardized language measures.

RESULTS: Structural equation modeling showed that children in the intervention condition made significantly greater improvements than those in the control condition on two latent language variables: lexical and syntactic skills (d = .15) and narrative skills (d = .19). Effects were not moderated by pre-test language scores, age, home language, sex, or parental education level.

CONCLUSIONS: The Dutch adaptation of NELI can effectively support the oral language development of preschoolers with relatively weak oral language skills. Although effect sizes were small, the findings indicate that NELI is a promising approach for supporting early oral language development in Dutch schools.

### Aberrant brain functional connectivity is associated with clinical symptoms in tourette syndrome: a triple network model perspective
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Wang X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 15

Tourette Syndrome (TS) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by motor/vocal tics and premonitory urges. The triple network model (TNM)-including the salience network (SAN), frontoparietal network (FPN, also known as control network, Con), and default mode network (DMN)-provides a framework for understanding large-scale brain dysfunction. However, the TNM's role in the "urge-to-tic" mechanism in pediatric TS remains unclear. This study aimed to characterize network-level functional connectivity (FC) within the TNM in pediatric TS, examine its associations with clinical symptoms, and propose a refined neurobiological hypothesis for the urge-to-tic process. We enrolled 68 children with TS and 65 matched controls. Resting-state fMRI was used to quantify FC within the TNM. Clinical severity was assessed using the Yale Global Tic Severity Scale (YGTSS), Premonitory Urge for Tics Scale (PUTS), and Sensory Phenomenon Assessment Scale (SPAS). Group differences in FC were identified, and random forest (RF) regression models were employed to predict symptom severity based on FC within the TNM. Children with TS exhibited significant inter-network FC alterations, specifically involving increased connectivity between the left DMN and bilateral SAN. While YGTSS and SPAS score were significantly associated with TNM connectivity, no significant correlation was found with PUTS scores. The RF models successfully predicted YGTSS (R 2  = 0.169) and SPAS scores (R 2  = 0.139), with the left DMN-right SAN circuit emerging as the most robust predictor. TNM dysconnectivity is a core neural signature of pediatric TS, highlighting the TNM as a crucial framework for understanding the pathophysiology of the "urge-to-tic" process.

### Predictors of developmental change in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a 25-year prospective study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mathewson KJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

For a significant proportion of children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), symptoms persist in adulthood, with enduring consequences for psychosocial functioning. While hereditary factors for this multifactorial disorder have garnered considerable attention, environmental risks for ADHD remain relatively under-researched. The main goal of this study was to identify characteristics of the child or the family environment that may influence longitudinal trajectories of ADHD. Using growth mixture modeling (GMM), we identified three distinct latent class trajectories of ADHD symptoms assessed at 8, 14, and 23 years of age in a prospectively-followed cohort of 306 participants (46% male; 94% White): a stable high trajectory (14.3%), a descending trajectory (24.7%), and a stable low trajectory (61.0%). As expected, the high class had more psychosocial impairments and lower socioeconomic attainment than the other classes at age 32 years. Male sex, low birth weight, child oppositional behavior, and childhood exposure to negative maternal mood were associated with ADHD symptoms in the high and descending classes. Retrospectively reported child maltreatment was more closely associated with membership in the high class than the low class. Sexual abuse was more closely associated with membership in the high class than the descending class. Further study is warranted to ascertain whether child maltreatment is instrumental in maintaining ADHD symptoms in children who are vulnerable to ADHD, and to determine the timing of its detrimental effects.225 words.
