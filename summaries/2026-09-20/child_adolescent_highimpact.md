### Health service use of young people after mental health admission far from home: a retrospective cohort study in England

BACKGROUND: Severe mental illness requiring inpatient admission is associated with poorer mental health and physical and social outcomes. Out-of-area admissions persist in child and adolescent mental health services (CAMHS) but are associated with longer length of stay and increased family burden. However, their association with future health service use is unknown. Using linked mental health and national health-care data, we examined patterns of health service use up to 60 months following out-of-area psychiatric admission versus within-area psychiatric admission in young people younger than 18 years.

METHODS: The Maudsley Clinical Record Interactive Search (CRIS) database was used to identify all young people (aged <18 years) who had at least one mental health-related admission between Jan 1, 2009, and Dec 31, 2023, to South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust in England. Admission was categorised as out of area (>50 miles [80 km or 60 min away from home address) or within area. Linkage to the Hospital Episode Statistics enabled analysis of health service use (emergency department presentations, and general hospital admissions for non-mental health-related or mental health-related reasons) in the 1 month, 12 months, and 60 months after discharge. Cox regression was used to investigate associations between admission out of area and subsequent health service use, adjusting for demographic, clinical, and admission characteristics.

### Adolescents' Experiences of Online Harms by Platform
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Pediatrics (JAMA Pediatr) *(IF: 27.6)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lee AY et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 18

IMPORTANCE: Concerns about the risks adolescents face online are rising, and existing research on online harms rarely examines platform-level differences.

OBJECTIVE: To estimate the prevalence of negative experiences reported by US adolescents online, offline, and across 20 different online platforms using a national survey.

DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This was a cross-sectional, nationally representative, pilot survey of US adolescents aged 13 to 17 years conducted from February to December 2025 through the probability-based Understanding America Study panel. Self-reported use and negative experiences on 20 online platforms (eg, Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, and YouTube) were assessed.

EXPOSURES: Self-reported use of 20 online communication platforms (eg, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, and YouTube) in the past 4 weeks.

MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Prevalence of adolescents' reporting experiencing major risks and harms online, offline, and for each of 20 online platforms in the last 4 weeks. Harms include harassment, sexual solicitation, exposure to violent content, and technology-related sleep disruption. Survey-weighted logistic regression examined demographic associations.

RESULTS: The weighted sample included 472 adolescents (mean [SE] age 14.9 [0.1] years; 46.8% female) from 47 states. Across online and offline contexts, 44.7% witnessed harassment and 29.8% reported experiencing it directly in the past 4 weeks. Online exposure exceeded offline exposure for several harms, including reported exposure to violent imagery (10.7% vs 1.6%), unwanted sexual images (13.4% vs 2.0%), and content encouraging self-harm (9.7% vs 5.2%), while direct harassment was reported at similar rates offline and online (18.5% vs 16.4%). More than half (51.5%) self-reported sleep disruption due to technology use. Prevalence varied by platform.
