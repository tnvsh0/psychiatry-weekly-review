### The Future of Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies in Mental Health: Prospects, Challenges, and a Roadmap for Personalized Treatment
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Qiu N et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 18

BACKGROUND: Artificial intelligence (AI), alongside emerging digital and neurotechnologies, is increasingly incorporated into mental health care, psychosomatic medicine, and psychotherapy. However, the clinical value of these tools cannot be judged by technical performance alone. These fields are characterized by diagnostic heterogeneity, reliance on language-based and self-reported information, complex biopsychosocial pathways, and substantial interindividual variability in treatment response. Clinically meaningful progress therefore requires integrating technological progress and biological markers with patients' lived experience, personal history, and social context to catalyze person-centered precision assessment, decision-making and intervention.

SUMMARY: This review examines how AI and emerging technologies may contribute to mental health care across diagnostic assessment, biomarker development, treatment selection, personalized neuromodulation, adaptive interventions, and conversational agents. We argue that AI should support and improve, not replace, clinical assessments and care by integrating complex biopsychosocial data into precision person-centered decision making and intervention. Realizing this potential will require careful attention to clinical validation, safety, equity, commodification and governance.

KEY MESSAGES: The clinical value of AI and emerging technologies will depend on their responsible integration into context-sensitive, person-centered mental health care. AI has the potential to strengthen diagnostic assessment, inform treatment selection, and expand therapeutic options while preserving the centrality of the human therapeutic alliance. Successful implementation requires combining computational precision with clinical judgment, ethical oversight, and demonstrated clinical benefit to improve clinical outcomes and quality of life.

### Individual level predictors of treatment use: A disaggregation of within- and between-person associations
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sorid S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 12

OBJECTIVE: Studies showing associations between impaired clinical functioning and treatment use are predominantly based on cross-sectional comparisons or a limited number of longitudinal assessments. Using these designs, it is unknown whether individuals engage in treatment on occasions of within-person symptom increases or social support reductions. This study examined the relations between depressive symptoms, family and friend support, and self-reported stressors and treatment use.

METHOD: Participants (N = 1417) completed up to seven annual assessments assessing the study constructs. Multilevel modeling was used to examine the relations between fluctuations in depressive symptoms, family and friend support, and perceived life events and treatment use concurrently and prospectively.

RESULTS: We found that within-person fluctuations in depressive symptoms were associated with treatment use concurrently, as well as at the next annual assessment, controlling for family and friend support and self-reported stressors. Fluctuations in family and friend support and self-reported stressors were inconsistently associated with treatment use controlling for depressive symptoms.

CONCLUSIONS: Consistent with prevailing expectations, we found that fluctuations in depressive symptoms were most consistently associated with concurrent and future treatment use. Thus, individuals are more likely to use treatment when they are experiencing higher levels of symptoms than they usually experience.

### Intrusive memories as conditioned responses to odours: A critical test of cue-specificity
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Vossoughi L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 15

Intrusive memories, a hallmark of trauma-related psychopathology, have been conceptualised as conditioned responses (CRs) to trauma-related cues. Yet, some real-world and laboratory findings challenge whether such memories are indeed a result of a neutral conditioned stimulus (CS) signaling a distressing outcome (unconditioned stimulus). Understanding which associative learning processes contribute to intrusive memories would help optimise treatments. The present study critically tested the conditioning account of intrusive memories by examining their cue-specificity. Undergraduates (N = 91) completed a within-subject conditioned-film paradigm using neutral odours as CSs (CS1+, CS2+, CS-) paired with three film types depicting accidents/injury, violence/threat, or neutral scenes. Participants recorded intrusive memories in daily life for eight days. To elicit cue-specific intrusions, participants sprayed an odour (CS2+) on their clothes either upon leaving the lab (Day 0, n = 45) or three days later at home (Day 3, n = 46). On Day 7, participants completed a task in the lab assessing intrusive memories cued by the CS odours. Although exposure to the odour cue on Day 3 produced more intrusive memories than a control odour, these were not selective for the associated film type. In the lab, partial cue-specificity emerged: one conditioned odour (CS1+) elicited mostly cue-specific intrusions, whereas the other (CS2+) evoked intrusions from both distressing film types. Explicit memory for the contingencies enhanced cue specificity in the lab, but not in daily life. These findings call into question the conceptualisation of intrusive memories as CRs, suggesting that associative learning contributes to intrusive memories, but that cue-specificity may be context dependent.

### A Transdiagnostic IPT-Based Intervention for Global Crises
**כתב עת:** 📄 Am J Psychother | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Haruvi-Catalan L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

This Psychotherapy Tools article presents an ultra-brief, transdiagnostic crisis intervention protocol designed for use during national emergencies such as war, natural disasters, or pandemics. Based on interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT) and integrating skills from cognitive-behavioral therapy and dialectical behavior therapy, the protocol addresses acute psychological distress, fosters resilience, and guides ongoing care. The intervention was adapted after the October 7, 2023, events in Israel and consists of five flexible sessions targeting emotional, interpersonal, behavioral, cognitive, physical, and spiritual domains. It is transdiagnostic and suitable for diverse populations, including individuals with anxiety, posttraumatic stress disorder, and depression. The protocol emphasizes rapid assessment, safety planning, skill development, and follow-up, aiming to reduce symptoms and prevent long-term psychopathology. Delivered by trained mental health professionals, this approach increases access to care, supports early intervention, and can be adapted for nonspecialist providers. Preliminary clinical experience suggests feasibility, although further research is needed to establish efficacy.
