### Developmental cascades of daily living skills matter for adult outcomes in autism
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Clarke E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

Daily living skills (DLS) predict adult success for autistic individuals. However, most research on DLS in autism relies on cross-sectional data and DLS summary scores, limiting understanding of how specific skills across development relate to adult outcomes. Using longitudinal data from autistic adults ( n  = 232, 19% female, 69% White) followed from early childhood into their early thirties (m age  = 32.6 years, SD = 7.58, range 19-35), this study used machine learning techniques to identify item-level DLS from ages 5 ( n  = 123), 9 ( n  = 152), 14 ( n  = 154), and 18 ( n  = 143) that predicted adult employment, residential status, social relationships, and well-being. DLS items demonstrated predictive utility for employment (age 9 model  R 2  = .557) and social relationships (age 14 model ROC AUC = .839). Notably, DLS measured at age 5 explained a large proportion of variance in adult vocational outcomes ( R 2  = .507). Predictive strength for residential status increased across development (age 18 model ROC AUC = .906), whereas DLS showed limited association with subjective well-being (age 14 model  R 2  = .096). Community-based skills (e.g., money use, telephone skills) consistently emerged as top predictors. Findings are interpreted within a developmental cascade framework, highlighting how DLS competencies in childhood and adolescence may inform experiences for autistic adults.

### Longitudinal predictors of internalizing and externalizing behaviors in autism: Examining relationships with ritualistic behavior and social diagnostic features
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Surmacz M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

Autistic youth commonly experience co-occurring mental health conditions. Core autism features have been linked to internalizing and externalizing symptoms, yet longitudinal pathways remain unclear. This study examined how ritualistic and social diagnostic features predict later internalizing and externalizing behaviors in school-age autistic children. Caregivers reported ritualistic behavior (PDDBI), social diagnostic features (SRS-2), and internalizing and externalizing behaviors (CASI-5; BASC-3) at two time points (baseline M age  = 8.50, SD = 1.62, range = 6.0-11.5; follow-up M age  = 12.67, SD = 1.79, range = 8.7-16.1). Children (n = 139) were 79.1% male (n = 110), 20.9% female (n = 29), 5% African American/Black (n = 7), 0.7% American Indian/Alaska Native (n = 1), 7.2% Asian (n = 10), 17.3% Mixed Race (n = 24), 1.4% Other (n = 2), 68.3% White (n = 95), 17.3% Hispanic/Latinx (n = 24), and 82.7% non-Hispanic/Latinx (n = 115). Ritualistic behavior and social diagnostic features were associated at baseline (r = .590). Baseline ritualistic behavior predicted later internalizing behaviors, while neither social diagnostic features nor ritualistic behavior predicted later externalizing behaviors. Findings underscore the importance of examining specific core autism features, particularly ritualistic behavior, as early indicators of internalizing and externalizing challenges across development.

### Changes in dyadic emotional flexibility and expressivity during parent-child interactions following successful treatment of child anxiety
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Development and Psychopathology (Dev Psychopathol) *(IF: 5.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Van der Giessen D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

Child anxiety is linked to emotions during parent-child interactions, and observing emotional flexibility and expressivity may reveal treatment-related changes. This observational study investigated whether changes in dyadic emotional flexibility and expressivity were associated with treatment non-response (responders vs. non-responders), and whether treatment modality (child vs. family cognitive behavior theory (CBT)) and parent gender (mother- vs. father-child dyads) affected these changes. Participants were 116 referred children with anxiety disorders (59.4% girls,  M age  = 12.4 years,  SD  = 2.7) and both parents, enrolled in a randomized controlled trial comparing child CBT ( n  = 58) with family CBT ( n  = 58). Anxiety was assessed using the Anxiety Disorders Interview Schedule. Father-child and mother-child dyads were videotaped separately pre- and post-treatment discussing a conflict. Affect (positive, negative, neutral) was continuously coded. Dyadic measures of emotional flexibility (transitions, dispersion, and average duration) and expressivity (positive, negative) were derived from state space grids. Hierarchical linear models showed improvements in dyadic emotional flexibility among treatment responders, and in dyadic positive affect regardless of whether CBT was successful changes did not differ by parent gender or treatment modality. Dyadic negative affect increased only in responders within father-child dyads and in individual CBT. Findings highlight changes in flexible emotional exchanges of parent-child dyads after successful CBT for child anxiety.

### Family communication and child outcome during the pandemic
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Developmental Psychology (Dev Psychol) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Fivush R | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct

The COVID-19 pandemic led to a worldwide crisis in which most families sheltered in place for long periods of time. We know that children, preschool through adolescence, generally suffered negative consequences of the shutdown, but we know less about the ways in which families communicated with each other during the pandemic or how this might be related to child outcome. The studies presented in this special issue examine this question from multiple theoretical perspectives and employing multiple methodologies. Both what was talked about and how it was talked about were studied. Individual difference factors, including family structure and resources, parental stress and depression, and child age, were considered. Across studies, findings converge on the role that open and elaborative parent-child communication plays in positive child and adolescent mental health outcome. Implications and lessons learned are discussed. (PsycInfo Database Record (c) 2026 APA, all rights reserved).

### Adverse Childhood Experiences and Cognitive-Functional Aging Trajectories: A Nationally Representative Study in China
**כתב עת:** 📄 Clin Gerontol | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Sun Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

BACKGROUNDS: Adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) are associated with long-term health risks, yet how childhood adversity shapes joint trajectories of cognitive and functional aging remains unclear.

OBJECTIVES: This study examined associations between ACEs and joint cognitive-functional trajectories and tested whether social participation moderates these relationships.

PARTICIPANTS AND SETTINGS: Data were drawn from 8,590 adults aged 45+ across five waves (2011-2020) of the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study.

METHODS: Group-based trajectory modeling identified joint cognitive-functional patterns. Multinomial logistic regression assessed associations between ACEs and trajectory membership, with social participation moderation tested.

RESULTS: Five distinct trajectories emerged. Each additional ACE was associated with 24% higher risk of the most disadvantaged trajectory. Both threat- and deprivation-related ACEs predicted adverse trajectories, with bullying and household mental illness showing strongest associations. Social participation was independently associated with more favorable trajectories, but did not significantly moderate the association between ACEs (cumulative, threat-related, or deprivation-related) and trajectory membership.

CONCLUSIONS: ACEs were associated with heterogeneous cognitive-functional aging pathways in a dose-response manner. While social participation was independently linked to more favorable trajectories, it did not buffer the adverse associations between ACEs and trajectory membership.

### Significance of perinatal trauma screening and care coordination to improve infant health outcomes
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Perinat Med | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jawed A | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 18

This commentary addresses the need for trauma-informed screening and coordinated mental health care across obstetric (OB) and neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) settings. Perinatal post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and related mental health conditions are common yet under-recognized, particularly among individuals exposed to medical trauma, prior adversity, or interpersonal violence. Perinatal PTSD prevalence estimates range from 3-6 % in community samples and 15-25 % in high-risk populations, including NICU parents, individuals with obstetric complications, and those with prior trauma histories. Infants hospitalized in NICUs experience their own forms of trauma related to pain, sensory overload, and caregiver separation, while parents are critical buffers of infant stress but may be unable to provide effective co-regulation when they are themselves traumatized. Guided by the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) trauma-informed framework, this commentary synthesizes current evidence on screening, perinatal PTSD risk factors, intergenerational trauma, and Collaborative Care models. The author describes how integrated mental health services can be operationalized across care environments and issue a call to action for universal screening, universal education about healthy and unsafe relationship dynamics, coordinated treatment pathways, and equity-focused implementation strategies to protect infant neurodevelopment and interrupt intergenerational cycles of trauma.
