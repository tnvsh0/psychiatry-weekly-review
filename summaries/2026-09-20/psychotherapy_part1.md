### Drawing and visual art interventions for anxiety in children aged 5-12: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials
**כתב עת:** ⭐ BMJ Paediatr Open *(IF: 105.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Jia Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 16

Abstract Objective To evaluate the effects of drawing- and visual art-based interventions on anxiety in children aged 5–12 years and to examine variation across comparator structures and settings. Methods PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, ERIC, DOAJ and China National Knowledge Infrastructure were searched for randomised controlled trials (RCTs). Postintervention anxiety was the primary outcome and was pooled as standardised mean differences. Trials using non-art or usual-care comparators and those using active art comparators were synthesised separately. Risk of bias was assessed using RoB 2. Results 10 RCTs involving 816 children were included. The primary meta-analysis included eight trials with non-art or usual-care comparators conducted in medical or procedure-related contexts. Drawing- and visual art-based interventions were associated with lower short-term anxiety compared with these controls (SMD=−2.70, 95% CI −3.73 to −1.68; I²=96%). Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses and exclusion of studies at high risk of bias did not materially change the direction of the effect although heterogeneity remained substantial. Two school-based trials used active art-based comparators and were synthesised separately. Conclusion Drawing- and visual art-based interventions may reduce short-term anxiety mainly in acute medical or procedure-related contexts. Because heterogeneity was substantial and school-based evidence used different comparator structures, the current evidence does not support a single generalisable effect across paediatric settings. ### Exposure quantity or engagement quality? A differential amplification framework of digital engagement and health outcomes
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Clinical Psychology Review (Clin Psychol Rev) *(IF: 12.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Liu D et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 4

Public guidance often treats screen time as a uniform exposure, yet associations with health may vary by outcome and use definition. We conducted a prospectively registered umbrella review of 59 meta-analyses published from July 1, 2015 to May 15, 2026; post-registration amendments were reported transparently. Methodological quality was assessed with AMSTAR-2, and representative effects were summarized using native-metric contrasts. General screen exposure showed small-to-moderate associations with myopia and overweight/obesity, whereas problematic or high-intensity indicators did not consistently amplify these outcomes. Sleep showed an intermediate contrast (descriptive OR ratio = 1.95; approximate interval [1.45, 2.64]). Mental health showed the largest selected within-metric contrast: time spent using social networking sites was weakly associated with depression symptoms (r = 0.11), whereas problematic use showed a larger association (r = 0.29); no correlation ratio was calculated. Neck pain was numerically higher under smartphone-overuse definitions (descriptive OR ratio = 1.72; approximate interval [0.97, 3.06]), but the interval included 1 under the independence assumption. Primary-study overlap was generally low, heterogeneity was high, and 56 of 59 reviews were rated critically low by AMSTAR-2. Publication-bias-restricted paired contrasts were available only for myopia, mental health, and sleep, precluding claims of complete cross-domain robustness. These findings motivate the Outcome-Differentiated Amplification Framework (ODAF), a hypothesis-generating account in which cumulative exposure, behavioral-postural disruption, and dysregulated engagement may matter differently across outcomes; ODAF is not a confirmed causal mechanism.

### The impact of ADHD on attentional bias modification outcomes: Evidence from gaze-contingent music reward therapy for social anxiety disorder
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Avni T et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 11

BACKGROUND: Attention Bias Modification (ABM) for social anxiety disorder (SAD) aims to reduce anxiety by training attention away from social threats. ABM training involves various attentional processes, including on-task sustained attention, a faculty that is typically impaired in individuals with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). Thus, ABM's efficacy for individuals with comorbid ADHD remains uncertain. This study examined whether comorbid ADHD moderates outcomes of Gaze-Contingent Music Reward Therapy (GC-MRT), an emerging eye-tracking-based ABM intervention for SAD.

METHODS: 112 treatment-seeking adults with SAD were analyzed, 42 with comorbid ADHD and 70 without. Patients underwent eight sessions of GC-MRT over four weeks. Clinical outcomes included the clinician-rated Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale (LSAS), the self-reported Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN), and the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9). Attentional learning was indexed by reduction in dwell time on threat faces from pre-to post-treatment and throughout the training sessions.

RESULTS: Both the ADHD and no-ADHD groups demonstrated significant and comparable reductions in social anxiety symptoms following treatment. Patients with ADHD exhibited a steeper reduction in dwell time on threat compared to the no-ADHD group. However, this advantage was not translated into greater SAD symptom reduction. Finally, baseline depression levels were comparable between the groups, and both groups demonstrated comparable significant reductions in depressive symptoms post-treatment.

CONCLUSIONS: Comorbid ADHD does not appear to hinder the clinical effectiveness of GC-MRT for SAD. It is conceivable that the reward-based nature of GC-MRT engages the on-task behavior of individuals with ADHD, assisting to bypass potential attentional barriers associated with this disorder.

### Embrace Pain-A Randomized Controlled Trial of Online Acceptance and Commitment Therapy for Painful Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy in Cancer Survivors
**כתב עת:** 📄 Psychooncology | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** van de Graaf DL et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep

ABSTRACT Introduction Chemotherapy‐induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) is a common and persistent side effect of chemotherapy, with up to 30% of patients reporting symptoms 6 months post‐treatment. Painful CIPN can impair sleep, mood, and physical functioning. We evaluated “Embrace Pain,” an online Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) based self‐help intervention that may be of support for cancer survivors with chronic painful CIPN. Methods The Embrace Pain Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) compared ACT with a waiting list condition (WLC) using a two‐armed randomized design. Adults (≥ 18 years) with painful CIPN ≥ 6 months post‐chemotherapy were randomized to an 8‐week guided online ACT program or WLC. Primary outcome was pain interference (Multidimensional Pain Inventory, MPI). Secondary outcomes included pain catastrophizing, psychological flexibility, CIPN severity, pain intensity, and quality of life (QoL). Linear mixed models and criteria for clinically significant change were used to evaluate outcomes from baseline to 3‐month follow‐up. Results 112 participants were included (57 ACT, 55 WLC). No significant treatment effect was observed for the primary outcome, pain interference, with ACT not outperforming the WLC over time. However, more ACT participants showed clinically significant improvement in pain interference at 3‐month follow‐up (47.4% vs. 23.6%) than WLC participants, and exploratory analyses showed greater reductions in pain catastrophizing in the ACT group. No between‐group differences were found for psychological flexibility, CIPN severity, pain intensity, or QoL. Discussion Online ACT did not demonstrate a significant treatment effect on pain interference in those with painful CIPN. Exploratory findings suggest potential benefits for pain catastrophizing and clinically meaningful improvement in pain interference for some participants. Larger studies are needed to confirm these findings.

### Five-year outcomes in a randomised controlled trial of prolonged exposure therapy and supportive counselling for post-traumatic stress disorder in adolescents: a task-shifted intervention
**כתב עת:** 📄 Eur J Psychotraumatol | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Rossouw J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

ABSTRACT Background Cognitive–behavioural therapies with a trauma focus are effective in reducing posttraumatic stress disorder and other psychological distress in adolescents. Long-term follow-up data on adolescents treated for PTSD remain scarce, with few studies extending beyond 12 months after treatment completion. Objective To evaluate the maintenance of treatment gains in a comparative study of effectiveness of PE-A and SC up to 60 months post-treatment. Method Sixty-three adolescents diagnosed with PTSD were randomly assigned to either treatment, provided by newly trained and supervised non-specialist health workers. The primary outcome measure was PTSD symptom severity, as independently assessed on the Child PTSD Symptom Scale (CPSS). We report on the 60-month post-treatment follow-up, building on post-treatment, 3-month, 6-month, 12-month and 24-month post-treatment data that have been published previously. Results Participants in both treatment groups maintained a significant reduction in PTSD symptoms up to 60-months post-treatment (F (7, 343) = 2.86, p < .01). Participants receiving prolonged exposure experienced greater improvement on the CPSS at all follow-up assessment timepoints, except for the 60-month FU ( p = .28; g = 0.33). Conclusion Adolescents with PTSD continued to maintain treatment gains up to 60-months post-treatment. These data, along with findings from the original RCT, indicate that a brief treatment protocol (averaging 9 sessions of PE-A or SC) in a LMIC, task-shifted to be delivered by nurses without prior psychotherapy experience, led to lasting improvements in PTSD and comorbid symptoms for up to five years. The sustained benefits and improved functioning over the first few years post-treatment support expanding both treatments, especially PE-A, in community settings.

### The effectiveness of the intervention model based on cognitive behavioral therapy in reducing risky sexual behaviors among MSM

Methods Based on CBT theory and the Internet, we established an intervention model targeting high-risk sexual behavior patterns in MSM. We then conducted a randomized controlled trial ( n = 50) to assess the impact of the CBT-based intervention on high-risk sexual behaviors and gut microbiota changes in this population. Results Prior to the implementation of the intervention, 44% of the participants engaged in high-risk behaviors despite having better cognitive levels. However, preliminary findings from post-intervention investigations indicate that intervention measures based on CBT demonstrate greater efficacy compared to traditional methods. After intervention with CBT, the incidence of unprotected anal intercourse in the intervention group was significantly reduced compared to the control group ( p = 0.016). Conclusion The high-risk sexual behaviors of MSM increase health risks. However, the method of intervention through the CBT courses can effectively correct the deviation between the cognition and practice. In this process, changes in gut microbiota were observed to be potentially associated with behavioral changes, but these associations are preliminary and warrant further investigation in confirmatory studies.

### Online and in-person Dialectical Behavior Therapy skills training for university students: a non-randomized comparative trial of feasibility and outcomes
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Ment Health | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bani M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Sep 17

INTRODUCTION: There is a growing need to implement cost-effective interventions for university students and Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) - Skills Training (ST) has shown promising results as a stand-alone intervention in various settings.

AIMS: This pre-post study aims to evaluate the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary outcomes of an 8-week DBT-ST program provided in in-person and online formats.

METHOD: Pre- and post-treatment self-report assessments were administered, including measures of distress, emotion dysregulation, self-compassion, and academic self-efficacy. Feasibility and acceptability indicators included attendance, dropout, and satisfaction. Data were analyzed using an intention-to-treat approach.

RESULTS: One hundred twenty-three undergraduate and graduate students were included. Seventy-three participants (29 in-person, 44 online) completed the assessment battery at the end of the program. Significant improvements were observed in emotion dysregulation, self-compassion, and academic self-efficacy with smaller changes in psychological distress. Greater improvements were observed among participants with higher initial distress. Treatment dropout was minimal and overall satisfaction was high across both formats.

DISCUSSION: Findings suggest that DBT-ST represents a feasible and acceptable group intervention for university students, with preliminary evidence of benefits in emotion regulation and academic self-efficacy. Further controlled studies are needed to establish effectiveness and clarify the role of delivery format.
