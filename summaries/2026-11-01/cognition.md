### Time to jump: Exploring the distribution of noise in evidence accumulation as a function of time pressure
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognitive Psychology (Cogn Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liss JV et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

The diffusion model conceptualizes binary decisions as emerging from continuous evidence accumulation with Gaussian noise. The Lévy-flight model (LFM) extends this by allowing accumulation noise to follow a heavier-tailed distribution, enabling sudden, large jumps in evidence accumulation. The heavy-tailedness is governed by the stability parameter α, with smaller α-values indicating more frequent and larger jumps. We demonstrate through simulation that the LFM can generate near-symmetrical response time (RT) distributions typical under strict deadlines. In Study 1, we explored the influence of intense time pressure on α in a letter-number discrimination task, expecting higher time pressure to entail smaller α-values. Participants (N = 341) were assigned to an accuracy (unlimited RT), implicit deadline (feedback discouraging RTs over 500 ms), or explicit deadline condition (timeout at 500 ms). Given the LFM's intractable likelihood, we used BayesFlow, a framework for simulation-based inference with deep learning. Model comparisons consistently favored the LFM over diffusion model variants-even in the accuracy condition. Importantly, α-values decreased with increasing time pressure and were especially low in the explicit deadline condition, where RT distributions also approached symmetry. Estimating both the LFM and a structurally matched diffusion model, with and without timeout modeling, further showed that accounting for timeout-related RT truncation led to substantially lower diffusion model drift rates, whereas LFM drift rates changed only modestly. Study 2 (N = 160) replicated α's sensitivity to time pressure in a brightness discrimination task. Overall, α responded systematically to temporal constraints across both tasks and timeout modeling specifications.

### Cross-linguistic semantic stability as a driving force of diachronic semantic evolution across lexica
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognitive Psychology (Cogn Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu Y, Yang X | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Word meanings change over time, yet the extent of change varies systematically within each lexicon. Although frequency, age of acquisition, and concreteness have been identified as within-language predictors, a language-general account of why some concepts show greater resistance to diachronic semantic change across languages remains elusive. Here, we propose that cross-linguistic semantic stability - the degree to which a concept's semantic neighborhood is conserved across languages - as a concept-level predictor of diachronic semantic change. We operationalized stability as neighborhood overlap between a target concept's nearest neighbors in English and the back-translated neighbors of its translation equivalents across 10 to 41 languages, and quantified diachronic change as neighborhood turnover in historical embeddings spanning up to 100 years in English, French, German, and Chinese. Higher cross-linguistic stability predicted less diachronic semantic change in each language and in an aggregated analysis of concepts shared across the four languages. This effect dominated all other predictors for shared concepts; remained significant after established psycholinguistic predictors were controlled; was represented as a direct conditional edge in partial-correlation networks; remained robust as the cross-linguistically shared concept set was expanded; and was replicated using historically derived stability estimates. Together, these findings reveal the topological consistency of a concept's semantic neighborhood across languages is a temporally durable, concept-level predictor of resistance to semantic change, linking synchronic cross-linguistic conceptual structure with diachronic lexical evolution across lexica.

### General versus specific: Investigating the structure of individual differences in post-error slowing across time
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hauenstein CE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Erroneous actions are typically followed by behavioral slowing, known as post-error slowing (PES), along with other post-error adjustments. These effects emerge on the first trial following an error and can persist across subsequent trials. While PES has been extensively studied experimentally, less attention has been given to inter-individual variability. Evidence suggests that the mechanisms underlying individual differences in PES are largely domain-general, spanning multiple tasks. However, as time elapses from the point of error, task-specific processes may increasingly influence these individual differences. In this study, we applied latent variable modeling to two large samples of participants who completed three canonical inhibitory control paradigms. Our findings indicate that individual differences in PES reflect both domain-general and task-specific abilities, with the influence of the domain-general factor seeming to decrease over time following error commission.

### Universal patterns in the acquisition of negation and disjunction: Parallels between L1 and L2
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Pagliarini E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

A fundamental question in language acquisition is how learners balance input and prior knowledge to infer meaning. Here, we present novel evidence about the mechanisms guiding adults' second language learning. We examined their interpretation of disjunction ("or") in negative sentences (e.g., "John didn't eat the cucumber or the pepper") as a test case. Languages vary in the assignment of scope to logical operators such as negation and disjunction, depending on their syntactic relationship. When multiple interpretations are possible, how do learners assign meaning? Does the native language of L2 learners influence this process? We report three key findings. First, native speakers of Mandarin and Spanish prefer an interpretation where "or" scopes over negation, meaning "John ate either the cucumber or the pepper, but not both" (the "not both" superset interpretation), whereas L2 learners from both backgrounds favor the opposite, subset (stronger) interpretation, where "John ate neither vegetable" (the "neither" subset interpretation). This pattern resembles child first language acquisition. Second, this preference persists even when the surface order of negation and disjunction does not match the native speaker's preferred interpretation, indicating that learners reconstruct underlying structure during interpretation, rather than rely on surface cues. Third, this tendency is stronger in less proficient learners and shifts with increasing proficiency. These findings suggest that L2 learners employ strategies similar to those of children acquiring their first language, initially favoring the stronger possible interpretation. These results shed new light on universal mechanisms guiding both first and second language acquisition.

### The emergence of language-specific biases in visual attention in pre-reading toddlers from two cultures
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Potter CE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Skilled adult readers show biases in their visual attention that reflect the direction of their native writing systems (e.g., English readers scan left-to-right while Hebrew readers scan right-to-left), but it not yet clear when these biases develop. We tested the hypothesis that language-specific patterns of behavior precede the ability to read by testing how toddlers (2.5-4 years) exposed to different writing systems allocated their attention in a picture naming task. Across two communities, children tended to name objects in the direction that matched their native language. Hebrew-learning toddlers labeled objects from right-to-left, while English- and Spanish-learning toddlers showed the reverse pattern, and language-specific tendencies strengthened with age. These results demonstrate that children have begun to discover directional regularities associated with their language even before they receive literacy instruction and illustrate how cultural conventions exert a direct influence on children's attention from a very young age.

### Working- and long-term memory target templates converge to produce coactive guidance of attention during visual search
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bahle B et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

Templates that guide attention during visual search can be maintained in visual working memory (VWM) or in long-term memory (LTM). A central challenge for theories of attention is explaining how these sources of bias combine to determine selection. Here, we tested whether they converge on a common priority signal. Specifically, we contrasted a coactive model of guidance, assuming convergence, with three non-coactive alternatives, each holding that guidance from VWM and LTM operate independently. Participants searched for the presence of a target defined by either of two cued colors. One cued color changed on each trial (dynamic, engaging VWM), whereas the other remained constant (static, engaging LTM). The target could match either one of the cued colors (single-target trials) or both (redundant-target trials). Through distributional analysis, we tested for the presence of redundant-target RTs that were faster than could have been generated by the two individual guidance processes operating independently in parallel. Such "violations" of the race model inequality (RMI) are observed only within a coactive system. For single-target trials, searches were substantially more efficient for dynamic-match targets than for static-match targets. Nevertheless, we observed robust violations of the RMI on redundant-target trials. Together, these results demonstrate that target templates can be maintained in qualitatively different states yet ultimately converge to jointly influence a common attentional priority signal.

### Unpacking the dynamics of cross-situational word learning strategies through trial-by-trial analysis: effects of word frequency distribution and learning context
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yin Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Word learning in natural environments often relies on cross-situational word learning (CSWL), whereby learners acquire word meanings from word-referent co-occurrences across different situations. Natural language follows Zipfian distributions where a few words occur very frequently while most occur rarely. However, how Zipfian distributions affect CSWL and how learning mechanisms vary across different contexts remain unclear. We addressed these questions in four CSWL experiments with adults. Experiments 1 (N = 36) and 2 (N = 38) found that Zipfian distributions may promote CSWL performance through prospective mutual exclusivity, whereby established mappings support the incremental disambiguation of new words, suggesting that the effectiveness of mutual exclusivity depends on contextual knowledge. Experiments 3 (N = 32) and 4 (N = 42) used trial-by-trial analyses to examine learning mechanisms under Zipfian and uniform distributions across varying levels of within-trial situational ambiguity, randomization, and contextual diversity. The Zipfian advantage emerged under high within-trial ambiguity, while its facilitative effect on associative learning required high contextual diversity. Moreover, hypothesis testing reduced reliance on associative learning under low within-trial ambiguity but enhanced it under high ambiguity. Together, these findings informed the Contextually Modulated Integrative Model of CSWL, in which the effects of Zipfian distributions and the interplay among learning mechanisms depend on learning context. The present study further advances the integrative model of CSWL and clarifies the sources of the Zipfian advantage.

### Mentalization as a mechanism for constructing meaning under ambiguity
**כתב עת:** 📄 Cognition *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Stagnitto SM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

Pseudowords lack conventional lexical meanings, but growing evidence suggests that they can nevertheless elicit structured (semantic) interpretations. The present study (N = 103) tested whether individual differences in mentalization are related to how people construct meanings for novel verbal stimuli. Participants generated definitions for real words and pseudowords and completed three mentalization-related measures: the Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI), the Reading the Mind in the Eyes Test (RMET), and the Triangles task. Using representational similarity analysis (RSA), we examined whether participants who were similar in mentalization-related profiles were also similar in their definition profiles. IRI and RMET response-profile similarity showed no evidence of an association with definition similarity, while similarity in open-ended descriptions of Triangles Theory-of-Mind animations predicted similarity in pseudoword definitions, but not real-word definitions. This association remained significant after controlling for similarity in Triangles control Action-clip descriptions, suggesting that it was not reducible to shared verbal-response format or general description style. Additional reconstruction analyses showed that pseudoword-definition embeddings can partially recover the participant-level representational geometry of Theory-of-Mind descriptions. These findings suggest that novel meaning construction is not only a lexical-semantic process but is also aligned with broader interpretive tendencies involved in assigning coherent social meaning to ambiguous experience, with implications for understanding the links between semantic cognition, mentalization, and developmental word learning.

### Effects of active learning and talker variability on preschoolers' novel word learning
**כתב עת:** 📄 Journal of Experimental Child Psychology (J Exp Child Psychol) *(IF: 3.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bulgarelli F, Rabideau M | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

Words sound different every time they are said, both by the same person and across people. This variability has been found to have mixed effects on learning, sometimes benefitting learning and other times making it more challenging. However, all prior studies investigating effects of talker variability have used passive exposure, presenting participants with predetermined orders or fixed types of variability. Here, we used a novel active learning paradigm wherein 3- and 4-year old preschoolers could choose who to hear words from during word learning. Then, we tested whether participants seek out variability during word learning, how these talker variability choices influence word learning and whether actively making these choices results in different outcomes than passively receiving them. We find that preschoolers choose to hear words from multiple talkers, but that talker variability does not predict word learning. Taken together, our results suggest that preschool aged children do seek out variability, but that word learning outcomes are most predicted by their relative vocabulary sizes and not talker variability choices. These results inform theories on the roles of both talker variability and active learning in language learning.
