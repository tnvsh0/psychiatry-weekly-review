### Three repeated intrathecal injections of allogenic umbilical cord-derived mesenchymal stromal cells in autism spectrum disorder: a Phase I clinical trial

Background Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a heterogeneous neurodevelopmental condition of etiology, characterized by deficits in social communication and restricted, repetitive behaviors. Despite its growing prevalence, no treatment has yet demonstrated consistent efficacy across the full spectrum of ASD phenotypes. This Phase I, open-label study investigates the safety and feasibility of three repeated intrathecal injections of allogeneic Umbilical Cord Tissue–Derived Mesenchymal Stromal Cells (UC-MSCs) in children diagnosed with ASD. Methods Five children (aged 3-7) with ASD, confirmed by Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders- Fifth Edition (DSM-5) criteria and on stable behavioral and pharmacologic regimens for at least two months, received three intrathecal (IT) injections of 20 × 10⁶ UC-MSCs at one-month intervals. Safety was monitored over six months based on Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE). Introduction Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a complex and heterogeneous group of neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by challenges in social interactions, communication difficulties, restricted interests, repetitive behaviors, and atypical sensory processing [ 1 ]. These traits often persist into adolescence and adulthood and are frequently associated with an increased risk of intellectual disability [ 2 ]. Worldwide, about 61.8 million individuals are affected by ASD, estimated at 1 in every 127 people. This prevalence imposes a substantial global health burden, estimated at 147.6 disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) per 100,000 population [ 3 ]. The etiology of ASD is multifactorial, involving a complex interplay of genetic and environmental factors. Genetically, ASD has been linked to various pathways regulating brain development and intercellular interactions [ 4 – 6 ], although specific single-gene conditions such as fragile X syndrome and tuberous sclerosis have been directly associated with ASD [ 5 ]. Environmental factors contributing to ASD risk include maternal nutrition and metabolic conditions, prenatal infection, exposure to certain drugs and toxins, such as Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) and antibiotics, during pregnancy [ 6 , 7 ]. Currently, there is no definitive cure for ASD, and treatment strategies primarily focus on managing disruptive symptoms and improving daily functions, including behavioral therapy (e.g., Applied Behavioral Analysis), occupational and speech therapy, biofeedback, neuromodulation, and vocational support [ 4 ]. Pharmacologic treatments are also employed to manage behavioral challenges such as irritability and attention deficit. However, they do not target the underlying pathology [ 8 ]. Emerging evidence suggests that neuroinflammation and immune system dysregulation within the central nervous system (CNS) play a critical role in the pathogenesis of ASD. Studies have identified significant microglial activation and increased levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interferon (IFN)-γ, interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, and Tumor Necrosis Factor (TNF)-α, in both brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Dysregulation of the immune system may disrupt neuronal survival and proliferation, alter neural connectivity and plasticity, and contribute to the production of brain-reactive antibodies, ultimately leading to abnormal brain function [ 9 ]. In light of these findings, immunoregulatory therapies offer a promising approach for ASD treatment [ 10 ]. Mesenchymal Stromal Cells (MSCs) have demonstrated immunomodulatory and neuroprotective properties. Through paracrine signaling and anti-inflammatory mechanisms, MSCs may inhibit microglial activation, suppress oxidative stress, and enhance synaptogenesis and neuronal survival, aiding in the restoration of brain functional pathways [ 11 ]. MSCs can be derived from various tissues, including bone marrow, umbilical cord, adipose tissue, and dental pulp [ 12 ]. Umbilical Cord-derived Mesenchymal Stromal Cells (UC-MSCs) offer several advantages, including high proliferative capacity, ease of isolation, and ethical availability [ 13 ]. Their hypoimmunogenic properties, attributed to the absence of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class II expression and low MHC class I level, make them suitable for allogenic applications [ 14 ]. Several cell-based therapies have been investigated in the treatment of ASD, including studies using mesenchymal stromal cells (MSCs) [ 15 , 16 ], umbilical cord blood [ 17 , 18 ], and human mononuclear cells (MNCs) [ 19 ]. However, the evidence remains insufficient to establish an optimal treatment protocol and reach a consensus on the safety and efficacy of intrathecal (IT) administration of allogenic MSCs in ASD. This open-label, phase I study aims to evaluate the safety, feasibility, and therapeutic potential of three consecutive intrathecal (IT) injections of allogenic UC-MSCs in children (3–7 years) diagnosed with ASD over a six-month follow-up. ### Non-Metric Dental Traits, Dental Caries Experience, Parental Perceptions, and Oral Hygiene Status of Children With Autism Spectrum Disorder-A Step Towards Sustainable Smiles
**כתב עת:** 📄 Spec Care Dentist | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Anu V et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov-Dec

BACKGROUND: Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is accompanied by poor oral health outcomes for children with ASD.

AIM: To assess the occurrence of non-metric dental traits (NMDTs), dental caries experience, parents' perception, and oral hygiene status of children with ASD.

METHODOLOGY: A cross-sectional study was conducted among 150 children with ASD and their biological parents. Children with severe sensory or behavioural difficulties who were unable to cooperate for oral examination were excluded. A pre-validated questionnaire was used to assess parental perception. Oral hygiene index-Simplified (OHI-S), Decayed, Missing and Filled Teeth index (DMFT) for permanent and primary dentition was used to assess oral hygiene status and dental caries in children with ASD. Non-metric dental trait (NMDT) was assessed using ASUDAS (Arizona State University Dental Anthropology System). Chi-square test and multiple linear regression analysis were done.

RESULTS: Most parents, 126 (84.0%) had a neutral perception score. The mean OHI(S) score for the children with ASD was 1.86 (SD 0.95). The prevalence of dental caries was 16% in deciduous dentition and 48% in permanent dentition. A statistically significant association was observed between palatogingival groove, cusp of Carabelli, and poorer oral hygiene status. Regression analysis showed that children with ASD whose mothers were employed showed a negative association with oral hygiene status (B = -0.533, p = 0.015). Higher maternal education was associated with lower DMFT scores (DMFT, B = - 1.044, p = 0.040 and for D, B = -1.319, p = 0.003).

CONCLUSION: Parents' perceptions regarding oral health among children with ASD are neutral with both behavioral limitations and oral hygiene practices affecting oral health. This study recommends early NMDTs identification and a multidisciplinary approach involving dental professionals, along with targeted parental education and regular follow-up, that may improve oral health outcomes.

### Sensory Overstimulation: A Virtual Reality Simulation to Foster Empathy Towards Autistic Youth
**כתב עת:** 📄 Stud Health Technol Inform | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Andersen AD et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

This paper investigates whether a Virtual Reality (VR) simulation of sensory overstimulation in a classroom can increase empathy and understanding toward individuals with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) among students aged 13-15 in Danish elementary schools. The project followed an iterative design process in which an initial prototype was refined through user testing and interviews with autistic adults and a special-needs teacher. The resulting simulation combined auditory overstimulation, environmental distractions, a listening-based classroom task, and hyperfocus mechanics intended to represent selected aspects of Sensory Over-Responsivity (SOR) in school settings. A mixed-methods A/B evaluation compared the VR simulation with a sound-based condition that communicated the same classroom scenario through narration. Fifty-nine participants took part in the evaluation. Broader dispositional empathy showed little change, but target-specific measures indicated short-term increases in empathic concern and personal distress immediately after the intervention. Interview findings suggested that participants gained a stronger understanding of ASD-related classroom challenges, particularly difficulty focusing under layered sensory input. The results suggest that immersive VR may be a useful medium for communicating sensory challenges in classroom environments, while longer-term empathy effects remain uncertain.

### Measuring Sleep at Home in Adolescents With Autism Using EEG Headbands: Protocol for a Remote Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Res Protoc | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hickey Townsend P et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

BACKGROUND: Sleep disturbances are prevalent in autism spectrum disorders (ASDs) and worsen daytime functioning, yet their neural bases remain poorly understood. This may reflect the challenges of laboratory-based polysomnography for individuals with ASD, including sensory sensitivities and difficulty adapting to changes in routine. The advent of wearable electroencephalography (EEG) devices allows for remote data collection in the home and facilitates large-scale sleep studies.

OBJECTIVE: The aim of this study is to test the hypothesis that thalamocortical circuit dysfunction contributes to poor sleep in ASD by characterizing sleep oscillations that rely on this circuitry. To achieve this goal, we monitored sleep in the home using wearable EEG headbands in adolescents with ASD, including those who are profoundly affected, and their typically developing peers. When necessary, we used a desensitization protocol to gradually acclimate participants to wearing the headband overnight to promote the inclusion of individuals with sensory sensitivities and high support needs.

METHODS: Participants and their families completed assessments and questionnaires and received remote training to use a wearable EEG headband. Those needing time to acclimate followed a personalized 2-week desensitization protocol with a practice headband. Sleep was monitored at home for 3 consecutive nights, the first of which was for adaptation to the headband. Sleep data quality was checked daily, and remote technical support was provided as needed. Using sleep rhythms as physiological biomarkers of thalamocortical circuit function, we will test the hypothesis that abnormal circuit function in ASD is related to worse sleep quality.

RESULTS: This study was funded by the Simons Foundation Autism Research Initiative (SFARI) and approved by the Mass General Brigham Institutional Review Board in November 2021. Data were collected between July 2023 and November 2025. A total of 146 participants (ASD: n=77; typically developing [TD]: n=65; profound autism: n=4) completed the study and produced at least 1 night of usable sleep data. Data analysis is underway, and the results will be submitted for publication in November 2026.

CONCLUSIONS: At-home sleep monitoring enables large samples that are needed to characterize the neural underpinnings of sleep disturbances across the autism spectrum. The personalized desensitization protocol promotes inclusion by accommodating individuals often excluded from research due to sensory or behavioral challenges. These methods are adaptable for other conditions and research goals beyond ASD, such as longitudinal studies across development or characterizing sleep in other neurodevelopmental disorders. Desensitization protocols may also increase the tolerability of diagnostic laboratory-based sleep studies and other medical procedures, improving both patient experience and quality of care.

### Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder Treatment Pattern and Its Associated Outcomes Among Children Referred to Psychiatry and Mental Health Services in Malaysia: Protocol for a Retrospective Cohort Study
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Res Protoc | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Muhamad NA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Abstract Background Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a common neurodevelopmental disorder associated with significant impairments in academic, social, and daily functioning. Despite the availability of clinical practice guidelines in Malaysia, evidence on real-world treatment patterns and outcomes remains limited. The lack of multicenter data on treatment use and clinical outcomes hinders the evaluation of current ADHD care and opportunities for service improvement. Objective The primary objective of this study is to observe treatment patterns among children with ADHD referred to psychiatry and mental health services in Malaysia after receiving treatment for 18 months. This study will also compare treatment outcomes between children with ADHD who do receive treatment and those who do not receive any ADHD treatment. Methods This multicenter retrospective cohort study will use secondary data from clinical records of children aged 4 to 16 years with a confirmed diagnosis of ADHD. Data on sociodemographic characteristics, clinical features, ADHD subtype and comorbidities, treatment modalities, health care use, treatment trajectories, and clinical outcomes will be extracted using a standardized case report form and will be anonymized prior to analysis. Data collection will be undertaken by trained site investigators and medical officers across participating centers. Statistical analyses will be conducted using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize characteristics, treatment patterns, health care use, and clinical outcomes of children with ADHD. Treatment patterns will be characterized according to pharmacological and nonpharmacological interventions, treatment modifications, and service use. Comparative analyses will be performed to examine associations between treatment patterns and clinical outcomes across children with ADHD and clinical subgroups. Results This study will be funded through a Ministry of Health Research Grant obtained in August 2026. It will commence in August 2026 and end in December 2028. Based on the sample size calculation, a minimum of 2771 eligible children with ADHD records will be included in the study. The study will be reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines for retrospective cohort studies. The findings will be disseminated through presentations and article publications at the national and international levels. Conclusions This study will address a critical evidence gap in ADHD care by providing comprehensive real-world data on treatment patterns and outcomes among Malaysian children with ADHD. Findings will identify variations in treatment approaches, treatment trajectories, and clinical outcomes across hospitals and regions, assess the alignment of real-world practice with national recommendations, and highlight potential disparities in access to ADHD specialist services. The findings are expected to support evidence-based clinical decision-making, guide strategic health care planning, optimize resource use, and contribute to the development of a more coordinated, efficient, and equitable system of ADHD care across diverse health care settings in Malaysia.

Introduction Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is one of the common neurodevelopmental disorders that are typically first diagnosed in childhood. ADHD is defined as persistent symptoms of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that may significantly interfere with daily functioning and development. Globally, the prevalence of ADHD among children aged 3 to 12 years and adolescents aged 12 to 18 years is 7.6% and 5.6%, respectively [ 1 ]. In Malaysia, 1.61% of primary school students aged 6 to 12 years are diagnosed with ADHD, with male children observed as having a higher prevalence compared to female children at a ratio of 4:1 [ 2 ]. Additionally, the prevalence of ADHD cases among children has increased from 4.6% in 2015 [ 3 ] to 7.1% in 2023 [ 4 ]. According to the DSM-5 ( Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders [Fifth Edition]), ADHD is divided into 3 subtypes: attention deficit (ADHD-I), impulsive (ADHD-H), and mixed type (ADHD-C) [ 5 ]. Children and adolescents diagnosed with ADHD-I often may fail to pay close attention to details or may make careless mistakes [ 6 ], often have difficulty in sustaining attention when completing tasks, do not seem to listen when spoken to directly most of the time, often do not follow instructions, fail to finish tasks, have difficulty organizing tasks and activities, avoid eye contact most of the time, dislike or are reluctant to engage in tasks that require sustained mental effort, often lose things necessary for tasks or activities, are easily distracted by extraneous stimuli, and are often forgetful during daily activities [ 5 ]. Meanwhile, children and adolescents diagnosed with ADHD-H often fidget with or tap their hands or feet [ 6 ] or squirm in their seat [ 7 ], often leave their seat in situations when remaining seated is expected, often run about or climb in situations when it is inappropriate, often are unable to play or engage in leisure activities quietly, often are “on the go” [ 8 ], acting as if “driven by a motor,” often talk excessively, often blurt out an answer before a question has been completed, often have difficulty waiting their turn, and often interrupt or intrude on others. To be diagnosed with ADHD, children up to age 16 must exhibit at least 6 symptoms within a specific domain or at least 6 from each domain for the combined presentation, and these symptoms must have emerged prior to age 12. These symptoms must persist for at least 6 months, be present in two or more settings (such as at school and at home), and cause clear evidence of functional impairment [ 5 ]. In addition, these symptoms must not occur exclusively during the course of schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder, and they must not be better explained by another mental disorder [ 5 ]. The occurrence of ADHD in children and adolescents is usually associated with genetic factors. The heritability of ADHD in a family is approximately 74%, and familial aggregation of ADHD increases with genetic relatedness [ 9 , 10 ]. The presence of genetic variants may lead to a risk of ADHD. In fact, in one study, it was found that 33% of children with ADHD had a parent with ADHD, and the child showed more severe ADHD than the parent [ 11 ]. However, the heritability of ADHD cannot be explained by genetic variants alone; it is reported to be due to gene-gene interaction [ 12 ], gene-environment interaction [ 13 ], and gene-environment correlation [ 14 ]. Environmental factors, such as prepregnancy maternal obesity, prenatal factors, and perinatal factors, may also contribute to the development or severity of ADHD among children and adolescents. Studies have shown evidence that maternal smoking during pregnancy, maternal obesity during prepregnancy, and maternal psychosocial stress increased the possibility of the offspring to be diagnosed with ADHD [ 15 - 17 ]. Persistent exposure to environmental stress may interrupt the neurodevelopment of offspring. Additionally, psychosocial factors like traumatic brain injury, the child’s diet, and the amount of screen time may also worsen the condition’s symptoms [ 18 - 20 ]. Globally, the main approaches to ADHD treatment can be categorized into pharmacological and nonpharmacological interventions. Nonpharmacological methods include occupational therapy, organizational skills training, and psychological interventions.
