### Microglial NUS1 deficiency drives dopaminergic neurodegeneration through intercellular iron dysregulation in Parkinson's disease models
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Liu L et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

The selective loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta is a defining feature of Parkinson's disease (PD), yet its underlying mechanisms remain unclear. NUS1, which encodes neurite outgrowth inhibitor-B receptor (NgBR), was recently identified as a PD risk gene. Here we show that NgBR is highly expressed in microglia, where its deficiency drives progressive PD-like motor deficits and dopaminergic neurodegeneration via the LCN2-SLC22A17 iron-transport axis in mice. NgBR deficiency in mouse and human microglia triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress and PERK-ATF4-NUPR1 signaling, which preferentially induces LCN2-mediated iron efflux over cytokine release. Subsequently, microglia-derived iron-loaded Holo-LCN2, but not iron-free Apo-LCN2, is internalized by SLC22A17, a receptor highly expressed in dopaminergic neurons, leading to iron overload and ferroptosis. Pharmacological inhibition of LCN2-SLC22A17 axis or ferroptosis alleviated dopaminergic neuron loss and PD-like symptoms caused by microglial NgBR deficiency in vitro and in vivo. Our findings identify disrupted intercellular iron homeostasis as a pathological driver of PD and highlight the LCN2-SLC22A17 axis as a potential therapeutic target.

### The neurobiology of menopause: Implications for brain aging and Alzheimer's risk
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Mosconi L | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

A growing body of evidence implicates the menopausal transition as a neurobiological inflection point in women's brain aging and Alzheimer's disease (AD) vulnerability. Menopause is a midlife neuroendocrine transition characterized by a decline in sex steroid hormones and widespread systemic effects. The brain is not a passive recipient of ovarian hormones but an active endocrine-sensing organ. Menopause, however, does not act in isolation: the ovarian-brain axis is established embryonically, activated at puberty, remodeled during pregnancy and postpartum, and reconfigured during the menopausal transition, with implications for later-life brain aging. This review places menopause within a lifespan neuroendocrine framework, examining mechanisms by which the transition may influence the brain, including altered hormone signaling, bioenergetics, synaptic plasticity, neurovascular function, inflammation, and AD-related pathology. It then reviews evidence linking menopausal timing to neurobiological and AD vulnerability, discusses estrogen therapy, and considers menopause as a biologically plausible window for risk stratification and prevention.

### KCNH1 variants associated with neurodevelopmental disorders exhibit prominent gain of function
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Thompson CH et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Pathogenic variants in KCNH1, encoding the human ether-á-go-go voltage-gated potassium channel (EAG1, KV10.1), are associated with the neurodevelopmental disorders Temple-Baraitser syndrome (TBS), Zimmermann-Laband syndrome (ZLS), epileptic encephalopathy, and other undefined neurodevelopmental disorders. Investigating the functional consequences of KCNH1 variants can provide information about molecular and cellular pathophysiological mechanisms and help guide therapeutic directions. In this study, we determined the functional properties of 26 disease-associated KCNH1 variants including three recurrent and six that were previously unpublished. Findings were compared to the wildtype (WT) channel along with six rare population variants. Our approach used automated patch clamp recording of heterologously expressed KCNH1 variants in both the homozygous state (variant alone) and the clinically-relevant heterozygous state (variant co-expressed with WT channels). We also examined the impact of alternative splice isoforms on functional consequences of specific variants. We demonstrated that the majority of disease-associated KCNH1 variants exhibit features consistent with gain-of-function including hyperpolarized voltage-dependence of activation, accelerated activation kinetics, or both that promote hyperpolarization of the resting membrane potential. By contrast, one unique variant discovered previously in an autism cohort had a depolarized voltage-dependence of activation and was associated with a depolarized resting potential in cells. Pharmacological testing of KCNH1 variants demonstrated differences in potency for channel block by imipramine and an investigational non-selective potassium channel blocker (LY97241) for specific variants. Collectively, our findings support gain-of-function as the most common, albeit not exclusive, molecular mechanism underlying KCNH1-related disorders and suggest opportunities for potential therapeutic drug repurposing strategies for these conditions.

### One-shot learning of paired association navigation with biologically plausible schemas
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Kumar MG et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

After learning a multiple paired-association (MPA) navigation task, rodents can rapidly learn new cue-location associations after a single exposure, a phenomenon proposed to be facilitated by cognitive schemas. However, it remains unclear what schemas support this behavior and how they could be implemented in biological neural circuits. Here, we identify a set of schemas sufficient for gradual then one-shot paired-association navigation, and implement them in a fully neural agent using biologically plausible plasticity rules. The agent gradually acquires a low-dimensional spatial metric representation using a path-integration based temporal-difference error, enabling self-localization across different environments. It also contains a memory network that forms one-shot associations between sensory cues and goal locations, implemented using reward-modulated exploratory Hebbian (EH) plasticity. A third network performs vector subtraction between the agent's current and goal location to compute goal-directed actions. Supplementing these schemas with an actor-critic enables one-shot navigation past obstacles. Lastly, learning a working-memory gating mechanism using the temporal-difference error enables one-shot learning even with brief cue and distractor presentation. The model reproduces key behavioral features of rodent learning in the MPA task and generates testable predictions about the neural mechanisms underlying schema-based one-shot learning. Significance Statement  Schema-dependent learning is exemplified by better recall of a lecture when one already has a schema for understanding. A rodent navigation task showing a shift from slow to rapid learning has been proposed to reflect such schema use, yet how schemas are neurally implemented and enable faster learning is poorly understood. We clarify this gap in this computational study by describing several schemas and implement them as neural networks with biologically plausible learning rules. Composing them into an agent reproduces schema-dependent rodent learning behavior. Because schemas also shape self beliefs, clarifying their neural basis has implications for psychopathology. At the same time, understanding how biological schemas support rapid learning may inform advances in machine learning.

### Object detection through dynamic motor-sensory convergence in freely moving rats
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Nelinger G et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

To detect objects in natural environments, animals must coordinate movement and sensation. This process unfolds dynamically, as perceivers actively shape sensory input through ongoing motor actions in a closed loop. In this study, we use precise tracking of behavioral kinematics to investigate the initial phase of object interaction, asking: what motor-sensory strategies support object detection during natural approach? This motor-sensory ratio cannot stabilize during free-air whisking and thus can serve as a signature for object presence. Rats gradually converged toward this object-dependent mechanical signature across contacts, and proximity to [Formula: see text] predicted subsequent head movements and the termination of object approach. Convergence to [Formula: see text] was invariant to object shape and texture and to whisker's length. Moreover, touch-induced pumps both signaled and reinforced convergence. The results suggest that rats use successful convergence to this object-dependent motor-sensory contingency as a signature of object presence. Object detection thus emerges as a closed-loop dynamical process in which action, mechanics, and sensation jointly shape perceptual inference. Significance Statement  How does a rat know it is touching an object with its moving whiskers? We trained freely moving rats to discriminate objects while tracking whisker kinematics at high resolution. We found that the detection phase involved whisker-object interactions that converged toward a distinct ratio between changes in whisker angle and curvature. This ratio can arise mechanically only when whiskers contact an external object, making its attainment a reliable indication of object presence. During object approach, rats steered their exploratory movements to seek this motor-sensory contingency, marking the transition to focused exploration or disengagement. These findings reveal a dynamical, closed-loop mechanism for object detection in freely moving rats.
