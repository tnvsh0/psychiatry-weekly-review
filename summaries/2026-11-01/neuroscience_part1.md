### Circuit mechanisms for disentangled representations and generalization in the hippocampus
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tang W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Understanding how the heterogeneous activity patterns of individual neurons give rise to coherent population dynamics and cognition is a central challenge in neuroscience. In the hippocampus, neurons are tuned to spatial location, yet their activity is also modulated by multiple external and internal variables and appears to drift randomly over time, making it difficult to link hippocampal computations to behavior. By analyzing hippocampal population dynamics in behaving mice, we reveal a structured organization underlying the diversity of single-neuron responses-the hippocampus encodes different aspects of experience as disentangled variables represented in the co-activity dynamics of cell assemblies. The temporal and contextual components of these representations are governed by local inhibitory interactions and input-driven cell-assembly dynamics, respectively. Our findings reveal the circuit basis of disentangled hippocampal representations, offering a unifying geometric principle of population coding that supports flexible memory representations and enables rapid generalization of prior knowledge to new situations.

### Stimulus-specific recruitment of human amygdala neurons predicts episodic memory encoding success
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Nature Neuroscience (Nat Neurosci) *(IF: 25.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Xiao J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Controlling whether an experience is encoded into long-term memory and thus later remembered is a crucial aspect of memory. Here we examined single-neuron activity in the human brain while participants formed memories for novel visual stimuli and later retrieved them with confidence ratings. Category-selective amygdala neurons exhibited elevated firing during encoding of later remembered items relative to later forgotten items. This effect was predictive of the episodic aspect of the memory, was restricted to the preferred category of each cell, emerged earlier and was stronger in the amygdala compared to the hippocampus, and did not depend on stimulus valence and arousal. Successful memory formation was accompanied by enhanced spike-field coherence between the activity of amygdala category cells and hippocampal field potentials. These findings demonstrate that stimulus-specific recruitment of the amygdala predicts memory formation and suggest category cells in the right amygdala as a target for interventions to treat disorders affecting episodic memory in humans.

### Remak bundle size, structure, and function are regulated by the Schwann cell basal lamina
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Neuron *(IF: 17.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lillis M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

Remak bundles are groups of small-diameter unmyelinated nociceptive axons ensheathed by Schwann cells (SCs). Whereas the development and maturation of myelinating SCs have been extensively studied, the formation of Remak bundles remains poorly understood. By preventing the secretion of type IV collagen from SCs, we observe normal myelin but abnormal Remak bundles. There is a decrease in Remak bundle density, an increase in bundle size, improperly ensheathed axons, and a decrease in mechanosensitivity. While Remak bundle formation appears to be independent of integrin signaling, the loss of type IV collagen leads to a decrease in postnatal SC proliferation. Inducing the emergence of supernumerary SCs rescues Remak bundle architecture and mechanosensitivity. Taken together, our results demonstrate that SC-secreted basal lamina critically regulates Remak SC number, bundle size, and structure. Our findings provide insight into the functional role of Remak bundles and highlight their potential involvement in peripheral neuropathies and neuropathic pain.

### Short-term outcomes, longer-term disability and relapse risk in LGI1-antibody encephalitis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Brain *(IF: 14.5)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Ramanathan S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Leucine-rich glioma-inactivated-1 antibody encephalitis (LGI1-Ab-E) is a common and immunotherapy-responsive form of encephalitis. However, it is not known which immunotherapy regimens improve short-term outcomes, longer-term disability and relapse risk. Further, it is not known if the widely-utilised modified Rankin Scale (mRS) accurately captures quality of life (QoL) in LGI1-Ab-E, complicating disability measurements and interpretation of immunotherapy responses. A multicentre, international, real-world comparative analysis of patients with LGI1-Ab-E. Retrospective data from patient records evaluated short-term seizure and cognitive outcomes, 1-6 year disability, and future relapse risk associations, by integrating demographics, clinical features investigations and exposures to immunotherapies and antiseizure medications (ASMs). Patient- and neurologist-rated QoL assessments were used to establish an evidence-based mRS threshold for defining clinically significant disability. Cox proportional hazard models, Anderson-Gill, and generalised linear mixed-effects models were employed. 181 patients were included, with median age at onset 63 years (range 23 - 92); 122/181 (67%) were male. Short-term outcomes, evaluated at both five and ten weeks post-treatment initiation, identified exposure to immunotherapies associated with seizure resolution (OR = 13.97, 95%CI = 6.10-32.01; p < 0.0001 and OR = 8.53, 95%CI = 4.14-17.55; p < 0.0001, respectively), notably with corticosteroids, plasma exchange and rituximab, but not ASMs, intravenous immunoglobulins or steroid sparing agents. Conversely, seizure recurrence was observed after discontinuation of oral prednisolone, but not other immunotherapies or ASMs (OR = 39.53, 95%CI = 12.70-122.20, p < 0.0001). To evaluate longer-term disability, first, mRS scores were calibrated using QoL assessments: this identified a significant increase in patient-rated disability at mRS score of 2. Hence, using mRS 0-1 as a favourable outcome, we identified worse mRS at nadir associated with greater one-year disability (OR = 2.56, 95%CI = 1.56-4.20, p = 0.0003), and each month of delayed immunotherapy associated with a 16% increased risk of disability (HR = 1.16, 95%CI = 1.06-1.35, p = 0.004). Additionally, absence of immunotherapy associated both with greater one-year disability (OR = 5.67; 95% CI 1.19-26.54, p = 0.03) and a relapsing course (OR = 7.64, 95%CI = 2.51-21.77, p = 0.0002). Both a relapsing course, observed in 38% of patients, and the number of relapses associated with step-wise worsening of disability and QoL-outcomes. Rituximab exposure was the only identified clinical factor associated with reduced future relapse risk, by 93% (HR = 0.07, 95%CI = 0.01-0.48, p = 0.002). Persistent serum LGI1-IgMs, but not LGI1-IgGs, at one-year, associated with future relapses (HR = 11.69, 95%CI = 1.58-145.80, p = 0.02). Distinctive clinical factors and select immunotherapies, particularly intravenous methylprednisolone and plasma exchange, but not ASMs, associated with favourable short-term outcomes, patient-centric longer-term disability and relapses in LGI1-Ab-E. Our findings provide evidence-based disability thresholds, highlight novel prognostic biomarkers, and pragmatically guide LGI1-Ab-E care, pending randomized clinical trials.

### PTPRO restricts higher-order branching of terminal arbors through TrkB dephosphorylation
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Li J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

The structural complexity of axon collateral terminal arbors shapes synaptic connectivity. In corticospinal projections, individual axon collaterals exhibit distinct patterns of terminal arborization, ranging from sparse local branches to dense, highly complex arbors in target regions such as the pontine nuclei. How this complexity is controlled remains poorly understood. Here, using corticopontine projections in male and female mice, we show that protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type O (PTPRO) selectively restricts higher-order branching of terminal arbors in the pontine nuclei without affecting collateral number. This function requires the intracellular phosphatase domain of PTPRO but not its extracellular region. We demonstrate that PTPRO dephosphorylates tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB), and that the excessive terminal arborization caused by acute PTPRO loss is rescued by TrkB depletion or phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) inhibition. Together, these findings reveal that PTPRO-mediated TrkB dephosphorylation acts as a molecular brake on corticopontine terminal arbor elaboration. Significance Statement  How individual axons regulate terminal branching within target regions is a fundamental question in neural circuit development. This study identifies protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type O (PTPRO), a receptor-type protein tyrosine phosphatase that negatively regulates signaling through tyrosine phosphorylation, as a selective regulator of higher-order branching in corticopontine terminal arbors. PTPRO dephosphorylates tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB), a neurotrophin receptor tyrosine kinase that promotes neuronal growth and branching, thereby restricting terminal arbor complexity without affecting collateral number from the main axon. Given that terminal arbor architecture determines the synaptic territory of each cortical input, PTPRO-dependent constraint on arbor elaboration may represent a general principle by which projection-specific arbor complexity is established across long-range circuits in the central nervous system.

### Bivalent habenula modulation of human monoaminergic midbrain and cortical pathways
**כתב עת:** 🔷 The Journal of Neuroscience (J Neurosci) *(IF: 6.7)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hudson LJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

The habenula is implicated in signaling negative reward prediction errors (RPE), yet direct evidence demonstrating its influence on downstream human brain circuits is limited. Using ultra-high field (7T) fMRI and dynamic causal modelling across two negative RPE-inducing experimental tasks, we characterize habenula-directed connectivity with monoaminergic midbrain and cortical pathways in healthy adults (n = 47, 24 males and 23 females; n = 43, 23 males and 20 females). Negative feedback drove habenula inhibition of ventral tegmental area and dorsal raphe nucleus activity, while positive feedback had an excitatory effect. These core effects are replicated across reversal learning and perceptual decision uncertainty tasks, establishing a generalizable mechanism for behavioral adaptation. Task-specific modulatory effects emerged in pathways between the subcortical and cortical regions (ventral tegmental area→dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, dorsal raphe nucleus→medial prefrontal cortex, and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex→habenula), revealing context-dependent cortical elaboration of negative RPE-driven habenula signals. This provides the first evidence in humans, using model-based effective connectivity, for bivalent modulation of habenula function on monoaminergic midbrain and cortical pathways, demonstrating how the brain integrates negative and positive signals to guide adaptive behavior. Significance Statement  The habenula - an epithalamic brain region - plays a key role in the encoding of reward and punishment. Animal studies have demonstrated the habenula to be bivalently modulated by aversive and appetitive stimuli, resulting in the bidirectional modulation of monoaminergic brain region activity. Despite this evidence, the directional impact of bivalently modulated habenula activity on its circuitry has yet to be investigated in humans. This study leverages ultra-high field (7T) fMRI and dynamic causal modelling to reveal the impact of bivalent habenula modulation on monoaminergic midbrain and cortical regions across two distinct tasks. Through these methodologies, we demonstrate the habenula to have a key role in guiding adaptive behavior.
