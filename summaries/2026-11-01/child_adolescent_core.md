### Widening educational inequalities for young people attending child and adolescent mental health services: a longitudinal cohort study
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** O'Hare K et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

BACKGROUND: A 'mortality gap' is well-documented in individuals with severe mental illness but an 'education gap' emerges much earlier in life, with major long-term consequences. While educational attainment has improved in most high-income countries in recent decades, it is unknown whether these improvements have narrowed the education gap for young people who attend child and adolescent mental health services (CAMHS).

METHODS: This nationwide, longitudinal cohort study used linked administrative data from the Secure Anonymised Information Linkage (SAIL) databank for a large cohort of individuals in Wales born between 1992 and 2003 and followed to 2019. We compared performance on the General Certificate of Secondary Education (GCSE) exams, taken at age 16, between young people with and without prior CAMHS contact. We divided the population into two sequential birth cohorts (1992-1997 vs 1998-2003) and tested whether the association between CAMHS contact and failure to achieve key GCSE thresholds changed over time.

RESULTS: In the total population, the proportion of young people failing to achieve the basic GCSE level one threshold fell from 6.9% in the older (1992-1997) cohort to 3.7% in the younger (1998-2003) cohort. Young people who attended CAMHS, however, were significantly more likely to fail to achieve level one GCSEs in both cohorts (aOR = 4.28, 95% CI = 4.08, 4.48). Critically, this gap widened over time for GCSE Level-1 attainment, both for CAMHS outpatients (interaction aOR = 1.30, 95% CI = 1.20, 1.40) and CAMHS inpatients (interaction aOR = 1.80, 95% CI = 1.47, 2.19).

CONCLUSIONS: Despite overall improvements in educational attainment in the general population, there is a growing gap in educational attainment between young people accessing CAMHS and their peers. The failure to attain basic qualifications may lead to lifelong socioeconomic disadvantage. Research on how to effectively address the educational needs of young people attending CAMHS is an urgent economic and public health issue.

### Associations between Simple and Complex Executive Function skills and adaptive function in a sample of 3-year-olds enriched for variation in autism and ADHD traits
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hendry A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

BACKGROUND: Adaptive Functioning-including communication, socialization, and daily living skills-predicts outcomes and quality of life for individuals who are autistic or have ADHD, yet its cognitive underpinnings in early development remain poorly understood. Executive Functions (EFs) are a plausible mechanism linking developmental level or IQ to Adaptive Functioning, but prior work in preschoolers has relied largely on parent-reported EF measures, which are vulnerable to shared method variance with parent-reported Adaptive Functioning. Here, we examine associations between performance-based EF and parent-reported Adaptive Functioning among 3-year-olds enriched for variation in autism and ADHD traits.

METHODS: One hundred and twenty-nine children (109 with a family history of autism and/or ADHD) completed six EF tasks, reduced through factor analysis to scores for Simple EF (pertaining to simple response inhibition, holding information in mind, and applying a rule) and Complex EF (involving complex response inhibition, shifting response in accordance with a new task set, and updating information). Adaptive Functioning was assessed using the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales-Second Edition. Autism traits, ADHD traits and developmental level were measured using the SRS-2, CBCL-Preschool, and MSEL, respectively. Hierarchical regression models tested whether each EF index was positively associated with Adaptive Functioning, and whether these effects remained after controlling for developmental level, autism traits, and ADHD traits.

RESULTS: Both Simple and Complex EF were positively associated with Adaptive Functioning (β = .293, .316). While associations between Simple EF and Adaptive Functioning were accounted for by general developmental level, Complex EF showed associations with Adaptive Functioning above and beyond developmental level, autism, and ADHD traits.

CONCLUSIONS: EFs in general, and Complex EF in particular, represent a promising, developmentally-sensitive target for early support in the toddler or preschool years, which could positively impact the day-to-day functioning of children with a family history of autism/ADHD, or already showing elevated autism traits.

### Annual Research Review: A probe into sensitive periods - how the developmental timing of adverse childhood experiences influences brain structure and function - a systematic review
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry (J Child Psychol Psychiatry) *(IF: 7.2)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Hartanto S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

It is proposed that adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) - potent predictors of mental illness - exert time-dependent effects on neurodevelopment, reflecting periods of heightened neuroplasticity and developmental sensitivity. We systematically reviewed neuroimaging studies examining the role of timing in the association of ACE exposure with brain structure and function across the lifespan. Across 43 studies spanning childhood to adulthood, findings most consistently implicate earlier onset and longer duration of adversity in alterations to limbic and paralimbic regions, including the hippocampus, amygdala, cerebellum and global brain measures. However, evidence for discrete sensitive periods is mixed and varies by brain system, outcome and analytic approach, with no consistent pattern of sensitive periods emerging across adversity types and functional neuroimaging studies remaining comparatively limited. Progress in the field is further constrained by substantial conceptual and methodological heterogeneity, including inconsistent operationalisation of sensitive periods, reliance on retrospective ACE measures and predominantly cross-sectional designs. Moving forward, we argue that advancing the field will require reconceptualising sensitive periods as probabilistic, system-specific and shaped by the nature, timing and chronicity of adversity, rather than as fixed developmental windows. Integrating longitudinal assessment of adversity and neurodevelopment with measures of the neurobiological processes underlying plasticity may help determine whether sensitive periods are better characterised by underlying neuroplasticity than chronological age alone. Improved understanding of these developmental processes may ultimately help inform the timing and targeting of preventive and therapeutic interventions for young people exposed to adversity.

### Urbanization and the network position of loneliness in adolescent suicide risk: A four-stage analysis across 87 countries
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhu Q, Xin Y | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Loneliness is a well-established correlate of adolescent suicidal ideation, yet prior cross-national studies have mainly quantified average associations with suicidal behavior. Less is known about whether loneliness occupies the same position within broader suicide-risk networks across societal contexts. Using cross-sectional data from the public World Health Organization Global School-based Student Health Survey from 87 countries (N = 439,026; 52.1% female), we examined cross-national variation in the network position and predictive role of loneliness. Stage-specific samples were 317,141 adolescents from 64 countries for network estimation, 333,940 adolescents for prediction transfer, 42 countries for feature-importance screening, 296,863 adolescents from these countries for multilevel models, and 38 countries for edge analyses. Suicidal ideation was consistently among the most central nodes, but prediction models transferred poorly across regions, with a 17.3 percentage-point loss in area under the curve. Loneliness showed the greatest cross-context variation among predictors. Across 11 macro-level indicators, national urbanization had the strongest association with loneliness's model-based importance (Spearman r = .655, p < .001 after false discovery rate correction). Individual-level models indicated a stronger association between loneliness and suicidal ideation in more urbanized countries (odds ratio = 1.096, 95% confidence interval [1.053, 1.140]), and country-level network models revealed stronger loneliness-suicidal-ideation edges where urbanization was higher (Spearman r = .639). These findings suggest that loneliness may become more tightly connected to adolescent suicide risk in more urbanized social contexts.

### "We are given this huge responsibility": Parents' experiences of physical health risk and medical care in ARFID
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** James RM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

Avoidant Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (ARFID) is associated with significant physical health complications, yet little is known about how families experience and manage these risks or access medical care. This study aimed to explore parents' experiences of physical health complications, medical risk, and healthcare in children and adolescents with ARFID. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 25 parents of children and adolescents (aged 4-17 years) with a formal diagnosis of ARFID or who met DSM-5 criteria for ARFID at study screening without a formal diagnosis. Participants were recruited across the UK using purposive sampling to capture variation in age, ARFID presentation and parent-reported weight status. Interviews were analysed using reflexive thematic analysis. Four themes were developed: Living in a Body with ARFID; Parenting on the edge: responsibility, guilt, and impossible trade-offs; Seeking Help in a System with limited knowledge of ARFID; and Care in Practice. Together, these themes captured parents' experiences of ARFID-related physical health concerns as extending beyond individual symptoms, shaping children's daily lives while placing substantial responsibility on parents to manage risk and navigate variable access to ARFID-informed healthcare. Parents perceived high levels of responsibility for managing physical health risk in ARFID amid limited clinical oversight. Findings highlight the importance of clinician-led assessment of physical health concerns that extends beyond weight alone, alongside accessible ARFID-informed multidisciplinary care.

### Beyond single risk factors: defeat and genetic variations linking cumulative interpersonal risk to suicidal ideation in Chinese adolescents
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Zhao X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 25

Suicide among adolescents is a major global health concern. While poor interpersonal relationships are well-established risk factors for adolescent suicide, most research has focused on isolated interpersonal domains, limiting our understanding of how cumulative interpersonal risks shape the developmental trajectories of suicidal ideation, particularly within a gene × environment framework. A total of 760 adolescents (Mage = 13.01, 54.6% girls) were assessed at baseline and followed up across three additional six-month intervals over one and a half years. Adolescents' saliva was collected at T2 for genetic extraction, and the multilocus genetic composite (MGC) was created based on the gene-by-environment interaction between SNPs and cumulative interpersonal risk for defeat via the leave-one-out machine learning approach. Findings of latent growth modeling revealed that adolescents' suicidal ideation increased over time. Cumulative interpersonal risk was indirectly associated with higher initial levels and a faster increase in suicidal ideation through defeat, particularly among adolescents with a higher MGC compared to those with a lower MGC. These findings suggest that intervention strategies should aim to reduce adolescents' exposure to interpersonal risk, alleviate feelings of defeat, and consider the genetic susceptibility.

### Heterogeneous decision-making in ADHD: computational subtypes and their distinct prefrontal connectivity patterns
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Hong H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

Children with ADHD exhibit decision-making deficits characterized by lower efficiency in evidence accumulation and a preference for smaller, sooner rewards in intertemporal choices. This study aimed to preliminarily explore computational subtypes of ADHD in children and to investigate differences in prefrontal functional connectivity between ADHD subtypes. A total of 94 children with ADHD and 63 typically developing children matched for sex, age, and IQ were included. All participants completed the Two Choice Impulsivity Paradigm (TCIP) and the ADHD Symptom Rating Scale, and 43 children with ADHD underwent resting-state fMRI. A hierarchical drift diffusion model was applied to analyze TCIP task performance, followed by two-step cluster analysis to identify decision-making subtypes. Differences in prefrontal functional connectivity among ADHD decision-making subtypes were then compared. The ADHD group showed fewer square selections in the TCIP (p < 0.001), lower drift rates (pBayes < 0.001), and shorter non-decision times (pBayes = 0.002). Two decision-making subtypes were identified: a decision-making deficit subtype and a decision-making normal subtype, characterized by lower and higher drift rates, respectively. The ADHD decision-making deficit subtype exhibited stronger functional connectivity between the left medial superior frontal gyrus and both the right middle frontal gyrus and the right postcentral gyrus, as well as between the right medial superior frontal gyrus and the right postcentral gyrus (all p < 0.05). The results reveal decision-making deficits and internal heterogeneity in children with ADHD during intertemporal choice, with this heterogeneity possibly associated with abnormal functional connectivity between the medial superior frontal gyrus and the middle frontal gyrus/postcentral gyrus.

### Daring-impulsive (DI) traits versus ADHD features in adolescent girls with conduct problems
**כתב עת:** 🔷 European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry (Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry) *(IF: 6.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bellamy NA et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

The current study examined differential associations between daring-impulsive (DI) traits and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) features in relation to conduct disorder (CD) and clinically relevant characteristics in 115 criminal justice-involved and non-criminal justice-involved girls (ages 16-17 years). Adolescents provided self-ratings of DI traits, ADHD features, externalizing and internalizing concerns, impulsivity, and academic and social outcomes. A semi-structured diagnostic interview to assess CD symptoms was also used. We estimated associations using bivariate correlations to examine the dimensional overlap and convergent and discriminant validity of DI and ADHD total, inattention, and hyperactivity-impulsivity scores. We also examined the criterion-related validity of DI scores in distinguishing adolescents with and without CD using independent samples t-tests. Finally, we tested the incremental validity of DI scores in explaining outcomes over and above ADHD scores using multiple hierarchical regression analyses. Results indicated weak to moderate associations between DI and ADHD scores (r's = 0.22-0.26). Further, DI and ADHD scores differed in relation to indices of CD symptoms (e.g., total number of CD symptoms: r's = 0.19-0.31), externalizing (e.g., proactive aggression: r's = 0.13-0.38) and internalizing concerns (e.g., anxiety: r's = -0.06-0.41), impulsivity (e.g., sensation seeking: r's = -0.03-0.40), and academic and social outcomes (e.g., number of detained friends: r's = 0.13-0.27). DI scores significantly distinguished adolescents with CD (p < .01), but ADHD scores did not. Additionally, over and above ADHD scores, DI contributed incremental variance in explaining CD, externalizing concerns, distinct domains of impulsivity, and academic and social outcomes (ΔR 2  0.04 to 0.18). These findings have important implications for understanding the usefulness of DI traits in better understanding the heterogeneity of CD among criminal and non-criminal justice-involved girls.
