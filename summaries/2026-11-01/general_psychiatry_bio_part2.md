### Genotype and phenotype analyses of 689 individuals with PCDH19-clustering epilepsy
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Grasso KM et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

PCDH19-clustering epilepsy (PCDH19-CE) is an infantile-onset neurodevelopmental disorder characterised by clusters of seizures, typically triggered by fever. PCDH19-CE follows an unusual X-linked inheritance pattern whereby heterozygous females, and males with X-chromosome disomy or pathogenic postzygotic mosaic PCDH19 variants, are affected, while hemizygous males are spared. We aggregate published PCDH19 variants and their corresponding PCDH19-CE phenotypes to analyse phenotype-genotype correlations. Data extracted from 110 publications identified 689/735 (94%) individuals with PCDH19 variants and the typical PCDH19-CE phenotype. Three hundred seventy-three unique PCDH19 variants were identified; 195 were not recorded in ClinVar. Seizure onset prior to 12 months of age was associated with more severe intellectual disability (p < 0.001) and the presence of psychiatric morbidity (p = 0.001). There were no phenotypic differences between missense, nonsense, and frameshift variants or between individuals of different geographical backgrounds, which was assessed as a proxy for ancestry. Twenty-nine hemizygous males with constitutive PCDH19 variants did not exhibit the typical PCDH19-CE phenotype, suggesting that the identification of variants in these individuals likely results from chance or ascertainment bias. The correlation between earlier seizure onset and poorer neurodevelopmental outcomes emphasises a critical window for therapeutic intervention. The lack of phenotypic difference between variant types demonstrates that loss of PCDH19 function underpins PCDH19-CE. Moreover, the identification of PCDH19 cytoplasmic domain variants in 78 affected individuals challenges the traditional view that PCDH19-CE is caused primarily by defects in cell-cell adhesion, as variants in this domain are expected to disrupt the nuclear function(s) of PCDH19.

### Mitochondrial complex IV disruption is a detrimental pathology conferring ssusceptibility to alzheimer's disease
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Tian J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

Mitochondrial complex IV (CIV) is an integral part of the mitochondrial electron transport chain (ETC) and plays a pivotal role in maintaining mitochondrial function. In contrast to the long-standing recognition of its dysfunction in Alzheimer's disease (AD), the exact role of CIV deficit in the pathogenesis of this neurodegenerative disorder remains poorly defined. In this study, by incorporating clinical resources, mouse- and human-based models of AD risk, and a genetic approach to bypass defective CIV via the ectopic expression of alternative oxidase (AOX), we examined whether CIV deficits are a pathogenic driver of or a beneficial adaptation to AD-associated pathologies. Our results showed a clinicopathological association between neuronal CIV dysfunction and pathological characteristics of AD. Further experiments linked defective CIV to the development of mitochondrial dysfunction and synaptic loss in the tested mouse- and human-based models of AD risk. In addition, a reciprocal relationship between CIV dysfunction and amyloid pathology was determined. Lastly, a proteome-wide association study (PWAS) using genetic data from large-scale AD cohorts identified that downregulation of COX11, a crucial CIV assembly factor, confers susceptibility to AD risk. Collectively, our findings suggest that CIV dysfunction is a detrimental pathology contributing to AD-associated mitochondrial and neuronal damage. The dual role of CIV dysfunction, both as a consequence of and a contributor to amyloid toxicity in AD pathogenesis, further justifies the therapeutic potential of strategies targeting CIV deficits to diversify the tools for prevention and treatment of this neurodegenerative disorder.

### Trends in Autism Spectrum Disorder Cumulative Incidence and Incidence Density: A Systematic Review
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Biological Psychiatry (Biol Psychiatry) *(IF: 12.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Kim YS et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 30

BACKGROUND: Claims of an "autism epidemic" imply a true increase in ASD occurrence and changing underlying risk yet largely rely on rising prevalence estimates rather than incidence. Both measures are influenced by diagnostic practices, service access, documentation, and ascertainment; prevalence is additionally affected by service-related migration. Incidence studies follow a defined population previously not identified, to examine outcomes over a specified period and study area; incidence is more likely to capture temporal changes in underlying risk. We systematically reviewed autism incidence studies across differing methodological designs.

METHODS: Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, we identified population-based studies reporting ASD or Autistic Disorder incidence in individuals under 18 years. Studies were classified as Category A (birth-defined cohorts with definable risk periods) or Category B (dynamic population annual incidence density). Due to heterogeneity, incidence estimates were compared narratively.

RESULTS: Twenty-seven studies met inclusion criteria (14 Category A; 13 Category B). While ASD incidence increased, patterns varied by incidence framework, diagnostic definition, risk period, and ascertainment comprehensiveness (population coverage and detection of undiagnosed cases). High-comprehensiveness birth cohort studies tended to show inconsistent patterns and lower male-to-female ratios, whereas studies with moderate or low comprehensiveness often showed consistent increases. Increases were stronger for broader ASD category.

CONCLUSIONS: ASD incidence increased in dynamic, administratively defined study populations with moderate or low ascertainment comprehensiveness. In contrast, birth-defined cohorts with fixed risk periods and high ascertainment comprehensiveness showed inconsistent patterns. Overall, the available data do not provide compelling evidence of a true increase in ASD occurrence-an "autism epidemic."

### Exposure to antidepressant and antipsychotic medicines in pregnant women and risk of gestational diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies
**כתב עת:** 📄 Arch Womens Ment Health | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Zhao X et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

BACKGROUND: Antidepressants and antipsychotics are commonly prescribed for psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, major depressive disorder, and severe anxiety disorders. Psychotropic medications are increasingly prescribed during pregnancy to manage severe mental illnesses. Concerns have been raised about their metabolic side effects, including the risk of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM). This study aimed to systematically review and meta-analyze existing evidence on the risk of GDM development in pregnant women with prenatal exposure to two common psychotropics, antipsychotics and antidepressants.

METHODS: We conducted a systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, and the Web of Science, up to June 2025, identifying observational studies reporting GDM outcomes following the use of psychotropic medicines during pregnancy. Eligible studies were reviewed, and pooled relative risks (RRs) were calculated using a random-effects model, with subgroup analyses based on medication classification.

RESULTS: Twenty-one studies were included in this review and meta-analysis. The pooled adjusted RR for GDM among women exposed to any antipsychotics and antidepressants during pregnancy was 1.43 (95% CI: 1.17,1.69), and 1.19 (95% CI: 1.10,1.27). Second-generation antipsychotics (SGAs) were associated with a significantly higher risk of GDM (RR = 1.6, 95% CI: 1.23, 1.97). First-generation antipsychotics showed no statistically significant association.

CONCLUSIONS: Prenatal exposure to antipsychotics, particularly SGAs, and antidepressants is associated with an increased risk of GDM. Clinicians should consider these risks when prescribing psychotropic medications during pregnancy and ensure appropriate metabolic monitoring.
