### The Unbroken Integrative Psycho-Trauma Care Model: Health System Implementation in Wartime Ukraine
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Berezyuk O et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

IMPORTANCE: The ongoing war in Ukraine has generated an unprecedented burden of psychological trauma, frequently compounded by posttraumatic stress disorder, traumatic brain injury, severe physical injury, and the psychiatric consequences of captivity among former prisoners of war. In large military conflicts, civilian hospital systems are essential for delivering trauma-informed mental health care at scale while managing patients with complex, overlapping medical and psychiatric conditions under active conflict, with limited implementation guidance.

OBSERVATIONS: This Special Communication describes the implementation and clinical workflow of the Unbroken Integrative Psycho-Trauma Care Model, a multidisciplinary health system approach embedded in the Lviv First Medical Union in Western Ukraine to organize trauma-informed mental health care within a wartime civilian hospital. From February 24, 2022, through December 31, 2025, the Lviv First Medical Union treated more than 15 000 individuals for war-related injuries and cared for more than 400 former prisoners of war. During this period, 7538 unique individuals received psychiatric care through the Unbroken Model, including 4165 who received trauma-focused psychotherapy. The model was implemented through proactive psychiatric rounds across medical and surgical departments, structured triage into 4 clinical pathways, stabilization-first care sequencing, and longitudinal case management integrated with neurological, surgical, and rehabilitation services. This organization of care enabled early identification of trauma-related distress, continuity between medical and psychiatric treatment, and adaptive delivery of trauma-focused psychotherapies within a high-volume conflict setting.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: The Unbroken Model demonstrates how trauma-informed mental health care can be delivered at scale within a civilian hospital during active conflict. Its primary innovation lies in the organization of care delivery, integrating proactive outreach, triage-driven pathways, multidisciplinary coordination, and stabilization-first sequencing into a unified, scalable framework. This approach may be applicable to conflict-affected and postconflict health systems worldwide.

### Transcriptomic Network Analysis Reveals Trauma Type- and Ancestry-Related Heterogeneity in Posttraumatic Stress Disorder
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Jiang W et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

OBJECTIVE: Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating psychiatric disorder that frequently co-occurs with major depressive disorder (MDD), a comorbidity that is partly attributable to shared genetic heritability and common environmental risk factors. Despite extensive efforts to identify peripheral transcriptomic biomarkers, findings have been inconsistent, potentially reflecting the substantial biological heterogeneity underlying these disorders. The primary goal of this study was to identify molecular signatures that reflect the clinical and biological heterogeneity of PTSD and MDD.

METHODS: The authors present a network analysis of blood-based transcriptomics from 466 pregnant women in South Africa, including 215 individuals with PTSD and/or MDD (mean age, 26.34 years [SD=5.77]) and 251 trauma-exposed control participants (mean age, 27.14 years [SD=5.82]). Gene expression modules associated with trauma-related phenotypes were identified, including childhood adversity, intimate partner violence, and recent life stress, as well as ancestry and depressive symptoms, highlighting multiple sources of molecular variation. To evaluate the predictive relevance of these transcriptomic features, random forest models were applied using a nested cross-validation framework with permutation testing.

RESULTS: Models achieved moderate and statistically significant performance in predicting trauma-related phenotypes (mean area under the receiver operating characteristic curve [AUROC], ~0.61-0.69 across models [SD=0.07-0.10]), whereas prediction of PTSD or MDD diagnosis showed near-chance performance (mean AUROC=0.52, SD=0.03).

CONCLUSIONS: These findings suggest that peripheral blood transcriptomic signatures are more strongly associated with trauma exposure profiles and population background than with diagnostic status. Collectively, the results support exposure-informed stratification as a complementary framework to traditional diagnostic categories for capturing biologically meaningful heterogeneity.

### Indirect moxibustion therapy improves insomnia and associated symptoms in patients with schizophrenia
**כתב עת:** 📄 Trials | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Cai J et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Jul 29

Objective To evaluate the efficacy of low-dose dexzopiclone combined with Indirect moxibustion (IM), compared with sham Indirect moxibustion (SIM), on sleep quality (PSQI) and quality of life (SQLS) in patients with schizophrenia and comorbid insomnia. Methods This single-center randomized controlled trial ( NCT06171308 ) obtained ethics approval from Shanghai Mental Health Center (SMHC-IRB-2023-08). A total of 120 participants were randomly assigned to the IM group ( n = 60) or the SIM group ( n = 60), receiving 8 weeks of standardized intervention. Results Baseline PSQI and SQLS scores were comparable between the two groups. After 4 and 8 weeks, the IM group demonstrated significantly lower PSQI and SQLS scores than the SIM group (PSQI at week 4: 10.10 ± 1.76 vs. 13.50 ± 1.65, week 8: 10.65 ± 1.54 vs. 12.77 ± 2.05, SQLS at week 4: 28.78 ± 8.84 vs. 35.56 ± 3.38, week 8: 30.36 ± 10.63 vs. 38.07 ± 3.42). The improvement in the IM group was significantly greater than that in the SIM group in terms of change from baseline (PSQI: −3.399 [95% CI: −4.078 to −2.719] at week 4, −2.115 [95% CI: −2.795 to −1.435] at week 8, SQLS: −6.801 [95% CI: −9.104 to −4.462] at week 4, −7.732 [95% CI: −10.071 to −5.393] at week 8), with all adjusted intergroup differences being statistically significant ( P < 0.001). Conclusions Adjunctive Indirect Moxibustion led to statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in sleep quality and quality of life compared to sham procedure in this patient population over an 8-week period. These preliminary findings suggest IM is a promising adjunctive therapy. Further multicenter, long-term trials with expanded outcome indicators are required to validate its generalizability and sustained efficacy. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-026-09915-w .

Highlights Indirect moxibustion significantly improved sleep quality in patients with schizophrenia and comorbid insomnia. Indirect Moxibustion effectively ameliorates quality of life in insomnia with schizophrenia. Indirect moxibustion is a promising adjunctive therapy for insomnia in patients with schizophrenia. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-026-09915-w .

Introduction Schizophrenia affects approximately 1% of the global population [ 1 ]. Insomnia is a common but often overlooked comorbidity, present from prodromal to chronic stages [ 2 – 4 ]. Epidemiological evidence indicates that an estimated 25–50% of patients with schizophrenia experience clinically significant insomnia, characterized by persistent sleep difficulties and daytime functional impairment according to DSM-5 criteria [ 5 – 9 ]. Pathophysiologically, insomnia involves psychological hyperarousal, cognitive factors, and disrupted circadian and homeostatic regulation [ 10 ]. Clinically, it is categorized into short-term, chronic, and other specified subtypes. Importantly, sleep disturbance is no longer regarded as a mere secondary symptom of schizophrenia, but as an independent risk factor that exacerbates psychotic symptoms, impairs neurocognition, and deteriorates quality of life [ 11 ]. Therefore, effectively managing insomnia is essential to comprehensive schizophrenia care and reducing disease burden [ 12 ]. Despite its significance, managing insomnia in schizophrenia remains challenging and understudied. Quality of life—closely linked to prognosis [ 13 ]—is strongly influenced by sleep quality in both healthy and schizophrenia populations [ 14 ], suggesting that sleep improvement may yield broad benefits. Current pharmacotherapy relies mainly on benzodiazepine receptor agonists. Except for dexzopiclone, which is exempt from the 35-day use restriction specified by the US FDA guidelines, most hypnotic agents are only approved for short-term application. Clinicians often prescribe sedative antipsychotics to alleviate insomnia in clinically stable schizophrenia patients [ 15 ], whereas long-term hypnotic monotherapy raises risks of metabolic complications, drug reliance, and psychotic exacerbation for schizophrenia patients [ 16 – 18 ]. These concerns are particularly salient in the Chinese context. A recent real-world retrospective analysis of 33,569 valid prescriptions from 10 medical institutions in eastern China (2015–2021) found a consistent year-over-year increase in the use of benzodiazepines (BZDs) and Z-drugs (including zopiclone and dexzopiclone), with pronounced rises among elderly and female patients [ 19 ]. This rising prescription trend highlights an unmet clinical demand for low-risk auxiliary interventions for psychiatric patients with insomnia. Given these limitations of conventional pharmacotherapy, moxibustion, a core Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) therapy, has emerged as a complementary intervention [ 20 , 21 ]. It exerts therapeutic effects via thermal stimulation generated by burning mugwort, which regulates yin-yang balance and harmonizes qi and blood circulation [ 22 , 23 ]. In TCM theory, insomnia results from visceral dysfunction, qi-blood imbalance, and yin-yang disruption, often triggered by emotional stress or chronic illness. Moxibustion delivers localized thermal stimulation to specific acupoints to correct the above pathological imbalances. Indirect moxibustion (IM), a classic moxibustion technique first documented in the Zhouhou Beiji Fang compiled by Ge Hong in the Jin Dynasty, adopts a medicinal buffer between moxa materials and the skin, enabling milder, more uniform, and safer thermal delivery [ 24 ]. This enhances safety and tolerability, especially in vulnerable psychiatric populations. Nevertheless, few studies have systematically assessed the efficacy of IM—particularly as an adjunct therapy for insomnia in schizophrenia. To address this gap, we conducted a preliminary randomized controlled trial to evaluate the auxiliary efficacy of IM as an adjunct to low-dose dexzopiclone for improving sleep quality and quality of life in schizophrenia patients with comorbid insomnia. We hypothesized that IM would improve sleep quality—particularly in the subdimensions of sleep latency, efficiency, and daytime dysfunction, as measured by the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)—through its proposed mechanisms of regulating qi, calming the mind, and harmonizing yin and yang. We further anticipated that such sleep improvements would translate into broader psychosocial and quality-of-life benefits, assessed by the Schizophrenia Quality of Life Scale (SQLS). The primary study outcome was the between-group difference in PSQI score changes, while secondary outcomes included alterations in SQLS scores and treatment acceptability. Materials and methods Design This single-center, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT06171308 ) was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Mental Health Center (SMHC-IRB-2023-08). All enrolled participants provided written informed consent prior to trial enrollment. Eligible participants were randomly assigned to the IM group (low-dose dexzopiclone + IM) or the sham indirect moxibustion (SIM) group (low-dose dexzopiclone + SIM) for an 8-week intervention. The detailed trial protocol has been previously published. The formal Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP, version 1.0) was finalized and archived prior to participant screening, in accordance with CONSORT 2025 Item 3. This SAP prospectively predefined all confirmatory intergroup comparisons and classified intragroup longitudinal pairwise comparisons as post hoc exploratory analyses. The complete SAP file is provided as supplementary material alongside this submission; full SAP text is not integrated into the main manuscript body, and qualified researchers may request a copy from the corresponding author for valid academic purposes (Fig. 1 ). ### Efficacy of Personalizing Cognitive Remediation for Schizophrenia by Targeting Impairments in Early Auditory Processing
**כתב עת:** 📄 Schizophr Bull | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Medalia A et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Abstract Background and Hypotheses Early auditory processing (EAP) deficits are common but not universal in schizophrenia, are linked to impairments in higher cognitive functions and when improved are associated with downstream cognitive improvements. Assessment of baseline EAP training need provides an opportunity for cognitive remediation (CR) programs to personalize treatment and optimize impact. This confirmatory efficacy study examined the differential benefit of EAP training for those with and without baseline EAP deficits. Hypotheses were: (1) Only EAP impaired (EAP–) patients would benefit more from CR that included EAP training, (2) cognitive gains made by EAP– patients during CR that included EAP training would mediate the relationship between gains in EAP and functional capacity. Study Design 184 outpatient adults diagnosed with schizophrenia were classified as having intact (n = 91) or impaired (n = 81) EAP and randomized to CR with restorative exercise plans that either included EAP training (n = 89) or did not (n = 83). Cognitive and functional outcomes were measured post-treatment and 3 months later. Study Results Across CR treatment conditions, cognitive gain effect size was moderate. Treatment condition did not significantly impact EAP, cognitive, or functional outcomes for either EAP group at final endpoint. EAP improved significantly but modestly for EAP– patients across conditions. Cognitive gains did not mediate the relationship between EAP gains and functional capacity. Conclusions These findings support the value of CR for people with schizophrenia, demonstrate that EAP deficits are malleable, and that cognitive and functional gains can occur for EAP impaired patients, regardless of whether EAP training is provided.

### Lithium in the Prevention of Postpartum Recurrence of Mood Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Clin Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Sharma V et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 26

Background:  Women with bipolar disorder are at increased risk for relapse/recurrence during the postpartum period. This risk decreases with prophylactic pharmacologic treatment, compared to those who remain medication-free. Lithium use in preventing relapse has been studied extensively in individuals with bipolar disorder, though its specific use in the postpartum has not received as much attention.  Objective:  The aim of this systematic review (CRD420261357096) was to estimate risk of postpartum relapse, specifically in women with bipolar disorder who received lithium prophylactically versus those who discontinued it during pregnancy.  Study Selection:  Following PRISMA guidelines, reviewers screened 45 studies focused on women with bipolar I or II disorders from searches of PubMed, PsycINFO, and Cochrane databases conducted on April 2, 2026, that were written in English.  Data Extraction:  Two independent observers extracted data using a data extraction form created on Covidence. Random-effects meta-analysis of recurrence proportions, between-study variance, heterogeneity, and leave-one-out sensitivity analyses were conducted.  Results:  Eight studies (554 women) providing information on the prophylactic effect of lithium during the postpartum period, and 2 studies evaluating the impact of lithium discontinuation on postpartum relapse, were identified. The pooled recurrence proportion was 0.21 (95% CI 0.15-0.29) in lithium-treated patients and 0.41 (95% CI 0.33-0.50) in lithium-untreated patients. The most common timing of prophylaxis initiation was during pregnancy (51.9%). Studies examining the impact of lithium discontinuation provided indirect evidence of its effectiveness in preventing postpartum recurrence.  Conclusion:  Findings from our meta-analysis suggest that lithium is effective in preventing postpartum recurrence of mood disorders; however, caution is warranted when interpreting study results because of confounding by indication and limited literature.
