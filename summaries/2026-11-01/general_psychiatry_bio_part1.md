### Tryptophan metabolism within the gut-brain axis in depression: mechanisms, association with heterogeneity, and therapeutic opportunities
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Qin Z et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Depression is a highly heterogeneous psychiatric disorder, characterized by substantial variability in clinical manifestations, treatment responses, and disease progression, thereby posing significant challenges to precision diagnosis and therapeutic intervention. Accumulating evidence indicates that dysregulated tryptophan metabolism, mediated through dynamic crosstalk with the gut microbiota and the gut-brain axis (GBA), plays a pivotal role in both the pathophysiology and biological heterogeneity of depression. In this review, we delineate the contributions of the three principal tryptophan metabolic pathways-the kynurenine, serotonin, and microbial indole pathways-in modulating neuroimmune signaling, neurotransmitter function, and gut-brain communication. Moreover, we synthesize current evidence demonstrating that microbiota-dependent tryptophan metabolism exhibits distinct profiles across clinical, demographic, immunometabolic, trauma-related, and genetically defined depressive phenotypes, thereby establishing its role as a unifying biological framework underlying depressive heterogeneity. We further provide a critical appraisal of emerging therapeutic strategies targeting microbiota-associated tryptophan metabolism, including pharmacological, nutritional, microbial, and traditional Chinese medicine-based interventions. Future investigations should integrate multi-omics approaches, longitudinal study designs, and mechanistic validation with artificial intelligence to elucidate causal relationships between microbiota-associated tryptophan metabolism and depressive heterogeneity, ultimately facilitating biomarker discovery and the development of precision therapeutics.

### Evolutionary neuropharmacology of serotonergic psychedelics: a cross-species perspective on mechanisms, consciousness, and therapeutic potential
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Zhai H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

Serotonergic psychedelics have revitalized psychiatric research by producing rapid and potentially sustained clinical benefits in selected neuropsychiatric conditions. However, the mechanisms linking receptor activation to neural plasticity, altered conscious experience, and clinical improvement remain incompletely understood, while the evolutionary origins of psychedelic signaling and the translational value of animal models continue to be actively debated. In this review, we integrate evolutionary biology, molecular pharmacology, comparative neuroscience, and computational psychiatry into a unified framework for understanding psychedelic action across biological scales. Building upon predictive processing and the Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics (REBUS) framework, we discuss how serotonergic psychedelics transiently reshape hierarchical inference, providing computational principles that can be experimentally investigated across species despite profound differences in neural architecture and subjective experience. Comparative analyses from invertebrates to non-human primates further suggest that conserved receptor pharmacology coexists with species-specific circuit organization, highlighting that animal models should be interpreted as complementary platforms for investigating molecular, cellular, and computational mechanisms rather than direct proxies for human hallucinatory experiences. We further discuss preclinical and emerging translational evidence suggesting that biased receptor signaling and non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens may partially dissociate plasticity-related therapeutic actions from hallucinogenic effects, providing new opportunities for mechanism-based drug development. By integrating evolutionary biology with molecular psychiatry, this review provides a mechanistic framework linking receptor pharmacology, neural plasticity, circuit computation, and therapeutic outcomes, thereby offering new conceptual and experimental strategies for developing next-generation psychedelic therapeutics.

### Real-world associations of antipsychotic use with clinical outcomes in schizophrenia: a systematic review and meta-analysis
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Molecular Psychiatry (Mol Psychiatry) *(IF: 13.4)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Yao H et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

To systematically synthesize evidence on the association between antipsychotic medication use and diverse outcomes in individuals with schizophrenia, we searched PubMed, Embase, PsycINFO, and Web of Science from inception to May 1, 2026. Eligible studies were observational studies reporting associations between antipsychotic use and clinical outcomes in individuals with schizophrenia. Two reviewers independently extracted data and assessed study quality. Data was synthesized using multilevel random-effects models. Outcomes were categorized into mortality (including all-cause and cause-specific), somatic disorders, and other clinical outcomes. Sixty-eight studies comprising 1,424,455 individuals were included. Among individuals with schizophrenia, antipsychotic use was associated with lower all-cause mortality (pooled estimate, 0.64; 95% CI, 0.54-0.77) and lower relapse risk (pooled estimate, 0.34; 95% CI, 0.12-0.99) compared with nonuse. In contrast, compared with nonuse, antipsychotic use was associated with higher risks of cancer (1.41; 95% CI, 1.17-1.69), fractures (1.35; 95% CI, 1.17-1.56), and lung infections (1.25; 95% CI, 1.04-1.51), whereas evidence for other outcomes was non-significant. Overall, the evidence base was limited, with several pooled estimates derived from a small number of studies. Subgroup analyses suggested variation by region and study design. Antipsychotic use was associated with lower all-cause mortality and relapse risk, alongside increased risks for some adverse outcomes such as fractures and lung infections. These findings should be interpreted cautiously, as residual confounding and bias may influence the observed associations and preclude causal interpretation. Further studies using rigorous designs are needed to clarify outcome-specific associations.

### Sex- and frequency-dependent acute pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of smoked cannabis: a randomized controlled trial
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Neuropsychopharmacology *(IF: 8.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Lake S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 29

Cannabis use is rising among women. Studies suggest females are more sensitive to the intoxicating and misuse-related effects of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). While preclinical studies point to sex-specific development of tolerance, this has not been translated to humans. In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study, 44 participants (22 F) smoked three cannabis strengths (0% [~0 mg] THC, 4% [~20 mg] THC, 10% [~60 mg] THC), provided blood for plasma analysis, and completed subjective mood and drug effects assessments and a self-administration task. Outcomes were analyzed by cannabis strength, sex, and use frequency ( > 4 vs. ≤4 days/week; balanced across sex). Relative to placebo, active cannabis strengths increased subjective effects and plasma THC and metabolites (ps < 0.05). Exploratory follow-up of interactions (cannabis strength*frequency p < 0.10) showed the frequent-use group exhibiting significant increases from placebo in misuse-related ratings ("Like", "Take again") and self-administering more cannabis than the occasional-use group (ps < 0.05), and this was not modified by sex. Ratings of "High", Sedation/impairment, Dysphoric arousal, and "Bad effect" were higher at active strengths for occasional-use males relative to sex- and/or frequency-matched counterparts (ps < 0.05; via exploratory follow-up for "High" and Sedation/impairment). THC and metabolites tended to be highest in frequent-use males. These findings contrast with previous evidence of greater cannabis sensitivity among females and point toward future investigation into sex-dependent exposure thresholds for tolerance development/escalation. Sex and frequency of use are both implicated in cannabis use disorder risk, and consideration of their interaction is critical in characterizing the acute effects of, and tolerance to, cannabis.

### Adjunctive Atypical Antipsychotics in Major Depressive Disorder: A Network Meta-Analysis Perspective and Consensus Panel Discussion
**כתב עת:** 📄 J Clin Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** מטה-אנליזה | **מחברים:** Cutler AJ et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

Objective:  The goal of this consensus panel was to generate expert perspectives on the comparative efficacy, safety, tolerability, and clinical relevance of FDA-approved adjunctive atypical antipsychotics for adults with major depressive disorder (MDD) and inadequate response to antidepressant therapy.  Participants:  Five academic and clinical experts in psychiatry and psychopharmacology participated as panelists: Roger S. McIntyre, Andrew J. Cutler, Christoph U. Correll, Joseph F. Goldberg, and Stephen M. Stahl. The discussion was conducted as a closed expert panel.  Evidence:  The panel primarily discussed research findings from two recent network meta-analyses (NMAs) evaluating approved adjunctive atypical antipsychotics in MDD, supplemented by relevant published literature and panel expertise. The NMAs differed in study inclusion, treatment-node definitions, and analytical approaches to examine efficacy, acceptability, and safety. Panel discussion was used to contextualize the findings and address their clinical applicability and limitations.  Consensus Process:  The panel reviewed and discussed the NMA methodology, comparative efficacy and safety outcomes, effect modifiers, and implications for shared decision-making. The consensus manuscript was developed from the panel discussion and evidence review, with subsequent expert review and incorporation of relevant comments and revisions.  Conclusions:  The panel concluded that the two NMAs provide complementary and broadly concordant comparative evidence that can inform clinical decision-making. Treatment selection should integrate efficacy with tolerability, patient characteristics, preferences, and practical considerations. The panel emphasized earlier consideration of effective, FDA-approved adjunctive treatment when appropriate, while recognizing remaining relevant gaps in long-term, head-to-head, and phenotype-specific evidence. The panel did not endorse any particular agent.
