### Global Burden of Anorexia Nervosa and Bulimia Nervosa, 1990 to 2023, With Forecasts to 2050: A Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 Analysis
**כתב עת:** ⭐ JAMA Psychiatry *(IF: 25.8)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Bai R et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

This cross-sectional study estimates the global, regional, and national prevalence and disability burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—and mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa—from 1990 to 2023, and to forecast prevalence to 2050.

Key Points Question What is the global burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa? Findings In this Global Burden of Disease 2023 analysis, an estimated 18.1 million people worldwide were living with anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa in 2023, accounting for 3.83 million disability-adjusted life-years. Burden was highest in high-income regions, the largest increases in age-standardized prevalence occurred in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia, and prevalent cases increased by 71% since 1990 and are forecast to rise through 2050. Meaning The burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa is increasing globally, highlighting the need for strengthened surveillance, earlier detection, and better integration of care into mental health systems.

Importance Eating disorders are a major cause of nonfatal mental health burden, yet global evidence on their long-term trends, geographic distribution, and future trajectories remains limited. Objective To estimate the global, regional, and national prevalence and disability burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—and mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa—from 1990 to 2023, and to forecast prevalence to 2050. Design, Setting, and Participants Using Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 methods, data were synthesized from systematic literature reviews, population surveys, and vital registration systems. Estimates were produced for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023, with forecasts through 2050. These data were analyzed from October 2025 to August 2026. Exposure Anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa diagnosis. Main Outcomes and Measures Counts and age-standardized rates with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) for anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa prevalence, years lived with disability (YLDs), and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs), and anorexia nervosa mortality, reported by age, sex, and location. Results In 2023, an estimated 18.1 million people (95% UI, 13.9-24.0) globally were living with anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa, of whom 12.6 million (69.4%) were female, 5.5 million were male (30.6%), and 41.2% were aged 10 to 24 years. These cases accounted for 3.83 million DALYs (95% UI, 2.23-5.69). Since 1990, the global number of prevalent cases increased by 71.0%, while age-standardized prevalence and DALY rates rose by about 21%. Females experienced substantially higher prevalence and DALY rates than males. The highest age-standardized prevalence rates were observed in high-income regions, particularly Australasia and Western Europe, whereas the largest relative increases in age-standardized prevalence occurred in East Asia, South Asia, and Southeast Asia. Forecasts indicate continued increases in prevalent cases through 2050. Conclusions and Relevance This GBD 2023 analysis found that anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa contribute substantially to global nonfatal health loss. These findings support the integration of eating disorders into mental health surveillance, prevention, and service planning worldwide.

Introduction Anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa are serious eating disorders characterized by substantial disturbances in eating or eating-related behavior. Anorexia nervosa is commonly characterized by restricted intake, low body weight, and fear of weight gain, whereas bulimia nervosa involves recurrent binge eating followed by compensatory behaviors and overconcern with body weight or shape. These disorders arise from both genetic and environmental risk factors, disproportionately affect adolescents and young adults, and often follow a chronic or relapsing course, with substantial medical, psychological, and social consequences. In the Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) 2010, the health burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa was first quantified at the global, regional, and national levels, with the most recent major update conducted in GBD 2017 following the incorporation of additional data sources. Since then, no substantial new data have been added to the estimation framework, apart from limited gray literature included in GBD 2019. Updated estimates are needed to inform evidence-based health care strategies, particularly in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, which may have affected service demand and symptom burden in some settings. While other eating disorders, such as binge eating disorder and other specified feeding or eating disorder, account for a substantial proportion of eating disorders, the absence of survey-derived disability weights for these disorders remains a barrier to quantifying their nonfatal health burden within the GBD framework. Accordingly, this analysis focuses specifically on anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa and does not represent the total burden of eating disorders. We extend previous estimates of the disease burden of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa through 2023 using updated data sources and refined methodological approaches, and we forecast future prevalence through 2050. This article was produced and conducted as part of the GBD Collaborator Network and in accordance with GBD Protocol. Methods Study Overview We estimated incidence, prevalence, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) for anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, as well as mortality attributable to anorexia nervosa, by age and sex for 204 countries and territories from 1990 to 2023. The GBD 2023 iteration incorporated newly identified data and methodological refinements, including changes to improve model fit in selected locations, the application of refined crosswalks for bulimia nervosa to harmonize diagnostic criteria, and adjustment for recall bias. This study follows the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting ( GATHER ) statement (eMethods 1 in Supplement 1 ). Deidentified data were used and the study was approved by the University of Washington Institutional Review Board (9060), which waived the requirement for informed consent. Other eating disorders were not included in the estimation because there were no available disability weights for those conditions that had been derived by disability-weight surveys. Detailed case definitions are provided in eMethods 2 in Supplement 1 . Updated systematic literature reviews were conducted to identify studies reporting the prevalence, incidence, and remission of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa, as well as excess mortality associated with anorexia nervosa. Peer-reviewed literature was identified through electronic database searches of PsycINFO, Embase, and PubMed. In addition, gray literature searches and expert consultation were undertaken. Detailed search strategies for the electronic databases are provided in eMethods 2 in Supplement 1 . In total, 59 new citations were incorporated compared with GBD 2017. DisMod-MR version 2.1 was used to estimate incidence, prevalence, and remission by age, sex, location, and year. ### Effects of External Trigeminal Nerve Stimulation on Neurocognitive Functioning in Children and Adolescents With ADHD: A Phase 2b Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** ⭐ The American Journal of Psychiatry (Am J Psychiatry) *(IF: 18.1)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Eraydin IE et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 28

OBJECTIVE: Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is associated with atypical cognition and related autonomic nervous system regulation. Trigeminal nerve stimulation (TNS) received U.S. Food and Drug Administration clearance as neuromodulation treatment for ADHD based on findings of clinical improvements from a pilot trial that were not confirmed in the authors' larger multicenter UK trial. Effects of TNS on cognition in ADHD, however, are relatively unknown. The authors therefore tested secondary outcomes, that is, short- and longer-term effects of active versus sham TNS in youths with ADHD on a broad neurocognitive task battery, physiological indicators of autonomic nervous system regulation, and head motion.

METHODS: In the first confirmatory, multicenter, double-blind, parallel-group, phase 2b randomized sham-controlled trial, 150 youths with ADHD (ages 8-18 years) were assigned to 4 weeks of nightly active (N=75) or sham (N=75) TNS. Cognitive performance was assessed using a comprehensive battery of executive function and attention tasks (inhibition, sustained attention, time estimation, processing speed, and intrasubject response variability) at baseline, posttreatment, and 6-month follow-up. Autonomic nervous system regulation (heart rate, heart rate variability, and electrodermal activity) was measured via a wristband device and head motion with an eye tracker during rest or cognitive testing.

RESULTS: Intention-to-treat analysis showed no significant differential treatment effects between active and sham TNS on any cognitive or physiological outcomes after 4 weeks of treatment or on cognitive measures at 6-month follow-up.

CONCLUSIONS: TNS does not appear to improve neurocognitive performance, autonomic regulation, or head motion in youths with ADHD, extending negative clinical findings. The findings do not provide evidence to support TNS as a viable treatment to improve cognitive or autonomic nervous system functioning in youths with ADHD.

### Efficacy of Non-Invasive Brain Stimulation on the Treatment of Late Life Depression: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis
**כתב עת:** 📄 Acta Psychiatrica Scandinavica (Acta Psychiatr Scand) | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר סקירה | **מחברים:** Lin C et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 23

OBJECTIVE: This network meta-analysis (NMA) aimed to evaluate the comparative efficacy of non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) interventions for late-life depression (LLD), with treatment rankings provided as a secondary outcome.

METHODS: We conducted a systematic review and NMA of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing NIBS for major depressive disorder in adults aged ≥ 60 years. Comprehensive searches of PubMed, EMBASE, and Cochrane Library were performed up to June 2025. Two reviewers independently selected studies, extracted data, and assessed quality using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool. A random-effects NMA was implemented using the netmeta package in R. Certainty of evidence was evaluated using the GRADE framework. Primary outcomes included changes in depressive symptoms, measured by the standard mean difference (SMD). Secondary outcomes included response rates and remission rates.

RESULTS: Twenty-five RCTs involving 1344 participants and 15 NIBS modalities were included. Bilateral electroconvulsive therapy (BL-ECT; SMD = 1.13, 95% CI 0.37-1.90) and high-frequency repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation over the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (rTMS-10Hz-F3; SMD = 0.42, 95% CI 0.02-0.81) consistently outperformed sham across all efficacy outcomes. Both interventions formed closed loops of the network geometry, enabling formal assessment of agreement between direct and indirect evidence. Although inconsistency was detected in the symptom severity network, both interventions demonstrated consistent benefits across efficacy outcomes and retained moderate-certainty evidence.

CONCLUSION: BL-ECT and rTMS-10Hz-F3 emerged as the most consistently effective NIBS treatments for LLD, supported by moderate-certainty evidence. Other highly-ranked modalities require cautious interpretation due to sparse data.

REGISTRATION: PROSPERO (CRD42024628560).

### Effect of Blonanserin on Neurotrophic Factors in Patients With Non-Responsive First-Episode Schizophrenia Treated With Early Risperidone Monotherapy-A Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 Int J Methods Psychiatr Res | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Wei Y et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Dec

OBJECTIVE: To investigate the efficacy of Blonanserin in patients with schizophrenia who were refractory to Risperidone monotherapy and its effects on various neurotrophic factors.

METHODS: A total of 120 patients with first-episode schizophrenia who were hospitalized in our hospital from January 2022 to December 2023 and did not respond to early Risperidone monotherapy were selected as the study participants. They were randomly divided into a control group and a research group, with 60 cases in each group. The control group was treated with Clozapine, and the research group was treated with Blonanserin for 3 months. The scores of the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), the scores of the Generic Quality of Life Inventory (GQOLI-74), the serum brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), the serum S100β protein, the neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and the Neuregulin-1 (NRG1) levels of the two groups were compared, and the adverse drug reactions of patients were recorded.

RESULTS: After 3 months of treatment, the PANSS scores of both groups decreased compared with those before treatment, and the PANSS scores of the research group were lower. The scores of physical function, social function, and psychological function of the two groups were higher than those before treatment, and the scores of the research group were higher than those of the control group. The differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05 for all). The serum BDNF and NRG1 levels of the two groups were higher than those before treatment, and those of the research group were higher than those of the control group. The S100β and NSE levels of the two groups were lower than those before treatment, and those of the research group were lower than those of the control group. The differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05 for all). No serious adverse reactions occurred in both groups. Most patients with adverse reactions could tolerate them or improved after symptomatic treatment.

CONCLUSION: Blonanserin can effectively relieve the symptoms of schizophrenia and improve the quality of life of first-episode schizophrenia patients who have failed to respond to Risperidone monotherapy in the early stage. Due to the short follow-up time and small sample size of this study, further prospective multicenter studies should be carried out in the future to observe the long-term treatment effect and provide more favorable reference for clinical practice.

### Combining Cognitive Training and Brain Stimulation: A Novel Approach to Address Disordered Eating and Antipsychotic-Induced Weight Gain in People With Severe Mental Illness
**כתב עת:** 📄 Early Interv Psychiatry | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Lopes M et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Nov

ABSTRACT Background Antipsychotic‐induced weight gain is a distressing side effect that most often occurs within the first 3–6 months of pharmacological initiation, significantly increasing cardiometabolic risk and mortality in individuals with severe mental illness. Aims To assess the feasibility of reducing food craving and binge eating using Approach Bias Modification Training (ABMT) combined with real or sham Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS). Method In this double‐blind, parallel‐group, two‐arm, sham‐controlled trial, adults with schizophrenia/schizoaffective disorder on stable doses of antipsychotics were randomly allocated to a total of five ABMT sessions together with either real or sham tDCS administered over 3 weeks. Assessments were made at baseline (T0), post‐treatment (T1) and at a 2‐week follow‐up (T2). Feasibility outcomes included recruitment, attendance, retention, tDCS safety, acceptability, tolerability, and blinding. Secondary outcomes were changes in food craving, binge eating, approach bias to food, body mass index, and psychological symptoms. Results Twenty‐eight participants were recruited (93% of target). Retention was high (T1 = 85.7% and T2 = 78.6%); blinding accuracy was 46.4%. Medium effect sizes favoured ‘ABMT + real tDCS’ for reducing binge eating ( d = −0.6, 95% CI −1.5, 0.3), and ‘ABMT + sham tDCS’ for reducing food cravings ( d = 0.5, 95% CI −0.4, 1.4). Within‐groups, only the ‘ABMT + real tDCS’ arm showed reduced approach bias to food and approach‐avoidance bias to food, and increased avoidance bias to food (all p < 0.05). This was followed by reduced binge eating episodes ( p < 0.05). Both groups reported frequent binge‐eating episodes (‘ABMT + tDCS’ = 43%; ‘ABMT + sham tDCS’ = 28.6%). Conclusion The initiation of antipsychotic therapy is a critical clinical window for early intervention to mitigate the long‐term metabolic and psychiatric consequences of antipsychotic‐induced weight gain. Combining ABMT + tDCS is acceptable and may help reduce binge eating in individuals on antipsychotics, with a potential synergistic effect between cognitive training and brain stimulation. A confirmatory trial is needed to assess its effectiveness and should include BED diagnosis as a stratification criterion. Trial Registration: ISRCTN Identifier: 13280178

Given that rapid weight gain usually occurs within the first 3–6 months of antipsychotic therapy, the initiation of treatment provides a critical clinical window for early intervention to mitigate long‐term metabolic and psychiatric consequences (Bushe et al. 2012 ; O'Mahony et al. 2026 ). AIWG arises from alterations in central neurotransmitter systems, (including histaminergic, serotonergic, and dopaminergic pathways), as well as in peripheral hormonal signalling (Coccurello and Moles 2010 ; Kowalchuk et al. 2017 ). These result in increased appetite, reduced satiation and energy expenditure, and increased fat storage (Coccurello and Moles 2010 ; Kowalchuk et al. 2017 ), which can lead to food craving and related disordered eating behaviours, for example, binge eating, (Blouin et al. 2008 ) or to binge eating disorder (BED), overweight and obesity (Theisen et al. 2003 ; Carolan et al. 2024 ). Pharmacological treatments, including metformin and GLP‐1 receptor agonists, show moderate efficacy in preventing and managing AIWG (Carolan et al. 2024 ; Campforts et al. 2024 ; Fink‐Jensen and Correll 2025 ; Fitzgerald et al. 2022 ). However, they are associated with physical side effects (Carolan et al. 2024 ; Campforts et al. 2024 ; Fink‐Jensen and Correll 2025 ), safety concerns in the mental health population, high costs (Carolan et al. 2024 ) and weight regain after drug discontinuation (Wilding et al. 2022 ). People with SMI are reported to have higher calorie intake, particularly from energy‐dense foods, and lower energy expenditure (Manu et al. 2015 ), and adherence to dietary and lifestyle interventions is challenging and limited in this group (Manu et al. 2015 ; Fink‐Jensen and Correll 2025 ). Consequently, there is a need for strategies that target the underlying causes of AIWG, such as food cravings and binge eating, to complement lifestyle and pharmacological interventions (Alvarez‐Jiménez et al. 2010 ; Bonfioli et al. 2012 ). Cognitive training and neuromodulation techniques are relatively accessible interventions that have shown the potential to modify food‐related behaviours in people with eating disorders (Brockmeyer et al. 2015a ; Brockmeyer et al. 2019 ). Approach bias modification training (ABMT), a computer‐based form of cognitive training, aims to change implicit attention biases by training individuals to avoid a negative stimulus (e.g., high‐calorie food) and to direct their attention to a neutral or positive stimulus (e.g., non‐food objects) (Brockmeyer et al. 2015a ; Brockmeyer et al. 2019 ; Beard et al. 2012 ; Kakoschke et al. 2017 ). ABMT has been reported to lower food‐related attentional bias and approach tendencies in people who binge eat (Brockmeyer et al. 2015a ), and has been linked to reduced eating disorder symptoms in people with bulimia nervosa and BED (Brockmeyer et al. 2019 ). Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS), a non‐invasive form of brain stimulation, has been shown to reduce symptoms in major depressive, anxiety and substance use disorders (Moffa et al. 2020 ; Kekic et al. 2016 ). As the regulation of eating shares neural networks with general self‐regulatory processes, the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) has become a primary target for intervention (Van den Eynde et al. 2013 ; Van den Eynde et al. 2010 ).
