### Predicting Depression Risk in Early Intervention in Psychosis Services: A Machine Learning Study Utilising Electronic Health Records and Natural Language Processing
**כתב עת:** ⭐ Psychotherapy and Psychosomatics (Psychother Psychosom) *(IF: 15.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Mena S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

INTRODUCTION: Depression is prevalent in early psychosis and associated with poorer functional outcomes and suicide risk. Early identification of depression risk is critical to inform targeted interventions. This study aimed to develop models to predict depression risk using electronic health records (EHRs) from patients accepted into South London and Maudsley (SLaM) Early Intervention in Psychosis Services.

METHODS: EHRs from 3,559 patients were analysed using a nested cross-validated machine learning framework. Structured sociodemographic, medication, and 66 natural language processing (NLP)-derived clinical note variables were used to train support vector regressors (SVRs) and classifiers (SVCs) to predict future changes in depressive symptoms, antidepressant prescriptions (AD0-6m) and treatment escalation for depression (TE0-6m). Model performance was generalised in out-of-cross-validation samples and across SLaM boroughs. Clustering analyses were also conducted to identify depressive symptom trajectories.

RESULTS: SVRs accurately predicted changes in depressive symptom scores over the first year following referral, with R² ranging 58.5%-72.1%. SVCs predicted AD0-6m with a balanced accuracy (BAC) of 65.6% and TE0-6m with a BAC of 68.0%. Patients with deteriorating trajectories were most likely to receive antidepressants, had higher antipsychotic treatment attempts and a psychotic-affective mixed presentation of symptoms. Model performance remained robust across clusters, and poor-prognosis clusters were captured in the +TE0-6m outcome.

CONCLUSION: This study provides an initial demonstration that predictive modelling of clinically documented depressive outcomes in EIP services is feasible using routinely collected EHR data. The findings address a major unmet clinical need and mark a critical step toward integrating depression risk prediction into psychiatric practice.

### Take no risk!-Persistent avoidance after interoceptive fear conditioning
**כתב עת:** 🔷 Behaviour Research and Therapy (Behav Res Ther) *(IF: 5.0)* | **סוג מחקר:** מאמר מחקרי | **מחברים:** Leissner SR et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 24

Maladaptive fear and avoidance of interoceptive threat are central to many anxiety and somatic disorders, often persisting even after exposure therapy and thereby contributing to relapse. While fear conditioning paradigms have mostly focused on avoidance in response to external threats (e.g., electric shocks), less is known about avoidance in response to interoceptive threat. Therefore, the present study investigated fear acquisition of interoceptive threat and subsequent avoidance behavior. Healthy participants (n = 35) underwent a differential fear conditioning paradigm in which one cue (CS+) predicted restricted breathing (US), whereas another (CS-) signaled safety. In the subsequent avoidance phase, participants were informed that they could prevent the US by pressing a button. During the consecutive response prevention phase, the avoidance option was no longer available. Finally, an avoidance test assessed the persistence of avoidance after extinction. No US was delivered during avoidance and response prevention phases. Results suggests that participants successfully learned to discriminate between CS+ and CS-, as reflected in US expectancy, fear ratings, startle eyeblink response and skin conductance responses. Although participants consistently reported greater fear of the CS + than the CS-, differential responding in psychophysiological measures was no longer observed following the introduction of the avoidance option. Participants demonstrated high levels of avoidance to CS+ and CS-, with avoidance persisting even after extinction with response prevention. The findings indicate a "better-safe-than-sorry" strategy despite awareness of threat contingencies. The generalization and persistence of avoidance may contribute to the development and maintenance of anxiety and somatic disorders.

### An mHealth Stress Management Intervention for Latino Millennial Caregivers: Pilot Feasibility Randomized Controlled Trial
**כתב עת:** 📄 JMIR Form Res | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Alshahwan S et al. | **תאריך:** 2026 Oct 27

Abstract Background Latino millennial caregivers comprise over one-quarter of millennial family caregivers in the United States. Latino millennial family caregivers face unique challenges due to cultural, structural, and economic burdens that may increase stress and stress-related health effects. These caregivers also report time limitations due to their multiple roles. Technology may be a way to deliver an accessible and flexible intervention to support stress management and emotional regulation in this population. Objective This study aimed to evaluate the feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary outcomes of a culturally tailored mobile health (mHealth) intervention to support stress management and emotional regulation for Latino millennial caregivers. Methods Participants (N=30) were recruited nationally in the United States using a recruitment agency and were randomized to the intervention or waitlist control groups. The 4-week fully automated intervention included daily mindfulness exercises and journaling prompts delivered via a custom mHealth platform. The waitlist control group received a written handout with mindfulness, journaling, and support information. Feasibility data, including recruitment and retention rates and intervention adherence, and postintervention acceptability surveys were collected. Study outcome measures were collected electronically at baseline and 2, 4, and 6 weeks. Primary outcomes included caregiver burden and perceived stress, and secondary outcomes included well-being, informational support, and emotional support. Feasibility and acceptability data were analyzed descriptively alongside study outcome measure data from participants who completed week 6 data collection (n=21). No efficacy testing was conducted. Results Enrollment was completed in 12 weeks (N=30), with a 70% (21/30) retention. Participants showed differential adherence, with 60% (6/10) completing a daily journal activity and 30% (3/10) completing a daily mindfulness activity. Adherence patterns were reflected in the acceptability findings, with journaling being preferred over mindfulness. Preliminary outcomes showed varying patterns between the intervention and control groups, with the intervention group showing decreased median burden levels from before (median 20, IQR 15-27.25) to after the intervention (median 15, IQR 10.50-28.75) but increased median stress levels from 19.50 (IQR 12-26.75) to 21 (IQR 8.75-22.75), while the control group burden and stress levels remained the same before and after the intervention. Positive affect and well-being and informational support increased for both groups. Median emotional support levels increased from 15 (IQR 12-20) before the intervention to 16 (IQR 13-19) after the intervention for the control group and decreased from 14.5 (IQR 11.5-17.75) to 14 (IQR 12-17.25) for the intervention group. Conclusions Findings demonstrate areas for further intervention iteration to address both adherence and acceptability issues with the mindfulness component of the intervention. Intervention implementation also needs to be addressed, including participant retention rates. Our team will use lessons learned alongside feedback from Latino millennial caregivers to address these issues.

Millennial family caregivers are those born between 1981 and 1996 and comprise 25% of caregivers in the United States [ 3 ]. Broadly, the millennial generation has experienced significant economic setbacks, including two global recessions and the COVID-19 pandemic [ 7 ]. These economic challenges have contributed to delayed important developmental milestones for millennial caregivers, such as educational progression, career development, home ownership, and retirement savings [ 8 ]. Millennials are also more diverse than previous generations across gender identity, sexual orientation, race, and ethnicity, and in the United States, there continue to be inequities in social and health outcomes among minoritized groups [ 6 , 9 ]. In addition to family caregiving, which is stressful, millennial family caregivers navigate complex economic, developmental, and social issues that impact their caregiving experiences [ 10 ]. Many within this group experience poverty and limited career prospects, which add to the stress of caregiving [ 16 , 17 ]. Additionally, Latino caregivers contribute a higher percentage of their income toward caregiving expenses compared to their non-Latino peers despite often holding lower-income jobs than their White, non-Hispanic counterparts [ 3 ]. These financial burdens, combined with the physical and emotional toll of caregiving, expose them to a higher risk of adverse health outcomes [ 17 , 18 ]. Finally, among Latino millennial caregivers, there are additional layers of stress due to cultural expectations such as familismo (loyalty to family) and gendered expectations ( marianismo and machismo) that may impact how the caregiving role is experienced regardless of gender [ 11 , 13 , 19 ]. Myruski et al [ 21 ] introduced the dynamic fit model as an approach to understanding acute and chronic stress. They propose that there are in-the-moment responses to high acute stress and sustained responses to lower-level chronic stress. They posit that well-being is best achieved when coping strategies are aligned with the type of stress. Mindfulness is a stress management and emotional regulation tool that emphasizes being in the moment, and it can be deployed readily without the need for specific tools or change in location [ 22 ]. Mindfulness techniques such as mindful breathing, meditation, and guided reflection exercises can induce emotional regulation, leading to a decrease in stress and depressive symptoms [ 23 ]. Through practice, mindfulness can be an orientation to life events that can facilitate stress management and emotional regulation in the moment [ 22 ]. To address sustained stress and deliberative coping, narrative interventions (ie, expressive writing, journaling, and gratitude journal) promote the ability to communicate life experiences, whether positive or negative, and have been linked to enhanced stress management and well-being and lower depression in family caregivers and other populations [ 24 - 27 ]. In our prior work with millennial caregivers, both overall millennial caregivers and Latino millennial caregivers noted the demands on their time [ 8 , 16 , 28 ]. ### A randomised feasibility trial of VR-enhanced cue exposure therapy versus neutral VR exposure for cocaine use disorder, with wearable monitoring and JITA-EMA: Feasibility randomised controlled trial protocol
**כתב עת:** 📄 PLoS One | **סוג מחקר:** RCT (מחקר אקראי מבוקר) | **מחברים:** Brobbin E et al. | **תאריך:** 2026

Background Cocaine use disorder (CUD) is associated with high rates of relapse, with craving and cue reactivity playing important roles in ongoing use. Cue Exposure Therapy (CET) aims to reduce conditioned responses to drug-related cues through extinction learning, but traditional approaches may be limited by poor ecological validity. Virtual reality-enhanced Cue Exposure Therapy (VR-CET) offers a potentially more immersive and realistic method of delivering cue exposure. Concurrently, advances in wearable physiological monitoring and just-in-time adaptive ecological momentary assessment (JITA-EMA) enable the real-time assessment of craving in everyday life. Objective To assess the feasibility of recruitment, consent, randomisation, blinding, intervention delivery and retention over 8 weeks post-randomisation. Method This is a two-arm, parallel-group, randomised feasibility trial conducted in UK community drug treatment services. Sixty adults receiving treatment will be randomised (1:1) to either VR-CET or an active VR-Relaxation control condition. Both arms will receive six VR sessions over four weeks, alongside continuous physiological monitoring using a wearable device and smartphone-based JITA-EMA for eight weeks. Feasibility outcomes will include recruitment, retention, intervention adherence, wearable wear time, EMA completion, acceptability, and safety. Clinical outcomes will include cocaine use measured by Timeline Follow-Back, cue reactivity assessed through subjective and physiological measures, and participant-confirmed craving episodes captured via wearable-triggered JITA-EMA. Qualitative interviews with participants and service providers will explore intervention acceptability and implementation facilitators, barriers and suggestions. Results Planned start date is July 2026, and enrolment is due to end in April 2027. Data analysis is estimated to be completed by the end of spring 2027. Conclusions To our knowledge, this is the first feasibility RCT of VR-CET for CUD within UK drug treatment services. Results from this trial will help assess the feasibility of integrating VR-CET, continuous physiological monitoring, and real-time craving JITA within CUD treatment. This can be used to inform the design of a future definitive trial and contribute to the development of personalised, technology-enabled approaches to relapse prevention and substance treatment. Trial registration: ISRCTN21409750, https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN21409750 .

Introduction Current treatment for powder or crack cocaine use primarily relies on psychosocial interventions, including cognitive behavioural approaches [ 1 , 2 ]. However, relapse remains common, with craving playing a central role [ 3 ]. A major clinical challenge is that craving is transient, strongly context-dependent, and often triggered by cues and emotional states encountered in everyday life [ 4 ]. Craving is both a recognised diagnostic criterion in DSM-5 and ICD-11 and a predictor of relapse. Despite its clinical importance, most evidence is derived from laboratory or clinic-based assessments, limiting understanding of how craving occurs in naturalistic settings [ 5 ]. Just-in-time adaptive ecological momentary assessment (JITA-EMA), combined with wearable physiological monitoring, offers an opportunity to capture craving and cue reactivity in real time and everyday environments. By prompting assessments during periods of elevated risk, JITA-EMA can increase the likelihood of recording clinically relevant events [ 6 ]. Wearable devices can continuously measure physiological signals associated with cocaine use and craving-related arousal, including heart rate, electrodermal activity, skin temperature, and movement [ 7 – 9 ]. Studies have also demonstrated physiological responses during cocaine cue exposure, supporting the potential value of these measures as markers of cue reactivity [ 10 – 14 ]. Virtual reality-enhanced Cue Exposure Therapy (VR-CET) uses immersive environments to simulate substance-related cues and high-risk situations within a controlled setting [ 15 , 16 ]. The approach is grounded in classical conditioning theory [ 17 ], whereby repeated pairings of cocaine use with environmental stimuli result in conditioned responses such as craving and physiological arousal. Cue Exposure Therapy (CET) seeks to weaken these associations through repeated exposure to cues without drug reinforcement, promoting extinction learning and reducing conditioned responses [ 18 ]. VR may enhance CET by providing realistic, repeatable exposure to complex scenarios that are difficult to recreate safely in clinical settings. It also enables the rehearsal of coping strategies without access to cocaine [ 19 ] and can be tailored to individual triggers across multiple environments or contexts, potentially improving generalisation of treatment effects [ 20 , 21 ]. Two linked studies conducted by this research team informed the development of the present study (Ref: 24/NW/0342 and 25/ES/0061). These studies demonstrated the feasibility of combining continuous wearable monitoring with smartphone-based EMA among people receiving treatment for cocaine use disorder and provided evidence that physiological changes associated with craving can be detected in everyday life ( in preparation ). The previous VR study further refined the VR-CET cocaine scenarios, in addition to the literature review [ 22 ] and ongoing patient and public involvement (PPI). Building on this work, the current feasibility RCT will integrate VR-CET, wearable monitoring, and JITA-EMA to evaluate feasibility, acceptability, safety, and deliverability, while generating preliminary estimates of effects on cue reactivity and cocaine use outcomes to inform a future definitive trial. The study is guided by an explicit programme theory proposing that repeated VR-CET without reinforcement will reduce cue-elicited craving and physiological reactivity through extinction learning. In parallel, wearable-triggered JITA-EMA will facilitate near-real-time identification and assessment of high-risk craving states, providing a foundation for future JITAI to support craving, relapse and recovery. To obtain variance estimates of key outcomes for future sample size calculations. To assess the feasibility and performance of the wearable-triggered JITA-EMA approach, including trigger frequency, response rates, and the proportion of triggers associated with participant-confirmed craving/high-risk states. Introduction The purpose of this systematic review is to provide supporting evidence for a clinical practice guideline on the treatment of adults with shift work disorder (SWD). Methods The American Academy of Sleep Medicine commissioned a task force of six experts in sleep and circadian medicine. A systematic review was conducted to identify randomized controlled trials, observational studies, and treatment trials in which treatments for the symptoms of excessive sleepiness and/or sleep disturbance/insomnia in adults with SWD, and for circadian adaptation to shift work schedules was tested. Evidence from controlled laboratory trials of simulated shift work and of field-based trials of shift workers with and without a diagnosis of SWD was included. Statistical analyses were performed to determine the significance of critical and important outcomes and clinically meaningful thresholds were set to determine the clinical relevance of treatments on the outcomes examined, where possible. Finally, the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) process was used to assess the evidence for making recommendations. Results The literature search resulted in 3,045 articles; 118 studies provided data suitable for statistical analyses. The task force provided a detailed summary of the evidence along with the quality of evidence, the balance of benefits and harms, patient values and preferences, and resource use considerations. Supplementary Information The online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s44470-026-00076-w .

Introduction This systematic review is intended to provide supporting evidence for a clinical practice guideline on the treatment of shift work disorder (SWD), and update the evidence review conducted for the previously published American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) guidelines. Primary critical outcomes examined include excessive sleepiness/alertness, accident risk, sleep quality, and cognitive performance/work performance. Secondary important outcomes examined include circadian alignment, quality of life, mental health, sleep outcomes other than quality (sleep latency, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, wakefulness after sleep onset), as well as disease severity. Background In our current society, shift work is a common phenomenon, whether out of necessity in fields such as healthcare, transportation, and public safety, or for economic reasons in manufacturing and consumer businesses. Shift work can be defined broadly as being required to regularly work during times when an individual would normally be sleeping. Shift work schedules can range from requirements to work fixed early morning, evening or overnight shifts or can consist of a schedule of rotating, split or irregular shifts. Based on those definitions, according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, approximately 16% of the US work force is engaged in shift work with some occupations having more than 33% engaged in shift work [ 1 ]. As a result of working on a shift work schedule, individuals can develop symptoms of insomnia from attempting to sleep when due to their circadian biology they would normally be awake and/or symptoms of excessive sleepiness from working during times they would normally be sleeping. To be diagnosed with shift work disorder (SWD), an individual must have clinically meaningful symptoms associated with working a shift work schedule that have been present for at least 3 months. It is important to note that not all individuals who engage in shift work develop symptoms of SWD. A recent meta-analysis determined the overall prevalence of SWD among shift workers to be ~ 26.5%, but with a range of 2.3% to 84% depending on the study [ 2 ]. Not all individuals with SWD will experience the same constellation of symptoms. As such, the International Classification of Sleep Disorders [ 3 ] has proposed three distinct phenotypes of SWD: 1) insomnia 2) sleepy and 3) mixed. When determining the appropriate treatments for a patient with SWD, it is important to evaluate which symptoms a patient is experiencing, as not all treatments recommended will be appropriate for all patients. In addition to symptoms of insomnia and sleepiness, leading to impairment in cognitive and work performance and safety, there are also health consequences associated with shift work and SWD, stemming primarily from a combination of insufficient sleep (i.e., < 7 h per 24 h day [ 3 ]) and circadian misalignment (e.g., wakefulness, food intake, and activity occurring when the endogenous circadian system is promoting sleep and sleep occurring when the endogenous circadian system is promoting wakefulness [ 4 , 5 ]). Whether from difficulty sleeping, or from competing demands of nighttime work and daytime familial or social responsibilities, shift workers experience an average weekly reduction in total sleep time of 10 h or more. Even when on a fixed shift schedule, individuals engaged in shift work often do not align their circadian system with the shift work schedule [ 6 ], with alignment being a near impossible goal while on rotating shifts. As a result, shift workers may be in a state of circadian misalignment with the environment (wakefulness occurring during the solar night and sleep occurring during the solar day), as well as in a state of internal circadian misalignment between components of the internal circadian system, resulting in symptoms that extend beyond difficulties with sleep and alertness. The daily production of melatonin occurs with a circadian rhythm and represents the timing of the brain’s endogenous master circadian clock located in the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the hypothalamus. For a day worker, melatonin levels typically increase in the evening before bedtime, representing the beginning of the biological night, and levels typically decrease in the morning after waketime [ 7 ], representing the beginning of the biological day. Exposure to light at night can acutely reduce melatonin levels as well as shift the timing of the circadian rhythm of melatonin [ 8 , 9 ]. The melatonin rhythm in some night shift workers remains similarly timed to that of day workers (i.e., high levels at night), whereas in others the melatonin rhythm is shifted/timed earlier or later [ 5 ]. In humans, the rise of melatonin levels is a circadian signal to prepare for sleep and associated functions that occur preferentially during the biological night. High melatonin levels are incompatible with behaviors associated with shift work schedules such as wakefulness, food intake, and being physically active. The endogenous circadian system also includes local clocks in brain and body tissues outside of the SCN [ 10 – 15 ]. These local clocks are important components of the internal circadian system that time cellular and tissue physiology to prepare the body for time-of-day relevant behaviors and environmental exposures (e.g., metabolic tissues are prepared for nutrient intake, cardiovascular and neuromuscular systems are prepared for physical activity, the immune system is prepared for exposure to pathogens). Shift work is associated with an increased risk for adverse health outcomes, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, hypertension, obesity, gastrointestinal disease, depression, negative reproductive outcomes, and cancer [ 16 – 23 ]. These adverse health effects are thought to be due to a combination of the impact of sleep disruption, circadian misalignment, and internal circadian desynchrony (i.e., misalignment among internal circadian clocks). ### The effectiveness of addiction prevention training based on dialectical behavior therapy on family friendship and loneliness in adolescent boys

BACKGROUND: The ever-evolving addiction poses a serious threat to the younger generation today. Addiction mostly threatens individuals with mental vulnerabilities and poor interpersonal relationships—both within the family and among peers. Thus, having an effective communication skill serves as a critical component in addiction prevention programs. This study aims to investigate the effectiveness of the dialectical behavior therapy (DBT)-based addiction program on adolescents, focusing on its impact on family connectedness and feelings of loneliness. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This semi-experimental study employed a pretest–posttest–follow-up design with two experimental and control groups. The study population consists male adolescents aged 12–18 in Isfahan during 2024. Forty male adolescents were selected through a purposive sampling, considering the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The selected participants were then randomly assigned to the experimental or control groups. All participants were assessed three times during the three stages of the study, using the Family Connectedness Questionnaire and the UCLA Loneliness Scale. The control group was placed on a waiting list, while the experimental group received ten 90-min sessions of the DBT-based addiction prevention training. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, including mean and standard deviation, as well as the repeated-measures analysis of variance. RESULTS: The results indicated that the intervention significantly improved both family connectedness and reduced feelings of loneliness ( P < 0.05).
